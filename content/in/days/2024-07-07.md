  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mark 6:1-6"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Jesus went out from Capernaum to his hometown, Nazareth, where he spent thirty years of His life. It is here he grew up, he learned to eat, walk and work, and it is here he prepared himself before He began His public ministry. He will now be surrounded by people who have known him all his life, who saw him grow from childhood to adulthood, who regard him as one of their own. Does he expect to be honoured as a success-story, who has brought honour to his place of origin? They belittled him, lack of formal education, they made light of his occupation as a carpenter and they criticized his family. You may recall that in the synagogue at Capernaum, the people were amazed and accepted his new way of teaching. But in the synagogue of Nazareth, they were offended and they rejected him. Crying out: where did this man get all this? Jesus knows very well that “nobody is a prophet in his own country”. In fact, where there is no acceptance or where there is no faith, people can do nothing. Thus, He goes to the other villages, gets the disciples involved in the mission, instructing them that this should be a lesson for you also to carry on with your mission.

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      The people of Jesus’ hometown found him too familiar, ordinary and like one of them. They knew him as the carpenter, the son of Mary. They were amazed at his work but failed to accept his divine power. In spite of being close relatives in his hometown, they lacked faith in Him. Due to their unbelief, Jesus was unable to perform any mighty deeds there, apart from healing a few sick people by laying his hands on them. Although we are aware of his presence in our lives, we still live in sin and fail to follow his teachings. Saint John says, “Whoever confesses that Jesus is the Son of God, God abides in him, and he in God” (1 Jn 4:15). Putting our faith in Jesus is the secret of the indwelling of God in our hearts. Let us not allow Jesus to be amazed at our shallow faith, but rather marvel at our deep faith in Him.

    audio: null
    body: |
      This learned and Apostolic Father who flourished in the 2nd century, hailed from Sicily (Italy). Pagan by birth and a Stoic Philosopher by profession, St Pantaenus’ esteem for virtue led him into an acquaintance with the Christians, and being charmed with the innocence and sanctity of their conversation, he opened his eyes to the truth and committed his life to it.

      He studied the Holy Scriptures under the disciples of the apostles, and his thirst after sacred learning brought him to Alexandria, in Egypt, where the disciples of St Mark had instituted a celebrated school of Christian doctrine. Pantaenus sought not to display his talents in that reputed mart of literature and commerce; but his great progress in sacred learning having been discovered he was drawn out of the obscurity of his humility and placed at the helm of affairs some time before the year 179, whence he further raised the status of the well-known Christian school above all other schools of philosophy. As Clement of Alexandria wrote about him, he, “the true Sicilian Bee, gathering the flowers of the prophetic and apostolic meadow, engendered in the soul of his hearers an unfading element of knowledge.”

      The Indians (i.e., probably people from South Arabia) who traded at Alexandria entreated him to pay their country a visit, whereupon he forsook his school and went to preach the Gospel to the Eastern nations, where he found some seeds of the Faith already sown. In fact, he brought back with him to Alexandria a book of the Gospel of Saint Matthew in Hebrew, which Saint Bartholomew had carried there.
