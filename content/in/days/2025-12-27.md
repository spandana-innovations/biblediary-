       </span></span>*Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      John was the son of Zebedee the fisherman and Salome, a close relative of Mary. John and his brother, James the Greater, were fishermen, partners of Peter and Andrew; they were disciples of John the Baptist before they were called by Jesus as his Apostles. John’s name is mentioned always after his brother’s name in Matthew, Mark and the Acts of the Apostles*.* John was the Apostle beloved by Jesus and one of the three constituting Jesus’ inner circle of friends who witnessed Jesus’ raising of the daughter of Jairus from the dead, His Transfiguration on the mountain and His agony in the garden of Gethsemane. After fleeing with the others from Gethsemane, John returned. He remained faithful to Jesus at the palace of the High Priest during Jesus’ trial by the Sanhedrin, and he had the courage to be at the foot of the cross, supporting and consoling Mary. He was entrusted by Jesus with the care of His mother, and, after the Resurrection, John was the one who first recognized the risen Jesus on the shore of the Sea of Galilee.



      Missionary activities: With Peter, John played a prominent role in founding and guiding the Church. John was with Peter when the latter healed the lame man (Acts 3:1), was in prison with him (Acts 4:3), and was with him when Peter visited the new Christians in Samaria (Acts 8:14). John left for Asia Minor and Ephesus when King Herod Agrippa I started persecuting Christians. He returned to Jerusalem in AD 51 to attend the Jerusalem Council. According to tradition, when the attempt of Emperor Domitian to murder John by putting him in boiling oil failed, John was exiled to Patmos Island. As an Evangelist, John wrote five books of the New Testament: The *Gospel according to John*, three epistles and the *Book of Revelation*. He preached always about God’s love in his old age. Returning to Ephesus, John lived there, dying when he was one hundred years old. John reminds us of the greatest commandment of love given by Jesus: “*Love one another as I have loved you.*”

      Every day of the Christmas Octave is filled with meaning that reflects on the Nativity – not just the Birth of Christ, but its impact on the lives of different people. Today we celebrate the Feast of St John, the Evangelist – the Martyr of Will and Love! The life of the Apostle of Love epitomized love, resonating through his writings and thoughts. This disciple “who was loved by Jesus” received two unique places that none of the other apostles received – 1) Near the heart of Jesus – in the bosom of His Divine Lord! 2) Near the Cross – in the crucible of the suffering of His Divine Lord! With St John, let us also seek nearness to our Beloved Lord – close to His Heart; and near His Cross, facing every pain courageously.

    body: |
      John and his elder brother, James the Greater, were “Sons of Thunder”, i.e. Zebedee, a wealthy Galilean fisherman. Both disciples of John the Baptist until Jesus called them, they and Peter were the three Apostles privileged to witness the Transfiguration. This “disciple whom Jesus loved” was permitted into the Palace of Caiphas on the night of our Lord’s betrayal, “being known to the High Priest”; the only one of the twelve to remain at the foot of the Cross, it was to his loving care that the dying Saviour entrusted his Immaculate Mother.



      The first to recognize Jesus in his appearance on the shores of Galilee, John bore witness to Jesus’ Incarnation which he saw as the living proof of God’s closeness to His people. On cessation of the persecution by King Herod Agrippa I, John went to Asia Minor and, with Ephesus as the centre, founded and governed “the Seven Churches”. Here, among the pagans where the “Logos” (Word) was a familiar term and denoted the divine Redeemer for whom they had longed, John wrote his “Spiritual Gospel”, carefully amplifying the three Synoptic Gospels in sublime detail, stressing Christ’s divinity.



      Banished to Patmos by Emperor Domitian, he wrote the Apocalypse detailing the prophecies concerning the culmination of the ever growing conflict between the followers of Satan and of God in the destruction of earth and the second coming of Christ. Termed the Divine, i.e., the “Theologian” for the sublimity of his writings, his ascribed symbol is the eagle that soars unafraid towards the sun. As such, his frequent admonitions - “Children, love one another!” “Let us not love merely with words, but in deed and in truth!” “If you do not love your brother whom you can see, how can you love God whom you cannot see?” “God is love!” imply his tender solicitude towards his flock.



      Saint John, revered as the Patron of Asia Minor, is invoked against rain, hail and lightning.
