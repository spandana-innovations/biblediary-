  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mathew 10: 24-33"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today’s Gospel passage comes from the end of Jesus’ instruction to the apostles, sending them forth to carry on the mission of preaching and healing and instructing them to live simple lives, expecting opposition and rejection. Predicting future opposition and persecution, Jesus encourages the apostles to stand firm, three times urging them, and us, *“Do not fear!” “Do not be afraid*!” Thus, we know that we, too, will be successful despite the opposition we encounter.



      Have no fear: Jesus gives three reasons why the apostles, and we, should not be frightened. Thefirst reason is that their mission will succeed, and opponents will not be able to prevent Jesus’ followers from succeeding in their mission because God will expose their evil plans and deeds: “*Nothing is covered up that will not be uncovered.”* The Lord “*will bring to light the hidden things of darkness”* (1 Cor 4:5), will vindicate the faithful, and will not permit evil to win (v. 26). The second reason not to be afraid is the limited power of our opponents. They can kill the body, which dies all too soon anyway, but they have no power over the soul. The third reason we should not be afraid is that the providential care and protection of their Heavenly Father who cares for all His creatures will never fail. Jesus tells us that we are more important to God than sparrows “sold at two for a penny.” The God Who cares for a trivial bird like the sparrow also cares about our smallest problems – even counting the hairs on our heads. While this is an encouraging assurance, we may find it difficult to believe in the midst of persecution.Life message: 1 Be not afraid: We can suffer from many fears: (A) Fear of Loss: i) Loss of life by illness or accident; ii) Loss of dear ones – spouse, children, parents; iii) Loss of belongings and property or savings; d) Loss of job; e) Loss of good name and reputation by slanderers (B) Baseless fears due to mental illness. C) Global fears about terrorist attacks, nuclear holocaust, plagues, like Covid-19, war etc. 2) When we are afraid let us remind ourselves that God cares – we are each a dear child of His and He cares for each of us. “*Don’t be afraid; you are worth more than many sparrows*.”



      *(Fr Tony: https://frtonyshomilies.com/)*
  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      The key phrase in today’s Gospel text is ‘do not fear’, through which Jesus encouraged His disciples to face persecution boldly. This phrase occurs three times, and the reason for this exhortation too is threefold. The disciples should not be afraid because, in the course of time, everything will be fully revealed and they will be vindicated. Further, their persecutors can destroy only the body but not the soul, which is more precious than the whole world. However, the most important reason is the Father’s providential care, which never fails. Even the insignificant sparrows experience His daily care. Since Jesus’ disciples are more valuable than many sparrows, He is concerned about the most trivial aspects of His children’s lives, namely, the hairs on their heads. Since the Father loves and cares for them beyond measure, they need not fear their persecutors.

    saintName: "Saint Benedict of Nursia Abbot Confessor (c.480 - c.547)."
    ref: "Saint Benedict of Nursia Abbot Confessor (c.480 - c.547)."
    audio: null
    body: |
      Born of noble parents in Nursia in Italy, Benedict spent his youth in Rome, but the dissolute lives of his fellow students and the state of the country, then under the Arian Theodoric the Goth, prompted him at age 16 to seek salvation as a lone hermit in a hidden cave. In time, maturing rapidly in mind and character, his sanctity, miracles, and his gift of directing souls attracted numerous earnest disciples whom he organized into 12 monasteries, each comprising 12 monks, with its own superior.



      Accepting a donation of land on Monte Cassino, he established the great abbey which became the motherhouse of the Benedictine Order for the reclamation and regeneration of Christian Europe, an accomplishment that was to earn him the title “Father of Europe” from Pope Pius XII, and “Patron Saint of all Europe” in 1964 from Pope Paul VI.



      His monks vested manual labour with a new dignity, adding to it prayer, the “Opus Dei”, the humble acknowledgement of God’s presence throughout the day by brief public acts of faith, praise and adoration. Their vows comprised poverty, chastity, obedience and the stable attachment to one monastery for life. In contrast to the later Franciscan idea of poverty, the Benedictine monastery was to be not only self-supporting, but in a position to succour the poor, the sick, the afflicted and give shelter to the stranger.



      By their teaching, studying and copying of manuscripts, the Benedictines preserved the Greek and the Roman classics through the Dark Ages. Though not a priest himself, Benedict’s Order has given the Church no less than 24 popes, 4,600 bishops and over 5,000 saints!
