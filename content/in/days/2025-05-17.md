      Reflection: 1



      Anyone who knows Jesus knows the Father also because the Father and Jesus are one. But the Apostles do not grasp this for now. Therefore, Philip asks Jesus, “Show us the Father.” They are still not able to understand how Jesus is in the Father and the Father is in him. But Jesus insists that this is the truth which they have to believe. The works that Jesus had done prove that fact. Jesus enables those who believe in him to do what he has done, and even greater works because the physical presence of Jesus would not be with them anymore; he is going to the Father. Yet, if they ask anything in the name of Jesus, he would do it for them. Thus, they will be able to do the works of Jesus, for it is he who does it for them at their request. </span></span></span></span>



      *— Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP</span></span>*</span></span>



      Reflection: 2



      Answering Philip’s request at the Last Supper, Jesus explains, in today’s Gospel selection, the unity and oneness of the Three Persons of the Holy Trinity, the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit. Jesus clarifies the abiding presence of each Person of the Holy Trinity in the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit. Hence, Jesus is the visible expression of the invisible God. In order to see what God looks like, we have only to look at Jesus, and in order to hear how God speaks, we have only to listen to Jesus. In Jesus we see the perfect love of God – a God Who cares intensely, and Who yearns for all men and women, loving them to the point of laying down his life for them upon the Cross. Jesus makes visible a God Who loves us unconditionally, unselfishly and perfectly. If we put our trust in Jesus and believe in him, Jesus promises that God the Father will hear our prayers when we pray in Jesus’ Name. That is why Jesus taught his followers to pray with confidence, *Our Father who art in heaven ..give us this day our daily bread … (Matthew 6:9,11; Luke 11:2-3). * Life message: 1) We believe that God dwells within our souls in the form of His Holy Spirit, making us the temple of God where we have the indwelling presence of the Triune God, the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit living. Hence, it is our duty to live always aware of the real presence of God within us and to adjust our life, accordingly, doing good to others and avoiding evil.

      The Gospel of today presents a question by Philip that has its basis in his doubt about the identity of Jesus. We are all characterised by our doubts about God. We doubt God and His promises during hard times, His existence in view of everything that is going on in the world, and at times everything about God. Our questions and eventual answers would never deeply satisfy us as they lead to more such questions. Here we need to focus on the answer given by Jesus to Philip. He reminds Philip of his presence with them all these years. For us also, all doubts related to God would have satisfying answers when we realise God’s presence with us “all these years, i.e., the many instances of God’s presence in our concrete life situations.

    ref: "St Paschal Baylon, Confessor (1540-1592)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      A peasant Spanish lad, St Paschal was so named after the Spanish term for Pentecost, viz., “Pasch of the Holy Spirit”. Even as a child he showed a glowing devotion to the Holy Eucharist. He worked as a shepherd until his 22nd year, when he entered a Franciscan monastery of the strict Alcantarine Reform as a lay brother and served mostly in the capacity of door-keeper at various friaries in Spain.



      Paschal’s unfailing courtesy and tender charity towards the poor and afflicted was proverbial. He would be lost in prayer for hours before the Blessed Sacrament, sometimes in ecstasy, and from this divine source of wisdom would receive instruction in the most profound truths of religion. Although he had never received more than a most rudimentary education, his spiritual insight and sanctity were evident. He was on terms of intimate friendship with contemporary Saints, and his counsel was sought by people in every walk of life.



      Journeying on foot through Huguenot France in 1570, he so triumphantly defended the Catholic doctrine of the Real Presence of Our Lord in the Eucharist against the blasphemous assertions of a Calvinist minister that he was all but lynched by a mob.



      St Paschal died on 17 May 1592. Canonized by Pope Alexander VIII in 1690, he was declared patron of all Eucharistic congresses and confraternities in 1897.
