      Reflection: 1



      After the event of the Transfiguration and the voice from the cloud had confirmed that Jesus is the beloved Son of the Father and His final Word that all should listen to, the disciples asked Jesus why the scribes say that first Elijah must come. Referring to John the Baptist, Jesus said, Elijah had already come, but the people did not recognise him, and did to him whatever they pleased. Similarly, Jesus himself, the Messiah, would suffer at their hands. The people failed to repent at the preaching of John and of Jesus. Let us pray that we may listen to God’s Word with docility and benefit from it by following His guidance.</span></span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel describes the warning and instruction given by Jesus to Peter, James, and John as they were coming down the mountain after witnessing Jesus’ Transfiguration. Jesus forbade them to give any publicity to what they had seen, because people were expecting a conquering political messiah with Elijah as his forerunner, and a powerful reformer who would destroy evil and restore justice in the land for the Messiah to rule.</span>



      The Expected Messiah. Then Jesus indicated that He was the expected Messiah, and that John was the Elijah they had been waiting for. John’s mission had been to prepare the way for the first coming of the Messiah, as Elijah’s mission would be to prepare the world for the Messiah’s second coming at the end of the world. The scribes misunderstood and taught that Elijah would come before the first coming of the Messiah. But Jesus told the disciples that (for those who were willing to believe it), John the Baptist had served as Jesus’ Elijah in announcing and preparing the people to receive a Messiah, who would fulfil the Messianic mission not by political power, but by suffering and death.</span>



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span></span>



      1) Let us accept Jesus as our Lord and Savior, who became our Messiah by dying for us on the cross. </span>



      2) We do so by cooperating with our Savior in our eternal salvation, by obeying Jesus’ commandment of love and by following the instructions given by the Church Jesus founded.</span>

      Life often finds us entangled in trivialities, veiling the profound promises of God. Something similar happened to the people of Israel as well. God had promised them of the coming of the Messiah, but they ‘missed the point’ and failed to recognise the Saviour when He came! They even refused to accept God’s message of repentance offered through the prophetic preaching of John the Baptist! Many times, we too ‘miss the point’ in our encounter with the Lord. As Christmas draws near, Jesus remains the focal point. Let us not miss His presence amidst the festivities and its distractions. Let us align our hearts with His Word, embrace His guidance and harmonise our lives with His Holy Will. Let us pray to remain focussed on God’s Word, God’s Will and God’s Promises!

    saintName: "Saint Lucy (Virgin, Martyr)"
    ref: "Saint Lucy (Virgin, Martyr)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      One of the earliest Christian martyrs, Lucy’s parents were wealthy nobles of Syracuse, Italy. Her father died young, and, though Lucy had consecrated her virginity to God, her widowed mother, Eutychia, insisted upon her betrothal to a young noble pagan. Perhaps Lucy hoped, like Saint Cecilia, to be able to convert her husband and persuade him to respect her dedication.



      Eutychia suffered acute haemorrhages and at her daughter’s suggestion journeyed with her to the miracle-working tomb of Saint Agatha at Catania, where she was instantly and completely cured. With a grateful heart she now gave in to her daughter’s plans to distribute a large part of her inheritance among the poor. This, however, aroused the covetousness of her worldly-minded fiancé to such an extent that he denounced her before the governor of Sicily as a Christian.



      It was the time of the fierce Diocletian persecution. In the Emperor’s mind the worship of the Roman gods was inextricably bound with unity and loyalty to the State. Thus, to strengthen the empire, the Christians would have to conform or be exterminated, though they might in every other respect be staunch patriots.



      Due to Lucy’s resoluteness, she was subjected to horrible and shameful torture—she was ordered to be exposed to prostitution in a brothel, but this was thwarted by divine intervention; she was next ordered to be burnt to death but proved impervious to the flames. She finally died on 13 December 304, a sword thrust through her throat. She is the Patroness of Syracuse (Sicily), and, possibly because of her name, which is suggestive of light or lucidity, she was invoked against eye-trouble in the middle ages.



      Reflection: “Those whose hearts are pure are the temples of the Holy Spirit” (Saint Lucy).
