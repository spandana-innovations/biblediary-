      Reflection: 1



      John the Baptist, in prison, heard about the activities of Jesus and wanted to know if he was the Messiah. Jesus told John’s messengers to go and report to him what they saw and heard: the blind, the lame, the lepers and deaf are healed, and the good news is preached to the poor. Jesus shows God’s preferential love for the poor and for those rich people who are kind and helpful to the poor. All the needy and the outcasts are poor, and they are privileged to receive the kingdom. Thus the good news is announced to the poor by Jesus the Messiah.



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s readings invite us to rejoice at the rebirth of Jesus in our lives as we are preparing for our annual Christmas celebration. Today is called *Gaudete* Sunday because today’s Mass begins with the opening antiphon: “*Gaudete in Domino semper,*” i.e., “Rejoice in the Lord always.” So, to express our joy in the coming of Jesus as our Savior into our hearts and lives, we light the rose candle in the Advent wreath, and the priest may wear rose-coloured vestments.



      Scripture lessons summarized: The prophet Isaiah, in the first reading, encourages the exiled Jews in Babylon to rejoice because their God is going to liberate them from slavery and lead them safely to their homeland. The Refrain for today’s Responsorial Psalm has us sing, *“Lord, come and save us!”* In the second reading, James the Apostle encourages the early Christians to rejoice and wait with patience for the imminent second coming of Jesus. Finally, in the first part of today’s Gospel reading, Jesus encourages John the Baptist in prison to rejoice by casting away his wrong expectations about the Messiah and simply accepting Jesus’ healing and preaching ministry as the fulfilment of the Messianic prophecy of Isaiah. In the second part of today’s Gospel, Matthew presents Jesus, the true Messiah, paying the highest compliments to John the Baptist as his herald and the last of the prophets, and giving special credit to the courage of John’s prophetic convictions, asking his listeners to rejoice in the greatness of his herald.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span>1) We need to learn how to survive a Faith crisis: If John the Baptist, even after having had a direct encounter with Jesus, the Messiah, had his doubts about Jesus and his teachings, we, too, can have our crises of Faith. On such occasions, let us remember the truth that all our Christian dogmas are based on our trusting Faith in the Divinity of Jesus who taught them, and on his Divine authority which he gave to his Church to teach what he taught. Hence, it is up to us to learn our Faith in depth and ask the Lord to remove our doubts.



      2) “Go and tell others what you hear and see.” We rejoice at the thought that Jesus is going to be reborn in our lives, deepening in us his gifts of love, mercy, forgiveness and the spirit of humble and sacrificial service during this Christmas season. Hence, let us joyfully share God’s bountiful grace, forgiveness, and mercy with others. What Jesus commanded John’s disciples, he commands us as well: *Go and tell others what you hear and see.* This means that we have to share with others our experience of the rebirth of Jesus within us.



      3) We need to open our hearts and let God transform our lives: Today’s readings remind us that our lives can also be transformed if we are patient and place our trust in God. The message of Advent is that God is present among us, in our everyday lives. We must prepare our hearts to recognize and welcome him by allowing a *metánoia* (a change of thinking about God, ourselves, and the world) wrought by the Holy Spirit, to take place in us during Advent.

      The Gospels present John the Baptist in three important locations: the wilderness, near the River Jordan, and in the prison. Here is an important lesson that John the Baptist teaches us: There will be times when you are in the limelight – preaching powerfully and baptizing mightily. But there would also come times when you are banished to isolation like a prison cell – with only the four walls of loneliness for company! In every moment of life – be it the ‘glory of the wilderness,’ ‘the beauty of the rivers of admiration’ or the ‘isolation of the prison’, can we remain faithful to God’s Will? We need to realise that God is pleased with our fidelity more than our performances! We need to be zealous for the ways of God, knowing that He will turn everything for our good.
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint John of the Cross - Confessor, Doctor of the Church"
    ref: "Saint John of the Cross - Confessor, Doctor of the Church"
    audio: null
    body: |
      John was born premature in 1542. Given the kind of upbringing fate provided, he obtained his education at a paupers’ school. After that he worked for seven years in a hospital, simultaneously frequenting a Jesuit school at Medina. At 21 he became a Carmelite and strictly followed the ancient unmitigated rule of the Order in all its austerity and was ordained a priest four years later.



      At the persuasion of Saint Teresa of Avila he did for the Carmelite Order of men what she was doing for the nuns. Thus the first reformed house for Discalced Friars was established at Duruelo on 28 November 1568, with the first three friars took their vows in “surpassing poverty, austerity and fervour”. Other foundations followed in rapid succession, and for five years John of the Cross, as he now called himself, was confessor to Saint Teresa and her nuns at Avila. The reform caused much internal strife and passionate disagreements which led to his imprisonment for nine months and mistreated and slandered until his miraculous escape. But it was precisely during his incarceration that he had his greatest spiritual illuminations.



      John died on 14 December 1591 and his incorrupt body was transferred to Segovia in 1592. Beatified in 1675, canonized in 1726 John of the Cross is known as the supreme “Mystic of the mystics”, the Church’s Doctor (since 1926) of mystical theology for his masterly exposition of Catholic mysticism in his “Ascent of Mount Carmel”, “The Dark Night of the Soul”, “The Spiritual Canticle”, and “The Living Flame of Love”.
