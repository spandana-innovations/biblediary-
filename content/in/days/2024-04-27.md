  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "John 14:7–14"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Answering Philip’s request at the Last Supper, Jesus explains, in today’s Gospel selection, the unity and oneness of the Three Persons of the Holy Trinity, the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit. Jesus clarifies the abiding presence of each Person of the Holy Trinity in the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit. Hence, Jesus is the visible expression of the invisible God. Jesus identified Himself totally with the Father. At every moment he did what the Father asked him to do (Jn 5:30; 8:28-29.38). So, in order to see what God looks like, we have only to look at Jesus, and in order to hear how God speaks, we have only to listen to Jesus. In Jesus we see the perfect love of God – a God Who cares intensely, and Who yearns for all men and women, loving them to the point of laying down his life for them upon the Cross. Jesus makes visible a God Who loves us unconditionally, unselfishly, and perfectly. If we put our trust in Jesus and believe in him, Jesus promises that God the Father will hear our prayers when we pray in Jesus’ Name. That is why Jesus taught his followers to pray with confidence, *Our Father who art in heaven ..give us this day our daily bread … (Mt 6:9,11; Luke 11:2-3).*</span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">*Life message:*</span></span></span></span>



      We believe that God dwells within our souls in the form of His Holy Spirit, making us the temple of God where we have the indwelling presence of the Triune God, the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit living. Hence, it is our duty to live always aware of the real presence of God within us and to adjust our life, accordingly, doing good to others and avoiding evil. </span></span></span>

    body: |
      We are familiar with an old hymn, ‘In the name of Jesus...’In today’s Gospel passage Jesus says, ‘If in my name you ask me for anything, I will do it.’ Jesus categorically tells his disciples that he and the Father are one. That is why the Father will grant anything to us if we ask in Jesus’ name. Where did Jesus get this conviction? The gospels tell us of how Jesus used to withdraw to a lonely place to pray. Sometimes Jesus spent the whole night in prayer after having had a busy day. It is in prayer that Jesus experienced a deep communion with his *Abba*. It is only when we spend some quiet moments every day with our heavenly Father that we experience how close He is to us.</span></span></span></span>

      Saint Zita</span></span> - *Virgin (1218-1278)*</span></span></span></span>



      Born of poor but devout parents at Monte Sagrati, near Lucca in Italy, Zita as a child, was pleasing to God. Her elder sister was a Cistercian nun and an uncle a saintly hermit. At 12, she entered the service of the Fatinelli family of Lucca where she remained until her death in 1278.</span></span></span></span>



      Zita performed her duties faithfully. She slept on the bare floor, having given up her bed to a beggar; she rose up at night for prayer, attended Mass daily, fasted often and distributed her portions of food. She endured calumny and contempt and every trial with singular meekness, and manifested to her enemies the divine magnanimity inspired by the pure love of God, overcoming them by her humility and forgiving attitude. Realizing what treasure they possessed in Zita, her employers entrusted their house and family entirely to her care.</span></span></span></span>



      One Christmas Eve as she was leaving for church her master, Pogano, threw his fur-coat over her, bidding her not to lose it. But at the entrance of the church she encountered a scantily clad man, who asked for the coat. She readily gave it to him telling him that he might retain it till she came out of the church. When the service was over, the man was not to be seen anywhere. Crestfallen, Zita returned home to the reproaches of her master. But as they sat to dine a stranger appeared at the door and returned the fur-coat before quickly vanishing, leaving in their hearts the celestial joy of Christmas! </span></span></span></span>



      Canonized in 1696, Saint Zita was declared the patron of domestic workers in 1933.</span></span></span></span>
