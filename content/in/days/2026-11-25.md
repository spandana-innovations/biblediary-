  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 21: 12-19"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today’s Gospel gives Jesus’ prophetic warning to the apostles and disciples about the sufferings they will have to bear for their Faith in Him until Jesus’ *Second Coming.* Jesus advises them to bear witness to Him in spite of persecutions, for those persecutions would also encourage the disciples to flee to remote places and to preach the Gospel among the Jews and the Gentiles. Believers, Jesus warns, will be locked up in prisons and brought for trial before kings and governors. Jesus assures them that the Holy Spirit will give them words of defense and witness-bearing. (In the Acts of the Apostles*,* we read how Stephen was given the wisdom to bear splendid witness to Jesus in Jerusalem). Since there will be divisions in families between believers and non-believers, Jesus declares, close relatives will betray their Christian family members to the pagan authorities and cause their martyrdom. But Jesus assures the disciples in today’s Gospel passage that their suffering for Him will be amply rewarded.</span></span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span></span></span> 1) Although we may not get a chance to die for the Faith, we are invited to face “dry martyrdom,” a “living death” as outcasts in our contemporary materialistic, secular, liberal, agnostic, and atheistic society. 2) We are called to bear witness to Christ by loving those who hate us, by showing mercy and compassion to those who hurt and ill-treat us, by forgiving those who continue to offend us, by accepting our sufferings without complaint, and by continuing to keep Jesus’ word in our lives.</span></span></span></span></span>

      In today’s Gospel, Jesus warns, “They will lay their hands on you and persecute you … This will be your opportunity to bear witness.” Even in persecution, there is no need for fear, as God promises to give us wisdom and words that no adversary can refute. Yes, we may face hatred because of our faith, but Jesus assures us, “Not a hair of your head will perish. By your endurance, you will gain life.” Daily, we encounter moments to bear witness to God, especially when we uphold kingdom values despite opposition. How do we handle being hated, humiliated, or persecuted? We must keep our eyes fixed on heaven, not on fleeting approval, applause, or recognition. True endurance will lead to an eternal reward, strengthening us to stand firm in faith amidst all challenges.</span></span></span>
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Catherine of Alexandria, Virgin, Martyr (4th cent)"
    ref: "Saint Catherine of Alexandria, Virgin, Martyr (4th cent)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Numbered among early women martyrs, Catherine holds a special place among the prophetic saints in the Church because of her role as philosophical defender of the Faith. In Church art, she is depicted wearing a crown, for she was of royal birth; holding a book, for she was extremely learned; and steering a spiked wheel, indicating her martyrdom. </span></span></span></span>



      Converted by a vision of Our Lady, she boldly protested the persecution of Christians under the Roman emperor Maxentius who, unable to refute the arguments she raised with him directly, had a battery of some fifty eminent philosophers assembled to counter her views. However, their admittance of defeat before her prowess rung the death-knell both for them and for her—they were burned alive while she was put in prison where she succeeded in converting not only the gaolers but also the emperor’s wife, Faustina! Any wonder then why she is considered patroness of philosophers, too! Incidentally, the spiked wheel to which she was fastened mysteriously broke apart, leaving Catherine unscathed, thus emerging as patroness of wheelwrights. Finally beheaded, angels are said to have taken her remains atop Mount Sinai, where they were discovered (c 800). </span></span></span></span>

The Gospel says: “Having loved those who were his own in the world, he loved them to the end.” The final sign of his love is that which impelled him to die for us on the cross and to leave himself as food for our souls”* (Blessed James Alberione).</span></span></span></span>
