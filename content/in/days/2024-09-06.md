  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 5:33–39"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today’s Gospel passage gives Jesus’ reply to the question asked by a few disciples of John the Baptist about fasting and feasting. Prayer, fasting, and almsgiving were three cardinal works of Jewish religious life. Hence, John’s disciples wanted to know why they and the Pharisees fasted while Jesus’ disciples were seen feasting with him and never fasting.

      Jesus’ reply: Jesus responds to their sincere question using three metaphors: the metaphor of the “children of the bridal chamber,” the metaphor of patching torn cloth, and the metaphor of wineskins. First, Jesus compares the apostles with the children of the bridal chamber, the selected friends of the bride and groom who feasted in the company of bride and groom during a week of honeymoon. Nobody expected them to fast. Jesus explains that the apostles will fast when Jesus, the bridegroom, has been taken away from them. In the same way, we are to welcome both the joys of Christian life and the crosses it offers us. Jesus uses the comparisons of the danger of using new, unshrunken cloth to make a patch for an old garment and of using old wineskins to store freshly fermented wine, to tell the questioners that they must have more elastic and open minds and larger hearts to understand and follow the new ideas they are hearing, which are in many cases different from the traditional Jewish teachings.



      Life messages:



      1) We need to be adjustable Christians with open and elastic minds: The Holy Spirit, working actively in the Church and guiding the Church’s teaching authority, enables the Church to have new visions, new ideas, and new adaptations and to replace old ways of worship with new. So, we should have the generosity and good will to follow the teachings of the Church.



      2) At the same time, we need the assistance of the Holy Spirit, Who works through the Church’s magisterium to interpret and apply Scripture – the Old Testament revelations and the New Testament teachings — and Sacred Tradition to our daily lives.



      (Fr Tony: https://frtonyshomilies.com/)
  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      With the two imageries of ‘new wine’ and ‘new garment’, Jesus categorically affirms the ‘newness’ present in his person and message. The new life offered by Jesus is incompatible with the old traditions. Christian life, in which the bridegroom Jesus is present, cannot be reduced to mere traditions, observances and practices, but is a new relationship of love and intimacy with God. This new life, based on love of God and neighbour, brings us real joy just like the joy we experience during a wedding feast. It’s a time of rejoicing and not mourning. Do we daily experience the joy and newness of the presence of Jesus in our life? Is our life based on love of God and neighbour or on mere traditions and observances?

    audio: null
    body: |
      The holy man, old father Eleutherius”, is known for his simplicity and spirit of compunction. Chosen Abbot of St Mark’s near Spoleto, Italy, he was favoured by God with the gift of miracles.

      Once, when he was lodging at a convent of nuns he was asked to take over the care of a boy who was troubled every night by an evil spirit. Eleutherius did so, and for long nothing untoward happened to the lad, prompting the holy Abbot to say: “Since the child is among the servants of God, the devil dares not approach him!”—words that obviously smacked of vanity. Thereupon the devil again entered the child and tormented him mercilessly. The Abbot, realizing his folly, humbly confessed his fault and, with his whole community, fasted and prayed till the young lad was again freed from the tyranny of the fiend.

      St Gregory the Great, unable to fast on Easter Eve on account of extreme weakness, engaged Eleutherius to go with him to the church of St Andrew, there to plead with God for his health, that he might join the faithful in the solemn penitential practice. Eleutherius prayed with many tears, and the Pope, coming out of the church found his breast suddenly strengthened so that he was enabled to perform the fast as he desired.

      St Eleutherius died in St Andrew’s monastery in Rome, about the year 585.

      <span class="subhead">Reflection:</span> *“It was his will that after death the Church should give his image in the Crucifix—that he might appear to us in a condition of utmost ignominy. And why? Precisely because he knew the worth of humility and the danger of the sin which opposes it”* (St Vincent de Paul).
