      Reflection: 1



      Peter is the chief shepherd of Jesus’ flock. Like Peter, every pastor or leader is expected to be faithful to his duty at all times. They should never forget that the second coming of Christ may take place at any time. Wherever we are and whatever we do, we should never take a holiday from our faithfulness to the Master and the duty he has given us. By our constant readiness for action in his service, we should justify the trust the Master has placed in us.



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s passage from Luke’s Gospel is the second of three eschatological discourses in the Gospel. After Jesus’ exhortation to vigilance, Peter asks a question (v. 41). Responding to Peter, Jesus tells the second “Master – Servant” parable and the parable of the treasure and the thief. These stories emphasize the necessity for Faith and vigilant preparedness in the lives of Christ’s followers. Jesus wants his disciples to be ready to do God’s will at every moment, rendering humble and sacrificial service to others.



      The interpretation: In the parable, the chief characters are a master (representing the risen Jesus), and his servants (Jesus’ followers). Jesus’ words in this passage, understood in the narrower sense, refer to the Second Coming of Jesus. Taken in a broader sense, they refer to the time of our own death, when God will call us to meet Him and to give Him an account of our life on earth. In the first part of today’s Gospel, Jesus tells us what our real treasure should be and how we are to keep it safe. That treasure is our share in the Life of God Himself (the state of sanctifying grace), which is given us in the Sacrament of Baptism, and Jesus’ promise of eternal life. But this treasure can be stolen by the devil or lost by our lack of vigilance in the midst of temptations. Jesus warns that we should be vigilant, like dutiful servants. What Jesus teaches us through this comparison is that our relationship with God the Father and Jesus His Son and the Holy Spirit must constantly be strengthened and deepened by our prayers, our Sacramental life, and the reading of Holy Scripture. It is God Who daily gives us the grace and strength to remain faithful, and He will reward our faithfulness.



      Life message:



      1) We need to remain vigilant and ready to face the Lord, mainly through prayer (listening and talking to Him). Daily prayer will help us to wait for the Lord in our daily lives



      2) Prayer will also give us the Heavenly strength to serve Jesus whenever, and in whatever form, he appears. What we frequently rediscover every time we serve, love and help other people is that God comes to us through them.

      ‘It is not easy to trust someone else’ is an experience many go through at some time or other in life. One is often let down, betrayed, or cheated even by those whom one trusts very much. Trust and fidelity are like two sides of a coin; they are also used interchangeably: a faithful servant is trusted, and a trusted servant is faithful. Jesus however, speaks to us today of how a servant can also betray the master’s trust in many different ways. If we look into our own lives, we will realize that we are also unfaithful servants. We are called to get our act together, internalize the trust reposed in us and be faithful to the mission entrusted to us till the very end!

    ref: "Pope Saint John Paul II, Pope (1920-2005)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      When Cardinal Karol Józef Wojtyła, Archbishop of Krakow, was elected Pope in 1978, he made history by becoming the first non-Italian in over four centuries and also the first Polish ever to occupy the See of Peter! Naming himself after the two conciliar popes, he signalled his determination to have the fruits of Vatican II benefit every believer.



      The youngest of three children, he received his First Holy Communion at the age of 9 and his Confirmation at 18. He enrolled at the Jagellonian University of Krakow in 1938, but when the occupying Nazis closed the university in 1939, Karol worked (1940-1944) in a quarry and then in the Solvay chemical factory to earn a living and to avoid deportation to Germany. Ordained priest in 1946 on completing his seminary and university studies post-war, he obtained a doctorate in Theology for his dissertation on faith as understood in the works of Saint John of the Cross in 1948 and defended his thesis at the Jagellonian University on the possibility of grounding a Christian ethic on the ethical system developed by Max Scheler.



      Consecrated auxiliary bishop of Krakow in 1958, he contributed significantly to the drafting of the Apostolic Constitution *Gaudium et Spes* of Vatican II (1962-1965). Appointed Archbishop of Krakow in 1964, he was created Cardinal in 1967.



      He survived an assassination attempt in 1981 in St Peter’s Square. Saved by the maternal hand of Mary, he forgave and visited the would-be assassin, Mehmet Ali Agca, in prison. Aware of having received a great gift, he further intensified his pastoral commitments with heroic generosity. He was canonized on Divine Mercy Sunday 2014.
