      Reflection: 1



      In the Temple of Jerusalem the Jews ask Jesus to tell them plainly if he is the Messiah. Jesus had already told them, but they did not believe. The focus is again on the identity of Jesus. The Jews realize that Jesus is claiming to be God. According to the Old Testament, God is he shepherd of His people Israel. Jesus clarifies that his position of being the good shepherd does not cause any disparity between himself and God his father, because they are so intimately united to each other that he can say that he and the father are one. The Jews could not understand the depth of his words; they think he, a man, is making himself equal to God. But Jesus is only proclaiming the truth. If we listen to the voice of Jesus and follow him faithfully, no one can snatch us out of his hands or out of the hands of the father who is greater than Jesus. </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      It was December during the week of the Jewish *Feast of the Dedicationof the Temple* or *Hanukkah, *a week with the year’s shortest days and longest nights. The feast was also known as the *Festival of Lights* because during this feast the Jews lighted lamps representing the Mosaic Law and put them in the windows of their homes. *Hanukkah*served as a remembrance of the cleansing and rededicating of the Temple and its altar by the Jewish military commander Judas Maccabaeus in the year 165 BC, after he had liberated Jerusalem from the control of the Seleucid Kings of Syria. The Syrian King Antiochus IV Epiphanes had profaned the Temple and its altar. It was during *Hanukkah*, when he was teaching in Solomon’s portico, that the Jews plotted to trap Jesus by asking him to declare whether or not he was the promised Messiah.</span></span></span></span></span>



      Jesus’ reply:</span></span> 1) Jesus accuses the Jews of unbelief and challenges them to believe in his Messianic and Divine claims by truthfully assessing his miracles instead of holding to their own personal ideas about the promised Messiah as a political liberator. 2) Then Jesus gives the reason why the Jews cannot believe in him. They are not among his sheep. Faith and eternal life cannot be merited by man’s own efforts: they are a gift of God, and the Jews are refusing to accept this gift from God. 3) Jesus gives the assurance that his sheep – his followers – will have eternal life and will not perish because they are protected by God his Father Who is stronger than the Evil One. 4) Finally, Jesus declares that he and God the Father are one. In other words, Jesus reveals that He is one in substance with the Father as far as Divine Essence or Nature is concerned, but He also reveals that the Father and the Son are distinct Persons.</span></span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span></span></span> 1) When doubts about our Faith haunt us, let us try to read more about our Faith, to consult Catholic experts in our locality or reliable Catholic sources on the Internet, and to pray for the light of the Holy Spirit. 2) Let us find protection from the temptations of the Evil One in the sheepfold of the Church by frequenting the Sacraments of Reconciliation and Holy Eucharist, by meditative reading of the Bible, by personal prayers, and by works of charity. </span></span></span></span></span>

      Jesus assures us that those who believe in Him will receive eternal life – an imperishable life that cannot be taken away. He promises security because no one can snatch His followers from His hand. This assurance is reflected in the first reading, where the early Christians, despite being scattered by persecution, continued to proclaim Jesus as Lord. Their faith remained unshaken, showing that nothing – neither persecution nor the loss of loved ones – could destroy their hope in Christ. This hope of living a life rooted in Jesus is echoed in the responsorial psalm, which speaks of trust and security in God’s protection. As believers, we are called to live with the confidence that, in Christ, we have eternal life, and nothing can separate us from His love and care.</span></span></span>

      (1) Saint Peter Chanel</span></span> - *Martyr (1803-1841)*</span></span></span></span>



      Born in France (*12 July*), Peter was a shepherd boy whose first encounter with his Parish Priest, Abbé Trompier, led to his education in the latter’s Latin school. As a student and as a seminarian, Peter so won the esteem of teachers and students alike that a certain bishop once commented: “He has a heart of gold with the simple faith of a child and leads the life of an angel.”</span></span></span></span>



      Peter is famed for the religious revival he achieved within three years of his appointment in the ill-reputed parish of Crozet. In 1831, Peter became a Marist missionary. In 1836, Pope Gregory XVI canonically approved this fledgling congregation and entrusted to its care the island of Futuna, New Hebrides. Well received by the people, Peter’s work drew the ire of the chieftain whose own son sought to be baptized and Peter was clubbed to death (*28 April*), and his body chopped to pieces. Nevertheless, the whole island became Christian within two years.</span></span></span></span>



      Beatified in 1889, Peter was canonized in 1954.</span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Reflection:</span></span></span>* “In such a difficult mission (evangelizing) we simply have to be holy men” </span></span>*(Saint Peter Chanel).</span></span></span></span>



      (2) Saint Louis-Marie Grignon de Montfort - </span></span>*Priest (1673-1716)</span></span>*</span></span>



      Louis was born in Brittany, France (*31 January*). Ordained in 1700, he was assigned to Nantes as a rural preacher and then to a hospital for the poor in Poitiers, which he zealously upgraded. He founded the *Daughters of Wisdom* for women, to care especially for needy children, and the *Missionaries of the Company of Mary* for priests and brothers, to continue his retreat ministry and to spread Marian devotion.</span></span></span></span>



      An avid preacher of missions to the poor, Louis was denounced by his enemies and banned from Poitiers by his bishop. He, however, returned from Rome with papal approval and, named *Missionary Apostolic* for France by Clement XI, spent the rest of his life evangelizing western France.</span></span></span></span>



      Louis promoted Marian devotion as the best means of attaining union with Christ. His book, *True Devotion to the Blessed Virgin Mary,* continues to be in use to this day.</span></span></span></span>



      Louis, who died young and suddenly (*28 April*), was canonized in 1947.</span></span></span></span>
