  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mark 10:35-45"
    audio: null
    body: |
      The context of today’s gospel happened five days before Jesus’ crucifixion, four days before his betrayal and trial, one day before the clearing of the temple, a few hours before his triumphal entry into Jerusalem. If the disciples are going to start incorporating Jesus’ teachings in their life it ought to be now. But it doesn’t even seem to happen. Moments before the most crucial events in their life they are a bickering, petty, bad-tempered and quarrelling. What do we learn from this not-so-flattering moment in the life of the disciples?






      We learn about the three poison pills of position, prestige and power. First, James and John wanted positions. Position means climbing rank and exercizing authority over others. James and John felt that they were better leaders and Jesus would come around to their view if they could but argue their case to be in the first and second positions in the kingdom. It is a motive we all are guilty of. Jesus makes it clear to them that the kingdom of God will have no hierarchy. Why is this? It is because God is love. Love does not conquer but serves. Secondly, James and John wanted prestige. Prestige is, wanting the recognition and honour that the position deserves. Prestige is not coming to the disciples. It is the Passion. It is not glory for which they are headed but the cross. Thus the first and second positions are out of the question. ‘Least of all’ is the only option or nothing at all. Thirdly, James and John wanted power. Notice that the other ten disciples became indignant when they heard what James and John were up to. It was a powerplay, a strategic manoeuvre, and the other disciples resented it. Jesus shows James and John that real power resides in humble acts of service.






      *(Kindle Your Spirit, Fr John Pichappilly)*

      All the Readings of today’s Liturgy provide us with three insights into the mission of every baptized Christian: *a) Mission involves suffering:* a missionary is moved by the suffering of his people and is ‘ready to drink the cup’ for the sake of his suffering people. Jesus was moved with compassion for the dejected people.*b) Mission involves self-sacrifice:* a missionary does not have his own selfish agenda. His agenda is to do the will of God. Jesus was sent by the Father to save the world. His food and drink were to do the will of God.*c) Mission involves servanthood:* a missionary considers himself a servant of all. Jesus proved it by washing the feet of his own disciples. *Have I found my mission? Am I ready to suffer, sacrifice my selfish interests and become a servant for the sake of the mission of Jesus Christ?*
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "St Bertilla Boscardin"
    ref: "St Bertilla Boscardin"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Christened Anna Francesca but called Annetta, this saint was calm by nature, and, though lacking in health, intellect and initiative, the local priest, Don Capovilla, recognized in her a religious vocation. It was at the age of 16 that this ardent follower of the “Little Way” of St Therese of Lisieux was accepted by the Sisters of St Dorothy at Vicenza and given the name Maria Bertilla, after the abbess of Chelles.



      Gifted with a keen sense of practical judgement, she was sanctified in the unobtrusive execution of her daily chores. For a year Sr Maria worked in the scullery, bake-house and laundry, before being sent to learn nursing at Treviso, where the Sisters of St Dorothy ran the municipal hospital. Here she worked as a kitchen-maid before being promoted to help in the children’s ward. From then on, Bertilla devoted her time to the sick but soon after took ill herself and for the remaining 12 years of her life agonized on account of a malady that surgery failed to cure and which eventually claimed her in 1922.



      Fondly remembered as something of a Florence Nightingale, a memorial plaque in the hospital at Treviso reads: “To Sister Maria Bertilla Boscardin, a chosen soul of heroic goodness, who for several years was a truly angelic alleviator of human suffering in this place.....” Crowds flocked to her first grave at Treviso and then to her tomb at Vicenza, miraculous healings attributed to her intercession leading to her beatification in 1952 and canonization in 1961.



      Reflection: *“Christ, the Master of humility, manifests his truth only to the humble and hides himself from the proud”* (St Vincent de Paul).
