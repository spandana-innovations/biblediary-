  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 1: 39-45"
    audio: null
    body: |
      The mystery of the Incarnation comes to ordinary people living ordinary lives, who have the willingness to respond to God’s call and the openness and generosity to do God’s will. Luke, in today’s Gospel, tells us how two seemingly insignificant women met to celebrate the kindness and fidelity of God. In the Gospel, one definition of discipleship is to listen to God’s word and then carry it out. Mary did both, to become the most perfect disciple. The incident also shows us how sensitive Mary was to the needs of Elizabeth, her older cousin, who had miraculously become pregnant in her old age.</span></span></span></span>



      Mary’s visit to Elizabeth. There is a saying, “*One who is on fire cannot sit on a chair.*” Mary, carrying Jesus and filled with the empowering fire of the Holy Spirit, hurried to the mountain country where Elizabeth lived, thereby conveying the Holy Spirit to her cousin and the child in her womb. Like all good Jews, Mary was prompted in everything she did by her commitment to God’s word in her life.</span></span></span></span>



      The paradox of blessedness. Blessedness confers on a person both the greatest joy and the greatest task in the world. Nowhere can we see the paradox better illustrated than in Mary’s life. Mary was granted the blessedness and privilege of being the mother of the Son of God. Yet, that very blessedness was to be a sword to pierce her heart: one day she would see her Son hang on a cross, die, and be buried. So, to be chosen by God is often both a crown of joy and a cross of sorrow. God does not choose us to give us a life of ease and comfort, but in order that we may respond to His love by accomplishing His purposes.</span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span></span></span>1) We should recognize the real presence of Emmanuel (God Is with Us) and say “yes” to Him: The Visitation of Mary reminds us that, through his holy ministry, Christ continues to be present among his people. Let us recognize and appreciate the truth that the same Christ “dwells among us” in the Bible, in the Sacraments, in the praying community, and in our souls. 2) We should convey Jesus to others as Mary did to Elizabeth. We can make a real difference in the lives of others today by carrying Jesus to them. For that, we must be filled with the spirit of Christ, allowing his rebirth within us. Then Jesus will enable us to share his love with all whom we encounter, offering them humble and committed service, unconditional forgiveness, and compassionate caring service. </span></span></span></span>

    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today is December 21. Starting tomorrow, the nights will gradually grow shorter, and the days longer. This aligns beautifully with the spirit of Christmas, which celebrates the birth of the bright morning star, symbolizing the arrival of light and hope. Mary and Elisabeth celebrate this light at their genuine encounter as is reported in today’s Gospel reading. Elisabeth humbly praises Mary, though very young, as ‘blessed among women’ and ‘the Mother of the Lord’. In return, Mary begins to praise God in her Magnificat, which we will hear in tomorrow’s Gospel reading. We, too, have various types of encounters in our lives. Some of them are honest and sincere, becoming occasions of re-energising for us. But we also experience encounters where partners keep boasting about themselves, with hardly any sharing or listening, which destroy the little hope that we have. Let us prepare ourselves for an honest encounter with the Lord.</span></span></span>
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Peter Canisius - Confessor, Doctor of the Church (1521-1597)"
    ref: "Saint Peter Canisius - Confessor, Doctor of the Church (1521-1597)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Ordained Priest at 25 (1546), at age 26 Peter Canisius assisted Cardinal-Bishop of Augsburg as Theologian at the Council of Trent. After making his solemn profession as a Jesuit in 1549—the first German to do so!—he taught Theology at the University of Vienna, preached in the cathedral and at the court of Ferdinand I, and laboured in every way to restore true Catholic doctrine, for the Church in Vienna was in a deplorable state due to acute shortage of priests.</span></span></span>



      He kept himself free (in contrast to the Protestants) from all narrow-mindedness and personal vituperation, his exemplary humility, moderation and tact, making him the most influential leader of the Counter Reformation in those lands, the “Second Apostle of Germany”.</span></span></span>



      He corresponded enormously with Saints Charles Borromeo, Philip Neri and Francis de Sales, and was in demand as adviser to pope, bishop, king, prince and commoner alike. He zealously promoted Rosary sodalities, encouraged the publication of good books, and founded some 30 schools and Papal seminaries in order to form an instructed laity and a devout clergy. Thousands were won back to the Faith and crowds thronged his confessional and the Masses he celebrated.</span></span></span>



      His ascetic and homiletic writings aside he is most famous for his Triple Catechism—for children, young people and adults—containing a lucid treatise on Roman Catholic dogma, which even in his lifetime saw 200 editions in 12 languages.</span></span></span>



      Peter died in 1597, was beatified in 1864, and was canonized and declared Doctor of the Church in 1925.</span></span></span>
