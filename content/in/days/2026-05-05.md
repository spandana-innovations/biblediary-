      Reflection: 1



      The peace that Jesus offers is not merely the absence of war or slavery or mental tension. The peace of Jesus is an inner quality which cannot be taken away by any kind of sufferings of this world. This peace arises out of our experience of salvation, the presence of the Holy Spirit in us. Experiencing peace in the midst of sufferings and tribulations St Paul remained tranquil and serene. Jesus does not promise his disciples a life without trouble; but he promises to be with them to strengthen and guide them, though his physical presence with them will end in a short time. He is going back to the Father who is greater than he is; it is the Father who sends the Son and the Son who is sent by the Father But the Father, the Son and the Spirit are one in their plan, love, intention and action.



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      In his Last Supper discourse, Jesus gives two gifts to his disciples, namely, the gift of peace and the gift of the cross leading to glory. Today’s passage refers to the gift of peace. Wishing a person peace (*Shalom*), was, and still is, the usual form of greeting among the Jews and the Arabs. *Shalom* is a right relationship with God and with others. Arabs wish each other saying “Islam Alikum” in Arabic, meaning peace be with you. And the response is “alikum Islam” (and also with you). Moses instructed the Israelites to bless others with God’s peace: “*The Lord bless you and keep you; the Lord make His face to shine upon you, and be gracious to you; the Lord lift up His countenance upon you, and give you peace”(Nm 6:22-26).*“Peace be with you!” is the greeting which Jesus used, and which the Apostles continued to use. Hence, the Church uses it several times in the liturgy. Peace is one of the great Messianic gifts. St. Paul tells us that it is it is one of the fruits of the Holy Spirit. Jesus repeats his promise saying, “*My peace I give to you, my peace I leave with you.”* Pope St. Paul VI (canonized October 14, 2018), said: “True peace must be founded upon justice, upon a sense of the untouchable dignity of man, upon the recognition of an indelible and happy equality between men, upon the basic principle of human brotherhood.”



      Life message: 1) We are invited to live in the peace wished by Jesus. This requires that we be reconciled every day with ourselves, with our neighbors, and with our God. Reconciliation with God demands that we obey His commandments, repent every day of our sins, and ask God’s forgiveness. Reconciliation with others demands that we forgive others for their offenses against us, and that we ask for their forgiveness for our offenses against them in words and deeds. Reconciliation with ourselves comes from our grace-given humble recognition of our weaknesses and failures and our grateful acceptance and use of the Holy Spirit’s loving gifts to us of deepened love and trust that God loves us in spite of these weaknesses, forgives us our sins when we repent, helps us to do better, and uses our weaknesses to bring us closer to Him, and to demonstrate His own Love and Power working through us for His glory.

    body: |
      Farewells are always painful, but they do take place. Jesus promises peace to the disciples and urges them not to be troubled by His departure. Peace is the precious gift that Jesus is leaving with His disciples, a peace that the world cannot give. The person who departs feels sad, and shedding tears by the person himself and those around him is common. Jesus is also in a similar situation, for He understands the future plight of the disciples after His departure and so He offers them peace, an act through which He wants to calm down their fears and anxieties. After His departure, the disciples will have the formidable task of taking the mission of Jesus forward, which will need several qualities: peace of mind, courage and fortitude in the face of trials. As Jesus is returning to His Father, the Father too will come to the assistance of the disciples. As the Shepherd of His flock, the Father will not let His sheep to be snatched away or scattered.

      Very little is reliably known about St Angelo. His parents were Jews who were later converted to Christianity by a vision of Our Lady who announced to them that the long-awaited Messiah had already come and had redeemed his people. She also promised them two sons, and that they both would grow up as flourishing Olive trees on the heights of the Carmel — one as a patriarch and the other as a glorious martyr.



      Early on, the twins displayed great mental and spiritual gifts. At the age of 18, when they entered the Carmelite Order, they had already mastered Greek, Latin and Hebrew. After Angelo had spent five years on Mount Carmel as a hermit, Jesus appeared to him and bade him go to Sicily (Italy), where he would have the grace to offer the sacrifice of his life.



      Obedient to the call of the Lord, he promptly left Mt Carmel for Sicily. On his way and after his arrival in Sicily he succeeded in converting many by his teaching and miracles. At Palermo (Italy), about 200 Jews sought baptism as the result of his eloquence. Similar success was met with in Leocata, but there he aroused the fury of Berengarius whose wickedness and cruelty he had denounced.



      One day as he was preaching to a crowd, he was stabbed by a band of ruffians who attacked him under Berengarius’ influence. Mortally wounded, Angelo fell on his knees. Praying for his people, more especially for his murderers, he gave up his spirit.
