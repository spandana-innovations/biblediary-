  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 6: 1-5"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today’s Gospel passage gives Jesus’ teaching on the purpose of the Sabbath and on its proper observance. This was Jesus’ response to a criticism and a silly accusation made by some Pharisees against the apostles who, to satisfy their hunger on a Sabbath, had plucked ears of grain from a field for their snack, removed the husks by rubbing the grain between their palms and blowing away the chaff. The Pharisees accused them of violating Sabbath laws by performing three items of work forbidden on Sabbath, namely, harvesting, threshing and winnowing!</span></span></span></span></span>



      Counter-arguments: Jesus gives three counter-arguments from Holy Scripture defending the apostles. (1) Basic human needs, like hunger, take precedence over Divine worship and Sabbath observance. Jesus cites from Scripture the example of the hungry David and his selected soldiers. They approached Abimelech, the priest of Nob, who gave them for food the *“offering bread” *which only the priests were allowed to eat (1 Sm 21:1-6). (2) No law can stand against Divine worship. That is why the priests were not considered as violating Sabbath laws, although they did the work of preparing two rams for sacrifice in the Temple (Nm 28:9-10). (3) Jesus quotes the prophet Hosea to remind the accusers of God’s words: *“I want mercy, not sacrifice” (Hos 6:6).* Further augmenting the counter-arguments, Jesus, as Son of Man (a Messianic title), claims Lordship over the Sabbath itself.</span></span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span></span></span> Like the Jewish Sabbath, the Christian Sunday is to be 1) a day of rest and refreshment with members of the family; 2) a day for thanksgiving and the recharging of spiritual batteries, (through participation in the Eucharistic celebration, for Catholics); 3) a day for parents to teach religious Faith and the Bible to their children; 4) a day to do works of charity in the neighbourhood and in the parish and 5) a day for socializing with family members, neighbours and fellow-parishioners. </span></span></span></span></span>

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      The Pharisees added rules to the Sabbath which served more as burdens than ways to honour God. The Rabbis made an elaborate list of “do” and “don’t” items concerning Sabbath. When the Pharisees criticised Jesus’ disciples for picking a few pieces of corn to eat on the Sabbath, Jesus defended His disciples by referring to two examples from the Old Testament. In the modern world, people often fall prey to old customs and question the freedom of the individual to change, renew, and adapt. Let us seek the wisdom from our Lord, as He says, “The Son of man is Lord of the Sabbath.” As St Teresa of Kolkata derived her strength from the Holy Eucharist, let us draw power like her to serve the poor and needy who are in the periphery.</span></span></span>

    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Teresa of Kolkata (Religious, Founder) (1910-1997)"
    ref: "Saint Teresa of Kolkata (Religious, Founder) (1910-1997)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Teresa was born in Skopje, Yugoslavia and Christened Agnes Gonxha Bojaxhiu. The youngest of three children who lost their father in their childhood, she joined Loreto Abbey in Ireland at 18, taking the name “Teresa”, choosing St Therese of Lisieux as her patron. Five months later, i.e., in 1929, she came to Calcutta (now Kolkata), where she taught for sixteen years at St Mary’s School, Entally and mastered the local language, Bengali.</span></span></span></span>



      In 1946 she got what she referred to as “the call within the call”, viz., “to be God’s love in action to the poorest of the poor”. In 1948, clad in a coarse blue-bordered white sari, a crucifix pinned at her shoulder and equipped with a 6-month intensive training course in ‘nursing’, she nursed the sick and the dying with utmost tenderness, lovingly cleansed their maggot-infested wounds, washed their naked bodies, cradled them into the Lord’s peace the way only someone divinely possessed could. </span></span></span></span>



      In 1952, she started “Nirmal Hriday” (pure heart), a home for dying destitute, followed by the opening of “Shishu Bhavan” for children; in 1957, “Shanti Nagar” for lepers; in 1963, a parallel institute for men, the “Missionary Brothers of Charity”. Winner of the Nobel Peace Prize (1979) and the Bharat Ratna (1980), Teresa walked with royalty without losing the common touch and was comfortable with Pope, politician and pauper alike. </span></span></span></span>



      She was canonized in 2016.</span></span></span></span>
