    ref: "John 6: 22-29"

    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      When the people come to Capernaum, Jesus tells them: you are looking for me not because of the sign you saw but because you ate bread to your satisfaction. They fail to understand him correctly. Many people practise religion only for selfish gains. Why do we carry on our religious practices? Do we really love Jesus and his teachings? Jesus wants us to strive for spiritual food which will give us everlasting happiness. When the Jews ask him, “What are the works that God wants us to do?” Jesus tells them that God wants them to believe in the one He has sent. Faith is basically believing in the person of Jesus as the Christ whom God has sent to redeem us.



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel introduces Jesus’ famous discourse on the Holy Eucharist which emerged within a dialogue between Jesus and the Jews who had gone around the Lake and come to Capernaum searching for him. In answer to their question about his arrival, Jesus challenged them, saying that they were looking for him so they could get another free meal and that such meals would not satisfy them. He also instructed them to labor for food that would give them Eternal Life.



      Naturally, the Jews asked Jesus what they should do to get such a food. Since the Jews believed that the Torah was the “bread of life,” many may have thought that Jesus was instructing them to keep the Torah to attain Eternal Life. So, Jesus clarified that they had to do the work of God to attain eternal life; he told them that the “work of God” was not to work miracles for their own sake but to believe in Him as the Son of God, sent to give Eternal Life to those who believed in him. While regular food helps us to stay alive in this world, spiritual food sustains and develops our supernatural life, which will last forever in Heaven. This food, which only God can give us, consists mainly in the gift of Faith in Jesus and in the grace God gives us to live according to Jesus’ teaching. Through God’s infinite love, we are given in the Blessed Eucharist the very Author of these gifts, Jesus Christ, as nourishment for our souls.



      <span class="subhead">Life message:</span> 1) Most of the time, we work for food which only nourishes the body. Jesus teaches that he is the Heavenly food, who nourishes the soul and gives us eternal life in union with God in Heaven. Hence, let us receive this Life-giving food both in the Holy Eucharist and in the Holy Scripture with proper preparation and reverence while repenting of our sins.

      Today’s readings invite us to reflect on the true purpose of our faith and devotions. Jesus tells us: “Do not work for the food that perishes, but for the food that endures to eternal life.” Jesus, thus, challenges us to examine our motives. Are we seeking Jesus only for what He can provide us in this world, or are we seeking a deeper, spiritual relationship with Him? In verse 29, Jesus calls us to believe in the one whom God has sent. Genuine faith is tested not only by our beliefs but by how we respond to the struggles and crosses of daily life. St Stephen, as we see in Acts, embodies this faith — facing persecution with courage and grace. The responsorial psalm echoes this by showing the attitude of a true believer: trust, reliance, and praise. Our faith is proven when we endure challenges with unwavering hope and love for God.

    body: |
      Saint Agnes, the daughter of the Count of Segni in Tuscany (Italy), was remarkable for her spirituality. At 9, she entered the strict Franciscan sisterhood “del sacco”, i.e., “Sisters of the Sack” (so called because of their coarse garments) and at 15 was elected the first Abbess of the new Dominican convent at Proceno, at whose foundation she had laboured the two previous years at the instance of Pope Nicholas IV.



      Some years later, the townsfolk of her native Montepulciano, desirous of her return, offered to build her a convent in a place once occupied by a house of ill fame. Thus in 1298 she founded the celebrated convent there and, placing it under Dominican patronage, governed it for 19 years until her death.



      Saint Agnes possessed numerous mystical gifts, whereby she was favoured with visions of Our Blessed Mother, the Christ-child and the angels, and cross-shaped particles like manna were known to fall on her and wherever she prayed. Her outstanding virtue, besides the purity of her prayer, was her complete faith and trust in God, and to this was attributed her great power of intercession and her fame as a worker of miracles.
