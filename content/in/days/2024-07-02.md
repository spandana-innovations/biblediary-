  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Matthew 8:23–27"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Matthew’s emphasis on Jesus’ wondrous works helps him to reveal Jesus’ true Messianic identity. The role of God in calming the storms of life is the central theme of today’s Gospel. By describing the miracle, Matthew also assures his first-century believers that nothing can harm the Church as long as the risen Lord is with them. The incident reminds us today to keep Jesus in our life’s boat and to seek his help in the storms of life.



      The storm: The Sea of Galilee is a lake thirteen miles long from north to south and eight miles broad from east to west at its widest. It is notorious for its sudden storms. When a cold wind blows from the west, the valleys, gullies and hills act like gigantic funnels compressing the storms and letting them rush down to the lake to create violent waves. Unable to control their fears, the disciples wake Jesus up, accusing him of disregarding their safety. Jesus’ response is immediate. At once he rises and rebukes the winds and the sea, and instantly there is total calm. Only then does Jesus gently chide his terrified and now astonished disciples for the smallness of their Faith.



      Life message: We need to welcome Jesus into the boat of our life to calm the storms we face. All of us are making a journey across the sea of time to the shore of eternity, and it is natural that we all will experience different types of violent storms occasionally in our lives: physical storms, emotional storms, and spiritual storms. We face storms of sorrow, doubts, anxiety, worries, temptations, and passion. Only Jesus can give us real peace in the storm of sorrow or console us for the loss of our dear ones. When the storms of doubt seek to uproot the very foundations of the Faith, Jesus is there to still that storm, revealing to us his Divinity and the authority behind the words of Holy Scripture. He gives us peace in the storms of anxiety and worries about ourselves, about the unknown future, and about those we love. Jesus also calms the storms of passion in people who have hot hearts and blazing tempers.



      *(Fr Tony: https://frtonyshomilies.com/)*
  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null

    body: |
      Jesus was able to sleep through the storm because he knew that no matter what happened, his Father loved him and would not abandon him. The disciples, on the other hand, failed to put confidence in God’s goodness and power. The same question which Jesus asked his disciples, he asks of us today: “Why are you afraid, O you of little faith?” (Mt 8:36) Our lives can be transformed overnight by circumstances that are beyond our control; we may find ourselves surrounded by a raging storm without warning. Taking refuge in the Lord will bring us strength, as he is stronger than the storm that threatens us. The secret of remaining joyful despite adversity is our faith and our confidence that he is with us in the boat. Jesus will not let the boat capsize; he is ever present in our lives and will protect us from every storm.

    saintName: "Sts Processus and Martinian Martyrs (c. -100 -)"
    ref: "Sts Processus and Martinian Martyrs (c. -100 -)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Though about their life nothing much is known, the veneration in which these two martyrs were held by the

      Christians of Rome goes beyond the 4th century. However, according to Roman Martyrology, they were warders of the Mametine prison and were converted along with 40 others by the teachings and miracles of two prisoners, Sts Peter and Paul. The warders offered the apostles their liberty. It is said that a sudden flow of water miraculously sprang up in the prison thus enabling Peter to baptize them.

      Paulinus, the officer in charge, tried to persuade both Processus and Martinian to denounce Christianity. Finding them firm in their resolve to persevere in their new faith they were subjected to cruel torture. They boldly declined to offer incense on the altar of Jupiter, and all through their suffering their only utterance was “Blessed be the name of the Lord!” until they were finally slain by the sword. They were buried by a woman called Lucina, on her own property.

      In the 4th century a Basilica was built over their tomb, where, in the course of time many miracles and healings took place drawing great crowds. At the beginning of the 9th century St Pascal I had their relics transferred to St Peter’s where an altar was built in their honour.

Be at peace, then, and put aside all anxious thoughts” (St Francis de Sales).*
