  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 14: 1-6"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Since Jesus was invited to a Pharisee’s house, and since it was the Sabbath, the food had been cooked the day before the Sabbath (because cooking was work), and kept hot till the Sabbath. During the meal in a Pharisees’ house Jesus felt sympathy for a man suffering from dropsy (distension of abdomen with water, usually the result of liver and kidney infection from recurrent attacks of malarial fever, common in Palestine) and, after asking the lawyers and Pharisees whether it was lawful to heal on the Sabbath, and getting silence for an answer, healed him, For the Pharisees this was a gross violation of Sabbath law. No wonder they considered Jesus as a reckless Sabbath-breaker for doing seven healings on Sabbath! Jesus challenged them, asking if they would not save their son or ox on a Sabbath if the child or the animal had an accidental fall into a well, a rhetorical question for which the answer was yes. They remained silent.</span></span></span></span></span>



      The purposes of the Sabbath: The Sabbath was intended by God to be: 1) a day of worship and of praising and thanking God for His goodness, providence, mercy, and blessings; 2) a day for teaching God’s law to the children; 3) a day of rest from normal work, 4) a day for socializing with the members of the family and neighbours and 5), a day for doing works of charity in the community.</span></span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead"> Life messages:</span></span></span> 1) We need to observe Sunday as the Lord’s Day by actively participating in the Eucharistic celebration and various ministries in the parish, by sending the children to Sunday schools, and by instructing them in the Catholic Faith.</span></span></span></span></span>



      2) We are also to observe this day of rest and worship of God, who is Love, by socializing with the members of our family and neighbours.</span></span></span></span></span>



      3) We are also encouraged to engage in active works of charity in our parish and community – visiting the sick and praying for their recovery, comforting them, and encouraging them with words and deeds and, if possible and needed, with financial help. </span></span></span></span></span>

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      Jesus heals a man with dropsy on the Sabbath, challenging social and religious norms and upsetting the religious leaders. By choosing mercy over strict following of rules, Jesus shows that compassion is more important than rigidly following tradition. He reveals that true faith is grounded in love and kindness, not just in observing laws. For Jesus, saving a life holds greater value than keeping customs, traditions and rules. This narrative invites us to practise our faith in ways that reflect God’s love for humanity, especially by supporting those in need. “The name of God is mercy,” reminds Pope</span>Francis. True discipleship calls us to live out our faith by uplifting others, putting love above legalism, and allowing God’s mercy to guide our actions. Are we willing to act with compassion, even if it means going against the norms?</span></span></span>
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "St Gerard Majella, Confessor (1726 -1755)"
    ref: "St Gerard Majella, Confessor (1726 -1755)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Extremely devout, Gerard, the son of poor parents in southern Italy, was blessed with a gift of profound contemplation and an ardent love of the Blessed Sacrament. “Suffer only for God, and your very sufferings will bring you heaven on earth!” Admitted by the Redemptorists on probation as a lay brother at 23, Gerard, deeply rooted in humility and purity, proved a model of perfect obedience. To the customary three vows he added one to do always that which appeared to be the most perfect — “Everything that is done for God becomes a prayer!”</span></span></span>



      God granted him remarkable spiritual gifts: ecstasies, levitations, bi-locations, invisibility, prophecy, infused knowledge, discernment of spirits, and even power over the forces of nature and Satan himself. His obedience to his superiors was such that he knew and carried out their wishes even if unspoken or given at a great distance. He subjected his body to severe penances and rigorous fasts. Every spare moment was spent in adoration of the Blessed Sacrament, and at times, when passing the tabernacle, he had to do himself violence: “O Lord, let me go, I beseech thee! There is work that I must do!” When he accompanied the Fathers on their missions, he often converted more hardened sinners by his fervent prayers and winning persuasiveness than the older priests by their sermons. </span></span></span>



      Born on 6 April 1726 at Muzo Lucanzo, Gerard died on 16 October 1755 at Caposele, aged barely 29. Characteristic are the words he wrote on the door of his cell: “Here is done what God wills, as God wills, and for as long as God wills!”</span></span></span>
