  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "John 3:7b–15"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today’s Gospel is the continuation of the night visit of Nicodemus with Jesus. Nicodemus was a rich Jewish rabbi and one of the seventy members of Sanhedrin. He wanted to clarify whether the obeying of the Mosaic Law and the offering of prescribed sacrifices were enough for one’s eternal salvation. But Jesus used the occasion as a teachable moment, showing Nicodemus the necessity for a spiritual rebirth through the action of the Holy Spirit by means of the water of Baptism as an essential condition for one’s salvation.



      Jesus teaches Nicodemus the effects the Holy Spirit produces in the souls of the baptized. We know the presence, force, and direction of wind by its effects. It is so with the Holy Spirit, the Divine "Breath" (pneuma), given us in Baptism. In Hebrew and Aramaic, the scholars tell us, the same word pneuma means “spirit,” “breath,” and “wind.” We do not know how the Holy Spirit comes to penetrate one’s heart. But He makes His presence felt by the change in the conduct of one who receives Him. Jesus further explains that he himself comes from Heaven, and, hence, his teaching is credible. Then, by comparing how God saved the snake-bitten Israelites through the symbol of the bronze serpent, Jesus tells Nicodemus that “the Son of Man” is going to save mankind by his death on the cross.






      *Life message:*



      1) We need to begin every day by asking for His Divine strength and guidance and end every day by asking His pardon and forgiveness for our sins.



      2) We need, as well, to pray for His daily anointing and for His gifts, fruits, and charisms so that we may live as children of God.



      3) We also need to throw open the shutters and let the Spirit enter the narrow caves in which we bury ourselves.

      Life is precious and we preserve it as long as we can with the help of medical science. Jesus promises *eternal life* to all those who believe in him. What is this eternal life? It is not only a life without end but it is also the quality of life that one leads here and now as a follower of Jesus. Today’s First Reading spells out clearly the way the early Christian community lived. ‘The whole group of believers was united, heart and soul…. They had everything in common.... There was not a needy person among them....’ The world today is filled with needy people who are deprived of their basic needs of food, clothing, shelter, education and primary health care. Do I have the responsibility as a believer to do something for my needy brothers and sisters in my neighbourhood?

      Saint Waldetrudis - *Widow (-c 688)*



      Saint Waldetrudis belonged to an exceptional family of saints. Her parents were Saints Walbert and Bertilia; her sister, Saint Aldegundis, Foundress-Abbess of Maubeuge; her husband, Saint Vincent Madalgarius, and their four children Saints Landericus, Dentelinus, Madelberta and Aldetrudis. The last two named were both abbesses of Maubeuge.



      Waldetrudis and Madalgarius, a nobleman by birth, lived a happy Christian married life. Sometime after the birth of their last child, Madalgarius withdrew to the abbey of Haumont which he had founded, and there took the name of Vincent.



      Waldetrudis stayed on in the world two more years and then humbly withdrew to a life of poverty and simplicity at a place called “Castrilocus in Monte”, around which developed the abbey of Chateaulieu and the city of Mons (Belgium). Here she gave herself completely to works of mercy and was well known for her miracles of healing both before and after her death.
