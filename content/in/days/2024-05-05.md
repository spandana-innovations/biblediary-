  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "John 15:9-17"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today’s second and third readings have but one refrain: “Love one another.” Why do Jesus and the beloved disciple of Jesus insist so much on love?



      Jesus, who came that we may have fullness of life and joy, first of all teaches us with His example what true love is and how to love well and honestly. Secondly, He commands us, almost pleads with us, to love one another as He has loved us.



      Whatever other achievements we may have, we will never be fully satisfied, totally fulfilled until we learn to love and actually love well. And we learn to love well from Jesus, following His example and abiding by His teachings and remaining in His love.



      The success of a person as a person, as a human being, is to be measured by how well he/she loves. All else is mere chaff, perishable and fleeting. Love alone is enduring, eternal….



      In loving well, we discover the Divine in ourselves and in the other. Thus we discern the common ground of harmony that exists between us, others and God. It is this triangular love affair lived with passionate ardour that harnesses and fructifies all our full human potential for growth.



      This is what makes a person fully human and fully alive, fills one with joy and enables one to take delight in the other, and the wholly Other, God. Hence, the insistence of Jesus, the Master psychologist, the Master of man, that we love one another as He loves us.



      (Philip John SSP & Premdas SSP, New Horizon Homilies, ST PAULS, Mumbai, India)

    body: |
      Jesus summarizes his ethics in a single command: “Love one another as I have loved you.” The love that Jesus demands is not momentary emotion or an abstract idea. It must be expressed in concrete and visible actions, because the standard of this love is the love with which Jesus loved us. It is an attitude and a mode of action rather than an emotion. It is a gracious and genuine interest in the true welfare of others. It is a love that cares deeply for others and desires the other’s highest good. Benedict XVI says, “Love does not calculate, does not measure, does not worry about expenses, does not set up barriers, but gives joyfully; it seeks only the good of the other, surmounts meanness, pettiness, resentments and the narrow-mindedness that human beings sometimes harbour in their hearts.”

      St Angelo Martyr (A.D.-1220)

      Very little is reliably known about St Angelo. His parents were Jews who were later converted to Christianity by a vision of Our Lady who announced to them that the long-awaited Messiah had already come and had redeemed his people. She also promised them two sons, and that they both would grow up as flourishing Olive trees on the heights of the Carmel — one as a patriarch and the other as a glorious martyr. Early on, the twins displayed great mental and spiritual gifts. At the age of 18, when they entered the Carmelite Order, they had already mastered Greek, Latin and Hebrew. After Angelo had spent five years on Mount Carmel as a hermit, Jesus appeared to him and bade him go to Sicily (Italy), where he would have the grace to offer the sacrifice of his life.Obedient to the call of the Lord, he promptly left Mt Carmel for Sicily. On his way and after his arrival in Sicily he succeeded in converting many by his teaching and miracles. At Palermo (Italy), about 200 Jews sought baptism as the result of his eloquence. Similar success was met with in Leocata, but there he aroused the fury of Berengarius whose wickedness and cruelty he had denounced.

      One day as he was preaching to a crowd, he was stabbed by a band of ruffians who attacked him under Berengarius’ influence. Mortally wounded, Angelo fell on his knees. Praying for his people, more especially for his murderers, he gave up his spirit.

      <span class="subhead">*Reflection:*</span> “The Christian life is the continuation and completion of the life of Christ in us. We should be so many Christs on earth; continuing his works, labouring and suffering in a wholly divine manner in the spirit of Jesus” (St John Eudes).
