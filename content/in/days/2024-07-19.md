  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Matthew 12:1–8"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today’s Gospel passage gives us Jesus’ teaching on the purpose of the Sabbath and on its proper observance. This was his response to a criticism and a silly accusation made by Pharisees against his disciples who, on a Sabbath, to satisfy their hunger, plucked ears of grain from a field and ate the grains after removing the husks by rubbing the grains between their palms and blowing away the chaff. The Pharisees accused them of violating Sabbath laws by performing three items of work forbidden on Sabbath, namely, harvesting, threshing and winnowing.



      Counterarguments: Jesus gives three counterarguments from Holy Scripture defending his apostles.



      1) Basic human needs, like hunger, take precedence over Divine worship and Sabbath observance. Jesus cites from the Scripture the example of the hungry David and his selected soldiers. They approached Ahimelech, the priest of Nob, who gave them for food the “offering bread” which only the priests were allowed to eat (1 Sm 21:1-6).



      2) No law can stand against Divine worship. That is why the priests were not considered as violating Sabbath laws although they did the work of preparing two rams for sacrifice in the Temple (Nm 28:9-10).



      3) God desires that we practice mercy: Jesus quotes the prophet Hosea to tell the accusers God’s words: “I want mercy, not sacrifice” (Hos 6:6).



      Life messages: Like the Jewish Sabbath, the Christian Sunday is to be:



      1) a day for rest and refreshment with members of the family;

      2) a day for thanksgiving and the recharging of spiritual batteries through participation in the Eucharistic celebration for Catholics and through worship service for the Non-Catholics;

      3) a day parents should use for teaching religious Faith and Bible lessons to their children;

      4) a day for doing works of charity in the neighborhood and in the parish;

      5) a day for socializing with family members, neighbors, and fellow parishioners.

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      Jesus reminds us in today’s Gospel that God is merciful and it is more important to show mercy to others than to make sacrifices in the temple. He quotes the Word of God from the prophet Hosea: ‘I desire mercy, and not sacrifice’. The Pharisees emphasized the careful observance of the laws over being merciful and sensitive to the needs of others. According to Jesus, feeding the hungry and showing love and mercy is more important than keeping laws. Jesus revealed God to be a God of mercy, which is why he shared table fellowship with sinners, forgave the adulterous woman, allowed a sinful woman to anoint his feet and became a guest at the house of Zacchaeus. As God's mercy touches us through Jesus, we are called to allow it to reach others through our lives. Lord, help us to be channels of your mercy and compassion to all those we encounter in our lives.

    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Sts Justa and Rufina - Virgins & Martyrs (A.D. - 287)"
    ref: "Sts Justa and Rufina - Virgins & Martyrs (A.D. - 287)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Sts Justa and Rufina were two Christian women at Seville in Spain who maintained themselves by selling earthenware. But they refused to sell vessels for the use of heathen ceremonies so as not to concur in idolatrous superstitions. Infuriated, the heathens broke up their stock-in-trade. Justa and Rufina retaliated by overthrowing the image of a false goddess, whereupon, they were impeached before the Governor.

      The Prefect commanded them to renounce their Faith and offer sacrifice to the very goddess whose image they had overthrown. They politely declined, but boldly confessed Christ as their Lord. As a result, the Prefect ordered that they be stretched on the rack and their sides torn with hooks. An idol was placed near the rack with incense that if they should offer sacrifice they should be released, but their fidelity could not be shaken. Consequently, Justa died on the rack while Rufina was strangled to death and their bodies burnt.

      The two saints are greatly venerated in Spain and their names doubtless represent historical martyrs in that place.
