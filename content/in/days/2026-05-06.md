    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "John 15:1-8"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      Jesus uses the image of the vine to explain the activity of the new people he has formed. Jesus is the true vine; he is the trunk from which the branches sprout. All of us are like branches united to him and live by him. Jesus does not say the Christian community is the vine, but “I am the vine.” Each branch has to remain united with him to be able to bear fruit. We should therefore consider how we are united with him through faith and prayer. If we do not remain with him, we shall become fruitless and be cut off and thrown away. If we remain united with him, we shall be pruned, allowed to suffer, in order to deepen our union with Christ and to bear more abundant fruit. The strength and fruitfulness of the branch depends on the trunk. If, in spite of bearing good fruit we are pruned, hat is not a punishment but the Father’s way of making us better and more fruitful branches, to change us from good to better. </span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      During his Last Supper discourse, Jesus uses one of his favourite images, the vine and the branches, to help his disciples understand the closeness of their relationship with him and the necessity of their maintaining it. Jesus assures them, using the parable of the vine and branches, that the Life-giving Spirit, Whom Jesus will send them, will be present and active among his disciples and their successors. This Gospel passage also emphasizes the need for Christians to abide in Christ as an essential condition for producing fruits of kindness, mercy, justice, charity, and holiness. Paul further clarifies this idea in Colossians 1:18 using another metaphor, that Christ is the Head and Christians are the different members of His Mystical Body. Pruning is an essential part of growing fruit-producing branches. In the vineyards in Palestine, dead branches were pruned to save the vine. Fruitless, leafy branches draining life sap from the main trunk were also pruned away leaving only fruit-bearing branches. Jesus tells his apostles that they have already been pruned by the words he has spoken to them. Eventually, they will be pruned of all attachment to the things of this world so that they may be ready to attach themselves to the things of Heaven.</span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life messages</span></span></span> 1) We need pruning in our Christian life. Pruning, which cuts out of our lives everything that is contrary to the spirit of Jesus and renews our commitment to Christian ideals in our lives every day is the first type of self-imposed pruning expected of us. A second kind of pruning is accomplished by practicing self-control over our evil inclinations, sinful addictions, and aberrations. A third type of pruning is done by our permitting Jesus to prune, purify, and strengthen us as God allows us to face pain, suffering, contradictions, and difficulties with His grace and the courage of our Christian convictions.</span></span></span></span></span>



      2) Let us abide in Christ and let Christ abide in us: Personal and liturgical prayers, frequenting of the Sacraments of the Holy Eucharist and Reconciliation, daily, meditative reading of the Bible, and selfless, loving acts of kindness, mercy, and forgiveness enable us to abide in Jesus, the true vine, as fruit-bearing branches. </span></span></span></span></span>

      Trees and plants need manure and water in good measure for them to grow and produce fruit. Besides these, they also need pruning, and keeping the surroundings clean is also essential for farming. Jesus uses the activities of a farmer to teach His disciples a lesson about building their own lives. Branches remain attached to the trunk of the plant or tree to draw nutrition, which it uses to convert into flowers and fruits. The message is simple: the disciples should remain attached to Jesus, who will provide everything that is needed for them to be fruit-bearing people. By remaining attached to Jesus and getting rid of unnecessary burdens that we carry, we can become people who are competent, able, and useful to society. Jesus transformed the society, and with His help, we too can do this.</span></span></span>

    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "St Dominic Savio, Confessor (1842 -1857)"
    ref: "St Dominic Savio, Confessor (1842 -1857)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      One of St John Bosco’s first pupils was Dominic Savio who was born at Murialdo, in Northern Italy, on 2 April 1842, of Charles Savio and his wife Bridgid.</span></span></span></span>



      The holy life of young Dominic was nurtured both at home and in the parish church. A very peaceful Christian domestic environment helped prepare his soul to receive great light and graces from God. Don Bosco, on the other hand, helped enkindle in the child’s heart a deep love for God and a deep devotion to the Virgin Mary. Dominic went to church every morning at five, served at Mass, and then spent his time in devotion and prayer. Come rain or shine, freezing winter or sweltering summer, the boy was regular at his church duties.</span></span></span></span>



      From Don Bosco, who was to become his biographer in later years, St Dominic Savio learned how the joy of serving and making the Lord loved by others can become a powerful means of apostolate. So spiritually advanced was the lad, exhibiting many gifts, including that of prophecy, that it was one of his visions which, in 1850, influenced Pope Pius IX to restore the Catholic hierarchy to England. St Pius X said of him: “A teenager such as Dominic, who bravely struggled to preserve undefiled his baptismal innocence to the very last, is indeed a saint!” At 15, he had become a giant of the spirit. Pope Pius XI added: “He was, indeed, the perfection of Christian life – a life drawing its strength from three main sources, namely purity, piety, zeal!”</span></span></span></span>



      On 9 March 1857, the youngster, who longed to be a saint, burst into ecstatic exclamation: “Oh, what a beautiful sight I see!” before passing to his reward. He was beatified on 5 March 1950.</span></span></span></span>



      Pope Pius XII, on the occasion of the solemn canonization on 12 June 1954, stated: “Truly, it gives us great joy to raise this boy of just 15, Dominic Savio, to the honours of the Altar and to set him before the youth of our day as a shining example for their imitation, especially in these troubled times when such powerful and sinister forces are doing incalculable harm in a relentless effort to uproot all virtues from the hearts of the young... Among the heroes there appears the image of Dominic Savio, the delicate adolescent, weak of body, but with a soul determined upon a pure oblation of itself to the exacting love of Christ.” The four resolutions the saintly youngster made on Easter Sunday 8 April 1</span></span>849, the day he received his First Holy Communion at the age of seven, stand out as an important legacy for youth for all time – 1) I will frequent the Sacrament of Reconciliation and receive Holy Communion as often as my confessor permits; 2) I will keep feast days holy; 3) </span></span>My friends shall be Jesus and Mary; 4) Death rather than sin.</span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Reflection</span>: *“I am not capable of doing big things, but I want to do everything, even the smallest things, for the greater glory of God”* (St Dominic Savio).</span></span></span></span>
