    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      The testimony of those who have seen Jesus is a basic source of faith and it is important to believe it. When Jesus appeared to the eleven Apostles who were still mourning for him he rebuked them for their unbelief because they had not believed those had seen him after he had risen. Today’s Gospel text is a reminder to all of us that we should carry on the same mission Jesus gave to the Apostles: to proclaim the Gospel to the whole creation. The universal mission of the Church springs from the Resurrection of the Lord. This mission is both a gift and a challenge: a gift because it makes us participants in the mission of Jesus himself; a challenge because it takes courage and determination to carry it out when doubts, fear and obstacles come on our way.



      *— Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel mentions the three appearances of the risen Lord and Jesus’ entrusting to the apostles their mission to preach his Good News: *“Go into the whole world and proclaim the Gospel to every creature.”* (Mk 16:15). According to Mark (in the longer ending), Jesus first appeared to Mary Magdalene. But Jesus’ apostles did not believe her. Later, he appeared to two disciples during their trip to Emmaus. When they had returned to Jerusalem and while they were explaining their experience, Jesus appeared to the eleven apostles gathered there. After gently chiding them for their unbelief and hard-heartedness, Jesus gave them his preaching mission or the “universal Apostolic mandate.”



      <span class="subhead">Life messages: </span>



      1) The preaching and witnessing mission of Jesus applies especially to the successors of the Twelve Apostles, namely, the Bishops in communion with Peter’s successor, the Pope.



      2) However, Vatican II declared in the Decree on the Apostolate of the Laity (*ApostolicumActuositatem),* that the whole “Church was founded to spread the Kingdom of Christ over all the earth for the glory of God the Father, to make all men partakers in Redemption and Salvation” (*AA*, 2). “On all Christians, accordingly, rests the noble obligation of working to bring all men throughout the whole world to hear and accept the Divine message of salvation” (*AA, 3). (Fr Tony: https://frtonyshomilies.com/)*

      The disciples’ initial response of unbelief in today’s gospel passage underscores the dramatic transformation they underwent. Their encounters with the risen Christ turned them from fearful and sceptical individuals into bold and unwavering witnesses of the Gospel. Today’s gospel passage contains themes of post-resurrection appearances, doubt and belief, the Great Commission, signs and confirmation, baptism, the universality of the Gospel, and the role of witnesses. It encourages believers to embrace their mission to proclaim the Good News and emphasizes the importance of faith, testimony, and obedience in following Jesus’ commands. This transformation demonstrates the power of the resurrection to change lives and embolden believers to share their faith.

    audio: null
    body: |
      It is now generally conceded that the name Anacletus (or Anencletus), which is given in all the ancient lists of Popes from the second to the fourth century was probably abbreviated to Cletus. It was later erroneously believed that Cletus and Anacletus were two distinct figures, and they were therefore commemorated on 26 April and 13 July respectively.



      Anacletus succeeded Saint Linus to the papal throne about the year 76 and reigned for twelve years. The 6th century *Liber Pontificalis* states that he had a small chapel or “Memoria” erected over the resting place of the Prince of the Apostles atop Vatican Hill and another one over the tomb of Saint Paul on the property of the matron Lucina on the Ostian Way. Aside from this, little is known of the reign of this second successor of Saint Peter.



      Anacletus was believed to have been martyred under the cruel Emperor Domitian, the “Nero-come-to-life-again”.
