      Reflection: 1



      Jesus spends the whole night in prayer and then chooses from his disciples twelve Apostles. They are to form the inner circle of his cooperators in founding the new Israel, the new people of God. The Church is going to be built on these twelve foundations. Though the twelve are ordinary, simple persons, Jesus chooses ahem and enables them to carry out the function he entrusted to them, by giving them the Holy Spirit who would give them wisdom and power and guide them. In fact all of us Christians are cooperators of Chris and are given the Holy Spirit in Baptism and Confirmation to enable us to do our mission faithfully. </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel passage gives a short account of the call of the Apostles and of the preaching and healing mission of Jesus. Jesus was the first missionary, sent by His Father with the “Good News” that God his Father is a loving, merciful, and forgiving Father Who wants to save everyone through His Son, Jesus. Today’s Gospel describes how this First Missionary selected and empowered twelve future missionaries as Apostles to continue the mission.



      Special features: Jesus selected very ordinary people, most of them hard-working fishermen with no social status, learning or political influence. Jesus was sure that this strange mixture of people would be very effective instruments in God’s hands. Matthew was a hated tax collector serving the Roman Empire, while Simon the Cananaean was a Zealot, a fanatical nationalist or terrorist of those days, determined to destroy Roman rule by any means. The others were mostly professional fishermen with a lot of good will, patience and stamina. It was only Jesus’s love for them and their admiration and love for Jesus that united them. Jesus selected them after a night of prayer and gave them His own Divine powers of healing and exorcism and made them a key part of His own Messianic mission of preaching the “Kingdom of God.”



      <span class="subhead">Life Messages: </span>



      1) God wants to show us that a calling for ministry, or a vocation to priestly or religious life or family life, is an initiative of God.



      2) As Christians we have the same mission that Jesus entrusted to his Apostles.



      3) We fulfil this mission of preaching the word of God, primarily, by our living out of Jesus’ teachings and by promoting and helping world-wide missionary activities of the Church.

      Jesus selected twelve ordinary men to form his community and continue his mission after he returned to the Father. Despite their ordinary status, they had faith in Jesus and walked with him for three years. They were empowered with the Holy Spirit and sent on a mission to preach, teach and drive out evil spirits. The Gospel emphasises the importance of prayerfully discerning the will of God before making any decision or choice in life, as Jesus chose his apostles for his mission after a whole night’s prayer. The apostles, except for Judas, were faithful followers of the Lord and collaborators in Christ’s mission. By Baptism, we are called to collaborate with Jesus, lead holy lives, and be patient in suffering. Loving Jesus means facing hardships for his kingdom’s advancement.

    saintName: "St Peter Claver, Confessor (1581-1654)"
    ref: "St Peter Claver, Confessor (1581-1654)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      The son of a Spanish farmer living near Barcelona, St Peter became a Jesuit at the age of 20, and while finishing his novitiate on the island of Majorca, was inspired by St Alphonsus Rodriguez, the holy doorkeeper of the Jesuit college there, to devote his life to missionary work in America. In 1610 he arrived at Cartagena in Central America, was ordained priest, and for the next 44 years made that port city his headquarters for missionary work among the Negro slaves.



      Peter Claver was probably the first white man to take interest in these unfortunates. Accompanied by a small band of interpreters, he would lovingly minister to the sick and the dying in the horribly fetid hold of ships, and hand out fruit and cooling drinks to the half crazed, fear-struck Negroes who had only been permitted on deck as the ship cast anchor. Undeterred by the antagonism of the callous merchants and the misgivings of his own superiors who were apt to look askance at his “indiscreet zeal”, Peter calmly went about, instructing his charges in the elements of the Christian Faith. At a solemn baptismal ceremony in the large town square, he would present each Negro with a small but greatly treasured medal of Jesus and Mary. In all, it has been estimated, he administered the Sacrament to over 300,000 people.



      Peter Claver was canonized at the same time as his friend St Alphonsus Rodriguez in 1888 and declared special Patron of all Catholic missions in 1896.
