  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 11:47–54"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Jesus accuses the Pharisees of “taking away the key of knowledge”. He not only assured them that they themselves have not attained to any wisdom, but their teaching has prevented others from knowing the truth. The young are quick to detect hypocrisy in their elders. Each new generation of youth rises to accuse its elders of hypocrisy, to blame society for the world’s evils, to blame even the God of their fathers – but no generation ever seems to have done much about it. What have we done about it? The Pharisees of all centuries tried to solve this thorny problem by a multiplication of laws, a heavy armour of righteousness to be invoked freely by the authorities, to be too eagerly obeyed by the scrupulous, and easily ignored by the freewheelers. Hypocrisy is a two edged knife. We use it against others to cover up our own failures. Why is it always so easy to see hypocrisy in others, but not in ourselves? There is no way out of the vicious circle, but the way of open honesty. </span></span>



      *(Pray Today’s Gospel, Bernard C. Mischke O.S.C. and Fritz Mischke O.S.C.)*</span></span>

      “I am the wheat of Christ to be ground under the teeth of the wild beasts so that it can become pure bread.” These were the words of Saint Ignatius of Antioch when he was sentenced to death. Jesus had predicted such a persecution for all the apostles and prophets whom he would send to the people. However, such sacrifice never goes in vain. God will take a stock of things and reward those who are being persecuted. In today’s gospel, Jesus condemns not only sins of commission but also those of omission. The authority was supposed to use their knowledge for the wellbeing of people. But they were passive about it. *Which of my sins of commission and omission will keep me out of the kingdom of God?* </span></span></span></span>
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "St Ignatius of Antioch Bishop Martyr, Apostolic Father of the Church (c.45 -c.107)"
    ref: "St Ignatius of Antioch Bishop Martyr, Apostolic Father of the Church (c.45 -c.107)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      A native of Syria, a zealous convert and a disciple of St John the Evangelist, Ignatius was the third Bishop of Antioch. From 94 to 96 there raged the second great persecution of Christians under the Emperor Domitian, and St Ignatius was indefatigable in instilling hope and courage in the faithful, so that they might profess the faith unwaveringly, even at the cost of life. After a short period of peace, a third persecution ensued during his reign of Trajan (98-117). St Ignatius once again proved himself the faithful and intrepid leader of the Church in Antioch, both by word and example—“A Christian does not live for himself alone. He belongs to God!” </span></span></span>



      His fearless reply to a derogatory remark made by Emperor Trajan was: “Call me not a poor wretch, for I bear God within me!” Trajan sentenced him to be devoured by wild beasts in the Coliseum.</span></span></span>



      Ignatius was then 62, and the journey to Rome proved quite an ordeal; but it also took on the aspect of triumph for the large numbers of Christians who turned out to meet him all along the way across Asia Minor and northern Greece. </span></span></span>



      Ready and eager to die for the Faith, Ignatius was heard to say to those who were desirous of working for his release: “I am the wheat of the Lord and must be ground by the teeth of wild beasts to become the pure bread of the Lord Jesus Christ.” </span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Reflection:</span></span>*“Let us ask St Ignatius to help us understand Jesus’ words to his disciples: The man who loves his life loses it, while the man who hates his life in this world preserves it to life eternal!” </span>*(Blessed James Alberione)</span></span></span>
