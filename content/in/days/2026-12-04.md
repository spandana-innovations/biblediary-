  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mathew 9: 27-31"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today’s Gospel describes Jesus’ miraculous healing of two blind men who approached him with trusting Faith. Blindness was common in Palestine because of the intense glare of the eastern sun, clouds of unclean flies and people’s ignorance of cleanliness and hygiene. The two blind men followed Jesus from the street all the way to the house Jesus entered, loudly expressing their confidence in the “Son of David” and requesting mercy. Jesus found in these men what was required for receiving a miracle, namely a strong and expectant Faith, an earnest desire for vision and a sincere prayer for mercy. Although they were instructed not to tell anyone of their healing, as soon as they were healed, they immediately expressed their gratitude by bearing witness to Jesus’ healing power throughout the town.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span> 1) We, too, need light and eyesight because we are often blind to the needs and expectations of those around us and even living with us. We are also often blind to the presence of Jesus living in us and in others, to the blessings God showers on us and to the protection God gives us every day. Hence, let us pray for the spiritual eyesight to realize and experience the presence of Jesus in ourselves and others, and for the good will to do good to and for others.

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      Advent is a season of promises. Christians wait and pray for the coming of the promised Saviour. In today’s Gospel reading, Jesus heals two persons of their inability to see and thus fulfils the promise made in today’s first reading (Is 29:18). John of Damascus, whom we remember today, was known as the ‘golden storm’ because of his powerful sermons and wrote a book ‘Source of Knowledge’ to help others to see the light of faith. How often are we struck with blindness? Often, we simply do not understand certain things, and someone else has to open our eyes to specific connections, insights, and knowledge. We could also be healed of our blindness if we admit our blindness and earnestly believe that the Lord can cure us. The Lord is our light; we don’t need to fear anyone.

    saintName: "Saint John Damascene - Confessor, Father and Doctor of the Church (c. 650 – c. 753)"
    ref: "Saint John Damascene - Confessor, Father and Doctor of the Church (c. 650 – c. 753)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      John Damascene was the son of a fervent Christian who, as grand vizier (chief financial officer) in Damascus, the then capital of the Arabian empire, used his great influence and wealth to purchase the liberty of numerous Christian slaves being sold by Arab raiders. In 699 he thus redeemed a devout and erudite Sicilian named Cosmas and entrusted to him the education of his growing son, John. The latter proved to have a brilliant mind, and was, on his father’s death, appointed chief counsellor of the city of Damascus but soon resigned his post on account of the hostility of Caliph Abdul Melek towards the Christian Faith.



      In c. 730, with Cosmas, John became a monk at Mar Saba near Jerusalem and was ordained priest by Patriarch John V of Jerusalem where he taught, preached and counselled in matters of faith and doctrine. When, in 726, the Byzantine Emperor Leo III promulgated his first decree against the veneration of images, John led the faithful in disregarding the command.



      Saint John Damascene who was proclaimed doctor of the church by Pope Leo XIII in 1890, is best known for his great oratorial skills and his important theological writings. His famous “Fountain of Wisdom” is an invaluable compilation of ancient Church traditions and the theological opinions of the great ecclesiastical writers up to his time constitute the earliest “Summa Theologica” and have been held in the highest esteem both by the Greek and the Roman Churches.
