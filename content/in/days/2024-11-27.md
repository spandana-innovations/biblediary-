  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 21:12–19"
    audio: null
    body: |
      “By patient endurance you will save your lives.” Good advice Jesus gives here. I don’t think any of us have to think back very far to recall the last thing that tried our patience. How often children without thinking try their parents’ patience. Another area that puts our patience to the test is that of our own weaknesses and failures. We may never seem to say the right thing at the right time. We always have our foot in our mouths, so to speak. Or after getting to bed we can never remember whether we set the alarm or not. We may have resolved again and again to be more kind to someone, but again and again we have to confess our failure. The same old sins over and over. A very severe test of our patience, one over which we have no control, is sickness. How do we accept such a trial from God or other tragedies, deaths, various disappointments? These are all real tests of our patience. It may not be a serious sin. But it can lead us to such faults as anger, bitterness, murmuring against God. We also have to remember that to be patient means more than just bearing something, just being passive about it. Patience is love. And love must act. It means greeting others with a sincere “Good morning”, or “Glad to see you” even though we feel disappointed about something, a bit down in the mouth.

    ref: null

    body: |
      The Gospel speaks about the impending persecutions that Christians will have to undergo. Jesus assures us of giving us eloquence and due wisdom which cannot be contradicted by the enemies. Even if the world hates and tortures, no harm will touch the faithful if they remain firm in their faith. The First Reading explains again the vision of seven angels in Heaven with seven plagues to destroy the antichrist. Those who conquered the beast sang the song of victory. God’s wrath will crush evil and make His people to rejoice at the mighty power of the Lord. We confront the discouraging and distracting elements that challenge our faith and trust in God. We may have to pass through horrible experiences, betrayals and persecutions, but God is our guarantee who puts the right words in our mouth to escape from the persecutors and to conquer the world. Let us stand firm with perseverance and an unchanging faith that nothing can defeat us or restrict our freedom as children of God. Let us be sure of a truth that God loves us unconditionally and totally; hence we be confident in His plans. He will set all things right and restore everything in His time.

    body: |
      Saint Virgilius was a scientist far ahead of his time! As abbot of Aghaboe near Dublin, Ireland, he was known as “the Geometer” because of his knowledge of geography. In 743, he left Ireland for a pilgrimage to the Holy Land but got no farther than the court of Pepin, the father of Charlemagne. In 745, when Pepin defeated Odilo, Duke of Bavaria, he sent Virgilius to be abbot of the monastery of Sankt Peter and Bishop of the diocese of Salzburg.

      Contrary to continental tradition, Irish custom decreed that the bishop be subject to the abbot, who was the real head of the diocese. Virgilius thus had himself consecrated bishop on 15 June 767.

      Early on in Salzburg, he had locked horns with St Boniface over the form of baptism, which the pope decided in Virgilius’ favour. His most notable accomplishments were the conversion of the Alpine Slavs and sending missionaries into Hungary and other places where no missionary had yet gone. Returning from a preaching mission to a distant part of his diocese, he fell ill and died on 27 November 784.

      The discovery of his grave in 1181, consequent to the destruction of the grand cathedral he had built and dedicated to St Rupert at Salzburg, led to his canonization at the hands of Pope Gregory IX in 1233.
