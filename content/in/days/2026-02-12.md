    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      Today’s Gospel highlights the deep faith of a gentile woman. Jesus wants not to disappoint the woman but to show that not only Jews but gentiles also are welcome to believe in Jesus. He grants her request and approves her witness of faith. Jesus has come not for any particular person or group; he is close to anyone who believes in him, and he grants his graces to his believers. We may sometimes experience that God does not answer our prayers immediately. His silence tests our faith and strengthens it and increases our sense of dependence on Him. Jesus shows his disciples how strong the faith of the gentile woman was in meting Jesus, probably the first time, in contrast to the weak faith of the disciples who were living and moving with him. Let us pray to Jesus to deepen our faith in him. </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      In the Gospel, Jesus demonstrates that salvation is meant for the Gentiles as well as for the Jews by healing the daughter of a Gentile woman as a reward for the mother’s strong Faith. Thus, Jesus shows that God’s mercy and love are available to all who call out to Him in Faith.</span></span></span></span></span>



      This is one of the two miracles of healing Jesus performed for Gentiles. The other is the healing of the centurion’s servant. (Mt 8:10-12). These miracles foreshadowed the future preaching of the Gospel to the whole world. Jesus first ignored both the persistent cry of the woman and the impatient demand of his disciples that the woman be sent away. Jesus then tried to awaken true Faith in the heart of this woman by an indirect refusal. We notice that the woman was refused three times by Jesus before he granted her request. Finally, the fourth time, her persistence was rewarded, and her plea was answered. She recognized Jesus as the Messiah (*the Son of David*) and expressed her need in clear, simple words. She persisted, undismayed by obstacles, and she expressed her request in all humility: “*Have mercy on me.”* (Navarre Bible commentary). Jesus was completely won over by the depth of her Faith, her confidence and her wit, and responded exuberantly, *“Woman, great is your Faith! Let it be done for you as you wish.”*</span></span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span></span></span> 1) We need to persist in prayer with trustful confidence. Christ himself has told us to keep on asking him for what we need: “*Ask and you shall receive.”* Asking with fervour and perseverance proves that we have “great Faith.” We must realize, and remember, that we do not always get exactly what we have asked for, but rather what God knows we need and what is really best for us at the most appropriate time.</span></span></span></span></span>



      2) We need to pull down our walls of separation and share in the universality of God’s love. Today’s Gospel reminds us that God’s love and mercy are extended to all who call on him in Faith and trust, no matter who they are. It is therefore fitting that we should pray that the walls which we raise by our pride, intolerance and prejudice may crumble.</span></span></span></span></span>

      We come across a desperate woman who persisted in spite of everything that was thrown at her. At first, Jesus ignored her and later compared her to a dog. But she persisted and finally got the favour desired from the Lord. At times, we have cried out to the Lord and asked Him to do this or that in our life, but there has been no answer. Consequently, we gave up on approaching Him, forgetting that God’s silence is not an indication of His unwillingness to meet our needs. God’s silence, per se, serves to temper our faith and remind us that He operates on a schedule different from ours. Our faith is not defined by what we receive from God, but by what it takes to stop us from getting to God!</span></span></span>

    saintName: "Saintt Gaudentius, Bishop, Confessor (360-427)"
    ref: "Saintt Gaudentius, Bishop, Confessor (360-427)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      A native of Brescia, Italy, St Gaudentius received his education from St Philastrius, the Bishop of that city, before visiting monks in Palestine and Egypt. He was living in a monastery at Caesarea in Capadocia when his Bishop died and the people of the diocese insisted that they would have none other than Gaudentius for successor. Aged 27, and highly gifted, he would have refused the election, had not the Eastern Bishops threatened him with excommunication if he did. St Ambrose called him back under obedience and consecrated him.</span></span></span></span>



      When returning to Brescia Gaudentius brought back with him numerous relics of the Apostles, of St John the Baptist, and of the 40 Martyrs of Sebaste. These he enshrined in a basilica he named “Council of the Saints”. He was a loyal supporter of the cause of St John Chrysostom, whom he had met in Antioch, and when the Pope and the Western Emperor dispatched an embassy to the Eastern Emperor Arcadius on behalf of the banished Chrysostom, Gaudentius was one of the three Bishops sent. The mission was badly received at Constantinople, its members imprisoned in a fortress, grievously maltreated, and finally placed on an un-seaworthy vessel which, it was hoped, would flounder at sea. St Gaudentius returned home safely, however, after 4 months. He is remembered chiefly for his eloquent extemporé sermons.</span></span></span></span>
