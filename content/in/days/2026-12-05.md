  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mathew 9: 35 - 10: 1. 5a. 6-8"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today’s Gospel describes the three chief activities of Jesus’ mission — heralding, teaching, and healing — and tells how Jesus selected the twelve apostles as disciples and helpers in his Messianic mission. Jesus was primarily the Herald of God his Father, bringing mankind the Good News that God is a loving, forgiving, merciful, and compassionate Father Who wills that everyone to be saved. Secondly, Jesus was a Teacher and preacher who taught the Gospel, or the Good News of the Kingdom of God, by living an exemplary life, demonstrating God’s love, mercy, forgiveness, and compassion. Thirdly, Jesus was a Healer, spending much time healing people of their bodily, mental, and spiritual illnesses. The Gospel for today also mentions that Jesus selected ordinary men of no social status as apostles to continue this preaching and healing mission, and gave them both healing power and preaching authority to do so.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span> 1) As Christians, we share Christ’s mission of preaching and healing. This means that we, too, have to demonstrate by our exemplary, transparent Christian lives the mercy, the forgiveness, and the unconditional love of Jesus. 2) We are also called to act as the agents of healing by praying for the sick, by helping them to get the necessary medical and nursing help, and by encouraging them, supporting them and boosting their morale.

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      God cares not just for the human beings but reaches out His concern towards the cosmos as well. The first reading from Isaiah promises that the Lord will ‘hear’ the cry of the humans as well as the ‘cry’ of the cosmos. As He directs the humans by telling them in their hearts, “this is the way”, so also He cares for the cosmos by giving rain, grain, pastures and rivers. He lets the moon shine like the sun and multiplies the light of the sun sevenfold. However, His special care is for human beings, expressed through Jesus’ compassion, especially for those who are sick and in need. Jesus needs human beings, that is, you and me, to support Him in this mission of compassion to those who are like sheep without a shepherd. The best medicine for a human need is another fellow human being. Are we prepared to participate in the compassionate mission of Jesus and so proclaim the good news of the Reign of God?
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Sabas - Abbot Confessor (439 - 532)"
    ref: "Saint Sabas - Abbot Confessor (439 - 532)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Born in 439, Saint Sabas was a young nobleman of Cappadocia, Turkey, who travelled to Jerusalem and after spending some years in the desert, settled down to an austere life under the guidance of Saint Euthemius. Near the brook Cedron he later established the great Laura Mar Saba, which still flourishes. Here, the monks lived in separate cells, somewhat like the Carthusians today. Six other houses also owe their foundation to his zeal.



      In 491 Sabas was ordained priest and three years later made archimandrate of all the hermit colonies in Palestine. By setting an example of scrupulous observance of the rule, he was able to bring about much needed reform in their discipline. He was also famous for his charity towards the poor. A valiant champion of orthodoxy, he twice travelled to Constantinople to influence the Emperor against the Monophysite, Nestorian and Origenist heresies, and as an old man of 90 interceded with Justinian on behalf of the Samaritan rebels and obtained their pardon.



      Saint Sabas died on 5 December 532, revered as one of the founders of Eastern monasticism. His relics, stolen by the Venetians, are still venerated in Saint Mark’s Basilica at Venice.
