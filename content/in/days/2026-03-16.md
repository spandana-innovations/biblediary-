      Reflection: 1



      Jesus comes to Galilee and an official whose son was ill comes to Jesus and begs him to go with him and heal his son who was at the point of death. Jesus tells him to go, and assures him that his son will live. The official believes and acts as Jesus tells him. He trusts the word of Jesus and goes. When he is still on the way his servants come and tell him that his son had recovered. He learns from them that the boy was healed at the time Jesus told him, “Your son will live.” And he and his household believe in Jesus. Those who sincerely seek the truth recognise it when they find it. The believers must focus their attention not on miracles but on the person of Jesus and his word with full trust. This is at the same time a challenge and an invitation to all of us. </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      According to John’s Gospel, after facing rejection by his hometown, Nazareth, Jesus went to Jerusalem in Judea for the Passover feast. From Jerusalem, Jesus returned to Galilee and to his headquarters, Capernaum, where people received him as a miracle-working preacher and prophet. Today’s Gospel describes how Jesus did a long-distance (telepathic) healing for the dying son of an official employed in the royal court of king Herod. Jesus was preaching at Cana, twenty miles away from Capernaum. Herod’s officer came on horseback from Capernaum to Cana urgently seeking Jesus’ presence for the healing of his dying son. He was desperate enough to swallow his pride and make a public request to a wandering preacher for this healing. Jesus’ critical comment on the lack of belief in the Pharisees did not discourage the official. With expectant Faith he pleaded with Jesus, reminding him of the seriousness of the illness. So, Jesus told him, “*Go; your son will live.”*With trusting Faith in the assurance Jesus had given him, the officer rushed back toward his house. On the way there, he received the good news that his son had been healed at the very time Jesus gave his assurance. The officer was so grateful that he and the other members of his family accepted Jesus as the promised Messiah without fearing the ridicule of fellow-officers and friends.</span></span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life message: </span></span></span>1) Miraculous healings can take place in our lives, too, if we approach Jesus the healer with true humility, trusting Faith, earnest prayer and the willingness to surrender ourselves to God’s will. We might not experience immediate answers or healing, but no prayer ever goes unanswered. Each prayer is answered according to God’s will. </span></span></span></span></span>

      In our lives, we often let small obstacles block our vision, preventing us from taking the leaps of faith that God calls us to make. However, the royal official in today’s Gospel teaches us a profound lesson. He trusted in the Lord and took a leap of faith despite his possibilities of doubt. When Jesus assured him, ‘Your son will live’, he chose to believe and acted on that word. In moments of crisis, we must remember that faith often requires us to jump without seeing where we will land. Are we ready to leap over the walls of our fears and embrace the joy that faith brings? Trusting in God’s goodness empowers us to rise above challenges and experience His merciful love.</span></span></span>
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Heribert, Bishop of Cologne (970 -1021)"
    ref: "Saint Heribert, Bishop of Cologne (970 -1021)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      One of the most renowned Prelates of the Diocese of Cologne (Germany), St Heribert was born to Count Hugo of Worms in the Palatinate of the Rhine. Brilliant and always eager to learn, he was educated at the Cathedral School of Worms and the celebrated Abbey of Gorze in Lorraine. He was attracted by the lives of the Benedictine monks there and would have joined them had not his father recalled him peremptorily to Worms, where he was given a canonry and ordained priest.</span></span></span></span>



      Heribert gained the confidence of Emperor Otto III, becoming not only his chancellor, but also his friend, adviser and companion. On Christmas Eve 998 he was consecrated Archbishop of Cologne in the Cathedral of St Peter, and from that moment on he devoted himself indefatigably to the duties of his high calling. He never allowed his state affairs to hinder him from his spiritual duties.</span></span></span></span>



      He accompanied the Emperor on a visit to Italy, where Otto breathed his last. In keeping with the latter’s wishes, his mortal remains were laid to rest at Aachen.</span></span></span></span>



      Duke Henry of Bavaria who succeeded Otto misinterpreted the doings of Heribert. But Heribert won him over by his life of virtue, leading to a public reconciliation between the two.</span></span></span></span>



      On a visit to Neuss Heribert contracted fever, of which he died on 16 March 1021, after having received the Viaticum with great fervour. At his tomb at Deutz, many miracles came to be attributed to his intercession. He is still invoked for rain.</span></span></span></span>



      St Heribert was canonized by Pope St Gregory VII some time between 1073 and 1074.</span></span></span></span>
