      Reflection: 1



      In yesterday’s Gospel reading Jesus explained the mystery of our oneness with him. Today he reminds us of his new commandment of love. If we are united with Jesus, we shall receive from him the ability to love others as he has loved us. To receive this ability we have to share in his thinking. We must become his friends, knowing him as the one who loves us and acts in us. Thus we shall be able to bear authentic fruits of love, and the joy of Jesus will be in us and our joy will be complete. We shall thus become loving, joyful and fruitful like him. For that, we should live our daily life keeping his commandments. </span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      During the Last Supper discourse, Jesus teaches his disciples that love is the hallmark and the criterion of Christians. Jesus reminds his disciples that he has chosen them as his friends with a triple mission. First, they are to love others as he has loved them. Second, they are to bear the fruits of the Holy Spirit in their lives. Third, they are to ask God the Father for whatever they need in Jesus’ name.</span></span></span>



      The criteria of Christian love: First, Jesus modifies the Old Testament command from “*love your neighbour as you love yourselves” (*Lv 19: 18) to “*love others as I have loved you.”* This means that our love for others must be unconditional, forgiving, and sacrificial. Jesus invites each Christian to be in the inner circle of his friends by obeying his commandments including the new commandment of love. Such friends abide in Jesus, and Jesus abides in them, and their prayers in Jesus’ name will be answered promptly by God the Father. We express our love for Christ by obeying his new commandment of love. Jesus further explains that the real source of Christian joy is the certainty that God loves us. We, too, must be ready to express our love for others by our readiness to die for them as Jesus died for us.</span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life message</span>: 1) Let us remember that true Christian love is costly and painful because it involves sacrifice on our part when we start loving unlovable, ungrateful, and hostile people with Christ’s unconditional, forgiving, sacrificial love. But our Christian call is to love others as Jesus has loved us, and as Jesus loves them. </span></span></span>

    body: |
      Abiding in Jesus’ love and keeping His commandments are the two ideas that Jesus shares with His disciples. Coming immediately after the ‘wine and the branches’ episode, we understand what this abiding means. It is something required of every disciple. For a disciple, there is no alternative source, and a disciple’s life is totally and completely centred on Jesus. So, everything that Jesus stands for applies also for the disciples. The selfless life of Jesus, the simple life He lived, the beatitudes He practised, and His total dependence on the Father also become the guiding principles for the disciples. Imitation of Jesus is the new commandment the disciples should strive to keep. What Jesus taught his disciples in the washing of the feet can be seen as an act that the disciples can put into practice.</span></span></span>
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "St John of Beverley, Bishop Confessor (-721)"
    ref: "St John of Beverley, Bishop Confessor (-721)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      A native of Yorkshire (England) St John was in his early years educated at Canterbury under the care of Archbishop St Theodore and Abbot St Adrian. Later he joined the Benedictines at Whitby, under the authority of St Hilda. In 687 he was consecrated Bishop of Hexham and in 705 made Archbishop of York. This he governed for 15 years, then resigned and consecrated St Wilfrid the Younger to succeed him. Sensing his death to be near, he retired to Beverley monastery which he had founded and richly endowed, and died there on 7 May 721.</span></span></span></span>



      The cultivation of kindness being at the very heart of holiness and thus the surest sign of closeness to Christ, John went out of his way to be kind to all, generously providing for the poor and the unfortunate. A gifted preacher, he was particularly famed for the careful attention he paid to the training of the younger clergy. Not surprisingly then, one of his most notable pupils was the Venerable Saint Bede whom he ordained a Priest in 702.</span></span></span></span>



      St John’s shrine at Beverley was a great pilgrimage centre in medieval times.</span></span></span></span>
