    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      In the synagogue, on a Sabbath day, Jesus sees a man with a paralised hand. The Pharisees are looking for a chance to find fault with him in order to accuse him and kill him. Jesus challenges them, asking them if it was lawful on the Sabbath to do good or to do harm, to save life or to kill. Jesus intended to save life; the Pharisees intended to kill Jesus. The Pharisees were in fact perverting the law and planning to do what religion actually forbids. But Jesus does not waste time; he immediately heals the man with the paralised hand. Do we look for excuses in the rules to deny help to our needy and suffering brethren? </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel describes a miraculous healing done by Jesus on one Sabbath as a public violation of Sabbath law to prove that God’s intention for the Sabbath was to* do good and to save life* rather than *to do evil or to destroy life*.</span></span></span></span></span>



      The incident and the reaction: Ex 20:8 and Dt 5:12 instructed the Jews to *keep the Sabbath holy. *But the Scribes and the Pharisees had amplified God’s law on the Sabbath by misinterpreting it and had made it burdensome for the common people through man-made laws. Jesus wanted to demonstrate in public the original intention of God in declaring the Sabbath holy. For Jesus, the Sabbath was a day of rest to be used in adoring God, learning and teaching His laws, and doing good to/for others. Hence, Jesus took the liberty of granting healing to a man with a withered hand in the local synagogue immediately after the worship service, thus infuriating the scribes and the Pharisees.</span></span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span></span></span> 1) Our Christian Sabbath, that is, Sunday, observance of participating in the Eucharistic celebration is meant to recharge our spiritual batteries for doing good to/for others and avoiding evil. 2) Our Sunday observance is also meant to be an offering of our lives to God on the altar, to ask God’s pardon and forgiveness for our sins, to present our needs before the Lord and to participate in the Divine Life by Holy Communion. 3) It is also a day for us to spend time with the members of the family and to participate in the activities of our parish and neighbourhood. </span></span></span></span></span>

    body: |
      Today’s Gospel text focuses on being courageous and fearless in the face of opposition and conflict. Mark narrates that Jesus healed a man with a withered hand in the synagogue on a Sabbath day. The merciful as well as fearless act of Jesus brings Him into fresh conflict with the Jewish religious establishment, who accuse Him of disregarding the sacred law. Moreover, they conspire to destroy Him following the incident. However, Jesus does not wait for another day or a better occasion to do good but responds immediately to people’s needs around Him. Have we denied goodness or postponed kindness by giving lame excuses? If so, may the liberating activity of Jesus become an inspiration for us not to deny life to those in need.</span></span>
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Agnes of Rome - Virgin Martyr (- c.304)"
    ref: "Saint Agnes of Rome - Virgin Martyr (- c.304)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Saint Agnes has ever been held in the highest honour throughout the Church, and her name occurs in the Canon of the Mass. Little is known with certainty about her short life. Yet, even from that little it is clear that she desired to consecrate herself solely to Christ and so declined the hand of many a suitor. This led her to be exposed in a brothel, where, all who saw her were awestruck. A lone youth who dared to attempt violating her modesty was struck blind but regained his eyesight through her prayers. </span></span></span></span>



      Martyred at the age of twelve and buried at her parents’ country place on the Via Nomentana on the outskirts of Rome. A basilica constructed over her tomb in 324 was completely rebuilt by Pope Honorius about 630 and has remained almost unaltered since, a beautiful gem of old Roman architecture.</span></span></span></span>



      Agnes is the Patron Saint of purity, of the “Children of Mary” and of young girls in general, for tradition has always credited her with having been a model of purity and modesty and having maintained her faith in spite of every enticement, threat and torture. Gardeners also revere her as their patron.</span></span></span></span>



      Each year, this day, two white lambs, symbolizing innocence, are solemnly blessed at the high altar of her church and offered to the Pope, and from their wool are later made the Pallia with which the Holy Father invests his Metropolitans the world over as a sign of their sharing with him the “plenitude of the pontifical office”.</span></span></span></span>
