      Reflection: 1



      Jesus consoles his troubled Apostles and promises to send to them the Holy Spirit. Jesus and the Father will come and stay with anyone who believes in him and keeps his words. What Jesus speaks comes from the Father. The Holy Spirit dwells in us and makes the Father present in us. The Holy Spirit is the helper, the promoter, the advocate. It is He who makes us understand and remember the teachings of Jesus. The Holy Spirit is the gift of the Father and the Son. </span></span>



      The Old Testament insists on keeping the commandments while the New Testament insists on love of God and neighbour. It shows that the person who obeys God loves Him, and he who loves God obeys Him. Anyone who loves another is eager to do what the other one says. </span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel passage is taken from Jesus’ Last Super discourse. It was commonly held by the Jews that when the Messiah came, he would be revealed to the whole world as King and Saviour. Hence, Judas Thaddeus asks why Jesus is revealing himself only to his disciples. Jesus does not answer that question directly. Instead, He continues his work of preparing his disciples for his imminent departure from them by assuring them that he is not leaving them alone. Instead, Jesus is going to live in them along with God his Father and God the Holy Spirit. The presence of the Father can be experienced through the experience of love. It means that the criterion of the Father is always the same: love. “*If anyone loves Me, he will observe My word, and My Father will love him and We shall come to him and make a home in him*.”</span></span></span>



      Jesus promises the abiding presence of the Holy Trinity in his disciples who express their responsive love for him by keeping his commandments, especially his commandment of love, because only this type of loving will open them and make them receptive to the Divine Indwelling of the Trinitarian God. Jesus is referring to the indwelling of the Holy Spirit in the soul renewed by grace. God repeatedly revealed Himself in the Old Testament and promised to dwell in the midst of His people (cf. Ex 29:45; Ez 37:26-27; etc.). But here Jesus speaks of the presence of God in each person. We are each a part of the Divine chain of love. God loves man. He sent His Son to prove it. After Jesus’ Resurrection and Ascension, God the Father continues to live in us with His Son and the Holy Spirit. This abiding God gives us the Father’s protection and providence, the Son’s redemption and forgiveness of sins, and the Holy Spirit’s sanctification and guidance.</span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span> 1) Let us live in constant awareness of the abiding presence of the Trinitarian God within us and behave well in His presence. 2) During moments of doubts and temptations, let us seek the active guidance and strengthening of our indwelling God.</span></span></span>

      The word “love” refers almost exclusively in the New Testament to the love of persons, which is also the case in today’s Gospel passage. When love is expressed in mutual sharing and acceptance, its effects can be felt in people. Parents express love for children, friends show love and affection among themselves, and care and affection for neighbour are also love. By following Jesus, we can express our love for Him. Another idea that Jesus brings to His instruction to His disciples is that of the “Paraclete”. The word itself means “advocate”, or the Holy Spirit. The Holy Spirit, understood as a helper, will assist the disciples in all their activities.</span></span></span>

    body: |
      An officer of the Roman army, St Florian occupied a high administrative post in Noricum, now part of Austria. In the reign of Emperor Diocletian he bore witness to Christ by suffering death for his faith.</span></span></span></span>



      According to his own “acts” it is stated that he gave himself up at Lorch to the soldiers of Aquilinus, the Governor, as they were rounding up all who professed the Christian faith. He was asked to renounce his faith, but he wouldn’t budge and twice was scourged, half flayed alive and finally was thrown into the river Enns with a stone around his neck.</span></span></span></span>



      His body was recovered by a pious woman, who gave him a Christian burial, but was eventually removed to the Augustinian Abbey of St Florian, near Linz. It is said that Pope Lucius III, in 1138, offered some of the Saint’s relics to King Casimir of Poland and to the Bishop of Cracow. Since then, Florian has been regarded as the Patron Saint of Poland, Linz and upper Asia.</span></span></span></span>



      There existed a great devotion to St Florian in many parts of central Europe. Many miracles of healing are attributed to his intercession, and he is invoked as a powerful protector in danger from fire or water.</span></span></span></span>
