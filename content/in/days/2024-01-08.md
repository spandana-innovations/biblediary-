      Have you ever wondered why Jesus did need to be baptized? Michael Green, a British theologian says: “By submitting to baptism, Jesus acknowledged God’s claim on him, as on others, for total consecration of life and holiness of character” (The Message of Matthew – Michael Green).



      We can find out three reasons why Jesus had to be baptized.



      The first reason is that it was a sign of his complete dedication to follow the will of God. For Jesus baptism was simply a public declaration of his commitment to the Father.



      The second reason is that it announced the beginning of Jesus’ earthly ministry. Both John the Baptist and God the Father confirmed Jesus’ unique calling publicly. Thus Jesus’ baptism was an initiation for ministry.



      The third reason is that it gives us an example to us. Jesus taught his followers to be baptized – and here he is giving a firm lead. His baptism was an example that we do well to follow.



      (Fr John Pichappilly, Ignite: Homiletic Reflections: Kindle Your Spirit, ST PAULS, Mumbai)





      “At His Baptism, Jesus was declared by God to be his Son, His beloved. But many who saw Jesus and His works never really understood the significance of His ministry. It is only at His death that the centurion made his confession of faith, “Truly this was the Son of God.” To be called son or daughter of God is indeed great. That is what we become when we receive the sacrament of Baptism in faith and profess this faith continuously and in all circumstances. It is a reality signed and sealed by the gift of the Holy Spirit.



      What God did for Christ at His baptism is precisely what God gives us at our baptism – the gift of the Spirit and the public acknowledgement of each of us as His children. If this is the way we are honoured by God, then we have the duty to obey Him and do His will.”



      (Philip John SSP & Premdas SSP, New Horizon Homilies, ST PAULS, Mumbai, India)

      The Baptism of the Lord is a great Feast as it reveals all the three persons in the Holy Trinity. It is the official revelation of Jesus as the Son of God to the whole world by God the Father. It is narrated by all the four evangelists and marks the beginning of Jesus’ public ministry. Jesus’ baptism by John was the manifestation of Jesus’ acceptance and beginning of his mission as God’s suffering servant. Jesus submitted himself entirely to his Father’s will out of love for humanity for the remission of our sins. The opening of the heavens with the Holy Spirit descending as a dove upon Jesus and the affirming voice of the Father are God’s revelation to humankind of the mystery that He is Triune. For Jesus it was a moment of decision to enter into his public ministry, a point of identification with the people and a moment of approval from God. The BAPTISM of Jesus reminds us of our true identity and mission too. By Baptism we become the adoptive sons and daughters of God, the members of the Church and our mission to live as children of God in thought, word and action.

      (1) Baptism of our Lord



      Today’s liturgy helps recall to our minds the baptism of Jesus and this, in turn, ought to remind us of our own baptism. When baptizing at the Jordan, John the Baptist said: “I baptize you with water, but he who is coming….will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and with fire.” The baptismal rite of John was merely a penitential act; at our baptism—as at Jesus’ baptism—the Holy Spirit came into us and endowed us with the supernatural life.

      Baptized as we were as infants, we did not then understand what was happening. But the grace of God preceded us.

      <span class="subhead">Reflection: </span>*“The Lord was merciful to us and gave us the seed of the fruits of the redemption, which is the beginning of spiritual and supernatural life in us”* (Blessed James Alberione).



      Saint Apollinaris

      Saint Apollinaris was one of the most illustrious bishops of the second century. Eusebius, St. Jerome, Theodoret, and others speak of him in the highest terms, and they furnish us with the few facts that are known of him. He addressed an "Apology," that is, a defense, of the Christian religion to the emperor Marcus Aurelius, who, shortly before, had obtained a signal victory over the Quadi, a people inhabiting the country now called Moravia. One of his legions, the twelfth, was composed chiefly of Christians. When the army was perishing for want of water, the soldiers of this legion fell upon their knees and invoked the assistance of God. The result was sudden, for a copious rain fell, and, aided by the storm, they conquered the Germans. The emperor gave this legion the name “Thundering Legion” and mitigated his persecution.

      It was to protect his flock against persecution that Saint Apollinaris, who was bishop of Hierapolis in Phrygia, addressed his apology to the Emperor to implore his protection and to remind him of the favor he had received from God through the prayers of the Christians. The date of the death of Saint Apollinaris is not known, but it probably occurred before that of Marcus Aurelius, about the year 175.



      Saint Severin of Noricum - Abbot (-482)

      Nothing specific is known about the antecedents of Saint Severin. From the purity of his Latin writings, he seems to be a Roman who spent a part of his early life in the deserts of the East before opting to preach the gospel in Noricum (Astura) where his arrival was met with harshness. His prophecy concerning divine retribution soon came true when the city was invaded and destroyed by the Huns. This, coupled with his many charitable works, made him famous in Noricum, from where his fame spread to Faviana, a city on the Danube, whose people, distressed by a famine, implored his assistance. In response, he began preaching penance to them, which bore much fruit.

       He so fervently implored a rich woman who had hoarded up more than she needed that she distributed much of her provisions among the poor. He wrought numerous other miracles, redeemed captives, healed the sick, relieved the oppressed, averted public calamities and brought peace and blessing wherever he went, even declining the bishopric, conscious that the solitude he loved so dearly, rather than Episcopal dignity, would draw more people to the Faith.

      He established monasteries at Biotro near Passau and at Faviana. His own cell was so low that the kings and princes who visited him could not stand aright in it. He fasted until sunset and always walked barefoot.

      Severin passed away on the fourth day of his illness repeating over and over again the words: “Let every spirit praise the Lord!” His mortal relics are enshrined in the Benedictine Monastery bearing his name.

      <span class="subhead">Reflection:</span> *“Let every spirit praise the Lord!”* (Saint Severin)
