      Reflection: 1



      Jesus appoints seventy-two and sends them on a mission into every place he himself was about to visit, to prepare the people there. The number seventy-two represents all nations of the world. The mission given to these men indicates the Church’s mission to all nations. This mission to preach the kingdom is urgent and requires single-mindedness and haste. No time should be wasted in chatting with people, and the missionaries should not be choosy about food or accommodation. Rather, they should dedicate themselves to preaching the kingdom to the people and making it experiential to them through healing and assuring them that he kingdom of God has come near to them. The mission involves risk-taking; the preachers should be ready to face opposition and hostility. As they are doing God’s work, He will give them strength and protection. </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      St. Luke was a Syrian by race, born in Antioch as a Gentile. He became a Christian and follower of St. Paul. He had a Greek background and education. He knew Greek, spoke Aramaic in Antioch and became a scholar in Hebrew. He was a physician by profession (Col 4:14), and was considered an artist, probably from his graphic descriptions of the nativity scenes with shepherds and magi, from the parable of the lost sheep and from a sixth century copy of the portrait of Mary (kept at Maria Maggiore Church in Rome), the original of which was believed to have been drawn by Luke.



      A prolific writer: Luke could read and understand the Greek Septuagint version of the Old Testament and the Hebrew originals. He is the only non-Jewish Evangelist. He wrote the third Gospel and the Acts of the Apostles, between 70 and 85 AD. They were originally one book, and, when taken together, are longer than the fourteen epistles of St. Paul. Luke is represented in art by an ox or calf, for he saw Jesus as a sacrifice for all mankind and began his Gospel describing Zechariah and the Temple worship. It is believed that Luke wrote the Gospel when he was 74 and died at Boeotia when he was 84 years old. Luke presents Jesus as giving importance and recognition to women and the Gentiles. Contacts: Luke had close contacts with Mary and all the Apostles, and he would have been able to interview all of them to collect details for his Gospel and the Acts of the Apostles. He was a constant companion and doctor of St. Paul during Paul’s Jerusalem and Malta mission trips and during Paul’s imprisonment, first in Caesarea, then in Rome. Probably he was with Paul till Paul’s martyrdom.



      Life messages:



      1) We are to be apostles of prayer: Luke presents Jesus as a man of prayer spending much of his time in listening to God his Father in order to learn His will and in talking to Him in solitude.



      2) We are to be merciful and compassionate, becoming the voice of the voiceless: Luke describes Jesus siding with the poor and marginalized in the society (option for the poor) and trying to give a special status to women and Gentiles.

      On this Feast of Saint Luke, the Evangelist, we are all invited to revisit the mission that is entrusted to each one of us. The Synod on Synodality highlighted the essential dimensions of communion, participation and mission. This is so appropriately expressed in today’s Gospel passage from Saint Luke: the seventy-two are appointed and sent on a mission. The instructions that they are given are very clear: to prepare the way for Jesus’ visit ensuring that there is communion and participation among the people they visit. Mission involves great risks and Saint Luke takes pains to mention the dictum of Jesus: “I am sending you out as lambs in the midst of wolves.” Am I able to live the mission that is entrusted to me, whatever the consequences?

    body: |
      St Luke was a native of Antioch, the capital of Syria. A physician by profession (cfr. Col 4:14), he was consulted by St Paul during an illness on his 2nd great missionary journey, and converted by him to the Faith. Himself a gentile, he thenceforth associated himself with the great Apostle of the Gentiles, and while Paul was preaching in Thessalonica, Athens and Corinth, and during his 3-year stay at Ephesus, Luke carried on the work of Evangelization in Philippi. He returned with Paul to Jerusalem, and visited him frequently during his imprisonment at Caesarea; it was probably at that time that Luke wrote his extensive Gospel which Sts Jerome and John Chrysostom called St Paul’s Gospel. He is rightly said to have been “illumined by St Paul”, for he recorded and interpreted with utmost fidelity the oral preaching and catechizing of the great missionary. He is also credited with having painted a portrait of the Virgin Mary.



      Being highly educated, Luke could write in polished, elegant Greek, and he related the events to contemporary history. In his endeavour to furnish an historical background for the faith of the educated Greek converts of the young Church, he particularly stressed the fact that God’s mercy and forgiveness extended universally both to the Jews and the Gentiles who believed in him. Thus, the parables of the Good Samaritan and the Prodigal Son, for instance, appear only in his Gospel.



      St Luke is the Patron of physicians, artists, brewers, butchers, glass-workers and notaries.
