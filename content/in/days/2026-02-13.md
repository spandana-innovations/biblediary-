    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      Jesus heals a deaf and dumb man by opening his ears and releasing his tongue. And he man zealously proclaims the healing he received from Jesus and helps people to appreciate the power and goodness of Jesus. God has blessed us with the power of hearing and speaking. It is therefore our mission to hear the Good News of Jesus and to proclaim it to others. But sometimes we are deaf and dumb regarding the realities and needs of people around us, and to the promptings of the Holy Spirit. Jesus is the one who brings us God’s deliverance, and his healing miracles show that the time of final salvation has come. Let us identify the areas of our life where we need to be opened and approach Jesus to be healed by him. </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel describes how Jesus, by healing a deaf and mute man, fulfilled Isaiah’s Messianic prophecy, *“The eyes of the blind shall be opened and the ears of the deaf unstopped” (Isaiah 35:5)*. The Gospel invites us to become humble instruments of healing in Jesus’ hands by giving a voice to the needy and the marginalized in our society. It also challenges us to let our ears be opened to hear the word of God, and to let our tongues be loosened to convey the Good News of God’s love and salvation to others. Through this miracle story, Mark’s account also reminds us that no one can be a follower of the Lord without reaching out to the helpless (“preferential option for the poor”).</span></span></span></span></span>



      The miracle is described in seven ritual-like steps: (1) Jesus leads the man away from the crowd; (2) puts his fingers into the man’s ears; (3) spits on his own fingers; (4) touches the man’s tongue with the spittle; (5) looks up to Heaven; (6) sighs; (7) and speaks the healing command: “*Ephphatha*” (“be opened.”). Jesus carries out this elaborate ritual probably because the dumb man could not hear Jesus’ voice nor express his needs. Jesus applies a little saliva to the man’s tongue because people in those days believed that the spittle of holy men had curative properties. The miracle is about the opening of a person’s ears so that he will be able to hear the word of God, and the loosening of his tongue so that he will be able to profess his Faith in Jesus.</span></span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span></span></span> 1) Jesus desires to give us his healing touch in order to loosen our tongues so that he may speak to the spiritually hungry through us. Jesus invites us to give him our hearts so that, through us, he may touch the lives of people in our day.</span></span></span></span></span>



      2) We must allow Jesus to heal our spiritual deafness and muteness because otherwise we may find it hard to speak to God in prayer and harder still to hear Him speaking to us through the Bible and through the Church.</span></span></span></span></span>



      3) Let us imitate the dumb man in the Gospel by seeking out Jesus, following him away from the crowd, spending more of our time in getting to know him intimately through studying the Holy Scriptures and experiencing him personally in our lives through prayer. The growing awareness of the healing presence of Jesus in our lives will open our ears and loosen our tongues. </span></span></span></span></span>

      Saying ‘Ephphatha’ (Be Opened), Jesus opened the deaf man’s ears and loosened his tongue. We all need certain ‘Ephphatha moments’ in life because we are too self-satisfied and comfortable with the situations of our lives, and we do not want any improvement or change. Of course, we can hear and see well, but sometimes we pretend to be deaf and blind because we do not want to listen and respond to the realities of life. Our hearts can be closed to the promptings of the Spirit of God who wants to remind us of our sins, disbelief, unrighteousness, laziness, and what not! ‘Ephphatha’ is a friendly exhortation to us from God to get in touch with the realities of our lives. What are the areas we need to be opened more fully?</span></span></span>
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "St Catherine dei Ricci, Virgin (1522-1590)"
    ref: "St Catherine dei Ricci, Virgin (1522-1590)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Born Alessandra Romola on 23 April 1522 in Florence, Italy, St Catherine Dei Ricci was the daughter of an old patrician family of bankers and merchants. Her tendency to solitary prayer was already observed in early childhood, and, at 13, she was received into the Dominican convent of strict observance in the neighbouring city of Prato, where she took the name of her deceased mother. After 5 years, during which she was much misunderstood and calumniated against, her holiness was recognized and she was advanced to novice mistress, then sub-prioress and finally, in 1560, to prioress, which she remained until her death 30 years later.</span></span></span></span>



      Her famous “ecstasy of the Passion”, which would last from Thursday noon until 4 o’clock on Friday afternoon, began in 1542 and continued each week for 12 years, when it finally ceased at her own urgent pleading, for the confluence of great numbers of visitors, among whom were 3 future Popes, was seriously disrupting the religious life at the convent. During these ecstasies, which have been fully authenticated, Catherine would witness all the stages of Our Lord’s sufferings. The wounds which the Saviour suffered during the flagellation, the crowning with thorns, the carrying of the Cross and finally the Crucifixion became visible in her body. St Catherine also had the gift of miracles and is known to have held what might be termed telepathic conversation with such contemporary Saints as St Philip Neri in Rome and St Mary Magdalen de Pazzi in Florence — neither of whom she had ever met — without leaving her own convent in Prato.</span></span></span></span>



      Catherine dei Ricci died after a long illness aged 68 on 2 February 1590, and was canonized in the year 1746.</span></span></span></span>
