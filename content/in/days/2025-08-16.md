    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      People recognised Jesus as a holy and kind person, and they brought little children to him. In fact Jesus was much more than just a holy and kind person. The disciples rebuked the people, but Jesus welcomed the children and blessed them lovingly. On this occasion he also taught the people that the kingdom of heaven belongs to those who are like children – simple, humble and trusting. Like Jesus, we should also let little children come to us, and take care of them, even when they have not yet seen the light of day. Moreover, we should imitate children in their qualities of mind and heart, and thus become fit for the kingdom of heaven. </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel passage describes one of the loveliest incidents in the Gospel story. Jewish mothers used to bring their children to great rabbis to have them pray over the little ones, especially on their first birthday. Naturally, mothers wanted the healing touch and blessing of the most popular rabbi, Jesus. In an attempt to protect their master from the crowd of mothers and noisy children, the apostles started rebuking them. The passage describes Jesus’ reaction and teaching.



      Childlike qualities for entrance into Heaven: By showing his displeasure at the rough reaction of his apostles, Jesus made it clear that everyone is equally important to him as a child of God. The mothers came to Jesus because he was welcoming, warm, and approachable. Jesus decided to use the occasion as a teachable moment. He taught his disciples that entry into Heaven demands the childlike qualities of humility, innocence, obedience, simplicity, openness, teachability, freedom from prejudice, readiness for change and adaptation, total trust in a loving and providing God, confidence in the essential goodness of people and the readiness to forgive and forget. Only such people are ready to hear the message of the Gospel in its fullness and accept it.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages: </span>



      1) Let us live in the awareness that we are the children of a loving and providing Heavenly Father and that, by Baptism, we are members of God’s family. Hence, we are expected to behave well every day, as worthy children of a Holy Father.



      2) Let us pray for all the children in our families and for all our young parishioners and let us find time to cooperate in the parish ministries meant for children and young people.

    ref: "St Stephen of Hungary King Confessor (969 -1038)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Named Vajk at birth, Stephen was baptized in 985, together with his father. At 20 he married Gisela, the sister of Duke Henry III of Bavaria, who later became Emperor Henry II. On his father’s death Stephen is said to have obtained from Pope Sylvester II the title and crown of “Apostolic King of Hungary”. At the same time permission was given him by the Holy See to further Christianize his realm by the establishment of ten bishoprics and the assignment of prominent foreign monks. At his coronation on Christmas day in the year 1000 Stephen consecrated Hungary to the Blessed Virgin.



      Stephen decreed that every tenth town should build a church and furnish land and workers for the support of a Priest, who was chosen by the bishop and received the necessary books from him, but the king furnished and equipped the church buildings. Stephen also founded pilgrims’ hospices in Rome, Ravenna and Constantinople, and a monastery in Jerusalem.



      Stephen, who was canonized along with his son, Emeric, in 1083, is revered as Hungary’s greatest Saint and hero. His right hand, which has remained incorrupt and represents his country’s most treasured relic, is enshrined in a chapel in the great church of Our Lady of Buda.
