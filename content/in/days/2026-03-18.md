      Reflection: 1



      The Jews try hard to kill Jesus because he calls God his own Father, making himself equal to God. In his defense Jesus tells them that the Father sends His Son into the world to complete His work of giving life and salvation. The Father entrusts the Son with life-giving power and the power of judging. The Father and the Son are in perfect harmony and interdependence; they complement each other. The Son does only what he sees the Father doing. Jesus says, “He who hears my word and believes in him who sent me has eternal life; he has passed from death to life.” To have eternal life we must believe both in the Father and the Son. Jesus reveals to us the Father’s love and mercy. Jesus is the one who gives us life here and hereafter. He is our refuge in this life and in the next.



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel passage gives Jesus’ explanation, vindicating himself when he has been accused by the Jews of breaking the Sabbath by healing on that day, and of being a blasphemer, by claiming, as God’s Son, equality with God and the same authority and power as God.



      Jesus’ claims and justification: In general, Jesus claims that he is one with the Father in all he does as Mediator, and that there is a perfect understanding between him and his Father in the whole matter. But, at the same time, he is obedient, and so entirely devoted to his Father’s will that it is impossible for him to act separately from his Father in anything. Thus, Jesus claims that his identity with the Father is made visible in his complete obedience: Jesus always does what his Father wants him to do. Then Jesus proves his equality with the Father by doing some works that are the exclusively works of God Who is his Father. For example, it is God’s prerogative to forgive sins, and to raise the dead, restoring them to life, and Jesus exercises these prerogatives. Jesus has received Divine power from the Father to exercise His judgment and authority over life and death. That is why Jesus’ words bring healing and restore life to those who believe in the One Who sent him, and condemnation to those who do not. At the last judgment, all who have heard Jesus’ voice and obeyed his word will be raised to eternal life.



      <span class="subhead">Life message:</span> True Christian life is the surrender of our lives to God with the same love and obedience which Jesus demonstrated for his Father.

      In our pilgrim journey of faith, we are called to ‘imitate Christ as He reflects the life and works of His Father’.     body: |
      St Cyril succeeded Maximus as Bishop of Jerusalem at 35, but nearly 17 out of the ensuing 36 years of his stormy episcopate he spent in exile for maintaining firm faith in the divinity of Christ — he was banned twice by Arian synods and once by the Emperor Valens.



      In 381, Cyril attended the Council of Constantinople and, for the first time, Jerusalem was recognized as a patriarchal See along with Rome, Alexandria, Antioch, and Constantinople.



      Cyril was a man of gentle nature and a peacemaker. It was he who fostered the development of the “holy city” of Jerusalem as a pilgrimage centre for all Christendom. A pillar of orthodoxy and a great teacher of the early Christian era, his primary surviving work is the “Catecheses”, a collection of 23 catechetical lectures to candidates for Baptism — the first 18, based on the Jerusalem baptismal creed, given during Lent, the concluding 5 instructing the newly baptized during the week after Easter. His Eucharistic theology, an advance on that of earlier writers, interprets the Lord’s presence in the terms of the later dogma of transubstantiation, the rite pronounced in sacrificial language.



      Thought to have died in 387, St Cyril was declared a Doctor of the Church in 1882.
