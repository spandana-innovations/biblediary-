      Reflection: 1



      On New Year day the Church celebrates the solemnity of Mary the Mother of God. She conceived and gave birth to Jesus who is a divine person, the Son of God. As her son is a Divine Person, Mary is Mother of God. It is the mystery of Incarnation that qualifies her as the Mother of God. She is Mother of God only in reference to Jesus, and not to the Father and to the Holy Spirit. At the same time we should remember that the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit are one God. Today’s Gospel tells how the shepherds went to see and venerate the newborn saviour and then spread the good news to others. Mary treasured all these words and pondered over them. Mary’s life invites us to follow her example of listening to the Word of God, contemplating it and living it. </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      *Today’s Feast of Mary, the Mother of God, is a very appropriate way to begin a new year, reminding us to rely on the powerful intercession of our Heavenly Mother. The Church has, since 1968, also observed a yearly World Day of Peace; this year, 2022, marks the 54th celebration. On this day, the Church invites us to pray specially for lasting peace in the world throughout the New Year.*</span></span></span>



      Today’s first reading gives us the beautiful Divine blessing from the book of Numbers for the New Year, and the Responsorial Psalm (Ps 67) begs for that blessing. In the second reading, Paul reminds the Galatians and us that God’s Son has become one of us through Mary, and that it is through Jesus that we have become the children of God. Today’s Gospel describes how the shepherds spread to all their neighbours the Good News surrounding the birth of Jesus which the angel had revealed to them, and how Mary treasured “all these things” in her heart. The Gospel also tells us that on this day, the eighth day after His Birth, the Child was circumcised and received the name Jesus that had been chosen by God Himself.</span></span></span>



      We honour Mary primarily because God honoured her by choosing her to become the mother of Jesus, the Incarnate Second Person of the Holy Trinity, who remained God, when He took on human flesh and became Man, as stated in the Bible. The angel said to Mary*: “And behold, you will conceive in your womb and bear a son, and you shall call His Name Jesus. He will be great, and will be called the Son of the Most High…” (Lk 1:31-32a; RSV 2 Catholic*). After the angel had appeared to her and told her that she was to be the mother of Jesus, the Blessed Virgin Mary visited Elizabeth. At Mary’s greeting, Elizabeth said, *“Blessed are you among women, and blessed is the fruit of your womb! And why is this granted me, that the Mother of my Lord should come to me? (Lk 1:42-43; RSV 2 Catholic)*. Hence, the Council of Ephesus affirmed in AD 431 that Mary was truly the Mother of God (*Theotokos*), and in AD 451, the Council of Chalcedon affirmed the Divine Motherhood of Mary as a dogma, an official doctrine of the Holy Catholic Church*.*</span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span> </span></span></span>



      1) Let us strive to be pure and holy like our Heavenly Mother. All mothers want their children to inherit or acquire their good qualities. Hence, let us honour Mary, our Heavenly Mother, by practicing her virtues of trusting Faith, obedience to the word of God, purity, and humble, selfless, committed service. </span></span></span>



      2) Let us make the New Year meaningful by having every day a) some noble thing to dream, b) something good to do, and c) Someone to love, the first person being Jesus. </span></span></span>



      3) Let us sanctify every day of the New Year: a) by offering every morning, all the activities of the day to God for His glory, thus transforming them into prayers, b) by asking for the anointing and strengthening of the Holy Spirit to do good to others and to avoid evil, c) by remaining faithful to our family prayers and Bible reading at night, d) by asking God’s pardon and forgiveness for our sins committed during the day, and e) by seeking God’s special protection during sleep. Before we sleep, let us say, “Good night, Lord,” repeating Jesus’ last words from the cross, “Father, into Your hands I commend my spirit.” </span></span></span>

      At Christmas, our attention is centred on the child, but today, as we begin the New Year, our focus shifts to the mother. While the world rejoices in the arrival of the New Year, the Church celebrates the Solemnity of Mary, the Mother of God. The Gospel reading recounts the visit of the shepherds to Bethlehem, where they found Mary, Joseph, and the Child Jesus. Notably, Luke names Mary first because, in Semitic culture, the one listed first holds social significance. It is striking that in a male-dominated culture, Luke attributed that importance to Mary. However, what we celebrate today is the significance that God gives to Mary. As the mother of Jesus, she receives God’s immense blessings. She is graced by God as the “highly favoured one” and recognised by humans as the “most blessed among all women.” Mary, the Mother of God and the model of constant discernment, teaches us to acknowledge our blessings as we, too, are gifted with the arrival of a new year.</span></span>

    body: |
      Our attention, focused at Christmas on Jesus, the Incarnate Word of God, today centres on Mary, his Virgin Mother. The Church’s affirmation of Mary as Mother of God implies not that Jesus’ divine nature comes from Mary but that his human nature was formed from her humanity, the two natures uniting in her womb in the second person of the Blessed Trinity. Being Son of God, it follows that Mary is the Mother of God.</span></span></span></span>



      Paul’s assertion, “God sent the spirit of His Son into our hearts, crying out ‘Abba, Father!’” (Gal 4:6) helps us realize that Mary is also the Mother of us all having been made so at the foot of the cross by Jesus himself.</span></span></span></span>



      The title, “Mother of God”, dates back to the 3rd/4th century, when its Greek form, “Theotokos” *(God-bearer)* became the pivotal point of the Church’s teaching on the Incarnation. The Ephesian Council confirmed the title in 431 and Pius XI, on Christmas Day 1931, through *Lux Veritatis,* enjoined the celebration of its feast on the whole Western Church. </span></span></span></span>



      In *Fulgens Corona* (1953), Pius XII avers that from the title “Mother of God” flow “all the privileges and graces with which her soul and life were adorned in such an extraordinary manner and measure.” Paul VI’s *Marialis Cultus* declared that this feast commemorates Mary’s role in the mystery of salvation and exalts the singular dignity this mystery brings to the “Holy Mother through whom we were found worthy to receive the Author of Life”.</span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Reflection:</span></span></span> *“The divine maternity is the pinnacle of her greatness, the culminating point to which all the privileges of our Mother converge and from which they radiate. . . . Whoever understands her maternity, has understood Mary”* (Joseph Marie Perrin, OP).</span></span></span></span>
