  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 2:41-52"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today the Church celebrates the feast of the Holy Family. Christ, the second person of the Holy Trinity was born in this world in a family. He took flesh from the womb of Mary and was born of her as a little, vulnerable and weak infant. He was taken care of, nurtured, protected and taught by his foster father Joseph and his earthly mother Mary. He learnt the basic human values and virtues from his earthly parents. He was trained in his profession as a carpenter in the same family. He grew up and became wise and responsible adult in the family. The family of Joseph and Mary became a ‘holy family’ because of the presence of Christ, the holy one of God. And again, Joseph, Mary and Jesus, at all times did whatever God had planned for them and not once they went against God’s will and plan. At all times, they loved God, loved one another, and they shared that same love and concern with the people they lived. Today’s readings give us four sound advices for us change our families from ordinary families to holy families. a. The children are God’s precious gift to the family and they should have a deep respect and love for their parents, particularly when the parents grow old. b. Parents should help their children to grow in human wisdom and above all, in love for God and neighbour. c. Both parents and children should try to discover God’s will and they should do their best to carry it out, whatever it may cost them. d. Above all, every family should be a spiritual powerhouse; they need to find time for family prayer, which will make them stay together.



      (Fr Joe Eruppakkatt ssp)
  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      The Christian family is considered a mini-Church. It is in the family that children are introduced to live with others, accepting, caring, loving, understanding, tolerating, and forgiving each other. As they begin to know and love their earthly parents, they need to be introduced to know and love their heavenly Father, the creator. Together with the academic studies which widen their world of knowledge, they need to grow in the fear of the Lord which is the true wisdom. Mary and Joseph taught their child obedience to and love for God. His parents also set an example before the child by honouring and being obedient to God. Even today we see some extraordinarily talented children and we feel stunned observing them. The hard work of the parents behind this is remarkable. As we train our children to compete with the standards of the world, let us also train them to compete for the Kingdom of God.
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Thomas Becket - Bishop Martyr (1118 -1170)"
    ref: "Saint Thomas Becket - Bishop Martyr (1118 -1170)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Born in London to a wealthy Normandy merchant, Thomas Becket, well versed both in civil and canon law, became Archdeacon of Canterbury at 37 when the young and ambitious Henry II ascended the English throne and promptly appointed him Chancellor against his will.

      In 1161, when Theobald, Archbishop of Canterbury, died Thomas was nominated successor, again much against the latter’s wishes. Thomas, genuinely devout and scrupulously moral and on intimate terms with Henry, warned him thus: “I know your plans for the Church and that you will put forth claims which I, as Archbishop, must necessarily oppose.”

      Thomas’ decision to resign as Chancellor so as to be free of all civil responsibilities, brought into focus his resistance to the “Constitutions of Clarendon” which, purported to be a record of accepted customs in Church matters, included clauses rendering Church law subject to the King’s whims. Of all the bishops, Thomas alone, realizing the implications, resisted and, sensing danger to his life, fled in disguise to France. Received cordially by King Louis VII, he submitted his case to the judgement of Alexander III, Pope-in-exile, at Sens.

      Four years of negotiations later, reconciliation seemingly achieved, Thomas returned to Canterbury in 1170. But within less than a month, aware of his imminent end, he exclaimed: “To God and to the Blessed Mary, to the Patron Saints of this church, and to Saint Denis I commend myself and the cause of the Faith!” On 29 December, taking cognizance of Henry’s anguished cry, “Will no one rid me of this low-born clerk!” four of his knights slew the Archbishop while at the altar in his own cathedral, effectively stalling the secularization of the Church for several centuries!

      Thomas was canonized by Pope Alexander III in 1173. In 1174 Henry II, having done penance at the saint’s shrine which had by then become a pilgrimage centre as a result of the miracles taking place, was absolved.
