  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "John 14:21–26"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today’s Gospel passage is taken from Jesus’ Last Super discourse. It was commonly held by the Jews that when the Messiah came, he would be revealed to the whole world as King and Savior. Hence, Judas Thaddeus asks why Jesus is revealing himself only to his disciples. Jesus does not answer that question directly. Instead, He continues his work of preparing his disciples for his imminent departure from them by assuring them that he is not leaving them alone. Instead, Jesus is going to live in them along with God his Father and God the Holy Spirit. The presence of the Father can be experienced through the experience of love. It means that the criterion of the Father is always the same: love. “*If anyone loves Me, he will observe My word, and My Father will love him and We shall come to him and make a home in him*.”</span></span></span>



      Jesus promises the abiding presence of the Holy Trinity in his disciples who express their responsive love for him by keeping his commandments, especially his commandment of love, because only this type of loving will open them and make them receptive to the Divine Indwelling of the Trinitarian God. Jesus is referring to the indwelling of the Holy Spirit in the soul renewed by grace. God repeatedly revealed Himself in the Old Testament and promised to dwell in the midst of His people (cf. Ex 29:45; Ez 37:26-27; etc.). But here Jesus speaks of the presence of God in each person. We are each a part of the Divine chain of love. God loves man. He sent His Son to prove it. After Jesus’ Resurrection and Ascension, God the Father continues to live in us with His Son and the Holy Spirit. This abiding God gives us the Father’s protection and providence, the Son’s redemption and forgiveness of sins, and the Holy Spirit’s sanctification and guidance.</span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">*Life messages: </span></span></span>*</span>



      1) Let us live in constant awareness of the abiding presence of the Trinitarian God within us and behave well in His presence. </span></span></span>



      2) During moments of doubts and temptations, let us seek the active guidance and strengthening of our indwelling God. </span></span></span>

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      Love is a deeply felt emotion. Everyone needs to be loved. It is love that keeps us happy. Starved of love we cannot grow as normal human persons. Animals and birds too express love in different ways. It is in this context that today’s gospel becomes significant. Jesus came to reveal God to us. Through his words and deeds Jesus revealed that God is love. God loves each one of us unconditionally. However, we can experience God’s love only when we keep the commandments God has given us. Love is not meant to be one-way traffic. Love is love only when it is mutual and experiential. By keeping Jesus’ word, we express our love for him. Do I really love Jesus by living a life according to his teachings?</span></span></span></span>

      Saint Catherine of Siena</span></span> - *Virgin and Doctor of the Church (1347-1380)*</span></span></span></span>



      Catherine, the youngest of the 25 children of the Benincasa family of wool dyers in Siena, Tuscany (Italy), when still young, was favoured with celestial visions and began to practise severe austerities. At 16 she became a Dominican tertiary, lived for three years like an anchoress in her own home, and heroically undertook the care of advanced cases of leprosy and cancer which no one else would touch and, though meeting with nothing but abuse, succeeded in bringing about the sufferers’ conversion.</span></span></span></span>



      Subsisting for long periods solely on the reception of the Eucharist and praying incessantly for sinners who refused to pray for themselves. At 28, she received the sacred stigmata, but in response to her pleadings to God, these remained outwardly invisible while she was alive. The most famous of her writings is her *Dialogue* in which the Father converses with her on the spiritual life.</span></span></span></span>



      Catherine was instrumental in having Pope Gregory XI lift the interdict over Florence and finally return from Avignon to Rome in 1377. At the request of Gregory’s successor Urban VI she spent the last six years of her life in Rome, offering the sacrifice of her life for the unity and renewal of the Church. She was barely 33 when she died three long months after having had a seizure.</span></span></span></span>



      Proclaimed saint in 1461 and Doctor of the Church in 1970, she is the Patron Saint of Italy, and is invoked against fire, pestilence and headaches. </span></span></span></span>
