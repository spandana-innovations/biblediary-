  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 7:11–17"
    audio: null
    body: |
      St Luke, the writer of today’s Gospel incident, was a medical doctor. In the true spirit of his profession, he was a man who was very sensitive to people’s sufferings. That is why he gives more attention to the plight of the widowed mother, who lost her only son, than to the miracle of calling her son back to life.</span></span></span></span>



      Luke describes the situation; how her only son died at an early age. And at that time there were no widow’s pensions, no insurance policies, no social welfare, no nice jobs for women. Life could be very harsh for a lonely middle-aged woman. Our Lord was quick to realize the tragedy of the situation. He was moved to pity for the woman and told her not to weep. Then He gave life to the young man, her only son.</span></span></span></span>



      The message of the Gospel? Certainly, it is the compassion and understanding of Christ shown to someone in need. Henceforth for that widow the reality of God’s love could never be mere theory. She had experienced it in a real way.</span></span></span></span>



      And today – how is God’s love made real today? No other way than through us, who are Christ’s Body. To the sick, to the aged, to the poor, to anyone in need – Christ’s love becomes real only through our compassion, our kindness, our help to their need. We should ask ourselves how aware we are of the needs of others around us. Their needs may not be great – they may only need a kind word or a smile – they may simply need to be listened to with patience and understanding. But at that very moment our presence might – just mean for them the experience of Christ’s love, and that is what counts.</span></span></span></span>



      My hands and my feet, O Jesus, are Your hands and Your feet in all the acts I do as a member of Your Body.</span></span></span></span>



      *(Bernard C. Mischke OSC & Fritz Mischke OSC, Pray Today’s Gospel, ST PAULS, Mumbai, India, Pages 258-59).</span></span>*</span></span>
  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      By his word of power, Jesus restored the life of a man who was dead. Jesus felt compassion for the widow who had lost her only son. And he uses his extraordinary divine power to raise the dead man to life. He understood how desperate and hopeless the widow might have felt losing her only son. Feeling compassion for her, he works this miracle without even being asked. He is the Lord of the living and the dead. He died on the cross and rose again to new life, thus conquering the power of the grave to hold him in. And because he rose again, we shall rise too, and experience new life with him. Do I trust in the Lord Jesus to give me abundant life and everlasting hope in the face of trials, misfortunes, and moments of despair?</span></span></span></span>
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "St Robert Bellarmine"
    ref: "St Robert Bellarmine"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Robert was born of ancient, noble lineage. He joined the Jesuits at 18 and in 1569 was sent to Louvain, Belgium, the bulwark of Catholicism, to complete his studies. Familiarizing himself at close range with the Protestant heresies he gained a reputation as a controversialist. In 1576 he was called to Rome by Pope Gregory XIII to train the German and English seminarians for the spiritual battles which they would face in their homelands.</span></span></span></span>



      Robert’s systematic approach to an issue, his amazing memory and familiarity with the works of the Church Fathers, his knowledge of foreign languages, all combined to attract great audiences. His lectures continued for twelve years and resulted, even before their publication under the title *De Controversiis*, in their circulation throughout England and Germany, in transcriptions; his catechism, translated into 60 languages, is still in use in Italy today.</span></span></span></span>



      In 1592 he was made rector of the Roman College, in 1595 Provincial of Naples and in 1599 Cardinal by Pope Clement VIII who affirmed: “The Church has not his equal in learning.” He played a prominent role in the examination of Galileo’s writings. His famous treatise on the true relation between Church and State, to the effect that all authority comes from God but is vested in the people and that they entrust it to their rulers as they see fit, is the democratic concept on which the constitutions of many nations have been founded.</span></span></span></span>



      In the spirit of self-sacrifice, Robert Bellarmine, an ardent devotee of Francis d’Assisi, practised poverty to the hilt. He was canonized in 1930 and declared doctor of the Church the following year.</span></span></span></span>
