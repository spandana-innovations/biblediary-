  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 13:1–9"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Some people told Jesus about an uprising of Galileans in the Temple court and how Pilate’s soldiers slaughtered them there and then. We do not know what motives the people had for telling Jesus about this. However, Jesus uses the story to drive home the lesson that all of us, without exception, are sinners and in urgent need of repentance. The Galileans killed by Pilate are not more deserving of their gruesome fate than other Galileans, including those listening to Jesus. They were not more sinful than others. Their untimely death should serve as a warning of what can happen to us unless we turn to God and change our ways.</span></span></span></span>






      Ti further emphasize the point, Jesus tells the parable of the fig tree. In the Old Testament Israel is frequently likened to God’s vineyard, which makes this parable a pointed reference to his immediate listeners. Just as the fig tree is given a final chance, a period of grace, to bear fruit, so people are given time to repent and reform. In the period of persecution during which Luke is writing these passages of his Gospel are reminders to Christians that they must persevere in living lives of faith and repentance. There is no room for self-complacency. The ‘time of grace’ given us must be used fruitfully.</span></span></span></span>

      The theme of repentance is very prominent in the New Testament. John the Baptist begins his ministry by appealing people to ‘bear fruits worthy of repentance.’ Jesus asks his listeners to ‘repent and believe in the Good News.’ Peter, asks people ‘to repent and be baptized in the name of Jesus so that they too will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit.’ In today’s Gospel Jesus tell his listeners, ‘</span></span>Unless you repent, you will all likewise perish’ like the Galileans or the people of Siloam.     body: |
      Very little is known about the life of St Evaristus, the fifth Pope. Believed to have been the son of a Jew from Bethlehem who settled in Antioch, he succeeded St Clement about the year 100 and reigned until c.107.</span></span></span>



      According to the 6th century “Book of Popes”, it was St Evaristus who divided the city of Rome into 25 parishes or “titles”, each under its own Presbyter. He also decreed that whenever the Pope preached he was to be surrounded by seven Deacons, to give greater glory to God. Finally, he regulated the formal ceremonies for the consecration of churches, which had been handed down from the Apostles and were strongly reminiscent of the Jewish rites of Solomon; these Christian consecrations had to include the celebration of the Holy Eucharist. It was during this Pope’s reign that the last of the Apostles, St John the Evangelist, died at Ephesus.</span></span></span>
