  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 1:26–38"
    audio: null
    body: |
      One week ago, we solemnly celebrated the Assumption of Mary. Today we celebrate her Queenship, contemplating her who is seated with the King of kings, sharing in his celestial glory. Today’s memorial was instituted by Pope Pius XII in 1955, originally to be celebrated on 31 May. It has been shifted to this date to underline the relationship between Mary’s glorification and her Queenship.



      Mary is queen both metaphorically and really — metaphorically as a person of unique excellence, dignity and nobility; really as mother of Christ the King and Lord. Mary has also unequalled power of interceding for her children. On that score too she deserves to be called queen. She is so much in union with Christ the Lord and is the closest co-operator in his redemptive function. Above all she is the mother of the Lord; Elizabeth addressed her,

      “The mother of my Lord”. Mary is full of grace and blessed among women, and her dignity and nobility rise far above that of all other humans. Hence, she is queen in every sense, and the Fathers of the Church teach that her Queenship is based on her divine maternity. Besides, she belongs to the royal lineage of David. Therefore, the faithful offer her veneration above all other saints.



      Mary is the queen of the universe. She receives fame and honour among nations because she shares in the privileges of Christ the King. In heaven God clothed her in royal garments as

      queen of all so that she might distribute to them the graces of His kingdom. Since Mary has shared in her Son’s work of redeeming the world, the King of the universe has given her a share in reigning over the world. Finally in the Gospel story of the Annunciation we are shown that Mary deserves honour above all other creatures because she is going to be mother of a king whose kingdom will have no end (Lk 1:32-33). As the unique virgin who is full of grace, our Blessed Mother enjoys a spotless dignity that is fitting for the Mother of God, Mother of all the faithful and Queen of the world.



      Throughout the centuries Christians have addressed fervent prayers, like the Hail Holy Queen, and hymns like Regina caeli, and Hail Queen of the heavens, to the Blessed Virgin Mary who, because of her eminence, is entitled to the highest honour. She is the queen of all creation and the sovereign mistress of all people.



      Let us confidently approach the throne of our Queen and Mother to ask her for help in difficulties, for light in darkness, for solace in sorrow. Let us call upon this Queen of ours who brings the blessings of peace so that after this our exile on earth she may show

      us the blessed fruit of her womb, Jesus who will be our peace and joy for ever.



      Let us enthrone Mary as the queen of our hearts. Jesus Christ is King by nature; Mary is Queen by grace. The kingdom of Christ is in the hearts of people as he himself said, “The kingdom of God is within you.” So also Mary is the Queen of the spiritual kingdom,

      and it is in our hearts and souls that she is glorified with her son. If Mary is truly the queen of our hearts, then Christ will also reign as the King of our hearts. To be loyal subjects of Christ the King is the greatest honour we can do to Mary our Queen, for her advice

      is, “Do whatever he tells you” (Jn 2:5).



      *(Fr [Dr] George Kaitholil SSP, Feastday Homilies, ST PAULS, Bandra, Mumbai, India. Pages: 259-261).*

      The Blessed Virgin Mary becomes the Queen of Heaven and Earth through her surrender to God’s will. The feast gives us three lessons: a) Queenship means to be focused – Mary had God as her focus; b) Queenship means to discern, and to hold fast after discernment. At each moment of her life, Mary made a discernment, and held fast to it without making any compromise; and c) Queenship means to impact on other’s life positively – Mary left a positive impact on all the people she met.

    ref: "Queenship of Mary (1955)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      From the earliest centuries of the Church, Christians have addressed fervent prayers and hymns of praise to the Blessed Virgin Mary. Because of her eminence, Christians have always acknowledged the royal excellence of the Mother of God whose Son “will reign in the house of Jacob forever” (Lk 1:32).

      From the testimonies of the Fathers, theologians concluded that the Blessed Virgin is Queen of all creation, Sovereign Mistress of all humankind. “Since the great Virgin Mary was raised to the dignity of Mother of the King of Kings, the Church justly honours her, and wishes that she be honoured with the glorious title of Queen,” says Saint Alphonsus de Liguori.

      Proposing the traditional doctrine on the Queenship of Mary, Pope Pius XII says in his encyclical Ad Coeli Reginam: “We commend that on the festival there be renewed the consecration of the human race to the Immaculate Heart of the Blessed Virgin Mary. Upon this there is founded a great hope that there may arise an era of happiness that will rejoice in the triumph of religion and in Christian peace.

      “Therefore, let all approach with greater confidence more than before, the throne of mercy and grace of our Queen and Mother to beg help in difficulty, light in darkness and solace in trouble and sorrow; and let them strive to free themselves from the servitude of sin....”
