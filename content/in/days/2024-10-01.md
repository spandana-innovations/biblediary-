      St Theresa of the Child Jesus was a French Catholic Discalced Carmelite nun who is widely venerated in modern times and popularly known as "The Little Flower.” Theresa wanted to enter the Carmelite convent but the superior of the Carmelite convent refused to take Theresa because she was too young. The shy little girl went to the bishop for recommendation but he too said no. She decided to meet the Pope on a pilgrimage to Rome along with the family. At the recommendation of Pope Leo XIII, when she was barely 15, Mother Prioress finally admitted her on 9 April 1888. Her childlike simplicity, utter humility, constant self-sacrifice and a boundless love of God and trust in him were some of her most outstanding virtues which made her to be the Novice Mistress at the age of 22. She didn't want to just be good but she wanted to be a saint. During her short span of religious life, though misunderstood by her own confreres, her special task was to assist the priests and the missionaries through her prayers and sacrifices. She died on 30 September 1897 at the age of 24, after having lived as a cloistered Carmelite for less than ten years.



      *(Illustrated Book of Saints: Fr Michael Raj ssp)*
  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      By presenting a child as the perfect model for the one wishing to enter the Kingdom of Heaven, Jesus invites the disciples to have a childlike trust in God. In fact, it was the disciples’ preoccupation with rank and status in the kingdom that prompted the whole discussion. It is not supremacy but the humility that emanates from a perfect trust that is the basis of greatness in the kingdom. One cannot place one’s trust in God and at the same time in power and position. The disciples who walk in the way of the kingdom should be free from all other attachments and focus only on God. Saint Thérèse of Lisieux, who always called God “my heavenly dad”, is a great example of such childlike trust. *Do I have childlike trust in God even when things do not go well?*

    audio: null
    body: |
      Marie Francoise Therese was the youngest of nine children of devout French parents, Louis and Zelie Martin, both of whom were canonized in 2015. She was barely 15 when she fulfilled her cherished dream of entering the Carmelites in 1888.



      Sr Therese of the Child Jesus was favoured with signal graces, and advanced so rapidly in holiness that at the age of 22 she was appointed novice mistress. A childlike simplicity, utter humility, constant self-sacrifice, and a boundless love of God and trust in him were some of her most outstanding virtues as was evident from her now world renowned autobiography “The Story of a Soul”, which she wrote under obedience. Not to be conspicuous in anything, not to complain, not to reveal that she was ailing, to show a special friendliness towards those who were unsympathetic to her, and to answer pointed remarks with a winning smile—these were some of her axioms.



      After a martyrdom of both mind and body, and having offered herself in sweet resignation and heroic patience as a victim of God’s merciful love, Therese died of consumption in 1897 at the age of 24. “There is just one thing to be done here below: to love Jesus and to save souls for him, so that he may be more loved!”



      Canonized in 1925 and declared Doctor of the Church, she is the Patron Saint of aviators and of Russia, and with St Francis Xavier, of all Catholic missions.
