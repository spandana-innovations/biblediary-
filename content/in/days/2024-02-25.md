  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mark 9:2-10"
    audio: null
    body: |
      In the scene of the Transfiguration of Jesus, all the elements of the Divine Presence are in evidence: the mountain top, the cloud, the voice from heaven, and the presence of Moses and Elijah.



      It is a momentary, supernatural experience. For a moment they are in the realm of the divine, of an inexplicable joy.



      It is to show them that there is something beyond the struggles, burdens, pains, sorrows and the confusion of this world. There awaits them the joy and the glory of heaven.



      Peter wanted to stay on in that experience. However, the voice from heaven tells them: “This is my beloved Son, listen to him.” Listening to Jesus would mean climbing down the mountain and going back to the real, tough world, living out Jesus’ teachings, being tested and tried like him, walking with him on the way to Calvary and being crucified with him before they experience the joy of a glorious eternity with him in heaven.



      What influence does Jesus have on my life? Does my participation in the Eucharist transform me and make me a better Christian? How do I view my daily crosses?



      (Fr Joe Eruppakkatt, SSP)
  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      Peter, James, and John went up the mountain and were invited to witness the Lord in all His glory as he talked to Moses and Elijah, probably about his impending passion. It was a preparation for them as they would soon see Jesus their Master suffer on another mountain – Mount Calvary. ‘Master, it is good for us to be here,” exclaimed Peter. The Tabor experience was so captivating for Peter, James, and John that they wanted to stay there forever. Can we identify with their experience? Do we praise God both in our peak moments and in our weak moments, in our Tabor moments and in our Calvary moments? Abraham, our father in faith, was commanded to go up a mountain to sacrifice his only son Isaac. He listened, trusted, and obeyed and God blessed Abraham by saving his son Isaac. Lord Jesus, prepare me for both the good and the bad times ahead.
