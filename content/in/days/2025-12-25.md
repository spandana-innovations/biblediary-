      Reflection: 1



      Jesus was born in Bethlehem at the time of a world-wide census. This points to the significance of the birth of Jesus for the whole world, as the one who brings peace and salvation. Joseph and Mary, residents of Nazareth, went to Bethlehem to register, because Joseph was of the lineage of David, who was a Bethlehemite. This is also an indication that Jesus is a descendant of David.



      The newborn Jesus was laid in a manger, the feeding place of livestock. Jesus was to become the bread of life for the world. Our Christmas celebration should mean our recognition and acceptance of Jesus and welcoming him in our hearts, and our living in peace, joy and love.



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2God near to us



      Christmas is much more than all the superficial and manipulative things going on around us these days. A feast day much deeper and joyful than all the fads of our consumer society. We believers in Jesus must recover again the heart of this feast and look behind so much superficiality and bling to discover the mystery that gives us true joy. We need to learn again to «celebrate» Christmas. Not everyone knows what it is we’re celebrating. Not everyone knows what it is that opens our heart to such joy.



      We only understand Christmas if we know how to be silent in our heart, and open our soul to the mystery of a God who is near to us, and rejoice with the life that is offered us, and really taste this feast of the arrival of God our Friend.



      In the midst of a daily life that is too often boring, tired and sad, we are invited by God to rejoice. «Sadness has no place when life is being born» (St. Leo the Great). This is not a joy that is insipid or superficial, the partying glee of those who seek solace in abundance of food and drink. «We have cause for a radiant joy, one that is full and suited for the solemn feast: God has become human and has come to live among us» (Leonardo Boff). There is a joy that only can be enjoyed by those who open themselves up to God’s closeness and who allow themselves to be drawn by God’s tenderness. This joy is one that frees us of fears, mistrust and inhibition as we face God. Why fear a God who comes to us as a child? Why avoid One who comes among us as a fragile, defenceless baby? God hasn’t come armed with power to impose decrees on us humans, but in the tenderness of a child whom we can welcome or reject.



      God can’t be at the same time the «all-powerful» and «mighty» One that we must fear; rather, God is this child given lovingly to humanity, this little one who catches our gaze in order to make us happy with his smile. The fact that God has become a child says more about divinity than all our musings and speculations about the Holy Trinity. If we would know to pause in silence before this child and welcome God’s closeness and tenderness from the depths of our being, maybe we would understand why the believer’s heart can be pierced with a completely new kind of joy during these Christmas days.

With faith and joy, let’s revel in God’s intense and saving love this Christmas!

    ref: "Saint Anastasia - Virgin, Martyr"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Anastasia is believed to be one of those who suffered martyrdom on Christmas day under Emperor Diocletian. Named in the Roman Canon of the Mass she is honoured with a special commemoration in the second Mass of Christmas Day.



      According to tradition, Anastasia was the daughter of a Roman nobleman named Praetextatus and was married to a pagan named Publius, who died while on a mission to Persia (Iran); whilst caring for her fellow Christians, she was arrested and put on a ship along with another Christian and a number of pagan criminals to be abandoned mid-sea but was saved by Saint Theodata. She was then believed to have been martyred on the island of Palmaria—staked to the ground, limbs outstretched, and a huge fire kindled about her.



      A church was built in her honour and, in the fifth century, her relics were said to have been transferred to the sanctuary of a church in the Byzantine capital by Gennadius, Patriarch of Constantinople. Significantly, in the fourth century, a Roman Basilica was redecorated by Pope Damasus under the title of “Anastasia”, a name which means Resurrection. Cruciform in structure, this is one of the important churches of Rome.
