  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mark 1:29–39"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today’s Gospel tells us that preaching the Good News of God’s love, mercy, and salvation and healing the sick were the means Jesus used to enable his listeners to do the will of God and thus to build up the Kingdom of God, allowing God to take control of their lives. We are also told that Jesus recharged his spiritual batteries by talking with and listening to his Heavenly Father. Thus, preaching, healing, and recharging spiritual power by prayer were the three key points of Jesus’ public ministry.</span>



      Healing mission: Jesus was never tired of healing the sick, thus demonstrating the mercy and compassion of his Heavenly Father to every sick person who approached him with trusting Faith. As soon as he had finished the day’s preaching in the synagogue on one Sabbath, Jesus went to Simon’s home and healed Simon’s mother-in-law of a fever. In the evening when the Sabbath rest was over, people brought all their sick dear ones to Jesus for healing and exorcism, and he healed them all. Jesus began the next day very early, spending time in prayer in a lonely place.</span>



      <span class="subhead">Life messages: </span></span>



      1) We are called to continue Jesus’ preaching mission primarily by bearing witness to Christ through our day-to-day lives, as we radiate Christ’s mercy, love, forgiveness, and spirit of humble service to all around us. </span>



      2) We can participate in Jesus’ healing mission by praying for the sick, by visiting them, and by helping and encouraging the sick and shut-ins. </span>



      3) But in order to continue Jesus’ preaching and healing mission, we, too, need to have our spiritual batteries recharged every day by prayer as Jesus did. </span>



      *(Fr Tony: https://frtonyshomilies.com/)*

    body: |
      The word that could sum up the activities of Jesus in today’s gospel passage is *availability*. Jesus was available to people and his heavenly Father. He was available to meet the needs of people including those who were sick and demon-possessed. No one was turned away – no matter what the need. He also found time to be with his Heavenly Father in prayer and made himself available to Him who was the source of his power and energy. Availability means being willing to adjust our schedule, agenda and plans to fit the right desires of God and others. It is to reflect God’s priorities, so we are always available to Him, and others, when we are serving. How available am I to God and others?</span>
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Paul, the First Hermit - Confessor (229-342)"
    ref: "Saint Paul, the First Hermit - Confessor (229-342)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      A native of Thebes in Egypt, Saint Paul was orphaned at 15 but grew up to be wealthy and highly educated. Fearing that the tortures of a terrible persecution might endanger his Christian perseverance, he retired to a remote village. But denounced by his brother-in-law, a pagan, he fled to the desert to escape the Decian persecution, trusting that God would provide for his wants—a confidence that was amply rewarded, for at the place where Providence led him he found the fruit of the Palm tree for food, its leaves for clothing and the water of a brook for drink. When the palm no longer provided him with food, a raven brought him his daily bread.</span>



      The persecution fever of Decius having abated, Paul’s first impulse was to return to the world; but having tasted great delight in prayer and penance, he remained there the rest of his life, 90 years to be precise, in prayer and contemplation.</span>



      God miraculously revealed his existence to Saint Anthony, who sought him for three days. Seeing a thirsty wolf run through an opening in the rocks, Anthony followed it in a bid to quench his own thirst, and there found Paul. Even as Anthony was with him, the raven brought a double allowance of bread. Having spent the night in prayer, Paul told Anthony at the break of dawn that he was about to die and requested to be buried in the cloak given to Anthony by Saint Athanasius. Anthony hastened to fetch it and, on his way back, saw Paul’s soul rise to heaven in glory. As for his dead body, he found it kneeling as if in prayer. Two lions came and dug the grave for him. </span>



      Saint Paul, who died at the age of 113, is venerated as the Patron of weavers.</span>
