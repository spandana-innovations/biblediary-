      The godless say to themselves, with their misguided reasoning: ‘Let us lie in wait for the virtuous man, since he annoys us and opposes our way of life, reproaches us for our breaches of the law and accuses us of playing false to our upbringing.       Reflection: 1



      When Jesus was engaged in his salvific mission in Galilee, the Jews were seeking to kill him. When he taught in the temple, they pretended to know everything about him. They did not accept him as the saviour and the true Son of God. But he boldly carried on his mission. Only a humble seeker will be able to discover who Jesus is. To be saved, it is important to know who Jesus is and where he comes from. As he comes from God, he offers us the unimaginable gift of sharing the very life of God. In order to be saved, each one of us has to discover Jesus personally by prayer and sincere search for truth. Let us pray for the grace to recognize that Jesus is the Son of God and our Saviour, and to follow him faithfully.



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel passage describes Jesus’ secret journey to Jerusalem to participate in the Feast of Tabernacles (Booths), amid rumours of his possible arrest. But Jesus courageously made his public appearance in the crowd in Jerusalem and started teaching in the Temple. Naturally, people started wondering why the authorities did not arrest him.



      Jesus’ Messianic claim and the Jewish reaction: Jesus made two unique and seemingly blasphemous claims. 1) He claimed that he was the Messiah, God’s Anointed One. 2) Jesus made the additional claim that only he knew God as He is because Jesus had come from God. By this claim, Jesus contradicted the belief of the Jews that they had the perfect and final revelation of God given through Moses and the prophets. In addition, Jesus’ claim to be the Messiah, and the “Son of Man” prophesied by Daniel, with exclusive and intimate knowledge of God was, they thought, nothing but blasphemy. The Jews argued that a mere carpenter-turned-wandering-preacher from Nazareth could not be the Messiah because nobody was supposed to know where the Messiah would come from. According to Jewish belief, the Messiah would emerge quite unexpectedly from Mount Olivet, cross the Kedron Valley, enter the city of Jerusalem, be anointed by Elijah the prophet, take possession of the City and the Temple and establish His Messianic kingdom.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span> 1) Like the Jews, we, too, can be prejudiced and occasionally refuse to accept and follow the teachings of the Church. We need to have the humility to honour the teaching authority of the Church and its guidance by the Holy Spirit.



      2) We need to accept Jesus as our Lord and personal Saviour, experience him through prayer and the Sacramental life and surrender our lives to him.



      3) Like the Jews who expected the surprise appearance of a super-human Messiah we, too, show the tendency to seek God only in miraculous and extraordinary events, ignoring His presence within us and in everyone around us.

      Life’s beauty is truly enhanced when we take the ‘road less travelled’, guided by deep convictions. These convictions lead us to experience true serenity and happiness, allowing us to weather life’s storms. Our life may be the only Gospel that people ever encounter! In today’s Gospel, Jesus faces rising popularity and opposition with unwavering resolve, healing the sick and comforting the oppressed while boldly affirming His mission.     saintName: "Saint Cuthbert, Bishop Confessor (c.635 - 687)"
    ref: "Saint Cuthbert, Bishop Confessor (c.635 - 687)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Brought up by a foster mother named Kenswith, this famous English Saint was a shepherd, a native of Northumbria (England). As a young man, he had to help defend his country against the attacks of King Penda of Mercia; but when peace was restored after the battle of Winwidfield in 651, he carried out his boyhood dream of becoming a monk — he joined the Benedictines at Mailros (Melrose) abbey, and soon became conspicuous for his learning and holiness, which God confirmed by numerous miracles.



      He was appointed Prior at Lindisfarne, and was able, by his consummate tact and patience, and through the evident holiness of his life, to win over that entire community which had still obstinately clung to the ancient Celtic customs.



      In 685, Cuthbert was consecrated Abbot-Bishop of Lindisfarne by St Theodore of Canterbury and laboured with great success at the sanctification of the faithful; but after only a year, he resigned in anticipation of his approaching death. He died on 20 March 687.



      St Cuthbert is the Patron of sailors.
