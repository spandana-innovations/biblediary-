  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Matthew 10:7-13"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Barnabas was among the most amiable, life-affirming members of the early Church. Luke describes him in glowing terms as “a good man, full of the Holy Spirit and of faith.” A trusted confidant of the apostles, he shared much of his property with the poor, and his was the voice that had the former persecutor, Saul/Paul, accepted as genuine within the Christian community in Jerusalem. As he is portrayed in Acts, he had the sort of unselfish, reliable, encouraging personality that made him an ideal ambassador for the Gospel. This made him the perfect helper to be sent to support the growing church in Antioch.



      It seems that Saul (later called Paul) had been too forthright in preaching the faith in the synagogues to which he once had belonged. This resulted in the apostles sending him back to his native Tarsus, into virtual exile, to cool his heels until he learned to measure his speech to the volatile circumstances in Jerusalem. Fortunately, Barnabas did not want Paul’s great gifts to be wasted, knowing how precious they would be within the Antioch Christian community. So, as Luke relates with relish, he brought Saul to Antioch where, between them, the instructed many in the faith, building up an integration of Jewish and Gentile converts into what we may properly regard as the first truly “catholic” church. It was from this dynamic church community that Barnabas and Paul went out on the first explicitly missionary journey, to Barnabas’ home island of Cyprus, and then further afield up into Asia Minor. The Church in later centuries has incorporated elements of the ordaining ceremony whereby the two missionaries were sent out BOTH by the Holy Spirit’s impulse AND by a ceremony of prayer and laying on of hands.



      Our Gospel for his feast reports Jesus’ guidance to his twelve apostles, with all the urgency and goodwill that their mission required. They were to be zealous with the message and in the ministry of healing, and practice a sober, purposeful lifestyle, unconcerned for the trappings of wealth and status. How well Barnabas, and later Paul, measured up to those missionary requirements is well illustrated in their story as told by Luke, and then by Paul in his letters. Today’s feast can serve to recall all of us to those qualities in Barnabas which gave such a boost to the growth of the Church in his time. With pastors like him, our Church could have a great revival.



      (Association of Catholic Priests: Homily Resources).

      The feast of the Apostle Barnabas reminds us of the missionary impetus of the Gospel: “As you go, proclaim this message: 'The Kingdom of Heaven has come near’’’ (v 7). On the one hand, when we seek God’s plan for our life, and work for His purposes and glory, we proclaim the kingdom of God which has come near. On the other hand, the kingdom of God becomes present in the actions which Jesus recommends: healing the sick, raising the dead, cleansing the lepers and driving out demons. Whenever we reach out to others in service, we bear witness to God’s kingdom coming near. How would you announce the Kingdom in our midst today?

      St Barnabas (Apostle and Martyr) (c.1- 60)

      Barnabas, named Joseph at his birth on the island of Cyprus, settled in Jerusalem and is thought to have studied in the school of Gamaliel at the same time as Saul. Converting to Christianity after Pentecost, he sold all his possessions for the benefit of the Church, earning the surname Barnabas, meaning “son of consolation” or “encouragement”. It was he who welcomed the converted Saul to Jerusalem and sponsored him before the Apostles who at first had found it difficult to believe that their fiercest persecutor had become an ardent propagator of the faith. However, from his previous acquaintance with Saul, Barnabas knew Saul to be utterly sincere in his convictions. To both of these great men, the Church, before long, accorded the title of “Apostle”.

      Barnabas readily overcame his Jewish prejudices and rejoiced at the first manifestation of God’s grace among the Gentiles, and together with St Paul, whom he brought from Tarsus, spent an entire year preaching the gospel at Antioch.

      In 45, when Jerusalem was grievously afflicted by famine, the two apostles brought assistance from the Antioch Christians to the Mother Church. After their return they set out on their first great missionary journey, taking with them John Mark, a cousin of Barnabas, and began with the evangelization of the latter’s native island, Cyprus, followed by conversions and the establishment of churches with a native clergy in Asia Minor.

      Believed to have been stoned to death before the year 61, when John Mark was sent to assist Paul in Rome, Barnabas’ remains were discovered near Salamis on Cyprus in 482, with a copy of Matthew’s Gospel in Hebrew entombed with him.

      Reflection: “He was a good man, and full of the Holy Spirit and of faith” (Acts 11:24).
