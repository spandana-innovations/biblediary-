      Jesus’ disciples wanted to know why Jesus spoke in parables to the common people but explained to his disciples the parables’ implicit and hidden meanings.</span></span></span></span></span>



      Reasons: Jesus gives two reasons for using parables. 1) The so-called intellectuals, like the scribes and the Pharisees, had closed their minds and hearts to his preaching. That is one of the reasons why Jesus started speaking to the common people with open minds and receptive hearts, using their simple language and telling them stories and parables based on their lives. 2) The secrets of God’s kingdom are meant for humble people with open minds and large receptive hearts, not for proud intellectuals who are closed to them.</span></span></span></span></span>



      Blessing: Then Jesus congratulates his apostles and the common people on their good fortune in seeing the long-awaited Messiah, hearing his words and experiencing his company.</span></span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life message</span></span></span>: 1) We are more blessed than Jesus’ first-century audience because we have his message in written form and his Presence in the Holy Eucharist, in the Holy Bible and in the praying community.     body: |
      The contrast between the productive seeds and unproductive seeds is continued in Jesus’ explanation of why He was speaking in parables. The contrast is between His disciples and the crowds. The disciples have remained with Jesus and opened their minds and hearts to receive His message of the kingdom of heaven. They have responded positively to the grace that was bestowed on them. They proved themselves to be fruitful disciples of the Lord. They are truly blessed as they have been given the chance to see and hear what many prophets and righteous people longed for years. The crowds, representing Israel, blew hot and cold to the message of Jesus. There were moments of excitement and acceptance, but their responses lacked perseverance and earnestness. The message is clear: do not just be a member of the crowd; be a disciple.</span></span></span>
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Bridget of Sweden Widow (1303-1373)"
    ref: "Saint Bridget of Sweden Widow (1303-1373)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Bridget (or Brigitta), brought up by an aunt after the untimely demise of her devout parents, lived her life to the full first as a married woman and then as a religious. Married when barely 13 she lost her husband, Ulf Gudmarsson, in 1344, after 28 years of a happy marriage that bore them four sons and four daughters, of whom, Catherine of Sweden is a saint. </span></span></span></span>



      Her penitential exercises and prophetic visions constantly on the rise since the early age of seven, the Passion of Christ marked the centre of her faith that expressed itself in charity. In response to one of her numerous “revelations”, Bridget founded the double Order of the Holy Redeemer (for men and women, with separate enclosures for each) at Vadstena in 1346, with generous endowments from the king. There were, in imitation of Christ’s 72 disciples (12 Apostles and Saint Paul), 4 Deacons, 8 Lay Brothers, and in a nearby convent 60 Nuns, who by their prayers were to sustain the preaching apostolate of the priests. In keeping with what her name denotes, viz. courage and strength, she did not fear to reprove the king himself, besides princes, priests and popes, for dereliction of duty and morals.</span></span></span></span>



      During her Holy Land pilgrimage in 1372, she was blessed with four months of near continuous visions of the Lord’s life and Passion. These began with the young maiden Mary in her parents’ home, while in the humble cave at Bethlehem she witnessed Our Lady kneeling enthralled before her newborn Infant and exclaiming: “Be welcome, my Lord and my Son!” </span></span></span></span>



      Bridget was canonized in 1391.</span></span></span></span>
