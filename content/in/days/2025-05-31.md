      Reflection: 1



      Mary the younger woman, and her cousin Elizabeth, the elder woman, who are both going to be mothers, meet. At the same time, their sons, still in their wombs, also meet. Elizabeth is overjoyed at the visit of Mary, the mother of her Lord. And John, still within his mother, leap for joy on meeting Jesus who was still within Mary. Elizabeth twice calls Mary blessed. In response, Mary sings exulting in the Lord who has done great things in her. In her song Mary recalls briefly the great things God has done in the history of His people, from Abraham onwards. Mary visited Elizabeth as an act of charity, to help her in her time of need. Her visit is an inspiration for us to engage in works of fraternal help among ourselves. It is likely that through our charitable visit, God may be meeting us or the other person or both. Such meetings will be moments of grace.</span></span></span></span>



      *— Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP</span></span>*</span></span>



      Reflection: 2The Visitation of Mary



      The mystery of the Incarnation comes to ordinary people living ordinary lives, who have the willingness to respond to God’s call and the openness and generosity to do God’s will. Luke, in today’s Gospel, tells us how two seemingly insignificant women met to celebrate the kindness and fidelity of God. In the Gospel, one definition of discipleship is to listen to God’s word and then carry it out. Mary did both, to become the most perfect disciple. The incident also shows us how sensitive Mary was to the needs of Elizabeth, her older cousin, who had miraculously become pregnant in her old age.



      Mary’s visit to Elizabeth. There is a saying, “*One who is on fire cannot sit on a chair.*” Mary, carrying Jesus and filled with the empowering fire of the Holy Spirit, hurried to the mountain country, a distance of perhaps 100 miles, where Elizabeth lived, thereby conveying the Holy Spirit to her cousin and her child. Like all good Jews, Mary was prompted in everything she did by her commitment to God’s word in her life.



      The paradox of blessedness. Blessedness confers on a person both the greatest joy and the greatest task in the world. Nowhere can we see the paradox better illustrated than in Mary’s life. Mary was granted the blessedness and privilege of being the mother of the Son of God. Yet, that very blessedness was to be a sword to pierce her heart: one day she would see her Son hanging on a cross. So, to be chosen by God is often both a crown of joy and a cross of sorrow. God does not choose us to give us a life of ease and comfort, but in order that we may respond to His love by accomplishing His purposes.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span>



      1) We should recognize the real presence of Emmanuel (God Is with Us) and say “yes” to Him: The Visitation of Mary reminds us that, through his holy ministry, Christ continues to be present among his people. Let us recognize and appreciate the truth that the same Christ “dwells among us” in the Bible, in the Sacraments, in the praying community, and in our souls.



      2) We should convey Jesus to others as Mary did to Elizabeth. We can make a real difference in the lives of others today by carrying Jesus to them. For that, we must be filled with the spirit of Christ, allowing his rebirth within us. Then Jesus will enable us to share his love with all whom we encounter, offering them humble and committed service, unconditional forgiveness, and compassionate caring service.

      The Feast of the Visitation illustrates Mary’s heart filled with love for her neighbour, inspired by her unparalleled devotion to God. Just as to her cousin, she is the Visitor who always seeks to fill our lives with her maternal care, aid, and heavenly graces. As children, it is indeed our duty to create in us a welcoming atmosphere for Our Blessed Mother. In turn, Our Blessed Mother would expect us to be visitors who bring good news to the persons we meet. Mary, who brought physical, emotional, and spiritual consolation is our model in all corporal and spiritual works of mercy. Let us touch the lives of people around us with the love that is following the will of God, and not with arrogance, selfishness, and pride.
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Visitation of the Blessed Virgin Mary"
    ref: "Visitation of the Blessed Virgin Mary"
    audio: null
    body: |
      This feast commemorates the visit of the Virgin Mary, pregnant with the child Jesus, to her cousin Elizabeth, pregnant with John the Baptizer. It was at the Annunciation that Mary was informed of old Elizabeth’s pregnancy (Lk 1:36), prompting her to go “with haste” (Lk 1:39) to be of help to her cousin, thereby commencing her involvement in the work of redemption. What followed is awe-inspiring! At the sound of Mary’s greeting, the child in Elizabeth’s womb is sanctified and cleansed of original sin (cf. Lk 1:44) as foretold by the Angel Gabriel to Zechariah (cf. Lk 1:15) and in a leaping instant begins his mission of bearing witness to the light. Indeed, as David danced for joy before the Ark of the Covenant of old, John, the last of the prophets of the Old Testament, leaps for joy before Mary, the Ark of the New Covenant that brings God’s presence into the world! Then, “ . . . . filled with the Holy Spirit”, Elizabeth boldly declared Mary the most “blessed” among women, the Mother of God (cf. Lk 1:41-42) — the first formal acknowledgement as it were of the “Theotokos”!



      The feast of the Visitation was first heard of in 1263, when it was adopted by the Franciscans, its observance extended to the whole Church in 1389 by Pope Urban VI in supplication for the end of the Great Schism.
