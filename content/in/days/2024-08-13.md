  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Matthew 18:1–5, 10, 12–14"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Any unwanted incident in life is seen as bad omen and a curse from God. To most of us the exile might have been a total discarding of the people who rebelled against God, but we find that it was a moment of purification and He was constantly behind them with his Word. Here Prophet Ezekiel is asked to help the people to come back to God’s loving presence not with his intellectual knowledge but by taking the Word of God into himself and making it integral part of his life. Therefore whatever Ezekiel spoke became a word of God and there was no dichotomy between word and his life. Same way now Jesus goes after the lost one as he presents the parable of the lost sheep and he is ready to die to get us back to His fold. This search is not ended and it is to be continued by his disciples all throughout the life of the Church.

      But does the sheep find the credibility in the shepherd who searches and prophet who prophecies? Do I speak the Word of God or my own words?

      *(Fr Saji Antony, SSP)*

      Jesus does not portray Abraham or Moses or Elijah or David or Solomon as the greatest in the Kingdom of Heaven. A child becomes the model. The disciples are asked to become like little children. Becoming little means, a) humbling oneself like a child; b) receiving like a child; and c) searching for the lost. In the First Reading, Prophet Ezekiel, in childlike obedience, surrenders to the Lord. In the Kingdom, growing up means growing down!

    body: |
      St Hippolytus, whose name means “a horse turned loose”, was a learned priest in Rome and the most important theological writer of his time. He claimed to have been a disciple of St Irenaeus, while St Jerome refers to him as “a most holy and eloquent man”.

      At first, however, he was known for his “holier-than-thou” attitude towards the Church, unafraid of censuring even the Pope. He criticized Pope St Zephyrinus for not being prompt in detecting and denouncing heresy. Attacking the contemporary Trinitarian Modalism (an assertion that the three Persons of the Trinity are merely modes, or self-manifestations of one and the same God) and Patripassianism (a theory that God the Father himself suffered on the cross) he propounded a doctrine on the Logos that was close to subordinationism (viz., the Son and the Spirit merely proceed from the Father and are therefore subordinate to him).

      In 235, Hippolytus was banished to Sardinia by Emperor Maximus. Here, sharing his exile was none other than Pope Pontianus himself, who, during his reign (230-235), had held a synod which confirmed the excommunication of Origen in Alexandria.

      The election of St Anterus ended the schism but his near instant martyrdom was followed by the election of Pope St Fabian (r.236-250) who brought the mortal remains of Hippolytus and Pontianus to Rome and accorded them a solemn burial — to Hippolytus in the cemetery of the Via Tiburtina and Pontianus in the crypt of St Callistus.
