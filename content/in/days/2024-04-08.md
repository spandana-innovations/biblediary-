  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 1:26-38"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today we celebrate the memory of that crucial moment which changed the history of the human race. It was in fact the most mysterious and most solemn event in the history of the whole universe. God who is beyond time and creation enters into our world and our history. God speaks to Mary, inviting her to cooperate with Him in His plan of redemption. Today’s Gospel is one of the central passages of the New Testament.</span></span></span></span>



      God’s plan is outlined to Mary. The humble Virgin of Nazareth is going to become the mother of the Saviour. Mary hears the message from heaven, thinks it right to seek clarification, and gives her consent. Her response is characterised by prudence, wisdom, sincerity and humility. Mary surrenders to God’s will. At her humble and obedient “fiat” (let it be done), the great miracle of miracles takes place. With a *fiat*God created heaven and earth, but at Mary’s *fiat* God became man. God the Son comes into the world to become one of us and one like us in all things except sin.</span></span></span></span>



      Today, through the Holy Scripture, God speaks to each one of us. He asks you and me to believe in His work and to co-operate with Him. God creates, God saves, God sanctifies, but not without human participation. St Augustine says, “The God who created you without you will not save you without you.” In His goodness God comes down to our level to raise us up to His level. He humbles Himself to share in our humanity so that we may be enabled to share in His divinity. He is really Emmanuel, God with us, and God for us. God comes in person to dwell with us.</span></span></span></span>



      (Excerpts from: Fr Dr George Kaitholil, *Feast Day Homilies*, ST PAULS, Mumbai, India)</span></span></span></span>

    body: |
      Today the Church celebrates the Solemnity of the Annunciation of the Lord. The Annunciation is the first moment when God’s desire to save humanity and the entire cosmos is being actualized. From the time humans lost their friendship with God through disobedience, God’s plan to save them was set in motion. God needed a human response to carry out that plan. So, God sends His angel not to Jerusalem, the Holy City, but to Galilee known as ‘Galilee of the gentiles’ looking for a response. Mary, an ordinary girl, gives her wholehearted response to God’s invitation. Being fully human, Mary teaches us how to cooperate with God by saying yes to God in all circumstances of our lives. Am I ready, like Mary, to say an unconditional ‘yes’?</span></span></span></span>

      Saint Julie Billiart</span></span> - *Virgin (1751-1816)*</span></span></span></span>



      Early in life, Marie Rose Julie Billiart, the fifth of a devout family of seven children in Piccardy, France, showed a deep attachment to things spiritual and took a vow of chastity at 14. Two years later, the family business having failed miserably, Julie had to “hire out” and work for others. About 1770, radiating a contagious love of God and an eagerness to be spiritually helpful to the young around her, she was permitted to receive Holy Communion daily—something unheard of at the time.</span></span></span></span>



      An attempt made on her father’s life so unnerved Julie, then 23, as to leave her paralysed and bedridden for the next 30 years. Her saintly spirit intact, however, her mystical life and her intimacy with God deepened further. Repeatedly in danger of death at the hands of the Republicans during the French Revolution (1789-95) she had to be precipitously moved from place to place, Mass and Communion becoming possible only at odd intervals. Through it all Julie continued to direct her charitable works from her bed, particularly anxious to offset the evil of the day by providing, through pious friends, for the education of poor orphans and, later on, of girls in general.</span></span></span></span>



      In August 1803, with the cooperation of Francoise Blin de Bourdon, Visçountess of Gezaincourt, and of Father Joseph Varin d’Ainville, Superior of the Fathers of the Faith, Julie set her work on a permanent footing with the founding of the “Institute of the Sisters of Notre Dame de Namur”. The following June, the then 53 year-old crippled, urged by a certain priest named Father Enfantin, took ‘a single step for the love of the Sacred Heart’ and found herself completely cured! </span></span></span></span>



      In time, she founded ten convents in France and Belgium. Considerable opposition and misunderstanding, particularly on the part of the Bishop of Amiens, caused her, in 1809, to transfer the motherhouse to Namur, Belgium.</span></span> </span></span>



      Marie Rose Julie Billiart who died in 1816 was canonized in 1969. </span></span></span></span>
