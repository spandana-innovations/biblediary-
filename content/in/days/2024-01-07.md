    body: |
      The three Wise Men from the East had not only observed the signs of the time but also drawn conclusions from them. God knew the greatest desire of their heart. Whether they were actually kings or not, we are not very sure. But what do we notice about them? They did not remain obsessed with the affairs of their kingdom or set their hearts on how to play power politics. They had an ambition, a desire greater than all these things – to see the face of the Messiah.



      God knew the deepest longing of their hearts and gave them a sign: the Star. They readily responded to God’s call and set out without fear, though the way was unknown and dangerous. To get a glimpse of the new-born King, and to offer Him gifts – that was their aim, and no hazard, however great, could stop them. Finally they arrived at their destination, but found the ‘new-born king’ in an insignificant place, in conditions unthinkable for a king. Yet their faith gave them reassurance; they fully believed the Child was the King and Saviour of mankind and offered Him their gifts.



      Have you seen His Star? Have you heard His call to search for Him? He sends His Star even today to you in different ways and through different persons and situations, to find Him and adore Him. A wise person and only such a one will recognize the Star of the Lord. He is attuned to the Spirit who speaks to him. He will know his vocation and will have the courage and generosity to follow it.



      The question is: Are you ready to listen to, to recognize the One who calls you, and do you want to meet Him? Are you prepared to take the risk, to accept the challenges in following Him and to offer your life as a gift to Him, through faith, hope, love and service? Nothing could stop the Magi until they had met the Lord and could offer Him their gifts. What about you? Are you afraid? Can anything in the world, be it fame or glory or pleasures or wealth or fear, stop you from meeting the Lord?



      An intellectually puffed up adult won’t do it. It was the child in the wise men that discovered the Child in the crib and the Child in the crib smiled at the child in the wise men – the source and summit of religion. The wise men then forgot that they were wise. It was a child’s world all over, it was the kingdom of God where only children and the child-like have entry. It was God-man in a child and man with the God-child!



      (Philip John SSP & Premdas SSP, New Horizon Homilies, ST PAULS, Mumbai, India)

      As we celebrate the Solemnity of the Epiphany of the Lord, we are reminded that Jesus is revealed to the whole world, the shepherds, the Kings, the wise men, as the source of joy and peace in the world. This feast is God’s desire to reveal Himself to us in a personal relationship rooted in the love of God that has come into the world. Prophet Isaiah speaks words of hope and rejoicing. Joy, light, glory and beauty flood the scene. The birth of Jesus was first made known to the shepherds of Bethlehem. He is now revealed to the Magi who have come from the East to adore Him. These kings represent all the peoples of the Earth and our search to find Jesus the Saviour of the world. It is important that each of us must continually search for the many ways in which God reveals Himself in our lives. The prudence and the wisdom of the wise men together with their willingness to listen to the promptings of God gives us the right direction in our search for God.

      (1) Epiphany of the Lord



      This Feast is a threefold commemoration of Our Lord’s ‘manifestation’ amongst us: first to the Gentiles as represented by the Magi; next at his baptism in the Jordan when his divinity is manifested; and then at the wedding feast at Cana in Galilee when he performed his first miracle, changing water into wine.

      In the Eastern tradition where the feast of the Epiphany originated in the third century, the emphasis is on the Baptism of the Lord, whereas in the Latin rite it is on the coming of the Magi, commonly known as the Three Wise Men or ‘Three Kings’ or perhaps members of the priestly clan of the Medes, the star they sighted miraculously guiding them on their long journey to Jerusalem and Bethlehem. While rendering the Infant King in the manger their homage, they offer him gold in keeping with his royal status, myrrh to venerate him as man in preparation for his burial, and incense with which they adore him as God.

      Saint Paul avers that Isaiah’s prophecy concerning Jerusalem refers to Christ—the true Light of the world—and his Church, to which those seeking the true light turn, stepping out of the darkness of error into the light of the Truth.

      (2) Saint Raymond of Penyafort - Abbot Confessor (1175-1275)



      This great Dominican, a close relative of the King of Aragon, is named after the castle of Penyafort in Catalonia, Spain, where he was born. After teaching Canon Law for 15 years at the University of Barcelona, and obtaining a doctorate at Bologna, Italy, Raymond entered the Order of Preachers in 1222. Together with Saint Peter Nolasco he established the Order of Our Lady of Mercy, for the redemption of the captives from the Moors. A powerful preacher, he is credited with the conversion of over 10,000 Saracens, Waldenses and Albigenses.

      Pope Gregory IX called him to Rome in 1230 to be his chaplain and Grand Penitentiary, entrusting to him the enormous task of systematizing, condensing and codifying into convenient form the Church’s decrees which had been accumulating for several centuries. So thorough was his work that it remained the basis of canon law until the new codex was promulgated in 1917. Raymond declined the archbishopric of Tarragona and retired to Spain after completing this work; but in 1238 he was elected the third Dominican General.

      Raymond reorganized the constitutions of the Order and established schools in Barcelona and Tunis for the teaching of Oriental languages. It was he who directed Saint Thomas Aquinas to compile his great Summa contra Gentiles. He died at the age of 100 and is the Patron Saint of canonists.

      <span class="subhead">Reflection:</span> *“There is a simple method that has been the secret of the success of many undertakings: “Think it over.” Be prudent, calculate well, make use of means with the help of good advisers and light from the Tabernacle. All this comes under prudence, which brings peace of mind”* (Blessed James Alberione).
