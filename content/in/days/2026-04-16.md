      Reflection: 1



      Today’s Gospel reading continues the thought of yesterday’s Gospel text. Those who do not accept Jesus and his teaching are sure to incur the rejection of God. They remain in the same state humanity was in when man was expelled from paradise. If anyone wants to be saved from divine rejection and to be able to see God’s face of merciful love, he or she must believe in Jesus and live according to his teachings. Therefore today’s Gospel reading concludes, “Whoever believes in the Son has eternal life, whoever does not obey the Don shall not see life, but eh wrath of God remains in him.” These are the words of John the Baptist who came to bear witness to the light, and that light is Jesus Christ.



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      In today’s Gospel passage, Jesus explains his Divinity to Nicodemus and his relationship with God the Father and God the Holy Spirit. It is Jesus’ Divinity which gives authority and veracity to his teachings and credibility to his promise of eternal life for his followers.



      Jesus’ claims: 1) Jesus claims that, as Son of God, he “comes from Heaven.” Hence, he can speak of God and Heaven from his own experience, just as the native of a town can speak authoritatively and reliably about his town. 2) While the Jews believed that prophets were given only a small share in God’s Spirit, Jesus, as God’s only Son, shares the fullness of God’s Spirit and, hence, all his teachings and promises are always reliable. 3) Further, Jesus gives eternal life to his followers: “*He who believes in the Son has eternal life; he who does not obey the Son shall not see life, but the wrath of God rests upon him” (Jn 3:36).*



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span> 1) We need to seek the daily guidance and strengthening of the Holy Spirit living within us because it is He Who reveals Divine truths to us and Who gives us a better and clearer understanding of Scriptural truths taught by the Church. 2) Since our destiny depends on our own free daily choices, we need to choose Christ and his teachings and stand for Christ’s ideas and ideals. 3) We need to choose Jesus in order to choose Life. Before his death, Moses challenged Israel: *“See I have set before you this day life and good, death and evil…. Therefore, choose life that you may live, loving the Lord your God, obeying His voice, and cleaving to him” (Dt 30:15-20).*Joshua repeated the challenge in *Jos 24:14-15.* We face that challenge every day.

      In today’s Gospel, we are reminded of the supremacy of Jesus, who “comes from above” and speaks of heavenly things. John contrasts Jesus with those who are “of the earth”, showing us that Jesus alone reveals God’s truth fully, for He comes from God. He bears witness to what He has seen and heard, yet not everyone accepts His testimony. By believing in Him, we receive the gift of everlasting life; however, rejecting Him leads to separation from that life. John’s words encourage us to deepen our faith, listen to Jesus’ teachings, and embrace His promise of eternal life. In Acts, we see the apostles standing fearlessly before the authorities, choosing to obey God rather than give in to human threats. Their courage comes from their deep trust in Jesus.

    body: |
      The eldest of 9 children of Francoise [a miller] and Louise Soubirous, Sister Marie-Bernarde Soubirous, popularly known as Bernadette, was born at Nevers in Lourdes, at the foothills of the French Pyrenees.



      In 1858, Bernadette, together with her sister Marie and a friend named Jeanne, were out looking for firewood for the family hearth when, in a grotto of Massabielle along the river Gave, she beheld a vision of Our Lady which she described to her companions thus: “A Lady of unspeakable beauty, dressed in white, drawn in at the waist by a blue sash. She had a white veil on her head and falling down her back.... Her feet were bare and there was a rose on each of them. On her right arm, a rosary of white beads... whose chain was golden, all shining like the two roses on her feet.”



      Mary, who appeared to and conversed with Bernadette on 17 other occasions, said: “I promise to make you happy, if not in this world, at least in the next” (*18 February*); “Penance! Penance! Penance!” (*24 February*); “Go and tell the Priests that a chapel must be built here” (*27 February*); “I am the Immaculate Conception” (*25 March*).



      Bernadette endured much opposition from family, civil authorities and clergy alike, all of whom refused to believe her. The Bishop of Tarbes appointed a Commission of ecclesiastical and medical experts to inquire into the reality and nature of the apparitions and miracles that had occurred at Lourdes. Before the reports of the commission were even approved by the Bishop three years later, the fame of Lourdes had spread worldwide, its position as a place of pilgrimage firmly established!



      In 1866, Bernadette commenced her novitiate at the motherhouse of the Sisters of Nevers, and in 1878, the year she made her perpetual vows, she renewed before the representatives of the Bishops of Tarbes and Nevers the depositions she had made 20 years before, regarding the grotto apparitions. Bernadette died of Asthma and TB in 1879 with a smile on her face at her impending encounter with her “Lady in White” and was canonized in 1933.
