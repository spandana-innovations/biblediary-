  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 10: 25-37"
    audio: null
    body: |
      A scribe asked Jesus a very basic religious question: “What should I do to inherit eternal life?” In answer to the question, Jesus directed the scribe’s attention to the Sacred Scriptures. The Scriptural answer is, “Love God and express it by loving your neighbour.” However, to the scribe, the word “neighbour” meant another scribe or Pharisee, never a Samaritan or a Gentile. Hence, the scribe insisted on further clarification of the word “neighbour.” So, Jesus told him the parable of the Good Samaritan. The parable clearly indicates that a “neighbour” is anyone who needs help. Thus, the correct approach is not to ask who our neighbour is, but instead, to ask, “Am I a good neighbour to those I meet, helping them in their needs?” The Good Samaritan is a symbol of Jesus, himself, in his role as Saviour of the world. The parable: In the parable of the Good Samaritan, Jesus presents three philosophies of life concerning our relationship with our neighbour: 1) the philosophy of the thieves who robbed the Samaritan: *“What is yours is mine; I will take it by force.*2) the philosophy of the Jewish priest and the Levite: *“What is mine is mine; I won’t part with it.”* 3) the philosophy of the Samaritan: *“What is mine is yours as well. I shall share it with you.*</span></span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life message:</span></span></span> We need to have hearts of mercy: We need to remember that the road from Jerusalem to Jericho passes right through our home, parish and workplace. Jesus is inviting us to show mercy and kindness to those who are being hurt or mistreated on any of the “Jericho Roads” of our lives in our family, neighbourhood and parish.</span></span></span></span></span>

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      The name “Samaritan” has come to mean someone who shows charity, compassion, care, and service. In Jesus’ story, the Samaritan didn’t care about the wounded person’s background – he didn’t check if he was from the same neighbourhood, family, race, or religion. Instead, he chose to be a true neighbour, putting aside his own comfort to help the wounded person. This Samaritan represents Christ, the Son of God, who came to save humanity, wounded and hurt by sin. Jesus lifted us up, washed us clean with His own blood, healed us with His own suffering, and brought us home, paying the price with His life. He asked His followers to “go and do likewise.” This is the call to be a “Samaritan” or a true neighbour to those in need around us. The call to mercy is an invitation to recognise that each person in need is deserving of our kindness, regardless of their differences. Can I be a Samaritan, a true neighbour, to someone today?</span></span></span>
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Maria Faustina Kowalska, Virgin, visionary (1905-1938)"
    ref: "Saint Maria Faustina Kowalska, Virgin, visionary (1905-1938)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Named Helena at birth, this ardent promoter of the “Divine Mercy” devotion worked as a domestic since the age of fourteen in order to help her parents until, in 1925, she entered the Congregation of the Sisters of Our Lady of Mercy at Warsaw. Here, amidst the humble setting of the simple daily chores Jesus, in a vision in 1931, made her his secretary, confidant and messenger, instructing her to paint an image of his according to the profile she saw. She was blessed with other innumerable and extensive mystical experiences which she put down in her “Diary” at the behest of her spiritual director and of Jesus himself.</span></span></span>



      She took the name Maria Faustina at her veiling in the Novitiate in 1926 and made her final profession in 1933. In 1934, she visited the artist, E. Kazimirowski, in connection with the proposed painting. </span></span></span>



      Despite ill-health and humiliation, she was ever obedient to the dictates of conscience, superior and Lord. Suffering in silence, she prayed fervently for priests, for the Church and her needs, and for the world at large. The Lord for his part, made his satisfaction known to her thus: “My child, you are my delight. You are the comfort of my heart. I grant you as many graces as you can hold. As often as you want to make me happy, speak to the world about my great and unfathomable mercy.”</span></span></span>



      Sr Maria Faustina Kowalska was canonized by her compatriot, Pope Saint John Paul II, in the Jubilee Year, 2000.</span></span></span>
