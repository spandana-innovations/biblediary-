  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mark 4: 26-34"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      The parable of the seed growing by itself emphasizes the power of the word of Jesus, represented by the seed, to grow without any human intervention. If we respond wholeheartedly to the message of Jesus, it will become fruitful in us.



      The parable of the mustard seed carries a similar message about the growth of the kingdom which begins in a small way but finally becomes great. Though the mustard seed is among the smallest seeds it grows into a big shrub which can shelter many birds. Jesus uses parables to communicate in a simple way his message about the meaning of the kingdom of God. Those who are close to Jesus grasp its deeper meaning.



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Using the mini parables of the growth of wheat seeds and mustard *s*eeds in the field, Jesus explains the nature of the growth of the Kingdom of God or rule of God in human beings and human societies. In the case of both wheat and mustard seeds, the initial growth is slow and unnoticeable. But within days a leafy shoot will emerge, and within months a mature plant with numerous branches and leaves, flowers and fruits will be produced. The growth is silent and slow but steady, using power from the seed in the beginning and transforming absorbed water and minerals for energy in the later stages. Jesus explains that the Kingdom of God grows this way in human souls. The Kingdom of God is the growth of God’s rule in human hearts that occurs when man does the will of God and surrenders his life to God. It is slow and microscopic in the beginning. But it grows by using the power of the Holy Spirit, given to us through the Word of God, the Sacraments, and our prayers. Finally, God’s rule in the human heart transforms individuals and communities into God’s people, doing His will in His kingdom.



      Life message 1) As we learn God’s will from His words and try to put these words into practice, we participate in the growth of God’s Kingdom on earth, a growth which will be completed in our Heavenly life. But we need the special anointing of the Holy Spirit to be doers of the word of God, so let us offer our lives before God every day, asking for this special anointing.

    title: Reflection

    body: |
      In today’s Gospel, Jesus narrates two parables of the seed while speaking about the reign of God, i.e., the growing seed and the mustard seed. In the first parable, Jesus speaks about the entire process from the grain, scattered on the ground to the harvest. What this short parable conveys is the mysterious ways in which God guides the growth of the kingdom without any human knowledge or intervention. In the second parable, Jesus highlights the simple beginnings of the kingdom and the vast scale of the final outcome. Initially, the project of God might look like unimpressive, but it grows actively and vigorously. Thus, both parables invite us to cultivate patience and perseverance and surrender ourselves into the hands of God. Am I patient enough to witness God’s surprises?

    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Bathildis - Queen (-680)"
    ref: "Saint Bathildis - Queen (-680)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Saint Bathildis was a Catholic girl from England, who had been sold as a slave to the Majordomo of King Clovis II of Neustria and Burgundy. The King, impressed by her beauty and Christian virtues, freed her and married her in 649. She thus became the mother of Kings Clothaire, Childeric and Thierry.



      When Clovis died seven years later, Bathildis was proclaimed Queen Regent under the nominal reign of her five year-old son, Clothaire III. With the aid of certain holy Bishops she carried through such important reforms as the abolition of the trade in Christian slaves, simony among the clergy, and oppressive taxation.



      Numerous hospitals and monasteries owed their foundation to her generosity. Saint Bathildis concluded the last 15 years of her life as a humble nun, serving the poor. She breathed her last at Chelles on 30 January 680 after having endured a painful illness.
