  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mark 1:40–45"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today’s Gospel describes Jesus touching a man sick with a severe case of leprosy and healing him instantly. In this miracle we have all the essentials for any miracle, says, Rev. Dr L. Parker. We have a) a leper; b) a disease, leprosy; c) recognition of the disease by the man who has it; d) the presence of Jesus; e) Faith; f) trust, and g) humility enough for the sick man to ask for help from Jesus. Biblical “leprosy” rarely indicated Hansen’s disease (leprosy proper). Mostly, the term referred to skin diseases like ringworm, psoriasis, *leukoderma*, skin cancer, and *vitiligo. *The suffering of lepers in Biblical times was chiefly due to the way they were treated by the religious society of the day (*Interpreter’s Bible*). They were deemed unclean, unfit to be counted among a people who considered themselves “a kingdom of priests, a holy nation” (Ex 19:6). In addition, lepers were treated as sinners who were being punished by God with this contagious disease. The leprosy given by God as punishment to Miriam, the complaining sister of Moses (Nm 12: 1-3, 9-13) , to Gehazi, the greedy servant of the prophet Elisha (2 Kgs 5:22-27), and to the proud king Uzziah of Judah, also called Azariah (2 Kgs 15:3-5), supported the Jewish belief that leprosy was God’s punishment for sins. Finally, “leprosy” was considered a contagious disease, and, hence, its victims were separated from their families and society. The Mosaic Law, as given in Leviticus, demanded that the priest declare the leper unclean and that the leper a) keep his garments rent and his head bare, b) muffle his beard, c) cry out, “Unclean, unclean,” and d) dwell apart, making his abode outside the camp. As a general rule, when a Jewish leper was healed, he had to go to the local priest for confirmation that he was now clean and was permitted to mix with the general public. Here the healed leper started evangelizing everyone he met by sharing the Good News of God’s activity in his life and allowing the Holy Spirit to touch the lives of others.</span>



      Life Messages: 1) The strong Faith of the sick man prompted him to violate the Mosaic Law prohibiting him from joining a crowd and approaching Jesus. The sympathy and mercy of Jesus prompted Jesus to violate the Mosaic Law which forbade anyone to touch an untouchable leper. Thus, Jesus teaches the lesson that the essence of Christianity is to touch the untouchable, to love the unlovable, and to forgive the unforgivable. </span>



      *(Fr Tony: https://frtonyshomilies.com/)*

    title: Reflection
    ref: null

    body: |
      We see in the leper the predominant qualities with which one should approach God – courage, humility and faith. Being a leper, he was considered an outcast by society and thus had to keep a distance from others. When approaching Jesus, he took the courage to defy the rules about keeping away from people. The gesture of kneeling and imploring Jesus is an indication of his humility. The leper had deep faith *in Jesus’ ability to heal him*. Perhaps he had already seen it at work in other people who were healed. Seeing the courage, humility and faith expressed by the leper, Jesus had no second thoughts but granted his request. Do we express the same faith and confidence when we approach Jesus?

    audio: null
    body: |
      Ordained priest at twenty-five in Old Goa in 1676 by Monsignor Antonio Brandao, Archbishop of Goa, Joseph, born the third son to Christopher and Maria Vaz, went on to become one of the greatest missionaries produced by Asia. </span>



      Setting off for Kanara in 1681, Joseph’s ministry took him to Ceylon (now Sri Lanka) where, at Kandy, he proved his missionary acumen, facing bravely the vagaries of time and weather, and hardship of every kind including suspicion and imprisonment just so that the Lord’s Word is proclaimed and His love reigns supreme!</span>



      Long before inculturation became popular, Joseph had already shown how authentic Christian asceticism could be blended with Indian *sanyasism*. He learnt the local language, Sinhala, and compiled a English-Sinhala dictionary for the benefit of his companions! Such was his practice of the Faith that his whole life has come to be regarded as “an object lesson in missionary methods” (Father Cyril Papali) that drew, rather than repulsed, non-Christians to the Faith. His dealings with people of every rank and status earned him the distinction of being referred to as “the perfect model of an apostle” (Father Pierre Charles). </span>



      Joseph’s pioneering efforts in organizing the first ever indigenous missionary institute in the Third World, known as the Oratory of the Cross of Miracles of Old Goa, ensured the continuance of his work, saving the Church in Sri Lanka from virtual extinction. </span>



      His love for Our Lady was such that in a letter written shortly after his ordination, he states: “Let it be known to all who see this ‘Letter of Bondage’, angels, human beings and all creatures, that I, Father Joseph Vaz, sell and offer myself as a perpetual slave of the Virgin Mother of God.”</span>



      Dying as he had lived, serene and peaceful, a light to all around him, with a lighted candle in his hands and the sweet name of Jesus on his lips, Joseph was beatified in 1995 and canonized in 2015.</span>



      <span class="subhead">Reflection:</span>*“The one who illumines us… is our good JESUS, the saint of saints, the light of lights, himself eclipsed on the Cross. May he give you eyes of the body and much more of the soul. . . to desire to imitate him and follow his example and his doctrine in everything”*(excerpt from Father Joseph’s letter to his nephew, Deacon Joseph Vaz).</span>
