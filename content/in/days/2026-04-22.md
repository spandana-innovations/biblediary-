      Reflection: 1



      As we saw in yesterday’s Gospel Jesus is the Bread of life; he who comes to him shall never be hungry and he who believes in him shall never be thirsty. Those who believe in Jesus are those to whom the Father has given His special grace by which they are drawn to the Son, the saviour of the world. We should humbly submit to divine grace and God’s guidance to believe in the Eucharist. Jesus has promised to raise up such believers at the end of the world. The Jews, who were not led by divine grace, did not believe in Jesus but grumbled against him. But the Father wants no one to be lost. It is the will of the Father that everyone who looks at Jesus and believes in him should have eternal life; and Jesus will raise every such person on the last day. We should therefore never lose our hope, no matter what our failures are. W should believe in Jesus and trust in him who is our loving saviour. </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      In today’s Gospel passage, Jesus repeats his claim that he is “the Bread of Life.” He means that, just as God sent manna from heaven to sustain the physical life of his people in the desert, so He has sent His Son Jesus to sustain the spiritual lives of His people. Spiritual life is actually Sanctifying Grace, our living relationship with God the Father, through His Son Jesus and the Holy Spirit.</span></span></span>



      Jesus makes three claims: 1) He claims to be our spiritual Food and offers himself in order to produce God’s life within us. 2) He promises to those who believe in him unbroken friendship with God. 3) Jesus also promises to those who believe in him a share in his own Resurrection at the end of this world and share of Eternal Life with him in Heaven.</span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span> 1) We need to live dynamic spiritual lives, sharing in God’s Life, Sanctifying Grace, through the Holy Eucharist. 2) We can keep the friendship of Jesus only by leading holy lives free from sin. 3) We can enjoy and share the joy of Jesus’ Resurrection only by realizing and appreciating his presence within us and all around us. Only God can satisfy our deepest needs. </span></span></span>

      Jesus clearly states that His mission is to do the will of the Father, and He passes this mission onto his apostles, extending it to the whole Church. Before His ascension, He commissions the apostles to continue His work, and we see the fulfilment of this mission in the early Christian community. In the face of persecution, the scattered Christians remain focused on their calling – to proclaim the Word of God. Despite suffering, they carry out God’s will, just as Jesus did. Similarly, we are called to live according to the will of God, not our own desires. Like Jesus and the first Christians, we are sent into the world to witness to His love, by sharing His message of hope and salvation. Our mission is clear: to fulfil the will of the One who sent us.</span></span></span>

    audio: null
    body: |
      Saint Soter was a native of Fondi near Gaeta in Italy. It was upon the death of Saint Anicetus in 166, that Soter was raised to the Papacy thus becoming the 12th in the succession of Popes. One of the rising heretical sects which this Pope had to combat during his 9-year reign was that of Montanus, a Phrygian, who taught that certain sins could never be forgiven and claimed that he was God’s messenger for a new outpouring of the Holy Spirit. To Saint Soter is also attributed a precept that all the faithful, excepting only such as were guilty of a notable crime, should receive Holy Communion on Maundy Thursday.</span></span></span></span>



      A letter which the Pope sent to the persecuted Church at Corinth has unfortunately been lost, but an extant fragment of the reverent reply of Saint Dionysius, the Bishop of Corinth, is interesting for its reference to “the abundance which the Pope has sent to the saints, and the consolation which he has offered with words of comfort to the brethren who came to him, as a loving father would to his children.” “Today, the Lord’s day, we have read your letter and we will always wish to re-read it and to be admonished, the same as by the earlier letter which was sent to us by Clement.”</span></span></span></span>



      Saint Soter is believed to have suffered martyrdom in 175 under Marcus Aurelius.</span></span></span></span>
