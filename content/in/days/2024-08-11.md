  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "John 6:41-51"
    audio: null
    body: |
      The declaration “I am the Bread of Life” contains all the past promises of the provident Father to His children; it is the full flowering of the care and protection the Good Shepherd provided for His flock. It has roots in the heart of God, Father and Shepherd. In its light, the past history of Israel stands clarified. But its thrust is forward, towards the future. It is the bread of eternal life. What is this all-important revelation, this solemn proclamation? “I AM THE BREAD OF LIFE.” This is fulfilment, clarification, substance. In this fulfilment all past adumbrations vanish. In this clarification all past obscurations disappear. In this substance, all past shadows fade away. This ‘I AM’ is the Word of God. “In the beginning was the Word and the Word was with God, and the Word was God… and 19th Sunday of the Year (b) 320 the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, full of grace and truth” (Jn 1:1,14). This Word of God came and dwelt among us in Bethlehem (meaning ‘house of bread’). “One does not live by bread alone, but by every Word that proceeds from the mouth of God” (Mt 4:4). Jesus of Nazareth is the Word of God and the bread of God by which man lives. Jesus is the bread from heaven, the true bread from heaven, which the Father gives, the bread that gives life to the world. Jesus affirms: “I am the living bread which came down from heaven; if anyone eats of this bread, he will live forever” (today’s Gospel). Jesus, the bread of life, is God at His best for us as provident Father and Good Shepherd.



      *(New Horizon Homilies, Fr Joe ssp and Fr Philip ssp)*
  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      Prophet Elijah proves that the Lord God of Israel is the true God, and kills the four hundred prophets who challenged him. When Jezebel sought to kill him, he takes shelter under a broom tree. Being engulfed with a sense of guilt and shame he says, “take away my life … I am no better than my fathers.” But the Lord has a different plan for Elijah: “arise and eat, for the journey is too great for you.” In the Second Reading, Saint Paul instructs the church at Ephesus to let go of their bitterness, wrath, and anger, and invites them to walk in love. By walking in love, one will be able to offer oneself as a fragrant offering and sacrifice acceptable to God. The gospel reading runs on the theme of ‘life’ and ‘eternal life.’ The Jews of Jesus’ time take offence at Jesus on account of his humble origin. But Jesus instructs them to raise their minds high. How does learning take place in our lives? a) Through waiting; b) through our readiness to love; and c) by setting our minds on things that are above.
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "St Clare Abbess - Virgin (1193-1253)"
    ref: "St Clare Abbess - Virgin (1193-1253)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      When St Francis preached the Lenten sermons in the church of St George at Assisi (Italy) in 1212, he so inspired the 18-year-old Chiara (meaning “light” or “illustrious”) of the noble Favaronne family, that she determined to follow the Poverello in his life of Christian poverty. She secretly slipped out of her parental castle on the night of Palm Sunday (March 18-19), and, accompanied by her aunt Bianca and another companion, made her way through the forest to the little chapel of the Portiuncula in the valley, where she exchanged her rich clothing for a coarse tunic and veil, and, shorn of her long, golden hair, vowed her life to Christ’s service in utter poverty.

      Within a fortnight, Clare was joined by her younger sister (St) Agnes. The young Order of “Poor Clares” began to grow rapidly, and during the next few years her own mother, (Blessed) Ortolana, another sister, Beatrice, and her aunt Bianca also placed themselves under her direction. Foundations were established in many countries of Europe during her 40-years tenure of office, mainly for the care and education of poor girls.

      Cardinal Ugolini, the new Order’s protector, drew up a rule in 1219, based on the Benedictine one and forbidding communal poverty. Her firm insistence on St Francis’ ideal of absolute poverty and total dependence on alms, finally won from Pope Innocent IV, two days before her death, the solemn confirmation of the definitive rule.

      Clare’s funeral was attended by the Pope and his entire court, and two years later the holy foundress who was solemnly canonized in 1255.
