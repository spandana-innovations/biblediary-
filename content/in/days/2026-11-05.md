  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 15: 1-10"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today’s Gospel passage, from chapter 15 of Luke’s Gospel, is known as the “*Gospel in the Gospels,*” or the “*distilled essence of Christ’s Good News.”* In this chapter, using three parables, Jesus answers two accusations levelled against him by the Scribes and Pharisees, namely, that he is mingling with the sinners and sharing their meals. These parables teach us that our God is a loving, patient, merciful, and forgiving God. He is eager to be merciful toward us, not vengeful and punishing. He is always in search of His lost and straying children.



      The parables: Since the self-righteous Pharisees who accused Jesus of befriending publicans and sinners could not believe that God would be delighted at the conversion of sinners, Jesus told them the parable of the lost sheep and the shepherd’s joy on its discovery, the parable of the lost silver coin (a drachma, worth about a denarius, a farm worker’s “daily wage”), and the woman’s joy when she found it, and the parable of the lost son and his Father’s joy at His repentant son’s return Besides presenting a God Who is patiently waiting for the return of sinners, ready to pardon them, these parables teach us God’s infinite love and mercy. Christianity is not about man seeking God, but rather about a Holy God seeking a sinful man. In other words, in salvation, as in forgiveness, the initiative is always God’s. These three parables defend Jesus’ alliance with sinners and respond to the criticism leveled by certain Pharisees and scribes at Jesus’ frequent practice of eating with and welcoming tax collectors and sinners.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span> 1) We need to meet the challenge for self-evaluation and return to God’s mercy: If we have been in sin, God’s mercy is seeking us, searching for our souls with a love that is wild beyond all imagining. God is ready to receive and welcome us back as Jesus welcomed sinners in his time.



      2) Let us get reconciled with God, through the Sacrament of Reconciliation when we are in mortal sin, and in asking His forgiveness for our sins every night before we sleep. 3) We also need to ask God for the courage to extend this forgiveness to others who have offended us. As we continue with the celebration of the Holy Mass, let us pray as well for God’s Divine Mercy on those who have fallen away from grace.

    body: |
      Today’s Gospel reveals God’s love for each of us: “There will be more joy in heaven over one sinner who repents than over ninety-nine righteous persons who need no repentance.” Through the parables of the lost sheep and lost coin, Jesus shows God’s deep desire to bring back anyone who is lost, celebrating their return with joy. St Paul echoes this in Philippians, saying, “I count everything as loss because of the surpassing worth of knowing Christ Jesus, my Lord.” He sees all earthly gains – fame, honour, fortune – as insignificant compared to the gift of knowing Christ. He even calls them “refuse”. Today’s readings invite us to experience a true conversion, letting go of what the world values and embracing the lasting treasure of God’s love revealed through Christ Jesus. God seeks us, rejoices over our return, and calls us to reorder our lives in light of His unfailing love.

    body: |
      Bertilla was Born into one of the most illustrious families in the territory of Soissons (France) during the reign of Dagobert I, Bertilla grew up witnessing the many ills of the world at large, its deceptions and follies and earnestly sought to renounce it. Not daring to inform her parents about her decision she consulted St Ouen, the Bishop of Rouen, who encouraged her in her resolve and advised her to pray for perseverance.



      In time, when her parents were informed of her desire, God obviously inclined them not to thwart or oppose it. They sent her to Jouarre, a monastery near Meaux, founded not long before under the Rule of St Columban. Received there with great joy, she was trained up in the strictest practices of monastic perfection; by her humility and perfect submission to all, she seemed everyone’s friend in need and acquitted herself with such great charity and edification that she was soon chosen prioress to assist the abbess in her administration.



      The fame of this saint and her disciplinary acts in this community attracted a number of foreign vocations; it is here that the widowed Queen Bathildis retired, taking the habit from her hands and living a life of humility and submission, as also did another Queen, Hereswitha, widow of King Ethelhere of East Angels. But the holy abbess Bertilla seemed to all the most humble even among these humble Queens.



      Bertilla governed the community with equal vigour and discretion, until the close of her life.



      <span class="subhead">Reflection:</span> *“However great the work that God may achieve by an individual, he must not indulge in self-satisfaction. He ought rather to be all the more humble, seeing himself merely as a tool which God has made use of”* (St Vincent de Paul).
