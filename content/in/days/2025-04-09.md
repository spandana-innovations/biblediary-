      Reflection: 1



      Jesus was speaking to the Jews who believed in him, yet their faith was weak and shaky. He told </span></span></span></span>them that to be truly his disciples they need to abide in his word; then they would know the truth and the truth would set them free. They claimed that they were children of Abraham and that they were never enslaved to anyone, so why say “You will be free?” They were indeed offspring of Abraham, yet they did not believe the Father’s message that Jesus gave them. To be merely offspring of Abraham does not make anyone spiritual children of Abraham; for that one should live in faith as Abraham did, but they were actually doing the opposite. They even claimed that God was their Father, yet they did not love Jesus who came from God. Their minds and hearts were not open to Jesus; they rejected his divine origin. Jesus has the power to set us free from all that enslaves us. To practise our faith we should love and obey Jesus, or else our faith is of no avail. </span></span></span></span>



      *— Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP</span></span>*</span></span>



      Reflection: 2



      Today’s first reading, taken from the book of the prophet Daniel, tells us how King Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon had a 90 feet tall and 3 feet thick golden statue built, then commanded all his subjects to bow down in adoration before it as a test of loyalty. Many Jews did so, fearing persecution and death. But three young Jewish men, who were favourites of the king and were employed by the King in the royal court, refused to worship the statue because of their religious belief in one God, Yahweh. Hence, they were thrown into a fiery furnace to die. There, Yahweh their God protected them, as they were ready to sacrifice their lives rather than turn their backs on Him. It was their Faith, their loyal allegiance to God and their obedience to His will that saved them.



      In today’s Gospel, Jesus told the unbelieving Jews that it was such loyal Faith and obedience to his teachings that would make them his disciples, enjoying true freedom. Jesus explained to them that true freedom is freedom from sin, submitting their hearts and wills to God their Father speaking through His Son Jesus. The Jews argued that they had never been slaves to foreign gods, although they had been under Persian, Babylonian, Greek, and Roman rulers. They claimed they had always kept the Faith of Abraham their father. Jesus plainly told them that they were slaves of sin and the devil because they not only refused to accept him as the Son of God and obey his words but were also planning to kill him.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages: </span>



      1) Let us become true disciples of Christ by believing in Christ’s teachings, studying and mediating on his words in Holy Scripture, and obeying his commandment of love in our daily lives.



      2) Let us seek Jesus’ help and the guidance of the Holy Spirit that we may be freed from slavery to sins, evil habits, attachments, and addictions. 3) Let us grow in true Christian discipleship by freely submitting our heart, mind, and will to an all-merciful, all-loving, and all-wise God.

      Today’s gospel passage prompts us to consider the nature of true discipleship, the freedom found in Jesus’ truth, and the importance of our spiritual lineage based on faith and obedience. It also calls us to reflect on Jesus’ divine identity and to recognize Him as the source of ultimate freedom and truth. Nevertheless, it reveals a dramatic shift from belief to hostility among a group of Jews who initially believed in Jesus. Their response accentuates the challenges Jesus faced in his ministry, as well as the deep-seated resistance some people have to accept him and his divine claims. It serves as a cautionary tale, urging us to approach Jesus with open hearts and a willingness to accept the truth he offers.

    body: |
      Saint Waldetrudis belonged to an exceptional family of saints. Her parents were Saints Walbert and Bertilia; her sister, Saint Aldegundis, Foundress-Abbess of Maubeuge; her husband, Saint Vincent Madalgarius, and their four children Saints Landericus, Dentelinus, Madelberta and Aldetrudis. The last two named were both abbesses of Maubeuge.



      Waldetrudis and Madalgarius, a nobleman by birth, lived a happy Christian married life. Sometime after the birth of their last child, Madalgarius withdrew to the abbey of Haumont which he had founded, and there took the name of Vincent.



      Waldetrudis stayed on in the world two more years and then humbly withdrew to a life of poverty and simplicity at a place called “Castrilocus in Monte”, around which developed the abbey of Chateaulieu and the city of Mons (Belgium). Here she gave herself completely to works of mercy and was well known for her miracles of healing both before and after her death.
