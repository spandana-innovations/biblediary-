      Reflection: 1



      There is perfect oneness and harmony between the Father and Jesus. Jesus is divine and equal with the Father who shares with him His work, His authority and His power of judgement. They are united in their action and intention. They are united in love. The Father shares with the Son the power to give life.



      Whoever honours Jesus honours the Father who sent him. Jesus assures that whoever listens to his word and believes in the Father has eternal life. After the Death and burial of Jesus, even the dead will hear his voice and live. Those who have done good will come to the resurrection of life, and those who have done evil will come to judgement.



      Two things are central to today’s Gospel text:



      1) the divinity of Jesus; 2) faith in Jesus and the Father are necessary for salvation. At the same time it is a call to deepen our faith and recommit ourselves to live by faith.



      *— Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel passage gives Jesus’ explanation vindicating himself when he has been accused by the Jews of breaking the Sabbath by healing on that day, and of being a blasphemer, by claiming, as God’s Son, equality with God and the same authority and power as God.



      Jesus’ claims and justification: In general, Jesus claims that he is one with the Father in all he does as Mediator, and that there is a perfect understanding between him and his Father in the whole matter. But, at the same time, he is obedient, and so entirely devoted to his Father’s will that it is impossible for him to act separately from his Father in anything. Thus, Jesus claims that his identity with the Father is made visible in his complete obedience: Jesus always does what his Father wants him to do. Then Jesus proves his equality with the Father by doing some works that are the exclusively works of God Who is his Father. For example, it is God’s prerogative to raise the dead, and give life, and Jesus exercises these prerogatives. Jesus has received Divine power from the Father to exercise His judgment and authority over life and death. That is why Jesus’ words bring healing and life to those who believe in the One Who sent him, and condemnation to those who do not. At the last judgment, all who have heard Jesus’ voice and obeyed his word will be raised to eternal life.



      Life message: True Christian life is the surrender of our lives to God with the same love and obedience which Jesus demonstrated for his Father.

      Today’s gospel passage offers us profound theological insights into 1) the nature of Jesus’ divinity; 2) Jesus’ relationship with the Father; 3) his authority over life and judgment; 4) the role of faith in salvation; and 5) the importance of aligning our lives with God’s will. It challenges us to recognize and trust in Jesus as the source of eternal life and to seek conformity with God’s purposes in our lives. It also underscores the importance of faith and unity within the community of believers. The discourse presents Jesus’ claim to equality with God in various aspects, including shared work, sonship, authority, unity in purpose, the ability to grant eternal life, and divine sovereignty. It’s a foundational passage highlighting Jesus’ divinity and reinforcing the idea that he and the Father are one in purpose and mission. This concept of equality with God is central to Christian theology and underscores the divinity of Jesus Christ.

    ref: "Saint Francis of Paola - Confessor (1416-1507)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      The belief of Robert Martolella’s parents that his birth, after years of childlessness, was the result of the intercession of Saint Francis d’Assisi was further reinforced with the healing they obtained through his intercession for their young infant’s eye. The boy was 13 when they fulfilled their vow of having him spend a year in a Franciscan monastery, donning a Franciscan habit, in accordance with medieval practice, where he proved himself a model of extraordinary humility, abnegation and love of prayer.



      Returning from a pilgrimage to Rome, Assisi and other places, he took the name of Francis and began the austere, solitary life in a lonely cave on the sea-coast where, in 1435, he built a chapel and three cells when two companions joined him as anchorites, thus forming the nucleus of the Order of the Hermits of Saint Francis, later known as ‘Minims’, “the least in the House of God”. By 1454 there were monasteries established in southern Italy and Sicily, apart from a Second Order for nuns and a Third Order for those who would live out his charism in the world. Central to the severity of their life was charity, humility and poverty, abstinence covering all meat and animal products.



      Outspoken in reproving even the King of Naples for his evil ways, Francis’ fame reached France where the dying Louis XI sought him out before dying a penitent death. Resultantly, he spent 25 years in France as counsellor to Charles VIII and Louis XII, successive occupants of the throne, restoring peace between France and Spain.



      Francis died on Good Friday 1507, aged 91, leaving behind a legacy of 400 convents, the Order’s five provinces covering Italy, France, Germany and Spain, and was canonized in 1519.
