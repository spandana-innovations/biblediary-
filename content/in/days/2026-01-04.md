      Reflection: 1



      The wise men were respected religious leaders representing all non-Jews. While the Jewish people were hostile to Jesus, the wise men from abroad paid him great homage by worshipping him and offering him precious gifts. Gold signifies the kingship of Jesus, frankincense signifies his divinity and myrrh signifies his humanity. This shows that they fully recognised the identity of Jesus. God warned them not to be deceived by the hypocritical piety of Herod, and so they went back to their own country by another way. When we are guided by the word of God, we change our old ways and begin to lead a new life. Like the wise men, we offer to Jesus what is most precious to us. </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      The Greek Word </span></span>Epiphany (*&epsilon;&pi;&iota;&phi;ά&nu;&epsilon;&iota;&alpha;*), </span></span>means *appearance* or *manifestation*. First, the angels revealed Jesus to the shepherds. In the Western Church, the Feast of the Epiphany celebrates Jesus’ first manifestation to the Gentiles, represented by the Magi, while in the Eastern Church, the Feast commemorates the baptism of Christ, at which the Father and the Holy Spirit gave combined testimony to Jesus’ identity as Son of God. Later, in the synagogue at Nazareth, Jesus revealed Himself in words as the promised Messiah, and at Cana Jesus revealed His Divinity by transforming water into wine. These multiple revelations are all suggested by the Feast of the Epiphany.</span></span></span></span></span>



      Today’s Gospel teaches us how Christ enriches those who bring Him their hearts and offer their lives to Him. The adoration of the Magi fulfils the oracle of Isaiah (first reading), prophesying that the nations of the world would travel to the Holy City following a brilliant light, bringing gold and incense to contribute to the worship of God. Today’s Responsorial Psalm (Ps 72) verses 10-11 picture kings from foreign lands bringing gifts to pay homage to a just king in Israel. Paul’s letter to the Church of Ephesus (today’s second reading), expresses God’s secret plan in clear terms: *“the Gentiles are…co-heirs … co-partners in the promise, in Christ Jesus through the Gospel.”* Today’s Gospel reminds us that if God brought the Magi – foreigners and pagans – to recognize and give Jesus proper respect as the King of Jews, we should know that there is nothing in our sinful lives that would keep God from bringing us to Jesus. There were three groups of people who reacted to the Epiphany of Christ’s birth. The first group, headed by King Herod, tried to eliminate Jesus, the second group, priests and scribes, ignored Jesus, and the third group, represented by the shepherds and the Magi, came to adore Jesus.</span></span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life Messages:</span></span></span></span></span></span>



      (1) Let us make sure that we belong to the third group: a) by actively worshiping Jesus at Mass with the gold of our love, the myrrh of our humility and the frankincense of our adoration; b) by giving a new direction to our lives. Just as the Magi chose another route to return to their homes, let us choose a better way of life, abstaining from proud, unjust, and impure thoughts, words, and actions, evil habits, and selfish behaviour; c) by becoming stars leading others to Jesus, as the star led the Magi to Jesus. Let us remove the darkness of the evil around us by radiating the light of Jesus’ love through selfless service, unconditional forgiveness, and compassionate care. </span></span></span></span></span>



      (2) Like the Magi, let us offer Jesus our gifts on this feast of Epiphany and every day: (a) the Gift of our life by offering it on the altar during the Holy Mass and every morning as soon as we get up, asking for the strengthening anointing of the Holy Spirit to do good and avoid evil during the course of the day. b) the Gift of responsive relationship with God by talking to Him in personal and family prayers and listening to Him through reading the Holy Bible every day. c) the Gift of friendship with God by recognizing Jesus’ presence in, and offering loving, humble service to, everyone we encounter, and by getting reconciled to God every night, asking His pardon and forgiveness for our sins and failures during the day.</span></span></span></span></span>

      Today we celebrate the Epiphany of our Lord. The word “epiphany” in Greek means shining forth, showing, or manifestation. Hence, this feast highlights the revelation of the Messiah to the Gentiles and the inclusivity or universality of God’s salvation, which is not limited only to the Jews. Observing the star at its rising and later guided by it, some wise Magi discover Jesus, worship Him, and offer Him gifts of gold, frankincense and myrrh. They were not the chosen people of God but were outsiders or foreigners, yet they received the light of God. Today’s celebration invites us to shun our exclusivist attitudes and embrace diversity and differences, especially towards those who are different from us. The “gift” of Christmas is not meant only for people of one nation but for people of all nations! On this journey, let us allow ourselves to be guided by God’s Word, His creation and His people so that we become pilgrims of hope, bringing our giftedness only to be shared with others.</span></span>

    audio: null
    body: |
      Born of distinguished Protestant parents of New York City [28 August 1774], Elizabeth Ann Bayley Seton lost her mother when she was three and was brought up by her father with a sound education and impeccable moral training. Since her childhood, she nursed such a special concern for the poor and the sick and ministered to them so tenderly that she was called “Protestant Sister of Charity”. Her non-Episcopalian practices included wearing a crucifix, daily general examen of conscience, and reading *The Imitation of Christ* along with the Bible.</span></span></span></span>



      Her husband, William Magee Seton, a young wealthy merchant, died in the 9th year of marriage, leaving her a young widow with five children. During her first month as widow in the Catholic Filicchi household, Elizabeth came to know and appreciate the Catholic Faith, the family’s special devotion to the Blessed Virgin rubbing off well on her. Her reception into the Catholic Church [1805] resulted in her ostracization by her Protestant relatives and friends. </span></span></span></span>



      Proceeding to Canada, in response to a request from the Superior of the Baltimore Sulpicians, Father Dubourg, she founded a school for girls which her students solught to convert into a convent, whereupon the Bishop of Baltimore gave her a set of rules to be observed by them with her as Superior.</span></span></span></span>



      In Emmitsburg (Maryland), she started the Sisters of Charity [1809], under the rule of Saint Vincent de Paul. Laying the foundations for the American Parochial school system, she trained teachers, prepared textbooks and wrote many spiritual treatises. She helped the sick and the poor, led many to the Faith, and opened orphanages in Philadelphia and New York.</span></span></span></span>



      Following her death [1821], she was canonized in 1975 by Blessed Paul VI who officially designated the day as the Holy Year “Women’s Day” because “she was a woman who has passed all stages of womanhood: youth, maidenhood, widowhood and religious life.”</span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Reflection:</span></span></span>*“Her (Elizabeth’s) distinguishing characteristic was compassion and indulgence for poor sinners. Her charity made her watchful never to speak evil of others, always to find excuses or to keep silence” </span></span>*(Antonio Filicchi).</span></span></span></span>
