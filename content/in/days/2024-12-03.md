  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 10:21-24"
    audio: null
    body: |
      St Francis Xavier, apostle of India and Japan, was born on 7 April 1506 in a noble family at the castle of Xavier near Pamplona, Spain. His native language, like that of St Ignatius of Loyola, was Basque. As a boy he was ambitious and fond of sport, but was also generous by nature and capable of heroic love and endurance. After his schooling in Pamplona, at 19 he went to the University of Paris and took the degree of Master of Arts.



      Among his companions in Paris were Peter Faber and Ignatius of Loyola. Recognising that Peter and Francis had the qualities for the mission he had in view, Ignatius decided to gain them for God. Peter was easy to win over, but Francis took time to respond. Ignatius kept frequently whispering to Francis the words of the Lord, “What does it profit a man if he gains the whole world and suffers the loss of his own soul?” Eventually Francis accepted the call that came through Ignatius, and became one of the first members of the Society of Jesus. He made his vows with Ignatius and five others on 15 August 1534.



      God prepared him for his mission with visions of his future ministry. It was then that King John III of Portugal requested the Jesuits for missionaries to be sent to the East Indies. When Ignatius asked Francis to go to India as a missionary, he was overjoyed. After only one day of preparation, with the blessing of Pope Paul III, Francis left Rome on 15 March 1540 and reached Lisbon in June. For nine months he busied himself there catechising, hearing confessions and caring for prisoners. There he met Fr Rodriguez who was ill and cured him with a single embrace.



      On 7 April 1541 Francis received appointment as Pontifical Delegate for the East, and the same day he boarded ship for India. After a troublesome voyage of five months, the ship reached Mozambique and spent the winter there. On 16 March 1542 it set sail again and on 6 May, exactly thirteen months after he left Lisbon, Francis landed in Goa. He brought with him nothing but his consuming love for God and for the salvation of his fellow humans. Till September he was there learning the language, preaching, serving the sick and writing a text of catechism. From Goa he went to Kanyakumari, the southerly tip of India. There he worked for three years among the Paravas or pearl-fishers with remarkable success.



      St Francis Xavier laboured ceaselessly and tirelessly for ten years in evangelising India, Malaya and Japan, encountering innumerable difficulties and even the jealousy of his own associates. In ten years, he travelled across the major part of the Far East. This was an extraordinary achievement in his times. It is even more astounding that wherever he preached, he left behind a flourishing Church. He won thousands to the faith and baptised them. The fruits of his labour are seen even today.



      Francis was eager to bring the good news of Jesus to China. He appointed a vice provincial to look after the affairs of the Jesuits in India, and then organised an embassy to China. Francis and the ambassador were to set sail from Malacca, but the authorities there blocked the embassy, and Francis travelled in the ambassador’s ship to China. By the end of August 1552, the ship reached the island of Schang-chwan, not far from the Chinese coast.



      While trying for an entry into China, he fell ill on 21 November and on 3 December, invoking the Holy Trinity and the name of Jesus, and holding a candle, he died without the comfort of the sacraments. He was 46 years old.



      More than two months later his body was exhumed and found incorrupt and fresh. It was then brought to Goa where it is venerated in the basilica of Born Jesu. There, after more than four and a half centuries, his body still remains incorrupt. His right arm was severed and taken to Rome and is venerated there in the church of Jesus (Chiesa di Gesu).



      During the period of about eleven and a half years, Francis had travelled about eighty thousand kilometres in over fifty kingdoms without any of the conveniences we have today, overcame a thousand difficulties of language, culture and opposition, converted about thirty thousand persons to the faith, and opened new avenues for the Jesuit missions. Such was his zeal for the propagation of

      the faith.



      In 1662 Pope Gregory XV canonised Francis Xavier together with Ignatius of Loyola. Francis has been declared patron of the Orient, of the work of the Propagation of the Faith, and with St Therese of Lisieux, Patron of all missions.



      *[Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil SSP, Feast day Homilies, ST PAULS, Mumbai, 2016. Pages 409-12.]*

    body: |
      We have to see the Gospel passage in the context of the mission of the seventy. Hearing their wonderful experience, Jesus thanks the Father for having “hidden these things from the wise and understanding and revealed them to little children.” There is a double contrast: wise, understanding versus children; hidden versus revealed. What is being hidden from the wise and revealed to children, and why so? Compared to the wise and understanding, children are fresh and they are more open to receiving the Good News. Unlike the wise, whose convictions and belief system are already formed, those who are like children easily accept Jesus and His new teachings. They are open to receive salvation.

    body: |
      Born on 7 April 1506 to Don Juan de Jasu and Doña Maria de Azpilcueta y Aznárez, Francis Xavier was one of the six who, with Ignatius de Loyola, took their vows as the first Jesuits on 15 August 1534. Ordained priest on 24 June 1537, he celebrated his first mass after a rigorous 40-day fast and spiritual preparation. He was Ignatius’ secretary in Rome when he was sent by King John III of Portugal to the East Indies. At only a day’s notice, Francis set out with the Portuguese ambassador for Lisbon. The 11,000-mile sea voyage having wintered at Mozambique, he landed in Goa, the centre of Portuguese activity in the East, on 6 May 1542 and, learning the local language, nursed the sick, preached, and taught catechism, before proceeding to the Fishery Coast and baptizing the pearl fishers.

      In 1544 Francis proceeded to Ceylon, thence to Malacca and the Spice Islands and, some believe, even the Philippines. Despite language constraints and Buddhist opposition in Japan where he had landed on 15 August 1549, his preaching, accompanied by numerous miracles, was so effective that within some 40 years, the faithful in Japan numbered 400,000!

      Francis succumbed to fever on 3 December 1552 during a fruitless three month-long wait on the Sancian island of Canton for Chinese pirates who, for a tidy sum, were to smuggle him into China. In 1553, his body, found incorrupt and fresh after over two months of burial, is venerated till today in the Bom Jesus Basilica in Old Goa, even as his right arm, severed at the elbow at the instance of the then Jesuit General Claudio Acquaviva, lies enshrined in the Church of the Gesù in Rome.

      Beatified in 1619 and canonized in 1622, Francis has been named Apostle of the Indies and patron of foreign missions.
