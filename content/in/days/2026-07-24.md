  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Matthew 13:18–23"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today’s Gospel passage gives us Jesus’ interpretation of the parable of the sower, seeds sown, and the yield depending upon the type of soil. This parable was intended as a warning to the hearers to be attentive, and to the apostles to be hopeful, about Jesus’ preaching in the face of growing opposition to his teachings and ideas. The sower is God Who sows His word through the Church, parents, friends, and teachers. The seed sown is the high-yielding word of God which is *“a sharp sword” (Is 49:2),“two-edged sword” (Heb 4:12),* and *“fire and hammer” (Jer 23:29).*</span></span></span></span></span>



      Soil-type and yield: The hardened soil on the footpath represents people with minds closed because of laziness, pride, prejudice, or fear. So, “*the evil one comes and snatches away what is sown in his heart.”* The soil on flat rock pieces represents emotional types of people who go after novelties without sticking to anything and are unwilling to surrender their wills to God. *“I will remove the heart of stone from their flesh and give them a heart of flesh”* (Ez 11:19). Jesus interprets this to mean a man *“who hears the word and immediately receives it with joy; yet he has no root in himself, but endures for a while, and when tribulation or persecution arises on account of the word, immediately he falls away.” * The soil filled with weeds represents people addicted to evil habits and evil tendencies and those whose hearts are filled with hatred, jealousy, or the greed that makes them interested only in acquiring money by any means and in enjoying life in any way possible. Jesus tells us *“…this is he who hears the word, but the cares of the world and the delight in riches choke the word, and it proves unfruitful.”* The good and fertile soil represents well-intentioned people with open minds and clean hearts, earnest in hearing the word and zealous in putting it into practice. For Jesus, “….*this is he who hears the word and understands it; he indeed bears fruit, and yields, in one case a hundredfold, in another sixty, and in another thirty.”* Zacchaeus, the sinful woman, the thief on Jesus’ right side, St. Augustine, St. Francis of Assisi, and St. Francis Xavier, among others, fall into this category of the good soil.</span></span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life message:</span></span></span> Let us become the good soil and produce hundred-fold yields by earnestly hearing, faithfully assimilating and daily cultivating the word of God we have received, so that the Holy Spirit may produce His fruits in our lives. </span></span></span></span></span>

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      The explanation of the Parable of the Sower speaks of varying responses the seeds received. It is an invitation and a challenge to all the listeners and readers of the words of Jesus to respond appropriately to it. The appropriate response for a good disciple is to produce an abundant harvest. The word in itself has the capacity to grow and bear fruit. What counts is the disposition of heart that the hearer of the word possesses. The desired disposition calls for openness to hear the word, earnest hearing of the word, profound understanding, and then translating it into life. It involves clinging to the message of Jesus with single-minded devotion, regardless of the trials it brings. The hearers of the word must also root out every obstacle that hinders their becoming “good soil”. Jesus has already cautioned us, “The one who endures to the end will be saved” (Mt 10:22).</span></span></span>
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Francis Solano, Confessor (1549-1610)"
    ref: "Saint Francis Solano, Confessor (1549-1610)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Born to Matteo, then Mayor of the Andalusian town of Montilla, Spain and head of the Supreme Court, and his wife Anna, both of whom were known for their piety and virtue, Francis received his a Jesuit education before entering the Friars Minor at 20 and was ordained priest at 27. His boundless compassion for the sick and the sinner was amply demonstrated during an outbreak of the plague in Granada, Spain, when he gave himself over entirely to nursing the stricken people.</span></span></span></span>



      While on a mission to South America in 1589, he was shipwrecked south of Panama, before eventually reaching Lima, Peru. For the next twenty years, he successfully evangelized in Peru, Argentina, Paraguay and Uruguay at the cost of much personal sacrifice.</span></span></span></span>



      Francis’ zeal for making Christ known and loved by all expressed itself in a life of faith, piety, prayer and care, apart from speedily mastering the numerous difficult dialects of South America, winning the people’s interest and goodwill by playing for them on his violin and snapping his fingers to imitate the Castanets. His ministrations to the ailing were adequately backed by knowledgeable medical lore. Unfortunately, the numerous writings and musical compositions (carols, songs and dances in honour of the Christ Child and the Blessed Virgin) of this “Wonderworker of the New World”, which were sent to Rome in support of the process of his beatification, have since been lost.</span></span></span></span>



      Beatified in 1675, Francis was canonized in 1726. </span></span></span></span>
