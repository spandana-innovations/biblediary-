    ref: null

    body: |
      Youssef Antoun Makhlūf was born in the mountain village of Bekaa Kafra in Lebanon, the youngest of five children. Orphaned of his father at three, he grew up tending the family's small herd and praying in a grotto he had made into a chapel.

      At twenty-three he left home without telling his mother and entered the Lebanese Maronite Order, taking the name Sharbel after a second-century martyr of Antioch. He was ordained in 1859 and lived sixteen years in community at the monastery of Saint Maron at Annaya.

      In 1875 he was permitted to withdraw to the nearby hermitage of Saints Peter and Paul, where he spent the last twenty-three years of his life in silence, manual work and adoration of the Blessed Sacrament. He ate once a day, slept on a straw mat with a wooden log for a pillow, and refused any comfort.

      He was struck down while celebrating Mass on 16 December 1898, holding the chalice, and died on Christmas Eve. He was canonised by Pope Paul VI in 1977. His shrine at Annaya draws Christians and Muslims alike, and he is invoked especially by the sick.
