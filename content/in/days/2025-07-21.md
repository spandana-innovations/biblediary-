      Reflection: 1



      A faith which demands proof is no faith at all. Faith is a free decision made by persons who see God’s work and here His word with openness of spirit. The Pharisees ask for a sign only to test and trap Jesus. They have already seen the sign of Jonah; no more sign will be given to them. The sinful and pagan people of Nineveh listened to Jonah’s preaching, repented, did penance and changed their lives. So also the queen of the South came to listen to Solomon’s wisdom with genuine intention. Jesus is so much greater than Jonah and Solomon; people ought to listen to him and be deeply converted in her and soul. No matter how sinful they are, all are invited to listen to Jesus sincerely and to change the quality of their lives for the better.



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Something Greater. Not too long ago, I went to the hospital to give a blessing to a wonderful couple who had suffered the loss of their newborn baby. Their tragic loss did not come as a surprise to them. During a routine exam, doctors noticed the child was motionless and that there was no heartbeat. Regardless of all the advanced warnings, when the child was delivered the couple was devastated by their sudden loss.



      By the time I arrived at the hospital, the mother had the child in her arms. I shook hands with her husband and reached over and gave her a big hug. I opened up my prayer book and read from the book of Lamentations:



      "My soul is deprived of peace,



      I have forgotten what happiness is;



      I tell myself my future is lost,



      all that I hoped for from the Lord...



      Remembering it over and over



      leaves my soul downcast within me.



      But I will call this to mind,



      as my reason to have hope:



      The favors of the LORD are not exhausted,



      his mercies are not spent..."



      At the end of the ceremony, we prayed:



      May God give us peace in our sorrow,



      consolation in our grief,



      and strength to accept his will in all things.



      Amen.



      After the ceremony, the couple spoke openly to me of their heartache and pain when first told of their child's condition, and of their determination to do what would be pleasing to the Lord. Throughout their ordeal, the couple remained firm in the faith, accepting and doing only God's will.



      I found peace and strength in their incredible testimony of faith, hope and love.



      Before I left, the husband wanted to take a picture. He set his camera up, placed himself on the right side of his wife and child and pressed the button on a remote control that was in his hand. The camera's flash went off and a couple of pictures were taken.



      As I approached my car, I reflected on what I had seen and heard. I was astonished at something, but I couldn't put my finger on it. Finally, it dawned on me what it was: Never had I seen so much peace and joy in such a painful situation. What was the tipping point? Their smiles - my smile - our smiles - as the pictures were being taken!



      It was uplifting.



      They knew their child was a saint in heaven.



      O, what faith can do to the heart, soul and mind.



      "There is something greater here..." Yes. Someone and something greater than we could ever have imagined, but that only faith could ever have revealed.



      *(Fr Alfonse - fralfonse.blogspot.com)*

      People sought a sign from Jesus to establish his credentials as a preacher and miracle worker. The Pharisees asked for a sign not for the sake of believing in him but to test and trap him. Jesus comments on their double standards, pointing out that only an evil and adulterous generation asks for signs. They are not truthful to themselves. Jesus points out how people had genuinely repented at the preaching of Jonah and listened to Solomon’s wisdom. If they could repent and change their lives, those asking for signs from Jesus also ought to change at the preaching of Jesus because he is greater than Jonah and Solomon. Today’s gospel, therefore, is an invitation to all of us to listen to Jesus our Saviour and change our lives.

      Giulio Cesare de Rossi showed clear evidence of a religious vocation and his future oratorical prowess at an early age. At 16 he joined the Capuchins as Brother Lawrence and, while still a Deacon, preached the Lenten sermons with such fervour, his style carefully adapted to the spiritual needs of his listeners, his obvious sanctity capturing their hearts that he found himself subsequently called to preach in most of the large cities in Italy. A competent linguist, it was most specifically because of his excellent knowledge of Hebrew that Pope Clement VIII called him to Rome to instruct the Jewish populace, among whom he made many converts.



      Elected Superior General of the Capuchins in 1602, he declined re-election three years later. At the behest of Emperor Rudolf, Lawrence obtained from the German princes much needed help to oppose the formidable Turks. Animated by a supreme confidence that fired the enthusiasm of his contingent of 18,000 soldiers, he rode into battle heading it with a Crucifix raised high in his hand, putting the Turkish army of 80,000 at Stuhlweissenburg to flight!



      Lawrence’s achievements in the Post-Tridentine restoration of Catholicism earned him, in the words of Pope Benedict XV, “a truly distinguished place among the most outstanding men ever raised by Divine Providence to assist the Church in time of distress.”



      Lawrence died exactly 60 years after his birth when on a mission to Philip III of Spain on behalf of the people of Naples. He was beatified in 1783, canonized in 1881 and declared Doctor of the Universal Church in 1959.
