  - key: homily

    ref: "Mark 7:31-37"

    body: |
      God is not only the protector but also the helper of anyone in trouble. What was only promised and vaguely fulfilled in the past became a reality in Jesus. As man is prone to exploit the poor and the weak, God is bent on succouring them and championing their cause. In Jesus, God reveals and acts according to His true nature. After completing creation, God reviewed all His works and found them good. The people, seeing the actions and miracles of Jesus acclaimed: “He has done all things well; He even makes the deaf hear and dumb speak.” But in spite of God’s marvellously beautiful creation and in spite of Jesus doing everything well, oppression and suffering, if anything, are on the increase. There is a crying need for loving people and dedicated organizations that alleviate suffering, fight against injustice and champion the cause of the oppressed. . It is in this way that we complete in our own persons what is still to be accomplished in the passion of Christ for the salvation and liberation of the world. Nothing beautiful can be achieved without self-sacrifice. The Kingdom of God, the flowering of creation, grows and flourishes in the soil of selfsacrifice.

      *(New Horizon Homilies, Fr Joe Narivelil ssp and Fr Philip John ssp)*

      Healing power is a gift that is bestowed by God for the physical wellbeing of people. In the Readings of today, this power of healing is clearly highlighted both by Isaiah in the first reading and in the responsorial Psalm 146. The Gospel passage is a clear demonstration of this healing power shown by the action of Jesus. Jesus heals a deaf and dumb person. He seems to do it in private and uses also some physical things like touch and spittle. What is more significant in these Readings is that God is mindful of our ailments, pain, and suffering and He desires the welfare of everyone. Let us turn to the Lord in our troubles and the Lord will heal us of all our infirmities, for “He even makes the deaf hear and the mute speak” (Mk 7:37). Let us pray to Mary for her intercession, whose birthday is celebrated on this date.

      The Nativity of Mary








       The Church regards the day of a saint’s death a “birthday”, and rightly so! For it was on that day that the elect ended his/her life on earth to be born anew to the real life in heaven. There is, however, an exception made whereby the Church celebrates the natural birth of three persons, viz., Jesus, Mary and John the Baptist.









      The birth of a child is always a joyful event in the family. Indeed, as Rabindranath Tagore says, “Every child comes into the world with the assurance that God is not yet fed up with the human race.” Today we celebrate the birth a woman who came as “the dawn of hope and salvation to the whole world”.



      “Having been destined to become the Mother of the Eternal Word, this Child was enriched with so great a grace that even at the moment of her Immaculate Conception she exceeded all the Saints and Angels in sanctity, for she was given a higher order of grace, which corresponded to her dignity of Mother of God,” exults St Alphonsus de Liguori.



      Mary, the new Eve, is the Mother of the Church and a mother to each one of us. She is the delight of the Father, the joy of the Son and the faithful spouse of the Holy Spirit who would descend on her to form Christ.



      Today’s feast, with its origin in the Orient, first celebrated in Jerusalem in the fifth century, and introduced in Rome in the eighth, helped fix the date of the “Immaculate Conception” at 8 December.



      <span class="subhead">Reflection:</span> *“Today the Virgin is born, tended and formed, and prepared for her role as Mother of God who is the universal King of the ages”* (St Andrew of Crete).



      Blessed Jacques Laval- *Priest (1803-1864)*








       A medical practitioner by profession, Blessed Jacques Laval was so filled with love for his fellow human beings that he abandoned his lucrative practice as healer of the human body to serve God in his people as healer of souls.









      On becoming a priest, Jacques did a stint as pastor of a country parish until he came in contact with the “Congregation of the Holy Ghost” founded by Venerable Francis Libermann. Having joined that congregation, Jacques was sent to Mauritius to care for the African slaves who were liberated but still lost to themselves. His healing prowess came into play as he identified himself with these abandoned people, thus transforming them into fervent Christians.



      <span class="subhead">Reflection:</span> *“The heart that is full of God, full of love for the Lord is an upturned vase that spreads around its contents. You give Jesus Christ to persons who surround you because your heart is full. You want to be saved, and you want to save others, you want to give glory to God, and you want that the whole people give glory to God”*(Blessed James Alberione).
