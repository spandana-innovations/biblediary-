    ref: "John 6:16–21"

    body: |
      The event presented by today’s Gospel is the scene immediately following Jesus’ miraculous feeding of the five thousand with five small loaves of bread and two fish. Sensing the danger of having the people seize him and make him leader of a revolt, Jesus promptly instructed the apostles to leave the place by boat and, after dispersing the crowd, went by himself to the mountain to pray.</span></span></span>



      A double miracle in the sea: When the apostles in the boat were three to four miles away from the shore, they faced an unexpected storm, caused by the hot wind of the desert rushing into the Sea of Galilee through the gaps in the Golan Heights. Recognizing the danger, Jesus went to the boat, walking on the stormy sea. Jesus calmed the frightened disciples as he approached the boat, and as soon as he got into the boat it “reached land they were heading for.”</span></span></span>






      <span class="subhead">*Life messages:*</span> </span></span></span>



      1) We need to approach Jesus with strong Faith in his ability and availability to calm the storms in our lives and in the life of the Church. Church history shows us how Jesus saved his Church from the storms of persecution in the first three centuries, from the storms of heresies in the 5th and 6th centuries, from the storms of moral degradation and the Protestant reformation movement in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, and the storms of ongoing Clerical sex abuse scandals, particularly those beginning in the twentieth century. </span></span></span>



      2) We need to ask Jesus to protect us when we face storms of strong temptations, storms of doubts about our religious beliefs, and storms of fear, anxiety, and worries in our personal lives. </span></span></span>



      3) Experiencing Jesus’ presence in our lives, we need to confess our Faith in him and call out for his help and protection.</span></span></span>

      Saint Martin I</span></span> - *Pope Confessor (-655)*</span></span></span></span>



      The last of the Popes to have won a martyr’s crown, Saint Martin was born at Todi, Umbria, Italy. Known for his great learning and piety, he succeeded Theodore I to the Papal throne on 21 July 649. </span></span></span></span>



      One of the first acts in Martin’s 6-year reign was to call the Lateran Council in 649, in Rome, at which the 105 Bishops assembled condemned the Monothelite heresy that denied that Christ had a human will.</span></span></span></span>



      The Council’s decree infuriated Emperor Constans II, a staunch adherent of Monothelitism, who wanted to see its tenets widely propagated. An exarch whom he dispatched to Rome failed in his attempt on the Pope’s life, but a second emissary succeeded in breaking into the Lateran palace and secretly carrying off the ailing Pope to Constantinople. Here Martin languished in prison for several months until he was finally condemned unheard during a mock trial as a heretic and a rebel. He succumbed in exile to ill-treatment and starvation on 16 September 655, having been sent to the famine-ridden island of Cherson. </span></span></span></span>



      Incidentally, the Pope chosen on 10 August 654 to succeed Martin before his death was Saint Eugene I who carried on with the same courageous spirit as Saint Martin’s!</span></span></span></span>
