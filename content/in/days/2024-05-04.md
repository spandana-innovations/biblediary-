  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "John 15:18–21"
    audio: null
    body: |
      “If you find that the world hates you, know that it has hated me before you.” Jesus spoke a deeper truth here than we realize. Not only worldly persons, but the world’s “things” hate us, too. The soothing “creature comforts” of the world hate us. They refuse to bring us happiness or peace. We like the easy life: eating, sleeping, entertainment, pleasure.



      We know well enough that the easy life has never produced a saint. But what hurts is that the easy life has never even brought happiness! So, we have the choice of being either saturated with unhappiness and unrest, or becoming “strong for battle”, as the psalmist says, prepared

      for spiritual warfare. Sooner or later, and more than once, God will test us to see what we are worth.



      Are we actually victims of the ancient pagan error that earthly goods in abundance are the sure sign of God’s blessing on a nation and its people? Has our understanding advanced so little in three thousand years?



      Or isn’t it true that the saints have always been and always will be the people most blessed by God? Now, have any saints been raised up by the Church for veneration because they boasted of the greatest material comforts?



      “No rest on earth! There’s peace enough in heaven!” has been the battle cry of the saints. They have made Jesus the pattern of their life: “If you find that the world hates you, know that it has hated me before you. No slave is greater than his master.”



      What is the pattern of Jesus? “Our God has reigned from the Cross,” the Church tells us in her liturgy. But nowhere are we told that this glorious throne of Jesus was the latest in design for comfort and convenience.



      Yes, God chose us fools, confusing the wise,

      The strong ones of earth are weak in His eyes,

      That flesh may not glory before Him!

      The lowly He lifts, whom great ones despised,

      And those who are small the Lord has so prized

      That pride will be keen to adore Him! (1 Cor 1:2729).



      (Bernard C. Mischke OSC and Fritz Mischke OSC, Pray Today’s Gospel, ST PAULS, Mumbai, India. Page: 118-119)

      Jesus warns his disciples of the possible opposition from the world. Here the world stands for the system of the organized society hostile to God, which is controlled by the forces of darkness. Since the disciples have been chosen by Jesus and now belong to him, they live by the values of love, forgiveness, humility and service and do not adhere to the values of the world such as power, revenge, control and domination. They do not swim with the current, but against it. The disciples dare to be different. This automatically makes them objects of hatred and suspicion from the world. “If we seek to deepen our relationship with the Father, then we should not be surprised to find that we are misunderstood, opposed, or even persecuted for our beliefs,” reminds Pope Saint John Paul II.

      St Florian Martyr (- 304)

      An officer of the Roman army, St Florian occupied a high administrative post in Noricum, now part of Austria. In the reign of Emperor Diocletian he bore witness to Christ by suffering death for his faith. According to his own “acts” it is stated that he gave himself up at Lorch to the soldiers of Aquilinus, the Governor, as they were rounding up all who professed the Christian faith. He was asked to renounce his faith, but he wouldn’t budge and twice was scourged, half flayed alive and finally was thrown into the river Enns with a stone around his neck. His body was recovered by a pious woman, who gave him a Christian burial, but was eventually removed to the Augustinian Abbey of St Florian, near Linz. It is said that Pope Lucius III, in 1138, offered some of the Saint’s relics to King Casimir of Poland and to the Bishop of Cracow. Since then, Florian has been regarded as the Patron Saint of Poland, Linz and upper Asia. There existed a great devotion to St Florian in many parts of central Europe. Many miracles of healing are attributed to his intercession, and he is invoked as a powerful protector in danger from fire or water.
