      Reflection: 1



      The word “Jews” represents all those who are hostile to Jesus. Now Jesus enters where the disciples are hiding for fear of the Jews and leads them to peace, light and joy of the Risen Jesus and of the Holy Spirit. The transforming presence of the risen Jesus makes the eschatological gift a reality. He communicated His Spirit to his disciples as God breathed life into Adam (Gen 2:7). Then he commissions the Apostles to continue this act of creation by bestowing on others the life-giving power of the Spirit and forgiving ministry of the Lamb of God (Jn 1:29). The exclamation of Thomas is the sum total of faith; he openly confesses Jesus as Lord and God. His proclamation came from his experience of seeing the wounds on the risen body of the Risen Lord. Every one of us is called to make the confession that Jesus is our Lord and God. And because of Thomas, we have all received a special blessing from Jesus, “Blessed are those who have not seen and yet believed.”



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      The readings for this Sunday are about God’s Divine Mercy, the necessity for trusting Faith, and our need for God’s forgiveness of sins. The opening prayer addresses the Father as “God of Mercy.” The Response for today’s Responsorial Psalm (Ps 118), is *“Give thanks to the Lord for He is good; His Love*[mercy]*is everlasting!”* In the first section of that Psalm, we repeat three times, *“His mercy endures forever*.” God revealed His mercy to the world, first and foremost, by sending His only begotten Son to become our Savior and Lord by His suffering, death, and Resurrection. Divine Mercy is offered to us in the Holy Mass, the Sacrament of Reconciliation and the celebration of each of the Sacraments.



      The first reading, taken from the Acts of the Apostles, stresses the corporal acts of mercy practiced by the early Christian community before the Jews and the Romans started their persecutions. Practicing the sharing love, compassion, and the mercy of God which Jesus taught, this witnessing community derived its strength from community prayer, “the Breaking of the Bread,” and the apostles’ teaching, read at the worship service. The second reading, taken from John’s first Letter, deals with practicing both corporal and spiritual works of mercy by obeying God’s Old Testament Ten Commandments and focusing on Jesus’ commandment of loving others as He loves us with selfless, sacrificial, agape love. Loving others as Jesus loves us also demands that we treat others with God’s mercy and compassion. In today’s Gospel, as we recall Jesus’ appearance to the Apostles on that first Easter evening, we are vividly reminded of the Sacrament of Reconciliation – the power to forgive sins which Our Lord gave to His Apostles — *“Whose sins you forgive are forgiven them, and whose sins you retain are retained”(Jn 20:23).*[Bishop Robert E. Barron: “Our magnificent Gospel today declares that there is no greater manifestation of the Divine mercy than the forgiveness of sins. The risen Lord appears to his disciples and greet them with “*Shalom*,” peace. And then the extraordinary commission: *“Receive the Holy Spirit. Whose sins you forgive are forgiven them, and whose sins you retain are retained.”* Jesus’ mercy is communicated to his disciples, who in turn are sent to communicate it to the world. This is the foundation for the Sacrament of Penance, which has existed in the Church from that moment to the present day as the privileged vehicle of the Divine mercy.”] Today’s Gospel also emphasizes the importance of Faith in the all-pervading Presence of the Risen Lord of Mercy. To believe without having seen is every later Christian’s experience. We are invited to receive liberation from doubts and hesitation by surrendering our lives to the Risen Lord of Mercy. Let us ask God our Father to open our hearts so that we may receive His Mercy in the form of the Holy Spirit. [The Divine Mercy message is one we can call to mind simply by remembering ABC: A – Ask for His Mercy. God wants us to approach Him in prayer constantly, repenting of our sins and asking Him to pour His mercy out upon us and upon the whole world. B – Be merciful. God wants us to receive His mercy and let it flow through us to others. He wants us to extend love and forgiveness to others just as He does to us. C – Completely trust in Jesus. God wants us to know that our reception of the graces of His mercy are dependent upon our trust. The more we trust in Jesus, the more we will be able to receive.

      The readings of today beautifully connect with Divine Mercy Sunday, celebrating God’s boundless mercy revealed through Jesus. In John’s Gospel, Jesus appears to his disciples, offering them peace despite their fear and doubt. When He shows His wounds to Thomas, as He appears to them a second time, it’s a powerful reminder of His love and mercy, especially for those who struggle to believe. Jesus’ words, “Blessed are those who have not seen and yet believe,” invite us to trust in His merciful presence even when we cannot see it. In Acts, the early Christians lived out this mercy by caring deeply for one another, sharing their possessions so that “there was no needy person among them”. Their unity and selflessness show how mercy transforms communities. Divine Mercy Sunday calls us to receive God’s forgiveness and extend it to others. As we experience God’s mercy, we’re called to be like the early Christians, building a world of sharing, compassion, peace, and unity through our faith and acts of love.

    ref: "Saint Gemma Galgani - Virgin (1878-1903)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Gemma was the fourth of 8 children whose parents Enrico and Aurelia, were extremely devout. Aurelia died in 1886, having planted in Gemma the seeds of her devotion to Our Lord’s Passion. Unable to express her emotions about the moment of intimacy between her soul and Jesus at her First Holy Communion at 9, Gemma did make good her resolve to be “always good.”



      Gemma loved solitude and quiet, and was always most reserved in talking. One day, after she had received Holy Communion, Our Lord said to her: “Gemma, I expect you on Calvary, that Mount towards which you have been led.” After a long, painful illness patiently borne, she made a vow of chastity and in 1899, Saint Gabriel appeared to her, promising to make the novena in honour of Saint Margaret Mary with her, which he did each night. After a good confession and receiving Holy Communion, she arose completely cured. Her attempt at nun-hood was thwarted by the condition of her health.



      Gemma received the sacred “stigmata” on the vigil of the Feast of the Sacred Heart in 1899 apart from experiencing other phenomena of the Passion, such as the sweat of blood, the scourging, the crowning with thorns, etc. Under the spiritual direction of the Passionist Fathers, Gemma, who was also blessed with the gift of prophecy apart from ecstacies, visions and revelations, came to be widely known for her sanctity and many favours were obtained through her prayers.



      Gemma died in 1903, having offered herself as a sacrifice for sinners, particularly for unworthy priests. She was canonized in 1940.
