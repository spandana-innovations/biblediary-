  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mk 12: 28-34"
    audio: null
    body: |
      A scribe who believed in both the written Law and the oral tradition was pleased to see how Jesus had defeated the Sadducee who had tried to humiliate him with the hypothetical case of a woman who had married and been widowed by seven husbands in succession. Out of admiration, the scribe challenged Jesus to summarize the most important of the Mosaic Laws in one sentence. In the Judaism of Jesus’ day, there was a double tendency: to expand the Mosaic Law into hundreds of rules and regulations and to condense the 613 precepts of the Torah into a single sentence or few sentences.</span></span></span>



      Jesus’ novel contribution: Jesus gave a straightforward answer, quoting directly from the Law itself and startling all with his profound simplicity and mastery of the Law of God and its purpose. He combined the first sentence of the Jewish *Shema* prayer from Deuteronomy 6:5: … *Therefore, you shall love the Lord, your God, with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your strength”* with its complementary law from Leviticus 19:18: *“You shall love your neighbour as yourself.” *Thus, Jesus proclaims that true religion is to love God both directly and as living in our neighbour. Jesus underlines the principle that we are to love our neighbours as we love ourselves because both of us bear God’s image. For, to honour God’s image is to honour both Him Who made it and Him Whom it resembles. Besides, our neighbours, too, are the children of God our Father, redeemed by the Blood of Jesus. Love for our neighbour is a matter, not of feelings, but of deeds by which we share with others the unmerited love that God lavishes on us. This is the *agape* love for neighbours that God commands in His Law. Jesus then uses the parable of the Good Samaritan, as reported in Luke’s Gospel, to show them what God means by “neighbour.”</span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life Messages:</span> 1) We need to love God whole-heartedly: Loving God with all our heart, soul, mind, and strength, means that we should place God’s will ahead of our own, seek the Lord’s will in all things, and make it paramount in our lives. It also means that we must find time to adore Him, to present our needs before Him, and to ask His pardon and forgiveness for our sins. 2) God’s will be that we should love everyone, seeing Him in our neighbour. This means we have to help, support, encourage, forgive, and pray for everyone without regard to colour, race, gender, age wealth, social status, intelligence, education, or charm. </span></span></span>

    body: |
      Jesus presents the most important commandment: love God and love your neighbour. In the OT, God demanded complete love and loyalty from the People of Israel, and let’s recall the question of Jesus to Peter, “Do you love me more than these?” (Jn 21:15). The love for God is rooted in first experiencing His love for us (cf. 1 Jn 4:10). Therefore, first, we experience the love of God through our actual life experiences, then we respond to it, although inadequately, especially through our love for our neighbour. The philosopher Martin Buber offers great insight, saying, “You love yourself despite your faults, and so, love your neighbour despite theirs.” The question we need to ask ourselves is, where do I stand in my love for God and for my neighbour?</span></span></span>

    saintName: "Saint Francis Caracciolo (Confessor) (1563 -1608)"
    ref: "Saint Francis Caracciolo (Confessor) (1563 -1608)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Baptized Ascanio at his birth on 13 October 1563 in the Abruzzi, Italy, this scion of a noble family in Naples, fell victim in his youth to a terrible skin disease akin to leprosy. However, at 22, when he dedicated his life exclusively to God’s service, he found himself suddenly cured, went on to study theology at Naples, and was ordained to the priesthood in 1587.



      In 1589, together with St John Augustine Adorno, he founded the Minor Clerks Regular, a religious Congregation (both contemplative and active) which prohibited its members from aspiring to ecclesiastical dignities, called for perpetual adoration of the Blessed Sacrament in rotation and for continuous mortification in addition to the three usual vows. At his profession, Ascanio took the name of Francis. He became the first superior of the Naples congregation, and towards the turn of the century made three trips to Spain to found houses in Madrid, Valladolid and in the famous university city of Alcala, where his Clerks were to teach science.</span></span></span></span>



      Francis Caracciolo was an indirect descendant of St Thomas Aquinas on his mother’s side, and like his illustrious ancestor, was known for his angelic purity. A friend of the poor, he practised great austerities, always travelled on foot, and begged for crusts for his sustenance. God granted him the gifts of prophecy and of reading hearts. As an author he is famed particularly for his “Seven Stations of the Passion of Our Lord Jesus Christ”.</span></span></span></span>



      Francis died on 4 June 1608 and was canonized by Pope Pius VII on 24 May 1807.</span></span></span></span>
