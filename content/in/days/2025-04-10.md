    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      God grants eternal life to those who believe in Jesus and keep his word. Jesus emphatically states his divinity, “Before Abraham was, I am,” and promises life beyond death to those who obey him. Being divine, Jesus existed before Abraham, from all eternity. The Jews with whom Jesus was speaking were prejudiced against him and were not open to his teaching. In their hostility they wanted to stone him, but he went out of the temple. The right relationship with God necessarily includes faith in Jesus, love for him and obedience to his teaching. Without these one cannot participate in God’s eternal life and glory. Jesus in infinitely greater that Abraham, the father of all the faithful, because Jesus has not only come from the Father, but is one with Him.



      *— Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      In today’s Gospel, Jesus surprises and infuriates the Jews by the blinding lightning and deafening thunder of his Divine claims. First, Jesus claimed that one who kept his words would not die because his words were God’s words. The Jews responded, arguing that even a great man of Faith, like Abraham who kept God’s words, had died. Next Jesus claimed that he had unique knowledge of God because he came from God. The Jews believed that God had revealed everything about Himself through the Torah. Third, Jesus claimed a unique obedience to God, his Father, because he thought, spoke and acted as God wished. The Jews believed that their fathers and prophets had obeyed God perfectly. Fourth, Jesus claimed that he was not limited by time and, hence, that he was there even before Abraham and that Abraham had seen Jesus. The Jews believed that Abraham had been given a vision of the entire history of Israel including the vision of the Messiah. At this last claim, the Jews attempted to stone Jesus for blasphemy. But Jesus escaped.



      <span class="subhead">Life message: </span>



      1) We need to put our trust in Jesus because of His claims of Divinity. If we believe that we will receive eternal life by accepting Jesus as our Lord and Savior, we need to live out our belief by obeying him. As God, Jesus is present in all areas of our lives, so we need to talk to him about everything and listen to him always.

    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Fulbert - Bishop, Confessor (c 960-1029"
    ref: "Saint Fulbert - Bishop, Confessor (c 960-1029"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Born in the Picardy region of France, Saint Fulbert studied at the Benedictine abbey at Reims where he had as his instructor the famous Gerbert of Aurillac, who in 999 was to become Pope Sylvester II. Fulbert was the founder of the cathedral school at Chartres, which before long became the most renowned institution of its kind in France, attracting as he did students even from Germany, Italy and England. In appreciation of his wisdom and learning, his pupils styled him the “Venerable Socrates”.



      For the last 22 years of his life he was Bishop of Chartres and as such laboured at the reform of the clergy and against the practice of assigning church benefices to laymen; he did not hesitate to even rebuke some of his fellow Bishops for their worldly-minded, military adventures. A personal friend of Saint Odilo of Cluny, of Blessed Richard of Saint Vannes, and of Saint Abbo of Fleury, he wholeheartedly endorsed their great monastic reforms.



      Saint Fulbert is also hailed as a poet. He composed a number of hymns to Our Lady, for whom he had a special devotion, and introduced the feast of her Nativity in his diocese. He died on 10 April 1029 and was buried in the monastery church of Saint Pere-de-Chartres.
