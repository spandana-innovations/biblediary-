    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      Jesus tells his disciples about some things that will take place as a reminder of the transitory nature of earthly things, and of the necessity of keeping our attention on spiritual matters. Jerusalem would be trampled underfoot by the gentiles. Sky and earth will show frightening signs before the Son of Man comes in glory. We need therefore to be watchful and prepared for his coming. Then, when we see these signs, we can be confident that our redemption is drawing near. Though the warning appears to be frightening, it is actually also comforting, hopeful and assuring.



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Jesus is prophesying the destruction of Jerusalem and the end of the world in today’s Gospel passage. He says that God is going to punish the city of Jerusalem for its sins, for its indifference shown to Christ and its rejection of Christ’s preaching as foretold by the prophets: “*Behold the day of the Lord comes, cruel, with wrath and fierce anger to make the earth a desolation and to destroy its sinners from it”*(Isaiah 13:9-13; Joel 2:1-2; Amos 5:18-20; Zephaniah 1:14-18). Josephus, the Jewish historian, reports that over a million inhabitants died when the Romans destroyed Jerusalem with the Temple in AD 70. Jesus also warns his disciples about the end of the world and his second coming, this time in glory as Judge of the whole human race, quoting Daniel’s prophecy 7:13-14. There will be visible changes in nature, in heavenly bodies and in the universe as a whole: “The powers of the heavens will be shaken.” But Jesus encourages his followers to be prepared for the event with the assurance of their Heavenly reward. Sacred Scripture describes the solemnity of this event, when the sentence passed on each person in the particular judgment will be confirmed, and God’s justice and mercy to men throughout history will shine out for all to see.



      Life messages:



      1) Today’s Scripture readings warn us to be ever prepared to give an account of our lives to Jesus, our Judge, at the moment of our death, which nobody knows.



      2) They also tell us to be ready to meet Jesus coming again, this time as the Judge of the universe, by avoiding sins and doing good to others, seeing the face of Jesus in each one of them.

      Luke 21:20-28 contains a significant Gospel message that addresses eschatological themes and the coming of the reign of God. The events mentioned serve as a reminder of the impermanence of worldly structures and the importance of focusing on spiritual matters. Jesus speaks of cosmic signs and distress among the nations. These signs are not meant to cause fear. They serve as a reminder of the need to be spiritually prepared for the future. While there will be upheaval and distress in the world, Jesus encourages his followers not to be terrified. We need to stay alert and pray at all times, maintaining a deep and active connection with God. Prayer helps us remain spiritually vigilant and open to God’s guidance in times of uncertainty.

    saintName: "Saint Virgilius of Salzburg, Bishop Confessor"
    ref: "Saint Virgilius of Salzburg, Bishop Confessor"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Saint Virgilius was a scientist far ahead of his time! As abbot of Aghaboe near Dublin, Ireland, he was known as “the Geometer” because of his knowledge of geography. In 743, he left Ireland for a pilgrimage to the Holy Land but got no farther than the court of Pepin, the father of Charlemagne. In 745, when Pepin defeated Odilo, Duke of Bavaria, he sent Virgilius to be abbot of the monastery of Sankt Peter and Bishop of the diocese of Salzburg.



      Contrary to continental tradition, Irish custom decreed that the bishop be subject to the abbot, who was the real head of the diocese. Virgilius thus had himself consecrated bishop on 15 June 767.



      Early on in Salzburg, he had locked horns with St Boniface over the form of baptism, which the pope decided in Virgilius’ favour. His most notable accomplishments were the conversion of the Alpine Slavs and sending missionaries into Hungary and other places where no missionary had yet gone. Returning from a preaching mission to a distant part of his diocese, he fell ill and died on 27 November 784.



      The discovery of his grave in 1181, consequent to the destruction of the grand cathedral he had built and dedicated to St Rupert at Salzburg, led to his canonization at the hands of Pope Gregory IX in 1233.
