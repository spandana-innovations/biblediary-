  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Matthew 17:22–27"
    audio: null
    body: |
      There were division of opinion among the Israelites when they were going through the most difficult time of the exile. Few people of Israel were bitter towards God and they questioned among themselves ‘How the living God could punish us’. While some others were humble enough to accept their mistakes and understand the exile was because of their own mistakes. They became aware that it was they who had punished the loving God by denying the love and covenant that He offered to them. When there was division of opinion Prophet Ezekiel corrects some of their misunderstandings concerning God of Israel. He says ‘The God of Israel is not a God who takes pleasure in the suffering of his people, but narrates the magnificent presence of the living God in the midst of the people who are in exile.’ This same living God is constantly and in magnanimous way makes Himself present among the people in Capernaum but the scribes and Pharisees failed to understand this presence. As a consequence they miss the essence of life that is ‘love of God and love of neighbour’. So they insist Jesus to pay the tax. He did not run away from it but rather made himself one with the people who suffer because of the system. Jesus does not want to be seen as a bad example that would cause the downfall of others.



      Do I miss the real goal of life by focusing on unnecessary things?

      *(Fr Saji Antony, SSP)*
  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      In the gospel of Matthew, we have a unique passage where we see Jesus paying taxes. While it was not necessary that Jesus should pay the tax, he paid it in order to teach us to be responsible citizens. The event occurs in the context of Jesus predicting His passion a second time. The implied message is that even when death lurks, we have to be responsible towards civil society and government. Jesus does not want to give cause for offence to the state. Doing good and being responsible will always increase our credibility and help us to live in harmony and peace. How do we relate to the state? Are we ready to fulfil our duties?

    saintName: "St Murtagh - Bishop (6th Century)"
    ref: "St Murtagh - Bishop (6th Century)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Hailing from the royal family of King Laoghaire, St Murtagh is reputed to have been the first Bishop of Killala, Ireland, appointed by St Patrick. It seems probable, that he lived at a later date, for in the life of St Cormac, it is stated that the harbour of Killala was blessed in turn by Patrick, Brigid, Colmcille (St Columba), Kenny and Murtagh, which doesn’t sound, according to some, that St Patrick and Murtagh were contemporaries.

      Furthermore, he is stated to have met St Colmcille at Ballysodare, near Silago, after Colmcille’s conference with the Irish king at Drumkeith in 575.

      St Murtagh’s feast is kept throughout Ireland.
