  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 10:17–24"
    audio: null
    body: |
      We take natural pride in our work, when we have done it well. The disciples return in upbeat mood from a successful mission. With great satisfaction they report that they have even succeeded in casting out devils. Jesus affirms the success of their work, but advises them to focus on something else. More basic than outward success is in the fact that their names are written in heaven.</span></span></span></span></span>




      The deepest source of their joy, and ourse, must be our relationship with God. It is this link with God which ultimately makes our work worthwhile. Jesus declared, “Happy the eyes that see what you see.” They recognised the presence of God in the person of Jesus. They believed in his special relationship with the Father and felt a share in that relationship. That is the basic source of their joy, after doing their best.</span></span></span></span></span>




      Our sharing in Jesus’ relationship with God is our greatest treasure, beyond any success we may have in life. Sharing in Jesus’ relationship with the Father is what “many prophets and kings longed to see and hear”, and is our deepest reason for joy and gratitude. Even when our working life ends, our share in Jesus’ relationship with God endures.</span></span></span></span></span>

      The return of the seventy-two bring home three important points: a) One should rejoice not because of the external signs of the kingdom of God but the invisible fruit of his mission which only God knows. b) One should acquire a childlike heart that is receptive of the wisdom from above without any prejudice. c) One should consider oneself fortunate because of the privilege to see and hear things that one’s predecessors were not fortunate to see and hear. After all, God dwells in the heart that is faithful and loyal. *Which are the spiritual and heavenly fruits I am expected to bear in my mission? Who are the little ones in my community and who are the learned and the wise? What are the things that I am fortunate to see and hear?*</span></span></span></span>
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Maria Faustina Kowalska (Virgin, visionary) (1905-1938)"
    ref: "Saint Maria Faustina Kowalska (Virgin, visionary) (1905-1938)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Named Helena at birth, this ardent promoter of the “Divine Mercy” devotion worked as a domestic since the age of fourteen in order to help her parents until, in 1925, she entered the Congregation of the Sisters of Our Lady of Mercy at Warsaw. Here, amidst the humble setting of the simple daily chores Jesus, in a vision in 1931, made her his secretary, confidant and messenger, instructing her to paint an image of his according to the profile she saw. She was blessed with other innumerable and extensive mystical experiences which she put down in her “Diary” at the behest of her spiritual director and of Jesus himself.</span></span></span>



      She took the name Maria Faustina at her veiling in the Novitiate in 1926 and made her final profession in 1933. In 1934, she visited the artist, E. Kazimirowski, in connection with the proposed painting. </span></span></span>



      Despite ill-health and humiliation, she was ever obedient to the dictates of conscience, superior and Lord. Suffering in silence, she prayed fervently for priests, for the Church and her needs, and for the world at large. The Lord for his part, made his satisfaction known to her thus: “My child, you are my delight. You are the comfort of my heart. I grant you as many graces as you can hold. As often as you want to make me happy, speak to the world about my great and unfathomable mercy.”</span></span></span>



      Sr Maria Faustina Kowalska was canonized by her compatriot, Pope Saint John Paul II, in the Jubilee Year, 2000.</span></span></span>
