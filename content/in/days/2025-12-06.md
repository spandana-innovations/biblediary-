    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      Jesus had been preaching the kingdom and healing people who had various kinds of diseases. He saw crowds of harassed and helpless people and had compassion on them. He gave authority to the twelve disciples to continue the same mission as he did, and to proclaim that the kingdom of heaven was at hand. This was their first mission. All of us, disciples of Jesus, are his missionaries, and are called to continue the same service that he did on earth. He twelve symbolise the twelve tribes of Israel, and stand for the new people of God that Jesus instituted.



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel describes the three chief activities of Jesus’ mission, namely heralding, teaching, and healing, and tells how Jesus selected the twelve apostles as disciples and helpers in his Messianic mission.



      Jesus was primarily the Herald of God his Father, bringing mankind the Good News that God is a loving, forgiving, merciful, and compassionate Father Who wants everyone to be saved.



      Secondly, Jesus was a teacher and preacher who taught the Gospel, or the Good News of the Kingdom of God, by living an exemplary life, demonstrating God’s love, mercy, forgiveness, and compassion.



      Thirdly, Jesus was a Healer, spending much time healing people of their bodily, mental, and spiritual illnesses.



      The Gospel for today also mentions that Jesus selected ordinary men of no social status as apostles to continue his preaching and healing mission, and gave them both healing power and preaching authority to do so.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span>



      1) As Christians, we share Christ’s mission of preaching and healing. This means that we, too, have to demonstrate by our exemplary and transparent Christian lives the mercy, the forgiveness, and the unconditional love of Jesus.



      2) We are also called to act as the agents of healing by praying for the sick, by helping them to get the necessary medical and nursing help, and by encouraging them, supporting them and boosting their morale.

      Despite the popularity of His ministry, Jesus, moved by compassion, empathises with the harassed crowds. We are reminded that “challenges are what makes life interesting, and overcoming them is what makes life meaningful!” Though challenges were raging hard, Jesus remained courageous. He sends out the Twelve on a mission – to be the extension of His Self, His teachings and His works. They are commissioned into the art of mission with three important principles: focused preaching, balanced resilience, and unwavering trust. Each of us, by our discipleship of Christ, is a missionary. Though we may experience “the bread of adversity and the water of affliction,” we are called to remain faithful to the Master. Are we dedicated solely to His Kingdom, resilient against life’s pressures, and steadfast in trust amidst trials?

    saintName: "Saint Nicholas of Bari - Bishop Confessor"
    ref: "Saint Nicholas of Bari - Bishop Confessor"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Nicholas of Bari (Italy) was Bishop of Myra in Asia Minor for some time during the first half of the 4th century. Legend has it that on returning from a pilgrimage to the Holy Land, he came to Myra and early the next morning went to church. The local Bishop having just recently died, the clergy who had secretly agreed that the first person to enter the church that morning would be the new incumbent, consecrated Nicholas bishop. He suffered imprisonment during the Diocletian persecution, but was freed by the Edict of Constantine and is known to have attended the great Nicaean Council I (c.325), which condemned the Arian heresy.



      The principal miracle-legends that highlight Nicholas’ reputation for generosity and kindness speak of his liberation of three unjustly condemned officers; his secret provision of dowries for three daughters of an impoverished nobleman—he threw gold-filled purses in through the window during the night, thus making him the “bringer”; and his deliverance of three innocent youths condemned to death.



      After his death, the Myra Christians honoured his memory by leaving surprise gifts for their children by their bedside during the night before his anniversary. This tradition gradually led to the legend of “Saint Nicholas” which in popular parlance became corrupted to “Santa Claus”!



      His relics were allegedly stolen from his Church in Myra in 1087 by some Italian sailors and merchants, and brought to Bari, Southern Italy, where they are now enshrined and venerated in the church of Saint Nicola.
