      Reflection: 1



      Women are commissioned by Jesus to communicate the message that he is risen. The chief priests and the elders deliberately spread the lie that the body of Jesus was stolen. The women carry the message of life and resurrection. Only hearts that are simple, humble and open to love can share God’s truth. The Church that proclaims the risen Christ should not have earthly power and wealth; the Church is energized and sustained by the power of God. The Lord who has risen from death gives the Church also the power to rise. Therefore, in spite of all adversities, the Church community should never lose hope. Are we ready and prepared to live like those who have met the risen Jesus? Do we not sometimes hide ourselves from the truth of Christ by making excuses and by our false reasoning?



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel describes how the two Marys who had been at the foot of the cross – Mary Magdalene and “the other Mary” (sister or cousin of Jesus’ mother) – went to the tomb of Jesus early on Sunday morning. They were shocked at seeing an open tomb without Jesus’ body in it. As they ran back with fear and joy to report the news to the apostles, the risen Jesus greeted them and told them to inform the apostles that he would meet them in Galilee. The guards, too, went to the chief priests to report the Resurrection of Jesus. But they were promptly silenced by the Temple authorities who bribed them to spread the false news that Jesus’ body had been stolen by his disciples.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span> 1) The Resurrection of Christ is the most sublime and foundational truth of our Faith. It is the presence of the risen Lord everywhere that gives meaning to our worship and prayers, because Jesus is present everywhere to hear our prayers and grant our petitions. 2) The Risen Lord is present in the Holy Eucharist, in the Bible, in the praying assembly, and in believing Christians, so we need never be out of contact with him. 3) Let us renew this fundamental belief of our Faith every day and let us recognize and serve the risen Christ in everyone around us, thus becoming the Marys, “apostles to the apostles”.

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null

    body: |
      Today’s Gospel reveals a striking contrast: the good news of life – Christ’s resurrection – is entrusted to women who are marginalised in society, while the lie is spread by those in positions of power. This reversal echoes the Beatitudes in Matthew 5: “Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the Kingdom of heaven,” and “Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God.” It reminds us that God often uses the humble and outcast to proclaim His truth. Pope Francis once said, “The truth of Christ is not imposed violently or arrogantly; it is an invitation to love.” The women at the tomb responded to this invitation, carrying the truth of life without the backing of power or prestige. This passage calls us to recognise that God’s truth is best shared by hearts open to love, humility, and simplicity – values the world may overlook but God treasures.

    body: |
      Born in Campania, Italy *(date unknown)*, Celestine was a Deacon in Rome when he was elected to succeed Boniface I on 20 September 422. An outstanding feature of his pontificate which lasted till 432 was its vigorous attack on the twin heresies then prevailing, viz., Nestorianism and Pelagianism.



      It was Celestine himself who had approved the choice of Nestorius as Patriarch of Constantinople in 428. However, the latter’s teachings, stressing on the one hand that Christ’s human and divine natures were each independent of the other, and, on the other, denouncing Mary’s title of “Theotokos”—God-bearer—came as a rude jolt to him. His untiring efforts to change such heretical thinking having come to naught, Celestine entrusted Cyril, the saintly Archbishop of Alexandria with the task of condemning Nestorius’ heresy and obtaining his recantation at the Third Council of Ephesus held in 431. Celestine’s approval of the council’s decree deposing and excommunicating Nestorius eventually led to a schism that remained unresolved for over a century.



      Earlier, in 429, Celestine deputed two French bishops, viz., Saint Germanus of Auxerre and Saint Lupus of Troyes to Rome to refute the doctrine of Pelagius that minimalized the role of divine grace in man’s salvation. In 431, Celestine consecrated Saint Palladius at Rome and sent him as the first bishop of Ireland, to evangelize the Irish.



      Saint Celestine passed away on 27 July 432. Buried in the cemetery of Saint Priscilla on the Via Salaria, his mortal remains were subsequently moved to the Basilica di Santa Prassede.
