      Reflection: 1



      Jesus speaks about the bread of life to people who ask him for a sign so that they could believe him. They told him about the manna, the bread from heaven which Moses gave their fathers in the wilderness. But truly it was not Moses but the Father who gives the bread from heaven, that is Jesus himself who came down from heaven and gives life to the world. He is the real bread that satisfies all hunger and thirst whereas the manna satisfied physical hunger for a short while. On the other hand, the Eucharistic bread, the Body of Christ, can satisfy all earthly and spiritual hunger of those who believe in Christ. Those who eat this Bread with proper interior dispositions will have eternal life.</span></span></span></span>



      *— Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP</span></span>*Reflection: 2



      In reply to Jesus’ implied claim that he was the Messiah and his declaration that He had been sent from Heaven to give Eternal Life to those who believed in him, the Jews demanded a sign from Heaven. Moses, they said, gave a Heavenly sign to their ancestors in the form of manna, rained down on them from Heaven. The Jewish rabbis taught that the promised Messiah would repeat the miracle of the manna as a Messianic sign, and that the prophet Jeremiah would reappear and show the Jews the Ark of the Covenant where the original manna had been kept. Jesus explained to the Jews that it was not Moses but God, his Heavenly Father, who had given them manna from Heaven. He then claimed that he was more than a provider of bread like Moses because he* was himself the bread that the Father was providing.* In other words, Jesus is the Heavenly manna whom the Father has sent to the world as the Bread of Life. Thus, Jesus clarifies that the manna given to Moses and the people was not the real bread from Heaven, but only a foreshadowing of the Bread to come. Jesus also demands from them an absolute Faith in himself as the Son of God and the Bread of Life if they do not want to hunger and thirst again. Jesus uses the metaphor of food and drink to show that He is the One Who really meets all man’s essential needs and noblest aspirations.



      Life message: 1) Jesus kept his promise, and he continues to feed us with his Body and Blood in the Holy Eucharist. So, let us nourish our souls with this Heavenly manna. Let us also remember that our duty is to carry this Jesus to our homes and workplaces, radiating his love, mercy and compassion all around us.

      We have the Bread of Life discourse in Chapter 6 of Saint John’s Gospel. To the Jews who were familiar with the Manna of the desert, Jesus presented himself as the Bread of Life. The Manna satiated the physical hunger of the people in the desert, whereas the Eucharistic Bread would satisfy all the physical and spiritual desires of the believer. The call is to believe in the great power of this sacrament, in which we have the physical presence of the Lord. Saint Faustina Kowalska writes: “If angels could be jealous of men, they would be so for one reason: Holy Communion”. Padre Pio says that a even thousand years of human glory would not equal an hour spent in communion with Jesus in the Blessed Sacrament.

    ref: "St Dominic Savio, Confessor (1842 -1857)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      One of St John Bosco’s first pupils was Dominic Savio who was born at Murialdo, in Northern Italy, on 2 April 1842, of Charles Savio and his wife Bridgid.



      The holy life of young Dominic was nurtured both at home and in the parish church. A very peaceful Christian domestic environment helped prepare his soul to receive great light and graces from God. Don Bosco, on the other hand, helped enkindle in the child’s heart a deep love for God and a deep devotion to the Virgin Mary. Dominic went to church every morning at five, served at Mass, and then spent his time in devotion and prayer. Come rain or shine, freezing winter or sweltering summer, the boy was regular at his church duties.



      From Don Bosco, who was to become his biographer in later years, St Dominic Savio learned how the joy of serving and making the Lord loved by others can become a powerful means of apostolate. So spiritually advanced was the lad, exhibiting many gifts, including that of prophecy, that it was one of his visions which, in 1850, influenced Pope Pius IX to restore the Catholic hierarchy to England. St Pius X said of him: “A teenager such as Dominic, who bravely struggled to preserve undefiled his baptismal innocence to the very last, is indeed a saint!” At 15, he had become a giant of the spirit. Pope Pius XI added: “He was, indeed, the perfection of Christian life – a life drawing its strength from three main sources, namely purity, piety, zeal!”



      On 9 March 1857, the youngster, who longed to be a saint, burst into ecstatic exclamation: “Oh, what a beautiful sight I see!” before passing to his reward. He was beatified on 5 March 1950.



      Pope Pius XII, on the occasion of the solemn canonization on 12 June 1954, stated: “Truly, it gives us great joy to raise this boy of just 15, Dominic Savio, to the honours of the Altar and to set him before the youth of our day as a shining example for their imitation, especially in these troubled times when such powerful and sinister forces are doing incalculable harm in a relentless effort to uproot all virtues from the hearts of the young... Among the heroes there appears the image of Dominic Savio, the delicate adolescent, weak of body, but with a soul determined upon a pure oblation of itself to the exacting love of Christ.” The four resolutions the saintly youngster made on Easter Sunday 8 April 1849, the day he received his First Holy Communion at the age of seven, stand out as an important legacy for youth for all time – 1) I will frequent the Sacrament of Reconciliation and receive Holy Communion as often as my confessor permits; 2) I will keep feast days holy; 3) My friends shall be Jesus and Mary; 4) Death rather than sin.
