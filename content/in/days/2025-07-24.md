      Reflection: 1



      Every parable of Jesus challenges us to be deeply conscious of our situation and the quality of our life, and to take a decision. God’s love is made known to us by Jesus; we can either accept this love, which is the kingdom of God, and change our lives, or reject it and continue in our sin. Many of the listeners of Jesus did not accept or respond to his parables. Jesus points out that this was already foretold by Prophet Isaiah (Is 6: 9-10); people were obstinate in their prejudices and did not have the goodness of heart to be converted and saved. Jesus assures his disciples that they are blessed because they see, hear and understand Jesus and respond to him positively. </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      We all have our moments of spiritual insight but sometimes perhaps we drift off into spiritual apathy and blindness. Like our biblical forebears we need to consciously return to seek grace and meaning. Jeremiah urges us to keep alive our initial ideal, when speaking in God’s name he says: “I remember the devotion of your youth, how you loved me as a bride, following me in the desert, in a land not sown. Sacred to the Lord was Israel, the first fruits of his harvest.” The message is not unlike that of St Paul, when he urged Timothy to fan into flames the spiritual give he has received from God (2 Timothy 1:6).



      Each of us probably treasures some memories of inspiring moments in our lives but through the passage of time, we tend to lose sight of these moments, our personal “highs” of encounter with God. Perhaps we feel in ourselves an echo of Jeremiah’s words: “Two evils have my people done: they have forsaken me, the source of living waters; They have dug themselves cisterns, broken cisterns, that hold no water.” During economic growth and prosperity there always was a heightened danger of abandoning God in favour of material greed and a glitzy social life, which the prophet graphically describes as “broken cisterns, that hold no water.”



      We turn to Jesus to revive our finest ideals from the days of our fervour. If we remember our original call, its inspiration and our first enthusiasm for life, God’s grace can develop within us. In such a context we might gain new understanding of those puzzling words of Jesus: “To the one who has, more will be given until that one grows rich; the one who has not, will lose what little he or she has.”

    body: |
      We can identify three reasons why Jesus spoke in parables with his disciples. 1) He taught them the eternal truths that needed explanation through parables because the everlasting truths are not simple to understand. 2) The stories or parables serve as effective tools to get the attention of the audience. Jesus often taught in the open field and on the banks of the lakes and therefore had to capture the attention of the people through parables. 3) The parables were effective means for the listeners to analyse and examine their conscience. People do not like to receive advice all the time, but they like stories that appeal to them. Let us understand the deeper meaning of the parables used by Jesus and live the way He wants us to lead our lives.

    ref: "Saint Francis Solano Confessor (1549-1610)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Born to Matteo, then Mayor of the Andalusian town of Montilla, Spain and head of the Supreme Court, and his wife Anna, both of whom were known for their piety and virtue, Francis received his a Jesuit education before entering the Friars Minor at 20 and was ordained priest at 27. His boundless compassion for the sick and the sinner was amply demonstrated during an outbreak of the plague in Granada, Spain, when he gave himself over entirely to nursing the stricken people.



      While on a mission to South America in 1589, he was shipwrecked south of Panama, before eventually reaching Lima, Peru. For the next twenty years, he successfully evangelized in Peru, Argentina, Paraguay and Uruguay at the cost of much personal sacrifice.



      Francis’ zeal for making Christ known and loved by all expressed itself in a life of faith, piety, prayer and care, apart from speedily mastering the numerous difficult dialects of South America, winning the people’s interest and goodwill by playing for them on his violin and snapping his fingers to imitate the Castanets. His ministrations to the ailing were adequately backed by knowledgeable medical lore. Unfortunately, the numerous writings and musical compositions (carols, songs and dances in honour of the Christ Child and the Blessed Virgin) of this “Wonderworker of the New World”, which were sent to Rome in support of the process of his beatification, have since been lost.



      Beatified in 1675, Francis was canonized in 1726.
