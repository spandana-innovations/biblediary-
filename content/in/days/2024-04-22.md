    body: |
      The image of Jesus as the good shepherd is a picture most of us are familiar with from our childhood. These pictures depict Jesus carrying the sheep either on his shoulders or close to his bosom. Today’s Gospel reveals that Jesus is familiar with the life of shepherds and their closeness to their sheep. Moreover, Jesus is aware of thieves and robbers entering the sheepfold at times. But the sheep do not listen to them. It is in such a situation as this that Jesus compares himself as the door of the sheepfold and assures life in its fullness to all those who enter through him. Do I recognize the voice of Jesus, my good shepherd, whenever he calls my name? Am I prepared to experience abundant life as promised by him?</span></span></span></span>

      Saint Soter</span></span> - *Pope Martyr (-175)*</span></span></span></span>



      Saint Soter was a native of Fondi near Gaeta in Italy. It was upon the death of Saint Anicetus in 166, that Soter was raised to the Papacy thus becoming the 12th in the succession of Popes. One of the rising heretical sects which this Pope had to combat during his 9-year reign was that of Montanus, a Phrygian, who taught that certain sins could never be forgiven and claimed that he was God’s messenger for a new outpouring of the Holy Spirit. To Saint Soter is also attributed a precept that all the faithful, excepting only such as were guilty of a notable crime, should receive Holy Communion on Maundy Thursday.</span></span></span></span>



      A letter which the Pope sent to the persecuted Church at Corinth has unfortunately been lost, but an extant fragment of the reverent reply of Saint Dionysius, the Bishop of Corinth, is interesting for its reference to “the abundance which the Pope has sent to the saints, and the consolation which he has offered with words of comfort to the brethren who came to him, as a loving father would to his children.” “Today, the Lord’s day, we have read your letter and we will always wish to re-read it and to be admonished, the same as by the earlier letter which was sent to us by Clement.”</span></span></span></span>



      Saint Soter is believed to have suffered martyrdom in 175 under Marcus Aurelius.</span></span></span></span>
