  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Matthew 7:21–29"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      Not everyone who calls Jesus “Lord” or honour him with words is a true disciple of Jesus. To be his disciple and to participate in his risen life, one has to do the will of the Father in heaven, that is, put into practice the teaching of Jesus. Merely reading the Bible or listening to the words of Jesus or feeling good at prayer is not enough; these pious actions must be matched by good deeds and helping the neighbour. The way we live our daily lives show if we are or not true disciples of Jesus. If we hear his words and live according to them, we are wise people building our life on solid foundation; if we do no put his words into practice, we are building our life on shaky foundation, and it can be easily ruined.



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      In today’s Gospel, the concluding part of the Sermon on the Mount, Jesus gives us two warnings: that we must match our profession of Faith with actual obedience to the will of God, and that we must build our life on the firm foundation of Jesus’ teachings. Worship of God without commitment to the word of God is hypocrisy. Sincerity in a Christian can be demonstrated not by what one says but by what one does. Fine words can never be a substitute for fine deeds. Thus, today’s Gospel gives Jesus’ call to authentic discipleship based on the strong foundation of Gospel teaching. Acting on the words of Christ shows the authenticity of one’s Christian commitment. Jesus contrasts a wise man who practices what he believes with a fool who does not practice his religious beliefs, using the images of one man who built his house on firm rock and another who built his house on loose sand in summer. Only a house with solid and firm foundation can resist the storm and flood, and only a person whose life has strong spiritual foundations can stand the test. Building on loose sand is the way to destruction. Thus, the two builders sum up two ways – the way of perfect righteousness and the way of self-righteousness. On the Day of Judgment, the first will stand; the second will fall.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages: </span>



      1) We need to synchronize our practice of the Faith with our profession of it: The test of our Sunday worship is the effect it has in our homes and workplaces and on our relationships with friends and neighbours. The great test is the care and consideration we show to our neighbours, many of whom commonly experience the absence of affection, of words of encouragement and of forgiveness.



      2) We need to build our families on strong foundations: There can be*no* great marriage and *no* great family without a solid foundation. Such a foundation exists when the husband and wife are the love of Christ for each other and for their children in deeds as well as in words. Our culture and nation also need strong foundations based on the moral law of God and love of Jesus Christ, and this is possible only if our families are built on these foundations.

    body: |
      Today’s gospel passage is the conclusion of Jesus’ Sermon on the Mount. This section sums up the notion of who is a true disciple. Not everyone who says or affirms that Jesus is the risen Lord will enter the Kingdom of Heaven! For Jesus, it is how Christian life is lived out daily that demonstrates whether or not someone is his true disciple. Mere lip service does not matter much. The story of the two persons who built their houses on rock and sand respectively provides a fitting closure to the whole of the Sermon on the Mount. A strong foundation makes it possible for the house to survive even the worst of storms. And a weak foundation leaves the house in ruins. The foundation intended by Jesus is one’s ability and willingness to listen to and live by the word of God. Hearing and doing the word of God means a life of solid foundation and lasting survival despite the storms of life. Jesus, the incarnate Word, is a rock of truth on which we can make decisions and commitments in life.

    saintName: "St Josemaria Escrívá de Balaguer (Priest) (1902-1975)"
    ref: "St Josemaria Escrívá de Balaguer (Priest) (1902-1975)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Josemaria was a man who became a “master of Christian living” by reaching “the heights of contemplation with continuous prayer, constant mortification” and “exemplary docility to the movements of the Holy Spirit.”



      It was at the early age of 16 that Josemaria felt called to some as yet unknown but important task. With characteristic faith he responded with the words of scripture: “Here I am Lord, because you have called me” (1 Sam 3:5). Ordained priest in 1925, he took up the study of civil law in Madrid in 1927, his apostolic zeal soon having him interacting with a cross-section of society—artists and academicians, labourers and intellectuals, army-men and clergymen alike. To strive for sanctity by way of living Christ’s own life among his people as enjoined by Baptism was the call that became evident in 1928 when making a retreat. Thus was born Opus Dei whose mission would be “to open up in the Church a new vocational path aimed at spreading the quest for holiness and the practice of the apostolate through sanctification of ordinary work in the world.”



      With the rapid spread of Opus Dei came trials and hardships of every kind, financial difficulties assailing the worldwide expansion of apostolic works. Josemaria’s sense of humour, however, stood him in good stead. In 1954, he was miraculously cured of a serious form of diabetes that had troubled him for over a decade.



      The heart of Josemaria’s message is: *Sanctify your daily life, and sanctify yourself in and through that daily life.*Beatified in 1992, he was finally canonized in 2002.
