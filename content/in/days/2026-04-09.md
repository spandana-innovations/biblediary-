      Reflection: 1



      The eleven disciples are mentally and spiritually not ready to accept the gift of peace the risen Jesus offers them when he appears to them, though the gift of peace is h gift of wholeness of mind and body. But Jesus comforts them and encourages them by reassuring them that it is he, showing them his hands and feet and eating with them. Jesus offers us peace when we are frightened and doubtful. He gives his disciples the duty of sharing with all the message of repentance and forgiveness, for they are important witnesses of his message. They have to proclaim his message and his presence everywhere. To strengthen them for this purpose, he is going to send the Holy Spirit whom the Father promised. They were to remain in Jerusalem till they were empowered by the Spirit. Let us pray to the Holy Spirit to strengthen us to carry out our life’s mission. </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel passage describes the first post-Resurrection appearance of Jesus to the apostles while they were listening to the first-hand account of his appearance by the two disciples who had gone to Emmaus and had met Jesus on the way. This apparition is reported by both Luke and John.</span></span></span></span></span>



      The apostles could not believe that their Risen Lord was before them. Hence, Jesus had to show them the marks of the wounds in his hands and feet in proof. He also asked for a piece of broiled fish and ate it before them. Thus, Jesus confirmed his apostles’ Faith in his Resurrection by inviting them to touch Him, and by giving them these two proofs. Then Jesus explained to them that all that had happened during the past week had been done to fulfill the Messianic prophecies given in the Torah, by the Prophets, and in the Psalms. His suffering, death, and Resurrection took place exactly as they had been prophesied. (Throughout his account, St. Matthew cites the Old Testament prophecies that have been fulfilled in Christ, because the Evangelist’s immediate readers were Jews, who needed and would accept these fulfillments as proofs that Jesus was indeed the promised Messiah). The next unfolding of Jesus’ mission was to be the preaching of repentance and the forgiveness of sins. That would be begun by the apostles; it would be carried on (and will finally be brought to completion), by the Church at Jesus’ Second Coming and Final Judgment of the living and the dead.</span></span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span></span></span> 1) We, too, are called to bear witness for all around us to the Resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead, just as the first apostles were commissioned by Jesus to bring the Good News of salvation to all the nations. 2) What is essential for the success of our preaching and witnessing mission is our firm and lively awareness of the presence and support of the Risen Lord in our lives. </span></span></span></span></span>

      Today we reflect on Jesus’ appearance to His disciples in Jerusalem, as recounted in Luke 24:35-48. After His resurrection, Jesus appeared to His disciples, who were filled with fear and doubt. To reassure them, He showed them His wounds and shared a meal, a simple act that confirmed His true, risen presence among them. Jesus then opened their minds to understand the Scriptures, helping them see that His suffering, death, and resurrection were part of God’s plan of salvation. Today we are reminded that Jesus meets us in our fear and uncertainty, bringing peace and understanding. He calls us to be witnesses to His resurrection, sharing His message of forgiveness and new life with others. We are challenged to trust in His presence and to carry the joy of the resurrection into our daily lives, as He tells us, “See my hands and my feet, that it is I myself. Touch me, and see.”</span></span></span>

      Saint Waldetrudis belonged to an exceptional family of saints. Her parents were Saints Walbert and Bertilia; her sister, Saint Aldegundis, Foundress-Abbess of Maubeuge; her husband, Saint Vincent Madalgarius, and their four children Saints Landericus, Dentelinus, Madelberta and Aldetrudis. The last two named were both abbesses of Maubeuge.</span></span></span></span>



      Waldetrudis and Madalgarius, a nobleman by birth, lived a happy Christian married life. Sometime after the birth of their last child, Madalgarius withdrew to the abbey of Haumont which he had founded, and there took the name of Vincent.</span></span></span></span>



      Waldetrudis stayed on in the world two more years and then humbly withdrew to a life of poverty and simplicity at a place called “Castrilocus in Monte”, around which developed the abbey of Chateaulieu and the city of Mons (Belgium). Here she gave herself completely to works of mercy and was well known for her miracles of healing both before and after her death.</span></span></span></span>
