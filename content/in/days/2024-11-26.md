  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 21:5–11"
    audio: null
    body: |
      In Jesus’ day the temple of Jerusalem was an imposing and beautiful structure. Herod had just rebuilt it, from the years 19 to 9 B.C. It was twice the size of the former temple and more ornate. At this time it was still being furnished and decorated. No wonder, then, that as Jesus was in the temple preaching, there were many people on hand, admiring the structure and talking about its beauty and imposing size. As a people they were extremely proud of it. So they must have been shocked when Jesus predicted the destruction of this new temple, a destruction so great that “not a stone would be left on another.” Immediately they wanted details. When? What signs would point to the end? We can compare ourselves to these people of Jesus’ time. Just as they were proud of their temple and its glory, so we are very proud of our achievements, our buildings, our technology. “Be careful,” Jesus says, “Don’t be misled. All these things are merely passing. Yes, I want you to improve the earth, to use all the resources I gave you. But you are to use them for good, for my poor, to bring a better life to my least brethren.”

      (Pray Today’s Gospel, Bernard C. Mischke O.S.C. and Fritz Mischke O.S.C.)
  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      The Readings of the day tell us about the end of time which signifies the Second Coming of Christ. Jesus speaks about the end of time and the impending persecutions the followers will undergo in his name and advises us to consider such troubled times as opportunities to bear witness. Saint John explains his vision of the Son of Man seated on the clouds with golden crown and sickle as the judge and ruler of the world. It says everything which Jesus foretold would happen before the end of time, hence the need to wait for him in careful watch. The world in our time too goes through persecution, war, violence and the Church suffers for faith and lack of faith. It becomes hard for Christians to remain steadfast in faith. There are signs that warn us to be alert and watchful to meet the end of time and no one may deceive us before we meet the Son of Man. Let us remember, every end is a meeting point from where another journey begins and so the end of our time. The old passes and the new begins, and after the end of all struggles the real joy springs up.

    saintName: "Blessed James Alberione, Priest Founder (1884-1971)"
    ref: "Blessed James Alberione, Priest Founder (1884-1971)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Having finished high school at sixteen, frail James, fourth son of a devout peasant family, joined the seminary of Alba. On the midnight of 31 December 1900, the night that divided the two centuries, as he prayed, long and fervently, before the Blessed Sacrament in the Cathedral of Alba, a special light came from the monstrance and “he felt deeply obliged to prepare himself to do something for the Lord and for the people of the new century.” That “something” translated itself into the apostolate of the “good press” as he termed it, at a time when the Church looked upon the media hostilely in matters associated with the Gospel.

      Ordained priest in 1907, the Lord willed and guided James to use the media of social communications to spread the Gospel in the spirit of Paul the Apostle. In 1914, he laid the foundation of the Society of St Paul by opening a press training school in Alba. In 1915, he founded the Daughters of St Paul with the same mission. A man of vision, he started the Union of Lay Pauline Co-operators in 1917, giving lay involvement in the Pauline apostolate of the media due importance. Reading the signs of the times, he went on to found seven other Institutes, the ten collectively making up what is called the Pauline Family, the ideal of personal holiness and the mission of giving to the world ‘Jesus Master, the Way, the Truth and the Life’ being its source of unity and animation.

      He was beatified in 2003.
