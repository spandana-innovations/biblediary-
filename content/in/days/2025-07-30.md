      Reflection: 1



      These two short parables remind us of something very important: not to miss he opportunities to possess the kingdom of God. In our ordinary daily activities we may unexpectedly come across the kingdom, like he man who found a treasure hidden in a field. Or we may find it when we are in search of the kingdom, like the man looking for a pearl of exceptional quality. The kingdom of God is His gift, but it does not come without cost. In today’s parables, both the men sold all they had in order to be able to possess what they considered most precious. What are we ready to give up for the sake of the kingdom of God? Let us cast away earthly treasures from our hearts and fill them with heavenly treasures. </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel passage gives us twin parables with a common message. Both the pearl and the treasure represent the Kingdom of God, or God’s rule in human hearts, or our close relationship with God kept intact by our doing God’s will.



      Treasure & pearl: Since Palestine was the most fought-over country in the world in the first century, people hid their valuables underground when they fled from invading armies. The owners did not always get a chance to return to their land. Some other farm workers occasionally found such treasures, and the rabbinic law gave the ownership of the treasure to the finder. In order to avoid any later legal problems, the intelligent treasure-finder bought the land containing the treasure, selling all his other possessions to do so, if he had to. Pearls were the most valuable of the treasures. A merchant who located a superior pearl would be wise to sell the rest of his stock and property to acquire it.



      Acquiring a treasure or a pearl of great value means accepting the will of God in our lives and sacrificing everything to do God’s holy will — in other words, living as God wants us to live. That is God’s Kingship over us and within us in action. The Kingdom of God is also a group of people on earth who, with God’s grace, work to do the will of God as perfectly as it is done in Heaven. Hence, being in God’s Kingdom also means offering willing obedience to God.



      Life message: 1) A right relationship with God, or a state of Sanctifying Grace, is the most valuable treasure in the Christian life because it gives us a close relationship with God during this life and a life of everlasting bliss with God after our death. The Holy Spirit, the Holy Bible and the Sacraments are the treasures in the Church which enable us to do the will of God and possess the Kingdom of God. Hence, we must be ready to make any sacrifice to use these treasures, to practice self-control and to offer to those we encounter generous loving service, mercy, and forgiveness, thus keeping a right relationship with God.

      Jesus continues to explain the truths of the kingdom of God by using life-changing parables. He compares the kingdom of God with the precious pearl and the treasure in the field. The parable of the treasure in the field teaches us that the kingdom of God is our right and it comes with a great price. The parable of the precious pearl teaches us that we need to set right our priorities in life. We are inheritors of the kingdom of God. How worthy are we to stake claim to it? Let us seek what is everlasting and thus cherish eternal life.

    body: |
      In the 5th century, the capital of the Holy Roman Empire in the West was Ravenna, not Rome. And one of the most distinguished archbishops the See of Ravenna had was Saint Peter Chrysologus whose flair for preaching, a firm determination and fierce loyalty to the Church not only in terms of its teaching but its authority as well, stood him in good stead in the face of rampant pagan abuses, including the monophysitism of Eutyches.



      A native of Imola, Italy and a product of its education, Peter became bishop when he was only a deacon. He shepherded Ravenna with rare zeal, urging his flock to partake of the Eucharist more frequently. Speaking against the riotous New Year celebrations, he coined his now famous quote: “He who amuses himself with Satan cannot rejoice with Christ!”



      His writings and sermons, some 176 of which are extant [as is the mosaic covered cathedral he erected in Ravenna], contain no great originality of thought, yet are sound in doctrine and full of moral applications for daily living. His attitude towards learning gave substance to his exhortations and changed those who heard him, and, in time, he came to be called “Chrysologus”, i.e., “Golden Worded”.



      Saint Peter Chrysologus died on 2 December 450, the authenticity of content in his sermons earning him the title of Doctor of the Church in 1729.
