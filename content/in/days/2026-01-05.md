    ref: "Mathew 4: 12-17, 23-25"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      When Jesus knew that John the Baptist was arrested he went to Capernaum and there began his ministry of preaching and healing. Both John and Jesus began their preaching by exhorting the people to repent, for the kingdom of heaven was near. The kingdom of heaven is the definitive salvation the Jews were expecting God to give them. Jesus is therefore saying to all that this salvation has come. To receive this salvation everyone needs to repent and be converted. John insisted on turning away from sin, but Jesus speaks of turning towards God to experience His love. This experience is the gift of God’s grace, and results in a change of life that leaves sin and stays close to God in thought, word and deed. All believers of Jesus have the duty to share the good news with their fellow humans.



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel describes the beginning of Jesus’ mission of preaching and healing in Galilee, an ideal spot because it was the most fertile land in Palestine and well-populated with 204 villages around the Sea of Galilee housing Jews and Gentiles. The Jews there largely belonged to the tribes of Asher, Naphtali, and Zebulon. The people were open to new ideas because they had been exposed to various religious beliefs and the culture of traders from all over the known world.



      Preaching the Good News: Jesus started preaching the Good News about God the Father and about God’s Kingdom. Since it was God the King’s message, it carried God’s authority and certainty; it was “Good News” because Jesus introduced the hearers to God His Father as a loving, merciful, providing, and forgiving Father Who wanted to save everyone from the bondage of sin. The message also gave the listeners the “Good News” of the Kingdom of God or the rule of God in human lives. Continuation John’s message, Jesus, too, invited the hearers to repentance and the renewal of their lives so that God might start ruling their lives. Matthew identified Jesus’ preaching and healing ministry in Galilee as the fulfilment of the prophecy of Isaiah — Light to people living in darkness.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span> 1) As Christians we have been given Jesus’ mission to continue. 2) Hence, our exemplary, transparent lives must be and so carry our message radiating the light of Christ’s love, mercy, forgiveness, and spirit of humble and committed service.

      The Gospel reading offers a unique continuity between the ministry of John the Baptist and that of Jesus. The phrase “Repent, for the kingdom of heaven has come near” was announced by both of them. Moreover, their ministries are seen as the fulfilment of the Scriptures. For this reason, the Gospel of Matthew is known as the “Gospel of Fulfilment” wherein the divine purposes are fulfilled in John and Jesus. However, if John’s ministry focuses primarily on baptism in view of the reign of God, Jesus’ ministry is all-inclusive because the reign of God is made present by Him to all, through His words and deeds. How do I share the good news of the reign of God today?

    body: |
      We have here the first and probably the best known case of a Stylite, i.e., a “pillar-hermit”. An oriental ascetic who passed long years perched atop a high column, Saint Simon was born of well-to-do peasants near the northern border of Syria. At 15 he entered a monastery, where his self-imposed austerity was found to be so extreme that his brethren considered him unsuited to community life, and he thenceforth lived as a hermit.



      All through the Season of Lent he would remain in a standing position and would partake of neither food nor drink. His advice and spiritual counsel were sought by innumerable people, even by the Emperor Theodosius and the Empress Endocia. Many were the miraculous cures wrought by his prayers. But in order to escape the importunities of relic-seeking admirers, he had a column built on a hill near the boundary between Syria and Cilicia, at first only some ten feet high, but eventually raised to sixty feet; and on its unsheltered top he spent the remaining 36 years of his life. The space was only about three feet in diameter, so that he could stand, kneel or sit, but never lie down. From here he would preach to the faithful on feast days and give counsel to all who ascended to him by means of a rope ladder. Some of his letters are still extant.
