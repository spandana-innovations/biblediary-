  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mark 10: 46-52"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      Bartimeus, the blind beggar, persistently cries to Jesus for mercy, calling him “Son of David”, that is, the Messiah king. Every disciple of Jesus must be conscious of his inability to become God’s child except through the power of Jesus. Only Jesus can provide the divine light. Bartimeus received not only eye-sight but also insight and salvation from Jesus. We, the followers of Jesus must ask Jesus every day to help us to see the deep meaning of the teaching, life, death and resurrection of Jesus. He always hears our prayer and responds to our needs. Bartimeus has full trust in the gracious mercy of Jesus and his power to heal. He understood Jesus much better than people who had eye-sight and great learning. He becomes a model follower of Jesus. The incident invites us to join Jesus on our own way of the cross.



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



       Jesus was on his way to celebrate the Passover in Jerusalem along with a band of pilgrims. It was customary for rabbis to teach the pilgrims on the way. The pilgrims were generous in giving alms to the beggars on the roadside. Bartimaeus was one of the blind beggars lined up on the road begging for alms. When he heard people speaking about the approach of Jesus, the miracle worker from Nazareth, he started shouting for mercy. He believed that Jesus was the Messiah, the son of David who could give him eyesight, as foretold by the prophet Isaiah, “*The eyes of the blind will see”(Is 29:18).*Bartimaeus encounters Jesus: People walking in front of Jesus listening to his teaching tried, in vain, to silence Bartimaeus, but finally he got the attention of Jesus. Jesus was impressed by Bartimaeus’ declaration of Faith in the healing power of the Messiah, and asked him what he wanted. Bartimaeus replied, “*Master, let me receive my sight*.” Jesus said to him, *“Go your way; your Faith has made you well.”* Immediately he received his sight, and he promptly followed Jesus to Jerusalem as His disciple.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages: </span>1) We are very often blinded by our pride and prejudice and our innate tendency to be judgmental. Hence, we are badly in need of healing from our spiritual blindness. But we require trusting Faith in the power and mercy of God and the humility to pray persistently, as Bartimaeus did, to receive healing from God. We must have the Faith to sense the presence of Jesus the healer within us and pray to Holy Spirit for the anointing to ask for healing. Let us pray for this grace during the Mass today.

      Jesus encounters Bartimaeus, a blind beggar who calls out, “Son of David, have mercy on me!” Despite others trying to silence him, Bartimaeus persists, expressing his faith in Jesus’ power to heal. Jesus hears his cry, stops, and asks, “What do you want me to do for you?” Bartimaeus’ simple, honest, and only reply was, “Master, I want to see.” It reveals both his trust in Jesus and his deep desire for healing. Jesus acknowledges his faith, saying, “Go, your faith has healed you,” and immediately, Bartimaeus receives his sight and follows Jesus on the way. This story highlights the transformative power of faith and the importance of persistent prayer. Bartimaeus’ courage to seek Jesus despite obstacles inspires us to approach God with our own needs and trust His compassion. It reminds us that Jesus always hears us, responds to our deepest longings, and invites us to follow Him closely.

    ref: "Saint Germanus"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Germanus (c. 496 – 28 May 576) was the bishop of Paris and is venerated as a saint in both the Catholic Church and the Eastern Orthodox Church. According to an early biography, he was known as Germain d'Autun, rendered in modern times as the "Father of the Poor".



      Germanus was born near Autun in what is now France, under Burgundian control 20 years after the collapse of the Western Roman Empire, to noble Gallo-Roman parents. Germain studied at Avallon in Burgundy and at Luzy under the guidance of his cousin Scallion, who was a priest.



      At the age of 35, he was ordained by Agrippinus of Autun and became abbot of the nearby Abbey of St. Symphorian. He was known for his hardworking and austere nature; however, it was his generous alms-giving which caused his monks to fear that one day he would give away all the wealth of the abbey, resulting in their rebellion against him. While in Paris in 555, Sibelius, the bishop of Paris, died, and King Childebert had him consecrated as the bishop of Paris.



      Under Germain's influence, Childebert is said to have led a reformed life. In his new role, the bishop continued to practice the virtues and austerities of his monastic life, working to diminish the suffering caused by the incessant wars. He attended the Third and Fourth Councils of Paris (557, 573) and also the Second Council of Tours (566). He persuaded the king to stamp out the pagan practices existing in Gaul and to forbid the excess that accompanied the celebration of most Christian festivals. Germanus died in 576 AD.
