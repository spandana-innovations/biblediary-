  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 14:15–24"
    audio: null
    body: |
      “A man was giving a large dinner and he invited many. At dinner time he sent his servant to say to those invited: Come along, everything is made ready now.”



      Although these words of our Lord in the parable refer mainly to the call of the chosen people to the reign of God and to the Gentiles’ call as well, when many of the Israelites refused the invitation, we can apply it to ourselves, too.



      We, too, have been invited by the Lord Jesus to a banquet, to partake in an intimate friendship with Him. Right at this moment He is calling us to an encounter, a dialogue with Him in prayer. Have we taken this invitation seriously?



      Some of those invited in the Gospel parable made excuses: “I have bought some land and must go to see it.” “I bought oxen and I have to try them out.” “I’m newly married – goodness, don’t expect me to come”. The master was not very pleased with these excuses and immediately invited others instead.



      Do we regularly accept the Lord’s invitation to prayer? Or do we find excuses, and put off our quiet time with the Lord until time runs out? If we come to prayer, are we too burdened with worldly cares and concerns which shut out the Lord, keep Him from speaking to us, from feeding us with His word?



      There is no way our personal relationship of love, of friendship with Jesus can grow unless we accept His invitation to spend daily time with Him alone.



      No one is denied this banquet with the Lord. It is simply up to us to come, to put aside our worries and material interests for a short time daily, or put them in His hands, and let Him speak to us, love us, hear us, make us His own.



      Lord, we thank You for your call to friendship with You in prayer. Speak, Lord.

      We are here to listen. Speak Your Word and make us whole.






      *(Bernard C. Mischke OSC and Fritz Mischke OSC, Pray Today’s Gospel, ST PAULS, Mumbai, India. Page: 296)*

      The parable of the great banquet explains to us the mind of God. The Kingdom of God is open to all. God’s all-embracing love is like a great banquet, inviting all, but some refuse to enjoy it. God’s love was expressed in Jesus, but the world couldn’t recognize the importance of God’s invitation through him. Saint Paul asks us to put on the same mind that was in Christ Jesus. The mind of Jesus was to empty himself, denying all his privileges and glory for the sake of his mission entrusted by his beloved father. Jesus knew the heart of God and His will and he knew all that belongs to God is his own too. God trusts each one of us in the same way and expects us to bring back the straying children for His great banquet. While walking in the footsteps of Jesus and rendering our humble service in his mission, let us rise above worldly cravings and seek life’s nobler and divine values.

    audio: null
    body: |
      Bertilla was Born into one of the most illustrious families in the territory of Soissons (France) during the reign of Dagobert I, Bertilla grew up witnessing the many ills of the world at large, its deceptions and follies and earnestly sought to renounce it. Not daring to inform her parents about her decision she consulted St Ouen, the Bishop of Rouen, who encouraged her in her resolve and advised her to pray for perseverance.

      In time, when her parents were informed of her desire, God obviously inclined them not to thwart or oppose it. They sent her to Jouarre, a monastery near Meaux, founded not long before under the Rule of St Columban. Received there with great joy, she was trained up in the strictest practices of monastic perfection; by her humility and perfect submission to all, she seemed everyone’s friend in need and acquitted herself with such great charity and edification that she was soon chosen prioress to assist the abbess in her administration.

      The fame of this saint and her disciplinary acts in this community attracted a number of foreign vocations; it is here that the widowed Queen Bathildis retired, taking the habit from her hands and living a life of humility and submission, as also did another Queen, Hereswitha, widow of King Ethelhere of East Angels. But the holy abbess Bertilla seemed to all the most humble even among these humble Queens.

      Bertilla governed the community with equal vigour and discretion, until the close of her life.
