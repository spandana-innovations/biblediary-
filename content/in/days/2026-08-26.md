  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mathew 23: 27-32"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today’s passage, again taken from chapter 23 of Matthew’s Gospel, gives the seventh and eighth accusations made against the Pharisees on the third day of the original “Holy Week” in Jerusalem, as Jesus addressed them in the Temple precincts. Jesus called them hypocrites seven times told them plainly that they were whitewashed tombs containing rotten stuff inside.</span></span></span>



      Hypocrisy exposed: Jesus compared the scribes and Pharisees to the tombs on the sides of the road leading to Jerusalem. In preparation for the three major Jewish feasts, the Scribes and Pharisees used to have these tombs whitewashed, so that the pilgrims would not be ritually defiled by unknowingly walking over one. In this seventh charge, Jesus accused the Pharisees of moral filth, of hiding injustice and immorality inside themselves and covering the corruption with “whitewash” — the pretences of piety and religious fervour. In his eighth and final indictment, Jesus also criticized their false zeal in decorating the old monuments and rebuilding new monuments for the past prophets who had been persecuted and murdered by the forefathers of the Pharisees because these modern Pharisees had neither learned from nor been changed by the messages of the now-dead prophets.</span></span></span>



      Life message: 1) We need to be men and women of integrity, sincerity, and good character originating from our Christian Faith and convictions without any element of hypocrisy in our Christian life. We should not make a show of holiness and religious fervor when we are not internally holy. Here is St. John Chrysostom’s (4th century) comment on the matter: “*You have been counted worthy to become temples of God. But you have instead suddenly become more like sepulchres, having the same sort of foul smell. This is dreadful. It is extreme wretchedness that one in whom Christ dwells and in whom the Holy Spirit has worked such great works should turn out to be a sepulchre, a place for death, carrying a dead soul – a soul deadened by sins, a soul paralyzed – in a living body!”*</span></span></span>

    body: |
      Jesus confronts the religious leaders, calling them “whitewashed tombs”. This powerful imagery exposes their outward appearance of righteousness that conceals inner corruption. Jesus highlights the danger of hypocrisy, where external piety masks spiritual decay. His words serve as a warning to us, urging us to examine our own lives: Are we merely presenting a façade, or are we authentically living our faith? The passage invites reflection on the need for genuine repentance and transformation. By acknowledging our shortcomings, we open ourselves to God’s grace, fostering true spiritual growth. Furthermore, it challenges us to embody humility and integrity, encouraging a faith that reflects inner truth rather than mere outward observance. In doing so, we align ourselves more closely with the heart of Christ.</span></span></span>
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "St Zephyrinus, Pope, Martyr(-217)"
    ref: "St Zephyrinus, Pope, Martyr(-217)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      St Zephyrinus became the XVth Pope in 199 on the death of St Victor I. Three years later Emperor Septimius Severus, whose mother, a Christian, had at first inclined him to be tolerant of the faith, issued his edict of persecution, prohibiting all conversions to Christianity and Judaism under the severest penalties. </span></span></span></span>



      Pope Zephyrinus entrusted to his Archdeacon St Callistus I, the administration of the burial ground which the Church had acquired on the Appian Way (its first landed property), and this has ever since been known as the Catacombs of St Callistus. The narrow subterranean galleries of the Roman catacombs, lined on both sides with tiers of recessed tombs, were gradually extended to greater depths and to distances of many miles, so that they eventually became a maze way. The entrances were carefully concealed in vineyards or disused sandpits. As recently as 1849 a Papal crypt was discovered in these Catacombs which had served for the interment of the Popes.</span></span></span></span>



      Zephyrinus had to contend with a number of heretical sects, and it was during his reign that Tertullian went over to the Montanists, who were then very active in Rome. This Pope also abolished the use of wooden chalices, decreeing that these be replaced with glass ones if metal was too expensive. He also required all the faithful to receive Holy Communion at Easter. </span></span></span></span>



      Zephyrinus won the crown of martyrdom on 26 August 217. His relics are enshrined in San Silvestro at Rome, the church for English Catholics in the Eternal City.</span></span></span></span>
