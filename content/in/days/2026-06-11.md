  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Matthew 10:7-13"
    audio: null
    body: |
      For the Scribes and the Pharisees, the external fulfilment of the precepts of the Mosaic Law was the guarantee of a person’s salvation. In other words, a man saved himself through the external works of the Law. Jesus rejects this view in today’s Gospel passage, taken from the Sermon on the Mount. For Jesus, justification or sanctification is a grace, a free, strengthening gift from God. Man’s role is one of cooperating with that grace by being faithful to it, and using it as God means it to be used. Jesus then outlines new moral standards for his disciples.</span></span></span>



      Control of anger: Anger is the rawest, strongest, and most destructive of human emotions. Describing three stages of anger and the punishment each deserves, Jesus advises his disciples not to get angry in such a way that they sin.</span></span></span>



      1) Anger in the heart (“brief stage of insanity” Cicero): It has two forms: a) a sudden, blazing flame of anger which dies suddenly. b) a surge of anger which boils inside and lingers, so that the heart seeks revenge and refuses to forgive or forget. Jesus prescribes trial and punishment by the Village Court of Elders as its punishment.</span></span></span>



      2) Anger in speech: The use of words which are insulting (“*raka*“=“fool”), or damaging to the reputation (“*moros*” = a person of loose morals). Jesus says that such an angry (verbally abusive) person should be sent to the Sanhedrin, the Jewish Religion’s Supreme Court, for trial and punishment.</span></span></span>



      3) Anger in action: Sudden outbursts of uncontrollable anger, which often result in physical assault or abuse. Jesus says that such anger deserves hellfire as its punishment.</span></span></span>



      In short, Jesus teaches that long-lasting anger is bad, contemptuous speech or destroying someone’s reputation is worse, and harming another physically is the worst.</span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life messages: </span>1)Let us try to forgive,forget, and move toward reconciliation as soon as possible. St. Paul advises us “*Be angry* (righteous anger), *but do not sin”* (Eph 4:26). 2) When we keep anger in our mind, we are inviting physical illnesses like hypertension, and mental illnesses like depression. 3) Let us relax and keep silence when we are angry, pray for God’s strength for self-control, and ask Him for the grace, first to desire to forgive, and then actually to forgive, those who have injured us.</span></span></span>

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      St Barnabas was the companion of St Paul and it was he who introduced Paul to the other Apostles. The meaning of his name is ‘son of encouragement’. He encouraged the early Christians first by selling his land and contributing to the spread of the Good News. Secondly, he encouraged the faith of the Church by helping the Apostles in teaching the people and serving them. The Gospel of today reminds us of the missionary command of Jesus: “As you go, proclaim this message: ‘The Kingdom of Heaven has come near’’’ (v.7). On the one hand, when we seek God’s plan for our life, and work for His purposes and glory, we proclaim the kingdom of God which has come near. On the other hand, the kingdom of God becomes present in the actions which Jesus recommends: healing the sick, raising the dead, cleansing the lepers, and driving out demons. Whenever we reach out to others in service, we bear witness to God’s kingdom coming near. How would you announce the Kingdom in our midst today?</span></span></span>
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "St Barnabas (Apostle and Martyr) (c.1- 60)"
    ref: "St Barnabas (Apostle and Martyr) (c.1- 60)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Barnabas, named Joseph at his birth on the island of Cyprus, settled in Jerusalem and is thought to have studied in the school of Gamaliel at the same time as Saul. Converting to Christianity after Pentecost, he sold all his possessions for the benefit of the Church, earning the surname *Barnabas*, meaning “son of consolation” or “encouragement”. It was he who welcomed the converted Saul to Jerusalem and sponsored him before the Apostles who at first had found it difficult to believe that their fiercest persecutor had become an ardent propagator of the faith. However, from his previous acquaintance with Saul, Barnabas knew Saul to be utterly sincere in his convictions. To both of these great men, the Church, before long, accorded the title of “Apostle”.</span></span></span></span>



      Barnabas readily overcame his Jewish prejudices and rejoiced at the first manifestation of God’s grace among the Gentiles, and together with St Paul, whom he brought from Tarsus, spent an entire year preaching the gospel at Antioch. </span></span></span></span>



      In 45, when Jerusalem was grievously afflicted by famine, the two apostles brought assistance from the Antioch </span></span>Christians to the Mother Church. After their return they set out on their first great missionary journey, taking with them John Mark</span></span>, a cousin of Barnabas, and began with the evangelization of the latter’s native island, Cyprus, followed by conversions and the establishment of churches with a native clergy in Asia Minor.</span></span></span></span>



      Believed to have been stoned to death before the year 61, when John Mark was sent to assist Paul in Rome, Barnabas’ remains were discovered near Salamis on Cyprus in 482, with a copy of Matthew’s Gospel in Hebrew entombed with him. </span></span></span></span>
