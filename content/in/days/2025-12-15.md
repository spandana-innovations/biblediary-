    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      The chief priests and teachers of the Law question the authority of Jesus. In response, he also asks them a question which they fail to answer; their refusal to answer was a clever way to escape from taking responsibility. They try to trap Jesus, but find themselves trapped. Their attitude to the ministry of John the Baptist and of Jesus is harsh and not open to the truth. The popularity of Jesus disturbs them and they considered him as a threat. They close their hearts to goodness. What is our attitude to the truth? Are we humble and sincere enough to benefit from the teachings of Jesus?



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      After casting out the animal-merchants and moneychangers from the Temple immediately after the Palm Sunday procession, Jesus started teaching in the Temple courts. Hence, the chief priests and elders of the people approached Jesus, questioning his authority to enter the city in a triumphal procession, allowing the children to acclaim him, curing the sick, casting out merchants and moneychangers and teaching in the Temple area. It was a trap. If Jesus claimed Divine authority, as the Messiah, they would bring a charge of blasphemy. Jesus could not claim only human authority without denying His very Being as Son of God as well as Son of Man. But even if He could, His arrest as a mad zealot would give scandal, another sin, and would damage the simple Faith of the people in the Temple and what it stood for, destroying Jesus’ whole Messianic Mission. So, Jesus refused to answer, unless they would first answer His challenging counter-question about John the Baptist and his message – was this from God or man? Was this Divine or human? If they answered Divine, the questioners would be asked to explain why they had not accepted John’s message and his witness-bearing that Jesus was the Messiah. If they answered human, they would have to face the anger of the crowd who had accepted John as a prophet. Hence, they kept silent, opting for a shameful self-humiliation.



      <span class="subhead">Life message:</span> In religious matters we should not ask the question whether our stand is safe, politically correct, or useful. Instead, we need to stand for truth with the courage of our Christian convictions even if it costs our life.

      When the heart is closed to God’s spirit, the focus dwells only on external and peripheral factors. The Gospel presents a critically harsh and uncharitably closed attitude of the leaders towards the person and ministry of Jesus. The impact of Jesus was also on the rise, and this greatly disturbed the religious leaders. In Jesus, they perceived a threat to their attitude of subjugating people to their yoke rather than to the service of God. Their hearts had become clogged to examine their maligned conscience. Is such an attitude prevalent among us? We are called to root out all pessimistic mindsets; focus instead on growth in holiness and humility! Let’s never forget: ‘Closed hearts miss the melody of God’s goodness amidst life’s noise.’

    audio: null
    body: |
      Born on 6 November 1813 in Brescia, Italy, the sixth of nine children of a wealthy landowner, Paolina Francesca di Rosa lost her mother in 1824. Educated by the Visitation Sisters she took charge of her father’s household, manifesting a skill for organizing. Inspired by her father’s active Catholicism, she started a variety of charitable works; encouraged by her spiritual director, Monsignor Faustino Pinzoni, Archpriest of the cathedral in Brescia, she, with Gabriella Bornati, heroically cared for the victims of cholera in 1836.



      Due to her efforts, Brescia opened a school for the hearing-impaired and a home for wayward girls, passed laws to help indigent women, and initiated a programme of social work. Basic to her spirituality was the imitation of Christ’s life, which led her to infused contemplation on his crucifixion whereby she saw the mitigation of the suffering members of his Mystical Body as her mission. Taking the name Maria Crocifissa di Rosa in 1842, she founded the Handmaids of Charity, whose chief apostolate of caring for the poor, the sick and the suffering soon attracted several others. This angel of mercy was on hand with her sisters to nurse the wounded and dying on the battlefield of Valeggio in northern Italy in 1849 and staff a military hospital from which they boldly turned back a troop of unruly soldiers who tried to break in.



      Backed by Papal approval in 1850, her congregation soon spread rapidly in Spalato and Verona. On her death in 1855 in Brescia, crowds flocked to her funeral, fondly remembering her as a friend and succour of the sick, the dying and those in distress. Beatified in 1940 she was canonized in 1954.
