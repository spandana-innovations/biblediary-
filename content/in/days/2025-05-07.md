    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      Jesus continues his Eucharistic discourse. Not all who see Jesus believe in him, but those whom he Father has chosen and given to him will come to him in faith and obedience, and he will never reject them. He has come to do the Father’s will, that is, to save all whom the Father has given him. The Father has willed that everyone who looks at the Son with faith should have eternal life, and the Son will raise him up on the last day to eternal life. It is not enough to receive the Eucharistic Bread, but one must do it worthily, with faith and proper dispositions. Jesus feeds us with his Body and Blood so that we may have the strength to make our journey to heaven.



      *— Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      In today’s Gospel passage, Jesus repeats his claim that he is “the Bread of Life.” He means that, just as God sent manna from heaven to sustain the physical life of his people in the desert, so He has sent His Son Jesus to sustain the spiritual lives of His people. Spiritual life is actually Sanctifying Grace, our living relationship with God the Father, through His Son Jesus and the Holy Spirit.



      Jesus makes three claims:



      1) He claims to be our spiritual Food and offers himself in order to produce God’s life within us.



      2) He promises to those who believe in him unbroken friendship with God.



      3) Jesus also promises to those who believe in him a share in his own Resurrection at the end of this world and share of Eternal Life with him in Heaven.



      Life messages: 1) We need to live dynamic spiritual lives, sharing in God’s Life, Sanctifying Grace, through the Holy Eucharist. 2) We can keep the friendship of Jesus only by leading holy lives free from sin. 3) We can enjoy and share the joy of Jesus’ Resurrection only by realizing and appreciating his presence within us and all around us. 4) Only God can satisfy our deepest needs.

      In his Bread of Life discourse, Jesus presents partaking of the Eucharist as the only way to eternal life. The secret to eternal life is to believe in Jesus present in the Eucharist and to receive him worthily. This Eucharistic Lord is present with us, as he promised, on so many altars all over the world although he is so often offended and profaned by human beings. Just as Jesus fed the people in the desert so that they may not collapse on their way home, so too he wants to feed us with his body and blood so that we may travel securely toward heaven. The challenge before us is to believe in this powerful real presence and to be constantly and worthily in communion with him.
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "St John of Beverley, Bishop, Confessor (-721)"
    ref: "St John of Beverley, Bishop, Confessor (-721)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      A native of Yorkshire (England) St John was in his early years educated at Canterbury under the care of Archbishop St Theodore and Abbot St Adrian. Later he joined the Benedictines at Whitby, under the authority of St Hilda. In 687 he was consecrated Bishop of Hexham and in 705 made Archbishop of York. This he governed for 15 years, then resigned and consecrated St Wilfrid the Younger to succeed him. Sensing his death to be near, he retired to Beverley monastery which he had founded and richly endowed, and died there on 7 May 721.



      The cultivation of kindness being at the very heart of holiness and thus the surest sign of closeness to Christ, John went out of his way to be kind to all, generously providing for the poor and the unfortunate. A gifted preacher, he was particularly famed for the careful attention he paid to the training of the younger clergy. Not surprisingly then, one of his most notable pupils was the Venerable Saint Bede whom he ordained a Priest in 702.



      St John’s shrine at Beverley was a great pilgrimage centre in medieval times.
