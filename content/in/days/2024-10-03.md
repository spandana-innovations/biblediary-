  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 10:1–12"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Luke reports Jesus sending out seventy (or in some manuscripts, seventy-two) missionaries, urging them to pray to the Lord of the harvest to send out additional helpers to gather the harvest. This urgent mission is not limited to the twelve apostles. Sharing the Lord’s ministry is not the exclusive privelege of a minority, clerical group; it needs many, many helpers. Because the harvest is so great and varied, ultimately the whole human race to be evangelised, the more labourers the better. This is a vital message for our church today. More and more people need to take responsibility for sharing the spirit of Jesus, for doing the work of the Lord.</span></span></span></span></span>





      When sending them out Jesus warns of the difficulties they will face. Some towns will not make them feel welcome. But whether people make them welcome or not the disciples should share something of his message: “The kingdom of God is very near to you.” The Lord is near whether welcomed or not. He continues to work in and through those who want to play their part, whether appreciated or not. We are challenged to do at least *something* to promote God’s compassion and justice in our world.</span></span></span></span></span>

  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Two Ewalds Martyrs ( - 695)"
    ref: "Two Ewalds Martyrs ( - 695)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      The Ewalds were Anglo-Saxon brothers from Northumbria and both were priests. Both of them being equally fervent and zealous, they could be differentiated largely by the colour of their hair—one was called Black Ewald, and the other, White Ewald! </span></span></span>



      Having specialized in sacred learning and in eloquence in Ireland, they went over to the country of old Saxony in Westphalia in 604 to preach the Gospel, after the example of St Willibrord who opened the spiritual harvest in Friesland. There they were met by a certain official, whom they desired to conduct them to his lord, because they had tidings for his advantage. The man invited them into his house and kept them there for several days. The missionaries passed the time in prayer, singing psalms and hymns, and daily celebration of the Mass.</span></span></span>



      But the local pagans, observing this, feared that the preachers might prevail on their chief to forsake their gods and religion, and thus resolved to murder them both. They felled White Ewald with one blow of the sword and tortured Black Ewald before slaying him before throwing their bodies in the Rhine. But when the Lord of the territory heard of what had happened, he was furious and had the murderers put to death and their villages burnt for their failure to report the two strangers to him and for having taken the law into their own hands.</span></span></span>



      Honoured as martyrs, their mortal remains were enshrined in the church of St Clement [now renamed St Cunibert] in the time of Pepin.</span></span></span>
