  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mathew 1: 18-25"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today’s Gospel focuses on the story of the Virgin Birth, which is at the heart of our Christmas celebrations. It focuses also on the person and role of St. Joseph. In today’s Gospel, Matthew sees in the passage from Isaiah one of the most descriptive and definite prophecies foretelling that the future Messianic King, Christ, will be born as a descendant of David. In order for Jesus to fulfil this promise, Joseph had to, and willingly did, accept and name Jesus as his son, making Jesus a descendant of David because Joseph was a descendant of David. Matthew makes it clear that Jesus was not the biological child of Joseph. But because Joseph was the husband of Mary at the time Jesus was born, Jesus was legally the son of Joseph and, thus, a descendant of David in his royal line. Luke tells us of Mary’s obedience (Lk 1:38), and Matthew shows us Joseph’s obedience. Luke tells the story of the angel’s appearance to Mary (Lk 1:26-38), but Matthew tells us only that the child was from the Holy Spirit.</span></span></span></span>



      God’s message through His angel:</span></span> This is the first of four*occasions on which an angel appears to Joseph in a dream. The angel commands Joseph to take Mary as his wife. Mary’s role is to bear a son, and Joseph’s role is to name him. By naming him, Joseph makes Jesus his son and brings him into the House of David. Joseph’s hallmark is obedience — prompt, simple, unspectacular obedience. Joseph’s obedience allows Jesus to be adopted as a true Son of David; it is Mary’s free consent to the will of God that allows Jesus to be born Son of God. In the end, Joseph takes Mary as his wife, in spite of his fears, and he claims her son as his own by naming him. In spite of his earlier decision to divorce this woman quietly, Joseph nurtured and protected and watched over and loved both Mary and her child.*[The other three angelic vision-encounters are: 2) the message to take Mary and Jesus to Egypt and stay there, until 3) the angel comes to tell them to come home again, and then 4) to settle in Galilee instead of Bethlehem or Jerusalem.]</span></span></span></span>



      Life messages:</span></span> 1) Like Joseph, we need to trust in God, listen to Him, and be faithful. Like Joseph and Mary, we are called to be faithful, to trust in God as we do His will. Let us talk to Him and listen to Him speaking through the Bible. 2) Let us try to imitate Joseph and Mary, the humblest of the humble, the kindliest of the kindly, and the greatest-ever believers in God’s goodness and mercy, and welcome Jesus into our hearts and lives not only at Christmas but all year long. </span></span></span></span>

    body: |
      Today’s Gospel reading focuses on the beginning of Joseph’s role in the life of Jesus, and he is called a ‘righteous’ person. The first reading from the Book of Jeremiah also foretells that the coming ruler of the universe would be ‘The Lord of Righteousness’. Thus, we see Jeremiah attributing ‘righteousness’ to the Messiah and the gospel reading calling Joseph as ‘righteous.’ ‘Righteousness’ in the biblical sense means ‘living in the right relationship with God’ or ‘remaining in the covenantal relationship with God’. Joseph was righteous in this sense and not merely a perfect follower of the Jewish law. He sought the will of God in all that he did, even while he was ‘sleeping’. Thus, the mystery of the Son of God was revealed to him, and he was encouraged to cooperate with God’s plan. Would we too always seek God’s will in all that we do?</span></span></span>
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Winebald - Abbot, Confessor (702 -761)"
    ref: "Saint Winebald - Abbot, Confessor (702 -761)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Saint Winebald, his brother Saint Willibald and their sister, Saint Walburga were children of King Saint Richard and their mother was related to Saint Boniface, the Apostle of Germany. When, at 20, Winebald and Willibald were accompanying their father on a pilgrimage to Rome, the latter died on the way at Lucca. On reaching Rome, Willibald proceeded on a pilgrimage to the Holy Land, Winebald staying back to devote himself to study and to divine service.</span></span></span>



      Seven years later he returned to his native England, whence, having found candidates to the religious life, proceeded to Rome and dedicated himself to God in the religious state. In 739 he was enlisted by Saint Boniface to aid in the evangelization of Germany. He came to Thuringia, was ordained priest in 740, and later became Abbot of the double Benedictine monastery of Heidenheim, which he and his sister Walburga founded, the latter becoming Abbess over the nuns.</span></span></span>



      Saint Winebald had escaped numerous attempts on his life. But, of delicate constitution since childhood, it was the many years of sickness he endured that hastened his death. On his return from a visit to the Shrine of Saint Boniface at Fulda, after a tender exhortation to his monks, he passed away on 18 December 761 in the warm embrace of his brother and sister.</span></span></span>
