  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "John 20:11–18"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      Mary Magdalene, in her deep devotion to the Lord, does not leave the tomb of Jesus as Peter and John do. Therefore, she receives a very special blessing: the Risen Lord met her there itself. He sent her to the brothers to tell them that he was ascending to his Father and their Father. Jesus has a deep personal relationship with those who love and follow him. He meets them personally and changes their lives. All those who are thus privileged have the responsibility to communicate the message of Jesus to others; they are his ambassadors.



      *— Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel presents the great recognition scene in the New Testament in which Mary Magdalene, at the tomb early in the morning, was not able to recognize the Risen Jesus until Jesus called her by name. Gradual recognition, or misunderstanding, as a stage on the path to belief and understanding, frequently occurs in the narratives of John’s Gospel. [See, for example, the conversations Jesus had with Nicodemus (ch. 3), and the Samaritan woman (ch. 4).] In today’s passage, we find it once again: Mary thought at first that Jesus was the gardener.



      Mary Magdalene failed to recognize Jesus because of her false assumption that his body had been stolen. Her attention was concentrated on the empty tomb. Her tears of intense grief could also have blurred her vision. Once Mary had recognized Jesus, he gave her a message to be conveyed to his Apostles about His plan to leave them and ascend to his Father. She was the first to see the Risen Christ and the first to tell the apostles about the Lord’s resurrection. Mary’s message to Jesus’ disciples, “*I have seen the Lord,” (Jn 20: 18),”*became the basis and essence of later preaching and Christians’ witness-bearing. St Thomas Aquinas said that one old lady (*una vetera*), might have more Faith than a host of learned theologians.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages: </span>



      1) Just like Mary Magdalene, we too may fail to recognize the presence of the risen Lord in our neighbour because of our preoccupations, spiritual blindness, and evil habits. But we can be open to experience the presence of the Risen Lord in our lives through our prayer, our Sacramental life, and our meditative reading of the Bible. These all enable us to bear witness to the Risen Lord in our daily lives.



      2) It is our powerful conviction of the Real Presence of the Risen Lord, both in the Eucharist and in our lives, which gives us the strength to fight temptations and to serve our brothers and sisters in corporal and spiritual works of mercy.

    title: Reflection

    body: |
      When Mary Magdalene initially encounters Jesus at the tomb, she doesn’t recognize him. However, when Jesus calls her by name, her recognition is immediate. This moment underscores the deeply personal nature of Jesus’ relationship with His followers. He knows each of them intimately and calls them by name. This recognition speaks of the idea that faith is not a generic, one-size-fits-all experience but a deeply personal and individualized connection with Christ. Today’s gospel passage offers a powerful reflection on the human experience of grief, the recognition of the risen Christ through a personal encounter, and the transformational impact of encountering the Living Lord. It reminds us of the personal and intimate nature of our faith in Jesus and the call to share our testimonies with others, spreading the message of hope, joy, and transformation that comes through Him.
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Soter - Pope, Martyr (-175)"
    ref: "Saint Soter - Pope, Martyr (-175)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Saint Soter was a native of Fondi near Gaeta in Italy. It was upon the death of Saint Anicetus in 166, that Soter was raised to the Papacy thus becoming the 12th in the succession of Popes. One of the rising heretical sects which this Pope had to combat during his 9-year reign was that of Montanus, a Phrygian, who taught that certain sins could never be forgiven and claimed that he was God’s messenger for a new outpouring of the Holy Spirit. To Saint Soter is also attributed a precept that all the faithful, excepting only such as were guilty of a notable crime, should receive Holy Communion on Maundy Thursday.



      A letter which the Pope sent to the persecuted Church at Corinth has unfortunately been lost, but an extant fragment of the reverent reply of Saint Dionysius, the Bishop of Corinth, is interesting for its reference to “the abundance which the Pope has sent to the saints, and the consolation which he has offered with words of comfort to the brethren who came to him, as a loving father would to his children.” “Today, the Lord’s day, we have read your letter and we will always wish to re-read it and to be admonished, the same as by the earlier letter which was sent to us by Clement.”



      Saint Soter is believed to have suffered martyrdom in 175 under Marcus Aurelius.
