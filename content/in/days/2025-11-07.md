  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 16:1–8"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      Jesus narrates the parable of the dishonest manager to instruct us that the material possessions that we manage are not our own but entrusted to us by God. We have to give Him an account of how we use them. The manager in the parable is crafty and dishonest; he uses his master’s property to ensure his own earthly security. Unlike him, we should use whatever wealth is given to us in such a way as to be faithful to the Master’s purpose and to ensure an eternal reward. We must make the best use of the resources and opportunities given to us, with the eternal reward in mind. </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      In today’s Gospel, Jesus tells us the strange parable of a steward who was a rascal to teach us that serving God is a full-time job, not a part-time job or a spare-time hobby. Jesus also teaches us that, in matters spiritual and eternal, we should use the same ingenuity and planning which business people show in the business world. The parable challenges us to use our blessings — time, talents, health, and wealth — wisely and shrewdly, so that they will count for our reward in eternity. We are on the right road only if we use our earthly wealth to attain our Heavenly goal. The parable: In the parable, Jesus tells us how the slave-steward of an absentee landlord, caught red-handed in misappropriating his master’s wealth, ingeniously cheated his master by his unjust manipulation of the master’s business clients. His tricks were intended to make him the friend of his master’s debtors and gave him the prospect of becoming rich by working for them (or blackmailing them?) when he was fired by his master from the stewardship.</span>



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span></span>



      1) We need to be faithful in the little things of life: As Saint John Chrysostom said, “Faithfulness in little things is a big thing.” Our future opportunities in the eternal service of God largely depend on our stewardship in handling the little opportunities we have had on earth. As Mother Teresa used to recommend, “Do little things with great love.”</span>




      2) We have to act shrewdly, trusting in the power and assistance of God. Let us make use of our resources — like Hope in God’s justice, Faith in God’s assistance, and Trust in God’s grace, celebrating the Mass and the Sacraments as sources of Divine grace and prayerfully studying the Holy Bible as the word of God for daily meditation.</span>




      3) Let us remember that as God’s stewards we need to be prepared to give an account of our lives at any time.</span>

    body: |
      Luke 16:1-8 is often referred to as the Parable of the dishonest manager or the unjust steward. In this parable, Jesus tells the story of a manager who was accused of wasting his employer’s resources. Just as the manager was called to account for his actions, we are all called to be accountable for how we use the resources and opportunities we are given. If we prove ourselves faithful in managing small things, we will be entrusted with greater responsibilities. Worldly wealth is temporary, we are called to use it wisely to prepare for our eternal future and not be controlled by the spirit of worldliness. Just as the manager was focused on securing his earthly future, we, as children of God, should be concerned with our eternal destiny. How can we make wise use of our resources today?

    body: |
      Born in Northumbria in 658, St Willibrord was a Benedictine monk who had his early education at the Abbey of Ripon near York. At 20, he went to Ireland and spent 12 years under St Egbert who commissioned him and 11 companions to the mission to Frisia.



      When Willibrord visited Rome in 692 he received the apostolic authorization for his missionary labours. When he visited Rome a second time he was consecrated Archbishop of the Frisians by Pope Sergius I on 21 November 695 and given the name Clement, which means “merciful”.



      He founded a monastery at Utreht (Holland), built a church in the city which he made his cathedral and, in 698 established an abbey at Villa Echternach.



      In 716, Radbod gained possession of Frisia, forcing Willibrord into exile, destroying churches and replacing them with temples dedicated to idols. But soon after his death in 719, Willibrod returned and, with the assistance of St Boniface, undertook the repairs and rebuilding of churches and the work of conversion in Frisia.



      St Willibrord died at Echternach, Luxembourg, on 7 November 739 and was buried at the abbey church which has ever since been a place of pilgrimage.
