  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mark 6:7-13"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Jesus sends His disciples out to preach, teach and heal. He sent the disciples out in pairs. Jesus knew as beginning preachers, they would need support. He also realized that the disciples would be more effective if they had companions in this ministry. Thus, He sent them out in pairs. Jesus instructed them to take nothing with them on their journey – no food, no extra clothing nor money. This meant that His disciples would have to depend on the generosity of others to meet their needs. Were they tempted to carry some food or a change of clothing along with them? Or did they truly trust that God and many generous people would provide for them?

      The disciples left and began preaching. Amazingly, they also were able to work miracles. They drove out demons and cured people who were seriously ill. Imagine what it was like for these disciples to have the ability to perform healings and miracles, to preach and to teach. These men were not learned. They were simple fishermen and workers. Yet, Jesus empowered them to give to heal others and to give them spiritual food.



      *(The Dome Blog, Sr Kristine Anne Harpenau OSB)*
  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      Jesus sends out his disciples for the mission without any material support. He asked them to take nothing for their journey except a staff. He wanted his disciples to know that they were not in charge—God is ultimately in control, and they must learn to place their trust in Him rather than in themselves and in worldly possessions. No one can claim to be self-sufficient. Our close relationship with God fills us with satisfaction and meaning in life. Psalm 55 says, “Cast thy burden upon the LORD, and he shall sustain thee: he shall never suffer the righteous to be moved” (Ps 55:22). The disciples carried out the missionary activities not individually but in pairs. The Lord wants us to work together rather than as individuals - “For where two or three are gathered in my name, there am I among them” (Mt 18:20). Christian life involves giving priority to community, teamwork, and prayer.
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "St Camillus De Lellis Confessor (1550 -1614)"
    ref: "St Camillus De Lellis Confessor (1550 -1614)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Hailing from a distinguished family in the Abruzzi (Italy), Camillus lost both his parents at an early age. His education and spiritual development badly neglected, he took up soldiering and gradually lost all his inheritance through gambling. Incurable abscesses developed on his feet, compelling him to give up military life and seek employment in a hospital; but his quarrelsome disposition and uncontrollable passion for gambling soon got him the sack.

      Fully reformed after several years, at 26 he offered his services to the Hospital for Incurables in Rome where he quickly won everyone’s admiration by his selflessness and was soon made head of the institution. At 32, he took up the study of Latin and, two years later, was ordained priest.

      Camillus continued to be afflicted by his old infirmity throughout 46 years, and this with four other disabilities turned his life into one of continued suffering. But he would disregard it all and, calling his ailments the “five mercies of God”, drag himself from bed to bed to make certain that each patient was receiving due attention.

      Camillus died, invoking the sacred names of Jesus and Mary with touching devotion and was canonized in 1746.
