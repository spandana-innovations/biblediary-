  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 13:22–30"
    audio: null
    body: |
      It was an understandable question in today’s Gospel reading. Will many or few be saved? Behind that question, however, were several assumptions. It assumes the Kingdom was far the Jewish people and the Gentiles would be shut out. It assumed that simply being Jewish was an entry ticket into the Kingdom. It assumed that the people who appear to us to be righteous will be in the Kingdom. Jesus does not answer the numerical question directly but the Lord challenges the assumptions behind it.</span></span></span></span>






      Instead of speculating about how many people are in the state of grace, we should make sure that we are. We would try to enter by the narrow door.</span></span></span></span>






      People from all directions of the globe will come and be seated with the great Old Testament patriarchs in the Kingdom while many of the “chosen” people will be excluded. Simply being a Jew of being Catholic is not enough. We have to live the life that status entails. Otherwise, although we might have eaten in Jesus’ company, we will be strangers to Him and told to have His presence.</span></span></span></span>






      Finally, how people appear to us here may not necessarily reflect the true condition of their soul. Some of the first will be last and some of the last will be first.</span></span></span></span>



      The Lord’s point to us is that instead of speculating about who is faithful and how many are going to heaven, we should make sure that we are faithful not only in our words but in our deeds.</span></span></span></span>






      Jesus said these words on His way to Jerusalem to guide us on our way to heaven.</span></span></span></span>






      *(</span></span>S. Joseph Krempa</span></span>, Captured Fire, New Daily Homilies, Year Two, ST PAULS/Alba House, Page no. 177).</span></span>*</span></span>

      “When you have a choice between the easy and the difficult, choose the difficult,” says Saint Ignatius of Loyola. The world always looks for easy money, short cuts, fast food and almost everything on one click. Jesus invites us to take the road less travelled and enter through a narrow path. It is the narrow path that becomes the door to divine revelation. It is in the struggles of life that we learn life’s important lessons. Success in life may bring happiness but failure in life will definitely bring growth. People thought that because Jesus preached on their streets and ate with them, they would easily get entry into Heaven. Jesus detests their unrighteous behaviour. *What criterion do I use while making a choice between two options?*</span></span></span></span>
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "St Gerard Majella Confessor (1726 -1755)"
    ref: "St Gerard Majella Confessor (1726 -1755)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Extremely devout, Gerard, the son of poor parents in southern Italy, was blessed with a gift of profound contemplation and an ardent love of the Blessed Sacrament. “Suffer only for God, and your very sufferings will bring you heaven on earth!” Admitted by the Redemptorists on probation as a lay brother at 23, Gerard, deeply rooted in humility and purity, proved a model of perfect obedience. To the customary three vows he added one to do always that which appeared to be the most perfect — “Everything that is done for God becomes a prayer!”</span></span></span>



      God granted him remarkable spiritual gifts: ecstasies, levitations, bi-locations, invisibility, prophecy, infused knowledge, discernment of spirits, and even power over the forces of nature and Satan himself. His obedience to his superiors was such that he knew and carried out their wishes even if unspoken or given at a great distance. He subjected his body to severe penances and rigorous fasts. Every spare moment was spent in adoration of the Blessed Sacrament, and at times, when passing the tabernacle, he had to do himself violence: “O Lord, let me go, I beseech thee! There is work that I must do!” When he accompanied the Fathers on their missions, he often converted more hardened sinners by his fervent prayers and winning persuasiveness than the older priests by their sermons. </span></span></span>



      Born on 6 April 1726 at Muzo Lucanzo, Gerard died on 16 October 1755 at Caposele, aged barely 29. Characteristic are the words he wrote on the door of his cell: “Here is done what God wills, as God wills, and for as long as God wills!”</span></span></span>
