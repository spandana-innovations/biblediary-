      <span class="subhead">The context:</span></span></span> Today’s Gospel presents the head of the Holy Family, Joseph, faithfully obeying God’s law given through Moses concerning the purification of the mother and the redeeming of the child by presenting Mary and the Baby Jesus in the Temple. The events recounted are those we traditionally celebrate on February 2nd with the *Feast of Presentation of Jesus.*We celebrate them today in order to group all the events of Christ’s infancy within the Octave of Christmas. Today (and on February 2nd), we celebrate a combined feast, commemorating the Jewish practice of the purification of the mother after childbirth and the presentation of the child in the Temple. It is known as the *Hypanthe* feast or *Feast of the Purification of Mary* (by the offering two pigeons in the Temple); the *Feast of the Presentation of the Lord* (by prayers and, *“five shekels to a member of a priestly family*” (*Nm 18:15-16;* NAB; Note on *Lk 22:22*), to redeem or buy back the firstborn male child from the Lord); and the *Feast of Encounter* (because the New Testament, represented by the Baby Jesus, encountered the Old Testament, represented by Simeon and Anna). On February 2nd we celebrate these events as a formal ending of the Christmas season. On February 2, we also celebrate the*Feast of Candlemas*(because candles are blessed then for liturgical and personal use).</span></span></span></span></span>



      Purification and redemption ceremonies: The Mosaic Law taught that, since every Jewish male child belonged to Yahweh, the parents had to “buy back” the child (“redeem” him), by offering lambs or turtledoves as a sacrifice in the Temple. In addition, (Numbers 18:15), every mother had to be purified after childbirth by prayers and an offering made to God in the Temple. Joseph kept these laws as an act of obedience to God.</span></span></span></span></span>



      The encounter with Simeon and Anna: By the inspiration of the Holy Spirit, the old, pious and Spirit-filled Simeon and Anna had been waiting in the Temple for the revelation of God’s salvation. Simeon recognized Jesus as the Lord’s Anointed One, and in his prayer of blessing, he prophesied that Jesus was meant to be the glory of Israel and a light of revelation to the Gentiles. While he blessed Mary, he warned that her Child was “*was born for the fall and the rising of many in Israel, and for asign that is spoken against … that thoughts out of many hearts may be revealed,*” while *“a sword will pierce through your own soul also” (Lk 2:34-35;*RSV 2 Catholic). Simeon was prophesying both the universal salvation that would be proclaimed by Jesus and the necessity of suffering in the mission of the Messiah.</span></span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life message: </span></span></span>1)Every Holy Mass in which we participate is our presentation. Although we were officially presented to God on the day of our Baptism, we present ourselves and our dear ones on the altar before God our Father through our Saviour Jesus Christ at every Holy Mass. Hence, we need to live our daily lives with the awareness both that we are dedicated people consecrated to God, and that we are obliged to lead holy lives.</span></span></span></span></span>

      Old Simeon had the promise of seeing the Messiah during his lifetime. He is one of the two great personalities of faith presented to us today. Simeon carries baby Jesus in his hands and recognises Him as the Messiah. Perhaps Simeon could not see too well and hear properly because of his old age, yet he is able to look deeper and discover the Saviour of the world in a small child with his inner eyes. Simeon can be a good guide for us to see and recognise the essentially important things. The second personality is Saint Archbishop Thomas Becket, who was murdered in his cathedral in Canterbury during Vesper service on this day in 1170. Seeing deeply and standing firmly are required for an authentic witnessing.</span></span></span>
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Thomas Becket - Bishop, Martyr (1118 -1170)"
    ref: "Saint Thomas Becket - Bishop, Martyr (1118 -1170)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Born in London to a wealthy Normandy merchant, Thomas Becket, well versed both in civil and canon law, became Archdeacon of Canterbury at 37 when the young and ambitious Henry II ascended the English throne and promptly appointed him Chancellor against his will. </span></span></span>



      In 1161, when Theobald, Archbishop of Canterbury, died Thomas was nominated successor, again much against the latter’s wishes. Thomas, genuinely devout and scrupulously moral and on intimate terms with Henry, warned him thus: “I know your plans for the Church and that you will put forth claims which I, as Archbishop, must necessarily oppose.” </span></span></span>



      Thomas’ decision to resign as Chancellor so as to be free of all civil responsibilities, brought into focus his resistance to the “Constitutions of Clarendon” which, purported to be a record of accepted customs in Church matters, included clauses rendering Church law subject to the King’s whims. Of all the bishops, Thomas alone, realizing the implications, resisted and, sensing danger to his life, fled in disguise to France. Received cordially by King Louis VII, he submitted his case to the judgement of Alexander III, Pope-in-exile, at Sens. </span></span></span>



      Four years of negotiations later, reconciliation seemingly achieved, Thomas returned to Canterbury in 1170. But within less than a month, aware of his imminent end, he exclaimed: “To God and to the Blessed Mary, to the Patron Saints of this church, and to Saint Denis I commend myself and the cause of the Faith!” On 29 December, taking cognizance of Henry’s anguished cry, “Will no one rid me of this low-born clerk!” four of his knights slew the Archbishop while at the altar in his own cathedral, effectively stalling the secularization of the Church for several centuries! </span></span></span>



      Thomas was canonized by Pope Alexander III in 1173. In 1174 Henry II, having done penance at the saint’s shrine which had by then become a pilgrimage centre as a result of the miracles taking place, was absolved. </span></span></span>
