      Reflection: 1



      Mary's Magnificat, found in Luke 1:46-56, is a profound hymn of praise and faith. Upon visiting Elizabeth, Mary, filled with the Holy Spirit, proclaims God's greatness and rejoices in Him as her Savior. She acknowledges God's favor upon her lowliness, foreseeing that all generations will call her blessed because the Mighty One has done great things for her. This canticle highlights God's mercy for those who fear Him, His strength in scattering the proud, and His justice in exalting the humble and filling the hungry. It is a song that echoes Israel's prayer, celebrating God's redemptive love and faithfulness to His promises.</span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      The *Magnificat* and Hannah’s song (1400 B.C.) are properly mentioned together, because the former is literarily and thematically dependent on the latter. Mary as a young Jewish girl knew Hannah’s song as it was sung on every Jewish New Year Day in the Temple and the synagogues. Both Hannah and Mary are mothers rejoicing at the birth of an unexpected child. Hannah praises God that He has seen fit to end the curse of her barrenness, while Mary glorifies the Lord because He has chosen her to bear the promised Messiah. Each knew, to her sorrow, that she would have to give up her son one day. Just as Hannah dedicated her child Samuel to the Lord, so Mary offered her son Jesus for our salvation. On hearing Elizabeth’s greetings, Mary sang, praising and thanking God for the great things He had done for her. He had filled her with graces, overshadowed her with His Holy Spirit and made her the mother of His Son Jesus. Mary praised God also for the mercy He had worked by humbling the proud, by ousting the mighty from their thrones, and by exalting the lowly and filling the hungry with good things, a social, political, and economic revolution.</span>

      In the grand canvas of faith, Mother Mary’s life stands as a testament to embracing the enormity of God’s presence. Her Magnificat – a resounding anthem of courage – is a manifesto of unwavering belief in God’s transformative power. The mighty works of the Lord had made Mary declare her courage in God who overpowers the proud and casts away the self-asserting rich. How ‘big and mighty’ is God in our life? Do we trust in the awesome majesty of God even when the boat of our lives hits rock bottom? When we make God big in our lives, we live with daring faith, we face challenges with fearless obedience and we commit ourselves to passionate evangelization. With Mary, let us also proclaim the BIG GOD and make our life a ‘joyful Magnificat!’

    ref: "Saint Frances Xavier Cabrini - Virgin"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Born on 15 July 1850, Frances was the thirteenth child of Augustine and Stella Cabrini, well-to-do Italian peasants. Graduating brilliantly at 18, she devoted herself to nursing the sick poor in her home town. At 24 she was asked by the Vicar of Codogno, Don Antonio Serrati to take charge of a private orphanage named House of Providence, the institute from which emerged her own new congregation, the Missionary Sisters of the Sacred Heart, at the promptings of her Bishop, Domenico Gelmini. As a result of her organizing and administrative skills, her tact and her ability to read character, her congregation grew prodigiously. </span>



      Mother Cabrini was 39 when she set her sights on the Chinese missions, but, in obedience to Pope Leo XIII, proceeded to the USA where the huge number of poor Italian immigrants, largely illiterate and exploited outright by unscrupulous agents, some even losing their faith due to Protestant influences, became her special concern. As she went about opening orphanages, oratories and day schools in the face of every kind of difficulty, Cabrini, a woman of fine sensibilities, true humility and great holiness, taught her Sisters that the welfare of the poor did not depend upon them, but rather on God working through them - “Pray! Pray always, and ask without ceasing for the spirit of prayer:…..Have faith and you will behold miracles!” Repeatedly, she miraculously multiplied dwindling food supplies or cash reserves.</span>



      Mother Cabrini died suddenly on 22 December 1917, aged just 67. She was canonized by Pope Pius XII on 7 July 1946, the first American to be so honoured by the Church.</span>
