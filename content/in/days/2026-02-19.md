  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 9: 22-25"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      The role of Jesus includes rejection, suffering, death and resurrection. It is essential for the followers of Jesus to understand this. No one can follow Christ without the cross, nor is it enough to endure suffering without Christ. To gain eternal life is of greater importance and greater profit than to gain the whole world. If our discipleship does not cost us anything, if it does not involve self-denial, it is of little worth. True discipleship means taking up our own crosses and following the footsteps of Jesus. Whatever a person gives up and is ready lose for the sake of Christ will turn out to be his gain, for thus he ensures his eternal life. </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      After Peter had made his famous declaration of Faith in Jesus as God and the Messiah, Jesus plainly warned his disciples about his suffering death and Resurrection. But the Apostles were unwilling to accept such a fate for their master. Hence, Jesus declared the three conditions of discipleship which he expected from his followers, as given in today’s Gospel.</span></span></span></span></span>



      The Three Conditions:</span></span> 1) Deny yourself. 2) Take up your cross. 3) Follow Me. 1) Denying oneself involves a) cleansing of the heart by the eviction of self and the removal of all evil tendencies and addictions from the heart with the help of the Holy Spirit, b) the enthronement of God in the heart and the dedication of oneself to Him, and c) the surrendering of one’s life to the enthroned God through loving, selfless service of others for God’s glory. 2) Taking up one’s cross means, not only accepting gracefully from God our pains and suffering, but also accepting the pain involved in serving others, in sharing our blessings with them, and in controlling our evil tendencies. Carrying one’s cross becomes easier when we compare our light crosses with the heavier ones given to terminally-ill patients and to exploited people living under subhuman conditions. The realization that Jesus carries with us the heavier part of our cross also makes our cross-bearing easier and more salvific. 3) Follow Me means one is to follow Jesus by obeying the word of God and adjusting one’s life accordingly. One living as Jesus’ disciple should be ever ready to obey as Jesus directs one –through His words in the Bible and through the teaching authority He has instituted in the Church.</span></span></span></span></span>



      The paradox of saving/losing and losing/saving life:</span></span> According to Bible commentators, the word “life” is here used, clearly, in a double sense: the earthly life of man in flesh and time and his eternal Life of happiness in Heaven. Hence, what Jesus means is that whoever wishes to save his (earthly), life will lose his (eternal), Life. But whoever loses his (earthly), life by spending it for Jesus and the Gospel, will save his (eternal), Life.</span></span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life message:</span></span></span> We need to love the cross, wear the cross, carry the crosses we are given, and transform these God-given crosses of our life into the instruments of our salvation by working with the Holy Spirit.</span></span></span></span></span>

      Jesus spells out the cost of discipleship as self-denial, carrying our cross, and faithfully following Him. These costs reveal the heart of Christian discipleship, especially during the season of Lent, when we reflect deeply on the sacrifices Jesus made for us and how we, too, are called to live sacrificially in response. Denying oneself involves daily decisions to love and serve even when it’s inconvenient and placing others before our personal comfort or gain. Taking up our cross means accepting the challenges of life and trusting that even in suffering, God is at work in us. Thus, to follow Jesus is to align our hearts, minds, and actions with His example, even when it leads us down difficult paths. Are we ready to pay the cost of discipleship?</span></span></span>

    ref: "Saint Conrad, Confessor (1290 - 1351)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      A member of one of the first families of Piacenza in northern Italy, St Conrad married young and embarked on a military career. He was passionately fond of the chase and when one day, his quarry escaped into a thicket, he ordered it to be set on fire. But a sudden strong wind caused the flames to spread quickly over all the nearby fields and forests and caused widespread damage. For this a poor woodcutter who happened to be found in the neighborhood, was blamed, tried and condemned to death on circumstantial evidence.</span></span></span></span>



      Upon hearing of this turn of events, St Conrad came forward and acknowledged his own guilt, promising to make amends as far as lay in his power by selling all of his possessions. He and his wife then took up the religious life—she became a Poor Clare and he joined the Third Order of St Francis and lived thereafter as a hermit in a solitary cave near Noto in Sicily.</span></span></span></span>



      Each Friday for the next 30 years, he visited the celebrated Crucifix at Noto, and here he was found dead on his knees on 19 February 1351. St Conrad, whose mortal remains were laid to rest in the Church of St Nicholas in Noto, is invoked in cases of hernia.</span></span></span></span>
