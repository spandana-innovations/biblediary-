      Today, we celebrate the feast of St Andrew, the apostle, one of the twelve who bore witness to the life, death and resurrection of Jesus. This threefold mission is the responsibility of each one of us now and the question is how are we faithful to this mission today. As members of the body of Christ, in whatever state we are, either consecrated or married people, we are given several opportunities to proclaim the Gospel, teach others about Jesus, wherever we are, that could be in our work places, such as, schools, hospitals, churches, industries or IT sectors, in our communities and in our families. What is most urgently expected from all of us is, first and foremost we have to be convinced of this wonderful mission and remain committed not 99% but 100% to work for the Kingdom of God.     ref: null

    body: |
      In today’s gospel we read about the call of the disciples Peter and Andrew, John and James. When Jesus called, they left behind their occupation, their means of livelihood, their families and their security. They understood instantly that following Jesus was a liberating experience and a call to do greater things. Jesus calls people for His work not according to their merits but to make them into what he wants them to be. Every Christian has received the same call to follow Jesus, and to gather people and nations for God. Our vocation in the Christian faith demands of us that we proclaim the Good News and become instrumental in establishing the reign of God in people’s hearts and in gathering them for God, thereby guaranteeing their salvation. The First Reading says that by believing with our heart, we are put right with God, by confessing the faith with our lips we are saved. In other words, all those who believe and proclaim what they believe are saved. Let us share our faith with those around us and thus become real followers of Jesus.

    audio: null
    body: |
      Saint Andrew, whose name in Greek means “manly, courageous”, was the son of Jona and the elder brother of St Peter, and with him plied his fishing trade at Capernaum on the shores of Galilee. Initially a disciple of John the Baptist, when Jesus was pointed out to him as the Messiah, he immediately sought out his brother Peter, and with a sublime faith which needed no preaching and miracles to convince them, both thenceforth became “fishers of men” under Christ’s command.

      It was when Andrew, along with Peter, James and John, sought timely signs concerning the world’s end that the eschatological discourse from the Lord followed. After the Resurrection, Andrew preached the Gospel in western Asia Minor (Turkey) and among the Scythians. He appointed St Stachys as the first Bishop of Byzantium, and then turned his steps westward to Macedonia and Greece.

      Andrew was martyred during the reign of Nero at Patras in 60 AD, crucified on an ‘X-shaped’ cross. His body was brought to Byzantium (Constantinople) and buried in the Church of the Apostles, besides those of Sts Timothy and Luke. In 1210, when the Crusaders captured the city, his relics were taken to Amalfi in southern Italy, where they are revered to this day.

      St Andrew is the Patron of Russia and of Scotland, also of fishermen and of spinsters.
