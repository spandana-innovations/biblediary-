  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 1:39-45"
    audio: null
    body: |
      We are on the last Sunday of Advent, and Christmas is near and the birthday of the Saviour we have been waiting for, is at our door. The Church invites us to give the final touches to our preparation to receive the Lord who is about to come. For a Christian believer, the annunciation of the birth of the Lord Jesus is the most significant moment in history. World history thus gets divided into two – history before Christ and history after Christ. God’s own Son takes flesh and becomes a human being in the womb of a humble woman. God prepares this woman and preserves her as immaculate and full of grace in order to receive and give birth to the Son of God into this world. The woman, Mary, submits herself in total obedience and availability to this plan of God, saying, “Behold, I am the servant of the Lord; let it be to me according to your word.” She expressed her total readiness to do what God wanted from her. God took complete possession of Mary and the Messiah was about to take flesh from her flesh and blood. For nine months he would live in her like in a tabernacle. Thus the incarnation of the Son of God becomes a reality. The Word becomes flesh. God’s plan for the salvation of the world reaches its final stage. Underlying all these is God’s unconditional love for the sinful humanity.

      (Fr Joe Eruppakkatt ssp)

      Mary makes a surprise visit to Elizabeth who never ceased to thank the merciful Lord for his grace upon her and her family. She was able to recognize the Son of God from afar since she was “righteous before God, living blamelessly according to all the commandments and regulations of the Lord.” In front of the Son of God and His Mother, she must have felt so unworthy as later we hear her son say “the one who is more powerful than I is coming; I am not worthy to untie the thong of his sandals.” The humility and the courage to consider the other one, though according to the worldly standards lower than me, is the example Elizabeth set before us. Like Mary all those who come to us carry Jesus with them. Only those who are righteous and blameless before God could recognize the indwelling presence of God in the other.

    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Frances Xavier Cabrini - Virgin (1850 - 1917)"
    ref: "Saint Frances Xavier Cabrini - Virgin (1850 - 1917)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Born on 15 July 1850, Frances was the thirteenth child of Augustine and Stella Cabrini, well-to-do Italian peasants. Graduating brilliantly at 18, she devoted herself to nursing the sick poor in her home town. At 24 she was asked by the Vicar of Codogno, Don Antonio Serrati to take charge of a private orphanage named House of Providence, the institute from which emerged her own new congregation, the Missionary Sisters of the Sacred Heart, at the promptings of her Bishop, Domenico Gelmini. As a result of her organizing and administrative skills, her tact and her ability to read character, her congregation grew prodigiously.

      Mother Cabrini was 39 when she set her sights on the Chinese missions, but, in obedience to Pope Leo XIII, proceeded to the USA where the huge number of poor Italian immigrants, largely illiterate and exploited outright by unscrupulous agents, some even losing their faith due to Protestant influences, became her special concern. As she went about opening orphanages, oratories and day schools in the face of every kind of difficulty, Cabrini, a woman of fine sensibilities, true humility and great holiness, taught her Sisters that the welfare of the poor did not depend upon them, but rather on God working through them - “Pray! Pray always, and ask without ceasing for the spirit of prayer:…..Have faith and you will behold miracles!” Repeatedly, she miraculously multiplied dwindling food supplies or cash reserves.

      Mother Cabrini died suddenly on 22 December 1917, aged just 67. She was canonized by Pope Pius XII on 7 July 1946, the first American to be so honoured by the Church.
