      Reflection: 1



      When time began, Jesus already existed as the Word of God. All things were made through him. Now, with the Incarnation, the Word becomes flesh and Jesus becomes part of creation. This was the purpose of creation, that it should be saved by the Creator becoming part of the created world. Creation and redemption came through Jesus Christ. Divine life and light came to the human race through the life and teachings of Jesus. This light never fails though many do not recognise it. But hose who accept him, he makes children of God. In Jesus, the powerful presence of God has become more concrete, and the Apostles are eye-witnesses of his glory. Jesus is the fulness of God’s loving kindness, and we have received abundance of grace from him. Do we welcome Jesus and the light and truth he brings? Are we eager to become better children of God?



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Bible scholars generally agree that the Prologue (1:1-18) of John’s Gospel is a hymn, the overall purpose of which is to highlight the historical and theological significance of Jesus’ origins as “Word,” “true Light,” and the “Only-begotten Son,” thus tracing Jesus’ genealogy to God Himself. The Navarre Bible commentary summarizes the main teachings in the prologue thus: 1) the Divinity and Eternity of the Word; 2) the Incarnation of the Word and His manifestation as man; 3) the part played by the Word in creation and in the salvation of mankind; 4) the different ways in which people react to the coming of the Lord — some accepting the Word made Flesh with Faith, others rejecting Jesus; 5) finally, John the Baptist as witness-bearer to the presence of the Word in the world.



      The significance of the text: (Verses 6-9) introduce John the Baptist in a manner that clearly distinguishes him from Jesus – *“John himself was not the Light, but he came to testify to the Light. The true Light, which enlightens everyone, was coming into the world…”* Some scholars maintain that the author of the Gospel may be making such a forceful differentiation in order to counter a sect claiming that John the Baptist was the Light and the Messiah, and not simply the one testifying to the Light. In all he did and said, the Baptist always bore witness to Jesus and Jesus’ messianic identity: *“John testified to Him and cried out, saying, ‘The One Who is coming after me ranks ahead of me because He existed before me’” (Jn 1:15).*Jn 1:19-28 is an Advent and Christmas text that calls us to remember the origins and purposes of Jesus and His coming with the kind of devotion that challenges us to be witnesses for Jesus. John the Baptist demonstrates what it means to bear witness to the true Light coming into the world.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span>



      1) We need to bear witness to Christ the Light: By Baptism we become members of the family of Christ, the true Light of the world. Jesus said: “*You are the light of the world.”* Hence, our mission as brothers and sisters of Christ and members of His Mystical Body, the Church, is to reflect Christ’s Light to others, just as the moon reflects the light of the sun.



      2) It is especially important during the Christmas season that we reflect on Christ’s unconditional love and forgiveness. Very many people live in spiritual darkness and poverty and lack real freedom. There are others who are deafened and blinded by the cheap attractions of the world. Still others feel lonely, unwanted, rejected, useless, and marginalized. All these people are waiting for us to reflect the light of Christ and to turn their lives into experiences of joy, wholeness and integrity.

  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Sylvester I - Pope Confessor (-335)"
    ref: "Saint Sylvester I - Pope Confessor (-335)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Saint Sylvester succeeded Saint Miltiades to the See of Peter in the year after Constantine’s Edict of Milan, which ended three centuries of bloody persecution. Roman by birth, he was consecrated the 33rd Pope on 31 January 314. During his 21-year reign the discipline of the Roman Church was reorganized, the first martyrology of Roman Martyrs compiled, and a Roman school of chant established, the insertion of the Kyrie Eleison in the Mass presumably emanating from here.



      It was during his pontificate that Emperor Constantine moved his capital from Rome to Constantinople (330), giving the Pope his palace of the Lateran (where His Holiness set up his cathedra and established the Lateran basilica as the cathedral church of Rome), erected the first great Basilica of Saint Peter’s at the Vatican, besides those of St Lawrence beyond-the-Walls and of the Holy Cross. But the event of the most far-reaching consequences was the First Ecumenical Council of the Church at Nicaea (Turkey) in 325, when some 320 Bishops, mostly from the East, assembled under the presidency of the Bishop of Cordoba and, in the presence of the Pope’s legates, formally condemned the Arian heresy and formulated the Nicene Creed.



      Pope Sylvester died in 335 and was first buried in the cemetery of Saint Priscilla on the Via Salaria. In 762 his remains were transferred by Paul I to the Church of Saint Sylvester [the national church for English Catholics in Rome) within the city walls, where they repose to this day.
