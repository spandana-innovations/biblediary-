  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Matthew 17:14–20"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      Jesus assures us that if we have faith enough, we can ask even what is considered impossible, and that will happen. God responds abundantly to really deep faith in Him. The disciples did believe in Jesus, but their faith was rather weak. That was why they could not heal the epileptic boy. But with the coming of the Holy Spirit they became strong in faith and began working great wonders by His power. Jesus is not present with us physically, but he works through us by the power of his Spirit. Let in prayer ask for a deeper and firmer faith and seek the guidance of the Holy Spirit in our Christian living.



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s texts link faith with trust, and miracles with life’s normal routine. A balanced spirituality needs to take account of all aspects and not focus exclusively on any single side.



      The full impact of faith is seen in the Gospel, where faith cures the sick, drives out demons and moves mountains. This is a metaphor to emphasize Jesus’ final words, “Nothing will be impossible for you,” if you have faith. He reminds us that our life is involved in a struggle between superhuman forces of good and evil. We are called to daily expressions of faith, faith that reaches beyond human expectations – into the world to come.



      The gospel says that Jesus rebukes his disciples for their little faith. He doesn’t say that they have no faith but tells them that they have little faith. They had just failed in their efforts to do the work that Jesus had sent them to do, bringing healing to a sick child. Jesus attributes this failure to their little faith. Many of us may find it easy to identify with the disciples. We think of ourselves as people of faith but we sense that our faith is not as strong as it could be. We don’t trust the Lord enough. We have that striking promise of Jesus in the gospel that if our faith was as small even as a mustard seed we could move mountains. Jesus wanted his disciples to grow in their faith; it is what he wants for all of us. In response to that desire of Jesus for a stronger faith within us, we could make our own the prayer of the father of the sick child that we find in Mark’s version of this story, “Lord, I believe; help my unbelief.”

    body: |
      The disciples were called by God, they listened to his teachings and gave up everything to follow our Lord. Naturally, we assume that they had extraordinary faith because they were so close to Jesus. But their faith lacked substance, as does ours. That the disciples struggled and failed many times is comforting to us. For I know there is hope for me and many others whose faith is weak, fall into temptation, and sink when called to faithfully walk with Christ. The key to discipleship is to ask God questions in prayer, seek His direction in faithful fellowship, and pray for the Holy Spirit to guide us and reveal what we need to grow closer to Jesus. Jesus’ mercy is without measure for those who seek it. Let us all listen to our failing moments, seek God’s guidance in prayer and fellowship, and plant our mustard seeds of faith.

    saintName: "St Teresa Benedicta of the Cross (Edith Stein), Martyr (1891–1942)"
    ref: "St Teresa Benedicta of the Cross (Edith Stein), Martyr (1891–1942)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Only the fool says in his heart that there is no God”—the truth of this old saying was evident in the life of this most illustrious of Jewish converts to Christianity. Teresa Benedicta, formerly Edith Stein, suddenly, in her early teens, stopped believing in God and declared herself an atheist in the year 1904. In time, however, being not foolish but highly intelligent, this youngster became a convert not only to belief in God but to belief in Him as taught, propagated and practised by the Catholic Church.



      Having lost her father when she was barely 21 months old, Edith grew up to be a headstrong girl and showed much promise first as a student and later as a lecturer. An avid seeker of the truth, she had so brilliant an intellect that she could take and put together sets of opposing ideas or philosophies—as regards how life works—in a way that made perfect sense. It was obviously this intelligence that led her, in the autumn of 1921, to be captivated by the story of the life and times of the great mystic, St Teresa of Avila.



      She entered the Carmelite Cloister at Cologne in 1933 and, later, at her investiture in 1934 took the name Teresa Benedicta à Cruce, which literally meant Teresa blessed by the cross. This worthy “daughter of Israel”, was beatified in 1987 and canonized in 1998.
