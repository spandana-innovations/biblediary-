    ref: "Matthew 6:19–23"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      The followers of Christ make plans, use money and work in the world, but their hearts are gripped by God’s concerns. If we are attached to something, that becomes our treasure and it controls our hearts. Earthly possessions can be easily lost; therefore we should store up treasures with God, and our hearts will be with Him. No treasure deposited with God can ever be lost. The eyes are the lamp of the body. If our heart is kind and compassionate, it is healthy and it will cast light around us. If our heart is bound up with earthly goods, it will not spread the light of compassion around us. Excessive attachment to material wealth will only fill our life with worry, fear, enmity, anxiety and darkness.



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel passage from the Sermon on the Mount instructs us to amass secure and lasting treasures in Heaven by a life of righteousness on earth, doing the will of God and sharing our blessings with the needy. Jesus uses two metaphors, one explaining the folly of keeping perishable treasures on earth and the other of loving the darkness caused by pride and prejudice.



      The image of earthly & heavenly treasures: Man’s heart yearns for a treasure which will give him security and lasting happiness. But treasure in the form of riches very often gives him constant worry because riches can be lost, destroyed or stolen, or his life may be terminated abruptly. The only treasure which will last beyond this life is treasure stored in Heaven. Obtaining and keeping such a treasure is possible only by lovingly and sacrificially sharing God’s blessings with others and leading an upright life doing the will of God with His grace.



      The image of a sound eye and clear vision: Jesus compares the human eye to a lamp which provides the body with light. St. Thomas Aquinas in his commentary on Mathew gives the following explanation: “The eye refers to motive. When a person wants to do something, he first forms an intention: thus, if your intention is sound – simple and clear—that is to say, if it is directed towards God, your whole body (that is, all your actions), will be sound, sincerely directed towards good.” Bad eyesight is also a Biblical metaphor for stupidity and spiritual blindness. Such blindness is caused by pride, prejudice, jealousy, hatred, etc., which would destroy our fair judgment.



      Life message: 1) Let us spend our lives here on earth doing good for others without being blinded by pride and prejudice. Thus, we will store up everlasting treasures in Heaven.

      Today’s Gospel provides a picture of what our hearts should focus on - earthly possessions or heavenly treasures. What we consider valuable shows us where our heart is and what it is busy with. What is earthly is temporary; no amount of wealth or earthly possessions and hoardings can bring us sustainable happiness and eternal peace. They trigger more worry, anxiety, fear, insecurity and distress as they are simply fleeting agents of deception. We should be content with what we have and live our lives with a sense of gratitude and humility. Our real treasure is found within ourselves where God makes His home, and where virtues of love, goodness, generosity, compassion and kindness bloom freely. Is my heart a divided one, undecided, torn between different or conflicting loyalties, whose light is weak and does not illuminate my path in life?

    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "St Silverius (Pope, Martyr) (-538)"
    ref: "St Silverius (Pope, Martyr) (-538)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      St Silverius was the son of Pope St Hormisdas, who had been married before he became a priest. In 536 when Pope St Agapitus died, Silverius was only a sub-deacon in Rome, but on the insistence of the Ostrogoth King Theodatus, who wanted to forestall any action on the part of the Monophysite Empress Theodora at Constantinople, Silverius was consecrated Bishop and Pope. Theodora had in fact planned to have the Deacon Vigilius made Pope, thus hoping to let the Monophysites gain control of the Sees of Constantinople and Antioch. She now tried to obtain from Silverius the reinstallation of these Monophysite Prelates, and when he refused, attempted to gain her purpose by force and deceit.



      When the Gothic general Witiges was devastating the surroundings of Rome, the Byzantine general Belisarius appeared on the scene; the Pope turned to him for help and permitted him to occupy Rome. But Belisarius now proceeded to carry out his Empress’ plans, and on the basis of a forged letter, Pope Silverius was accused of treason and deported to Patara in Asia Minor. There the Bishop of Patara soon became convinced of his complete innocence and communicated the true facts to the Emperor at Constantinople. The latter had Silverius sent back to Italy for retrial, but as soon as he landed at Naples, he was kidnapped by Belisarius’ men and taken to a small island, where, within a very short period of time, he died on 20 June 538 because of privation and harsh treatment — a death considered a martyr’s death.
