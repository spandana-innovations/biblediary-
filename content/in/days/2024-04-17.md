  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "John 6:35–40"
    audio: null
    body: |
      In today’s Gospel passage, Jesus repeats his claim that he is “the Bread of Life.” He means that, just as God sent manna from heaven to sustain the physical life of his people in the desert, so He has sent His Son Jesus to sustain the spiritual lives of His people. Spiritual life is actually Sanctifying Grace, our living relationship with God the Father, through His Son Jesus and the Holy Spirit.



      *Jesus makes three claims:*



      1) He claims to be our spiritual Food and offers himself in order to produce God’s life within us.



      2) He promises to those who believe in him unbroken friendship with God.



      3) Jesus also promises to those who believe in him a share in his own Resurrection at the end of this world and share of Eternal Life with him in Heaven.






      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span>



      1) We need to live dynamic spiritual lives, sharing in God’s Life, Sanctifying Grace, through the Holy Eucharist.



      2) We can keep the friendship of Jesus only by leading holy lives free from sin.



      3) We can enjoy and share the joy of Jesus’ Resurrection only by realizing and appreciating his presence within us and all around us. Only God can satisfy our deepest needs.

      In the gospel we see Jesus facing the problem of unbelief in spite of the signs he had worked among the people. He knows the will of his heavenly Father because he is deeply united with his Father in prayer. God wants everyone to believe in Jesus and have eternal life. The First Reading of today gives us a glimpse into the life of the early Christian community. Though they are persecuted and scattered they continue to proclaim Christ wherever they go and the Lord continues to work wonders through them. When difficulties come our way how do we respond? Are we like the persecuted Christians of the early church proclaiming Christ in spite of adverse circumstances of life? Let us ask God to deepen our belief in Jesus.

      Saint Anicetus - *Pope Martyr (-166)*



      The 11-year reign of Anicetus, 11th successor to the Petrine throne, coincided with the disturbance caused to the Roman Church by two major heresies, viz., “Gnosticism” (Valentine’s teaching that humankind is divided into three classes, viz., material men, i.e., those predestined to destruction, psychic men, who may attain salvation through redemption, and spiritual or perfect men who, like his followers, were destined for eternal life) and “Marcianism” (Marcian’s teaching that Jesus Christ was not the Son of Yahweh, the God mentioned in the Old Testament and that the God of the Jews was different from the God of the Christians).



      Saint Polycarp, the aged Bishop of Smyrna, conferred with Anicetus on the long disputed question of a date for the celebration of Easter, for the Pope was anxious to bring uniformity in the entire Church. The Asiatic Church still followed its ancient tradition of the 14th day of the month of Nisan (regardless of whether this date fell on a weekday or a Sunday), because this was believed to have been the actual date on which Jesus arose from the dead. Of course, as Saint Irenaeus states, “neither could Anicetus persuade Polycarp, nor Polycarp, Anicetus”, the issue, known as the “paschal controversy”, thus remaining unsolved, the bonds of charity however staying intact. He was probably martyred under the persecution of Marcus Aurelius.
