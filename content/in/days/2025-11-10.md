      Reflection: 1



      Temptations and scandals are sure to come, says Jesus, but woe to the one who brings them. It is better to die than to cause someone to commit sin. Further, if anyone offends us, we should help him to correct himself, and if he repents, we should forgive him. If he person repeats the offence many times and then expresses sorrow, we must forgive again. We should not put any limit to our forgiveness. That kind of forgiveness is a miracle we cannot work by our own strength; it requires great faith and trust in the power of Jesus. Like the Apostles, let us also pray, “Lord, increase our faith.” </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      In today’s Gospel passage, Jesus gives us two warnings: 1) We should not give scandal to anyone. 2) We need to practice unconditional forgiveness. Jesus also stresses our need for trusting Faith in God’s power if we are to avoid giving scandal and to practice forgiveness.</span>



      The great sin of scandal: Literally, scandal is a trap or stumbling block. The catechism defines it as any saying, action or omission which causes an occasion of sin for another. Giving scandal to children and beginners in the Faith is a serious sin because it causes a chain reaction of sins for years, affecting so many, taking away the life of grace from the victims. That is why Jesus says that it would be better for its perpetrators to have their necks inserted in heavy circular millstones and to be drowned in the sea than to suffer God’s punishment for this sin.</span>



      The necessity of practicing forgiveness: Jesus commands his followers to forgive their offending brothers and sisters repeatedly, as often as they are repentant. Further, we need to offer fraternal correction to the offender with charity, without humiliating him or offending his feelings. At the same time, we should not allow the offender to violate our just rights. Sincere forgiveness leads us to forget the particular offense and to extend the hand of friendship, which in turn helps the offender to repent. Jesus concludes his instructions by reminding his followers that avoiding scandals and forgiving the offenders are possible only if they have the trusting faith in God which enables Him to work miracles in their lives.</span>



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span> </span>



      1) We need to avoid giving scandal to any one because it causes a series of sins and does damage to a number of innocent victims. </span>



      2) We should ask God to enlarge our hearts to forgive others and to help us to be ready to grant forgiveness to those who have offended us. </span>

      Jesus’ teachings are filled with positive energy and divine inspiration. He invites us to be channels of this divine energy, especially to those in our care, as found in Luke 17:1-6. To become these channels, we must practise forgiveness, avoid negativity, and show respect to others. We must never mislead others or harbour hostility. Forgiveness should have no limits, even when someone repeatedly makes mistakes. This requires a strong faith in the power and love of Jesus. When our faith is unwavering, miracles can unfold in our lives every day. So, it’s worth asking ourselves: How strong is our/my faith in the incredible power and love of Jesus? By embracing these teachings and fostering a deep faith, we can spread positive energy and bring more joy and light into the world.

    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Leo I, the Great, Pope Confessor, Doctor of the Church"
    ref: "Saint Leo I, the Great, Pope Confessor, Doctor of the Church"
    audio: null
    body: |
      At a critical time in the Church’s history, when the Western Empire was disintegrating and the Eastern Church was profoundly disturbed by dogmatic controversies and speculations, God raised to the throne of Peter in 440 a farsighted and wise Pontiff, Leo I; he was to become one of the three saintly Popes whom we honour with the title of “the Great”, Gregory I in the 6th century and Nicholas I in the 9th being the other two.



      In order to maintain the purity of the Church’s doctrine, Leo took sharp measures against the subtle infiltration of heresies into Italy and Spain, and in the East condemned Monophysitism or Eutychianism. In his famous dogmatic letter “Tome to Flavian”, Archbishop of Constantinople, he clearly set forth the Church’s doctrine that Christ is one person but possesses two distinct natures, the divine and the human; an early example of an infallible Papal pronouncement. At the 4th Ecumenical Council of Chalcedon in 461, some 600 Eastern bishops, assembled under the presidency of the Papal legates, officially acclaimed this great document as the Church’s true teaching thus: “Peter has spoken by Leo.”



      Pope St Leo I looked upon his office as that of Christ himself and strove to become “all things to all”. Combining prayer with labour, he proved himself a true father to his people. That he saw himself chiefly as a teacher is evident from the fact that his teachings remain the greatest hallmark of his pontificate.
