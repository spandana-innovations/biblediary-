      Reflection: 1



      At the Last Supper, after washing the feet of his disciples, Jesus was reclining with them at table. After receiving the morsel from the hand of Jesus, Judas goes out to betray his Master as he had already planned. Jesus tells the others, “Where I am going you cannot come.” He gives them a new commandment to love one another as he had loved them. He adds that afterwards they will also follow him. He also foretells Peter who wanted to follow him right then, that he was going to deny Jesus three times before the cock crows. We notice different things in today’s Gospel – love, discipleship, trust and betrayal. Love is what defines discipleship. Let us pray for the grace to grow deeper in our love for Jesus and to be his disciples who experience the heart-beats of Jesus as John did. </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      In today’s Gospel, we hear Jesus’ loving appeal to two of his chosen Apostles, Judas and Peter, who represent hard-heartedness and repentance respectively. Judas was a perfect actor and an accomplished hypocrite who could hide his motives from all except Jesus. He deliberately betrayed Jesus for money. Peter on the other hand was simple at heart, a deeply emotional disciple who really loved Jesus.</span></span></span></span></span>



      (A) Judas – a model of hard-heartedness: Jesus showed his last act of love for Judas: a) by allowing him to sit at His left so that Jesus could rest His head on his chest during the Last Supper; b) by giving him a piece of bread dipped in sauce, from Jesus’ plate, as a sign of honour; c) by making Judas the treasurer of the group and the keeper of money received as an offering to the rabbi from the people, thus giving everyone a sign of his trust and confidence in Judas. But Jesus could not save Judas from his hard-heartedness and greed for money. Judas probably expected the triumphant Messiah in Jesus and hoped to rule Israel with him as his finance minister. If so, he must have been totally disillusioned when Jesus predicted his suffering and shameful death. This might have prompted him to betray Jesus. We, too, can become hard-hearted like Judas when we have become addicted to evil habits, and we can refuse Jesus’ call for repentance and conversion even during Holy Week.</span></span></span></span></span>



      (B) Peter – a model of true repentance: Peter showed good will and generosity in repenting of his sin – denying Jesus out of weakness – by weeping bitterly and finally by suffering a martyr’s death for his Faith in Jesus.</span></span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life messages: </span></span></span>1) Let us imitate Peter by repenting of our choices against Jesus and his ideals in our day-to-day lives. 2) Let us also ask God to liberate us from our evil habits and addictions before they make us hard-hearted. 3) Just as Jesus considered death on the cross as his glory, let us consider humble, committed and sacrificial service for others as our glory and learn to love others as Jesus loves us. </span></span></span></span></span>

      The varied attitudes of the disciples reveal profound insights into our spiritual journeys. John, the beloved disciple, remained close to Jesus, embodying ‘the intimacy that every believer longs for’. In contrast, Peter’s wavering heart shows us the struggle of genuine discipleship amidst human frailty. Judas, consumed by darkness, serves as a stark reminder of the consequences of turning away from the light. ‘Am I growing in my love for the Lord, or am I drifting into uncertainty or spiritual darkness?’ As we prepare to receive the Lord in the Holy Mass, let’s reflect on the importance of participating in the Sacred Sacrifice, with pure hearts. Are we ready to embrace His presence and become a Eucharist for those in need? ‘In becoming a blessing, we become truly blessed.’</span></span></span>
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Daniel of Murano, Hermit (–1411)"
    ref: "Saint Daniel of Murano, Hermit (–1411)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      St Daniel was a German merchant who, on his frequent trips to Venice, came to know and admire the Camaldolese monks at Murano. So enamoured was he of their way of life that he spent long periods of time with them and finally decided to live like a hermit in his own house, under their direction, and donned their habit.</span></span></span></span>



      A deeply religious man, much given to prayer, he distributed nearly all his worldly possessions in order to alleviate the misery of the Venetian poor. He was murdered by robbers in 1411.</span></span></span></span>
