    body: |
      At the end of this great week of celebrations, the gospel fittingly sums up and reviews the appearances of the risen Jesus for our grateful remembering. We are told that he first appeared to Mary Magdalen, and that she hurried to bring the good news to the apostles. Then the two disciples on the road to Emmaus were favoured, and they too felt compelled to hurry to inform the apostles. Finally he appeared to that group itself, and Mark tells us that Jesus took them to task for “their disbelief and their stubbornness” in failing to put faith in his chosen messengers.</span></span></span></span>



      But those human faults did not prevent Jesus from commissioning these same apostles to go out and “proclaim the good news to all creation.” And why not? For three years he has been preparing them for this moment. Despite their human weaknesses, he believes in them (maybe more than they believe in themselves). For three years he has been preparing them to become the Church, his very own Body, and his new presence in which he would continue to live and preach and heal through all ages to come.</span></span></span></span>



      So what Jesus says and does today is crucial for understanding him and his life’s work. There are many followers of Jesus today who would like to possess and glory in the risen Jesus, but they see no need for the Church, which was the object of all his preaching. What he is really telling us is that you cannot have him without the Church—that frail, weak, vulnerable, doubting, human Church so exactly characterized by this original group who made it up in the beginning.</span></span></span></span>



      (Excerpts from: Emeric Lawrence OSB, *Daily Meditations for Easter Season*, ST PAULS, Mumbai, India)</span></span></span></span>

      Mark, the first gospel writer, gives us in a nutshell what other Gospel writers narrate in detail. Jesus appeared first to Mary Magdalene and then to the two on the way to Emmaus. They in their turn shared this good news with the eleven but they did not believe. Then Jesus appears before them and rebukes them for their unbelief and hardness of heart. What about us? Do we really believe that the risen Jesus is with us always? When life runs smooth, it is easy to believe; but what happens when we face knotty and painful situations in life? It is difficult for most of us to believe that the risen Lord is with us and is helping us to face life as it presents itself to us.</span></span></span></span>

      Saint Celestine I</span></span> - *Pope Confessor (-432)*</span></span></span></span>



      Born in Campania, Italy *(date unknown)*, Celestine was a Deacon in Rome when he was elected to succeed Boniface I on 20 September 422. An outstanding feature of his pontificate which lasted till 432 was its vigorous attack on the twin heresies then prevailing, viz., Nestorianism and Pelagianism.</span></span></span></span>



      It was Celestine himself who had approved the choice of Nestorius as Patriarch of Constantinople in 428. However, the latter’s teachings, stressing on the one hand that Christ’s human and divine natures were each independent of the other, and, on the other, denouncing Mary’s title of “Theotokos”—God-bearer—came as a rude jolt to him. His untiring efforts to change such heretical thinking having come to naught, Celestine entrusted Cyril, the saintly Archbishop of Alexandria with the task of condemning Nestorius’ heresy and obtaining his recantation at the Third Council of Ephesus held in 431. Celestine’s approval of the council’s decree deposing and excommunicating Nestorius eventually led to a schism that remained unresolved for over a century. </span></span></span></span>



      Earlier, in 429, Celestine deputed two French bishops, viz., Saint Germanus of Auxerre and Saint Lupus of Troyes to Rome to refute the doctrine of Pelagius that minimalized the role of divine grace in man’s salvation. In 431, Celestine consecrated Saint Palladius at Rome and sent him as the first bishop of Ireland, to evangelize the Irish.</span></span></span></span>



      Saint Celestine passed away on 27 July 432. Buried in the cemetery of Saint Priscilla on the Via Salaria, his mortal remains were subsequently moved to the Basilica di Santa Prassede.</span></span></span></span>
