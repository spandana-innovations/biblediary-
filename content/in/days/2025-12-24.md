      Reflection: 1



      When Zachariah regained his speech, filled with the Holy Spirit, he sang a song of praise (known as the *Bendictus),*to God for visiting and redeeming His people, for being faithful to the promises He made to Abraham. He also praised God for what his child was going to do as a prophet of God, going ahead of the coming Lord to prepare his ways by preaching to the people repentance and forgiveness of sins. Zachariah’s song is full of hope and joy. Even when we are going through difficult times, God keeps His promises and fulfils them at the right time. Even muteness or inability to speak cannot block us from singing God’s praises in our hearts.</span></span></span></span></span>



       </span></span>*Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel gives the prophetic hymn which Zechariah, filled with Holy Spirit, sang on the eighth day after his son John’s birth when all had assembled for his Circumcision and Naming ceremony. Although the Jews generally believed that Elijah the prophet would return to earth to prepare the way for the Messiah, Zechariah prophetically sang here that it was his son, John, who was going to prepare the way for the Messiah, Jesus.</span>



      Zechariah’s prophecy contains four steps of the Christian way we are supposed to take. 1) Preparation: Our life must be a preparation, leading us to our eternal salvation, enabling us to walk through/with/in Christ, the only sure Way.</span>



      2) Correct knowledge of the only true God: Jesus Christ is our Lord and Savior who taught us that God His Father is a loving and forgiving Father Who saved us through His son Jesus.</span>



      3) Forgiveness of sins: This is the restoring of our broken relationship with God, accomplished through the suffering, death and Resurrection of Jesus.</span>



      4) Walking in the way of peace: Peace is not the absence of trouble. It is the fullness of everything needed for man’s highest good. Jesus instituted in His Church all the means necessary for us to attain our highest good. He gave us the Holy Spirit, the Holy Bible, the Sacraments, and the centralized teaching authority of his Church, with Mary and the saints as role models and praying companions for our journey.</span>



      <span class="subhead">Life message</span>: As happened to doubting Zechariah, let us be filled with the Holy Spirit by asking for His daily anointing and strengthening. Let us prophesy as Zachariah did, by conveying to others the reason for our Christmas celebration as rebirth of Jesus into our lives.</span>

      In the Gospel’s grand narrative, Zechariah’s “Benedictus” resounds a hopeful hymn amid Israel’s bleakness under Roman rule. Picture a time when the nation was led by a ruler indifferent to God, plunging into moral decay. Amid this chaos, a ray of light broke through: the birth of John the Baptist. Zechariah, Elizabeth, and John – names hinting at God’s faithfulness – showed that even in tough times, God keeps His promises. Their story encourages us to sing praises through serving others, finding joy in God’s care, and staying hopeful even in adversity. On this eve of Christmas, let us fervently prepare our hearts – in holiness and sanctity – to prepare a beautiful crib for our Blessed Lord, Who comes as a Babe to our hearts, our lives and our homes!

    saintName: "Saint John of Kanty - Confessor"
    ref: "Saint John of Kanty - Confessor"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Born on 23 June 1390 to pious country parents, John was surnamed after his birthplace in the diocese of Cracow, Poland. Having done his Master’s in liberal arts from the University of Cracow in 1417, he served as rector of the school of the Templars of Miechów, later teaching at the school of Philosophy where he was appointed dean. After obtaining his Master’s in Theology, he taught Theology the rest of his life.



      In parish ministry, he endeared himself to all by his amiability and humility, and above all by his particularly outstanding generosity toward the poor; whatever he possessed in cash and kind ever at their disposal, he often returned home barefoot having given a beggar the very shoes he was wearing! In a spirit of penance he ate frugally and slept sparingly and on the floor. Deeply sensitive to human ingratitude and offences committed against God, he offered Mass daily for the appeasement of heavenly justice.



      During his lifetime he made four pilgrimages to Rome on foot and one to the Holy Land in the hope of being martyred there at the hands of the Turks. On one such pilgrimage he was waylaid. Hardly had the robbers let him proceed on his assurance that they had taken everything he had when, scrupulous to the core, he suddenly remembered some coins sewn into his clothing and, calling them back, offered them that sum also. Astounded, they promptly restored to him all that they had taken from him!



      John passed away on Christmas Eve 1473, aged 83 years, noted more for his sanctity than for the brilliance and originality of his teaching. Beatified in 1690, he was declared patron of Poland and Lithuania in 1737 and canonized in 1767.
