    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      In this miracle Jesus gives sight to a blind man as foretold by prophet Isaiah (29:18), as he is the light of the world. The Jewish leaders react negatively to this miracle and refuse to believe while the man who is healed responds very positively and believes in Jesus and becomes his disciple. The answer of Jesus to the question his disciples ask him shows that sin is not the cause of all suffering. Rather, God’s glory is manifested through the healing Jesus works. In the beginning some of the Pharisees believe that Jesus came from God, others find fault with him for healing on a Sabbath day. But later, all their interest was to trap the healed man and to reject the heavenly origin of Jesus. Thus they become more and more blind. The healed man who believes in Jesus is expelled from the Jewish community. In v. 39 Jesus tells the Pharisees that those who acknowledge their blindness shall see, and those who boast of their sight shall become spiritually blind. Jesus is the light that has come from heaven; if we follow him, we shall not walk in darkness. </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      “The Lord said to Samuel, ‘How long will you grieve over Saul?'”:</span></span></span></span></span>



      This is like our God questioning us “How long will you grieve over your past?” The past is past. Let us do what is needed for our future. Many times we worry over our past mistakes and wonder what we will do in future. But God has made a big plan for us and is waiting for us to walk through it. If we continue regretting the past, then we cannot live our present too. We will continue regretting our present days also in the future.</span></span></span></span></span>



      Our Lord Jesus said in John 16:33: “In the world ye shall have tribulation: but be of good cheer; I have overcome the world.” We can pray about everything and worry about nothing. When you are worried, not knowing what to do, say to God, “Lord, I know that you have a better plan for me at this moment, please show me what should be done”. Jesus is sincere. He will guide you in some way. God has not created us to live our lives without His support. That is why He has given us the Holy Spirit. The Holy Spirit is not living within us to watch whether we are living our life correctly. He is always there to guide us. So why not start living a worry free life? Our problem is not only ours, it is God’s also. As God had a next plan – David for Samuel, He will also show us a new path. Cast your care willingly on Lord Jesus and rely on Him.</span></span></span></span></span>



      “. the Lord looks on the heart”: When people reject you, be happy, it is the time the Lord notices you. Jesse chose seven of his sons to pass before Samuel. Samuel chose a good looking person in his mind as God’s choice. But God rejected all of their choices. For He was looking for a loving heart. In Israel there were people of many skills, scholars, good administrators and so on. But God rejected all of them. He longed to see only a heart longing for Him.

      The more you are rejected, it is an indication that the Lord has a special assignment for you.</span></span></span></span></span>



      Let us pray like David, “Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right spirit within me”. – Psalm 51.</span></span></span></span></span>



      “Live as children of light”: Our Lord Jesus is the light and He has told us “You are the light of the world”. It means that we need to follow the life of our Lord Jesus. Then our lives will become examples for others. We are called by His name “Christians”, so we represent Him on this earth. There was a girl who became Christian in a Christian minority region. She testified, “The first reason which drew me towards our Lord Jesus Christ is the pure life of my Christian friends”. If each one of us fully represents Christ, then everyone in the world will be a Christian.</span></span></span></span></span>



      “Rabbi, who sinned, this man or his parents, that he was born blind?”:

      When some people undergo terrific tribulations, many people mark them as “sinners”. They say that either that person, or his parents or his great grandparents have sinned, that is why they are undergoing such a huge crisis in their lives. Our Lord Jesus specifically made these incidents to happen and be recorded so that He could invalidate all these remarks. God can never allow a new born baby to carry the sins of his family. That is why Jesus bore all of them on a cross.</span></span></span></span></span>



      Then why are the special children born like the blind child in this passage?:

      Jesus answers, “Neither this man nor his parents sinned; he was born blind so that God’s works might be revealed in him”. Parents of this blind person were taking care of their special kid, with whatever their hands could do. It resembles the moment in Job’s story “God asked the devil, did you see my son Job? Is there any fault in him?” Likewise looking at every special child or special parent, our good God is asking the devil, “Did you see my child? Even though he is in huge agony, he is still worshipping me? He still fights the fierce battles trusting in me…” Special children fulfil the purpose of their parent’s lives.</span></span></span></span></span>



      Some of the Pharisees said, “This man is not from God, for he does not observe the Sabbath.” But others said, “How can a man who is a sinner perform such signs?” And they were divided.

      We read that the Pharisees were finding fault with Jesus since He did not observe the Sabbath law. They were blind to God’s love. Then they were divided among themselves, because some Pharisees could reason if not from God, who can perform such signs? But all these did not bother our Lord Jesus. He never focussed on pleasing people. He continued doing God’s will.</span></span></span></span></span>



      “If this man were not from God, he could do nothing”: When his parents were afraid and did not testify for Jesus, the blind person testified for him. He was not afraid of testifying for our Lord Jesus though he understood the dire consequences. He argued with the Pharisees so eloquently that the Pharisees did not know how to withstand his arguments. They drove him out. While the blind person could see God’s love, the Pharisees were still blind to God’s love.</span></span></span></span></span>



      “Jesus heard that they had driven him out, and when he found him, he said, “Do you believe in the Son of Man?”: When the blind person was driven out, Jesus came searching for Him and found Him. If we are rejected, our Lord Jesus will search for us and find us.</span></span></span></span></span>



      If the world abandons us, Jesus will be with us.</span></span></span></span></span>

      In the Gospel, we witness a powerful transformation as a man born blind encounters the ‘Light of the world’. This event illustrates two contrasting movements: the upward journey of faith and trust of the healed man, and the downward descent into scepticism and unbelief of those who resist the truth. The journey toward the Light brings blessing while the refusal to see leads to darkness. The blind man gradually recognises Jesus as the Son of God, culminating in his act of worship. Meanwhile, those entrenched in their prejudices remain hostile, blinded by their misconceptions. We are challenged today to reflect on the direction of our spiritual life: Are we embracing the light that leads to deeper faith, or are we retreating into darkness? Let us choose the path of illumination and transformation.</span></span></span>
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Louise De Marillac, Widow (1591 - 1660)"
    ref: "Saint Louise De Marillac, Widow (1591 - 1660)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Louise, the daughter of a military courtier, was a well-educated and socially prominent Parisian woman. At 16 she felt a strong desire to join the Poor Clares, but poor health intervened and five years later she was married to Antoine Le Gras, a secretary to Queen Marie de Medicis, and bore him a son, Michel.</span></span></span></span>



      With pious Antoine’s approval, she devoted all her free time to works of charity among the poor in Paris, though many considered it unbecoming of her social status. In 1619 she made the acquaintance of St Francis de Sales and St Vincent de Paul. When her husband died in 1625, Louise placed herself entirely under the latter Saint’s direction, and spent herself in superintending the various confraternities of charity which he had founded for the care of the needy. </span></span></span></span>



      This is regarded as the real beginning of the Sisters of Charity; for their benefit St Vincent de Paul began his famous spiritual conferences in 1634 which continued for 25 years. For 12 years there was no written rule or constitution, and no official approval was sought for the new Order. At a time when it was unheard of for nuns not to be enclosed, the services of these Sisters were urgently needed in the slums and hospitals. St Vincent wanted the fear of God to be their enclosure, holy modesty their veil, the parish church their chapel, a hired room their cell, and charitable works their Office. As their habit the Sisters adopted the blue-gray dress and white linen cornette of the peasant women in the Isle de France.</span></span></span></span>



      Louise de Marillac died on 15 March 1660, followed 6 months later by St Vincent. By then there were more than 40 houses in France. Today there are over 4,000 houses and some 40,000 Sisters in all parts of the world. The community is a lay one with only private vows, and the original rule and constitution have never been changed until recently.</span></span></span></span>



      Beatified on 9 May 1920, Louise de Marillac was canonized on 11 March 1934, by Pope Pius XI and, on 10 February 1960, named Patroness of all Christian Social Workers. </span></span></span></span>
