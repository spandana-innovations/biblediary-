  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "John 20:1-9"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Jesus’ resurrection easily draws the picture of the empty tomb in our minds. It goes along with the simple declaration, “He is not here” (Mk 16:6). It simply means that Jesus lives. He is present and active in and around us. “He is not here” opens up the quest for the living Lord. He is close to us, even when we think He is dead or far away. All we need to do is to look for Him with the eyes of faith, with believing hearts.</span></span></span></span>



      Easter is a celebration of a present reality. The reality is that Jesus lives among us and within us right here and now. Therefore, there is no need to worry or to fear. With Jesus, we know that we are journeying, not to sunset, but to sunrise. In reality, our life’s journey is a journey to the eternal Easter in heaven.</span></span></span></span>



      Easter is a celebration of future hope. This future hope is that we will all share in the fullness of the New Life. Think of the glorious future that awaits each of us, “For us, our homeland is in heaven, and from heaven comes the Saviour we are waiting for, the Lord Jesus Christ, and He will transfigure these wretched bodies of ours into copies of His glorious body” (Phil 3:20). Do you live like one who believes in such a glorious future? If you do, surely everything in this world takes on a new perspective.</span></span></span></span>



      Today we celebrate the victory of good over evil, the victory of life over death. The risen Lord gives us the grace to share in His victory and to live His victory in our lives. It is easy for us to get bogged down in the everyday events of the world. The challenge is to be part of the victory of Christ and celebrate our life as a personal Easter.</span></span></span></span>



      *Christianity is, in its essence, a resurrection religion. The concept of resurrection lies at its heart. If you remove it, Christianity is destroyed (John R. W. Scott). </span></span>*</span></span>



      (Philip John SSP & Premdas SSP, *New Horizon Homilies*, ST PAULS, Mumbai, India)</span></span></span></span>
  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      The Resurrection of Jesus is all about transformation. It transformed Peter, a boastful coward, into a mighty orator who on Pentecost Sunday stood up to profess his faith in Jesus and in his resurrection. He would turn out to be a source of strength for sick people, so that his shadow would fall them and cure them. Others who remained fearful behind closed doors came out in the open to testify to the resurrection with great power. It witnessed the transformation of people who fought for position and greatness into people who were of one heart and one soul. Since then, down through the centuries, thousands of ordinary men and women ‘turned the world upside down’. The risen Christ is an active, living reality. Today we carry the power of the Resurrected Lord with us wherever we go. Where there is despair, we bring hope; in troubled times, lasting peace. This is the essence of Easter.</span></span></span></span>
