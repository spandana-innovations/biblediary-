      Reflection: 1



      The people of Chorazin, Bethsaida and Capernaum, important cities for eh Jews, witnessed the mighty works Jesus did among them, but they did not repent. He rejected his message and refused to give up their evil ways. Jesus reminds them that more is expected to those to whom more is given. Tyre Sidon, gentile cities where the people lived in ignorance and sin, would have a better fate than the Jewish people who, in spite of having received divine revelation, rejected Christ. The message of Jesus calls for total conversion because that is the only way to belong to the kingdom of heaven that Jesus has come to establish. </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Jesus reminds these cities that they deserve God’s punishment because they have forgotten the responsibilities which their numerous meetings with the Messiah in their midst have laid upon them. They should have listened to his message, put it into practice, and borne witness to the miracles he had worked for them.



      Chorazin, Bethsaida, Capernaum: Nothing is mentioned in any of the Gospels about the “wonders” Jesus worked in these cities. Bethsaida was a fishing village on the west bank of Jordan at the northern end of the lake. Chorazin was a town one hour’s walking distance north of Capernaum. Jesus expresses his holy anger and sorrowful pity from a broken heart at the irresponsible disregard and indifference these three ungrateful cities have shown to the Good News. Jesus warns them, “*it shall be more tolerable on the Day of Judgment for Tyre and Sidon ..*[and] *the land of Sodom”*than for them, because Tyre, Sidon, and Sodom were not fortunate enough to hear Jesus and to receive the opportunity for conversion given to Bethsaida, Chorazin, and Capernaum.



      Life Messages:Privileges always carry responsibilities:



      1) We are privileged to have the holy Bible, so we have the responsibility of making use of it.



      2) We have the Eucharistic celebration every day in our Churches, so we have the responsibility of participating in it when we are able to do so.



      3) We have the Sacrament of Reconciliation, so we have the responsibility of using it to be reconciled with Jesus and his Church.



      4) We are blessed with having the Holy Spirit to guide the teaching authority in the Church, so we have the responsibility of studying and following the Church’s directives and teachings.



      5) We have Mary, the mother of Jesus, and numerous saints as our role models, so we have the responsibility of following Jesus in their footsteps.

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      Jesus comes down heavily on the people of the three cities that have not heeded his preaching. People of Chorazin, Bethsaida and Capernaum were the immediate and privileged beneficiaries of Jesus’ ministry. He brought the Good News of the kingdom to the poor, the sinners, the outcasts and the rejected. He healed the sick, liberated those afflicted by evil powers, and raised the dead to new life as he announced the kingdom of peace and joy. Yet they ignored or rejected his message and were not ready to change their ways. That is why Jesus denounced those three towns. Those of us who are gifted with faith in Jesus and filled with his own life have a greater responsibility to respond to him more than others. The teaching of Jesus calls us to the conversion of mind and heart. How open are we to his message? How willing are we to change?

    audio: null
    body: |
      At the age of four, Giovanni was cured of a grave illness through the intercession of Saint Francis d’Assisi. He was named “Bonaventure” — a name which variably means “good venture”, “good attempt” or “happy arrival” — when, in c 1243, he entered the Order of Friars Minor founded by Francis d’Assisi. In 1257 he received his doctorate and earlier, the same year, though hardly 39, he was elected Minister General, an office he held through 16 difficult years. He prudently endeavoured to curb the extremists, both among the Spirituals (or Observants), who insisted on the literal observance of Saint Francis’ original rule, and the Conventuals (or *Relaxati*), who favoured certain mitigations and relaxations.



      In 1263 Bonaventure ordered that a bell be rung and the Hail Mary recited at nightfall in each monastery in honour of the Annunciation which was popularly believed to have taken place at that hour — a pious practice that the Angelus seems to have taken its origin from. In 1273, he reluctantly became Cardinal Bishop of Albano, and when the emissaries of the Pope, Gregory X, arrived at his monastery near Florence, they found him washing dishes and were told to hang the Cardinal’s hat on a nearby tree until he had finished!



      Saint Bonaventure always attached the utmost importance to ‘preaching’ and seized every opportunity, be it with his friars, the clergy, the common people, Popes or Kings, to expound the Word of God, in which too he attained great fame.



      Canonized in 1482, he was declared a doctor of the church in 1588.
