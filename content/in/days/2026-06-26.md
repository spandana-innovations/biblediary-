  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mathew 8: 1-4"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today’s Gospel describes Jesus healing a leper as soon as he had finished his Sermon on the Mount and come down the mountain. In those days, all skin diseases were considered leprosy, and leprosy was known to be highly contagious. Hence “lepers” were separated from their families and considered socially and ritually unclean. In addition, they were treated as sinners who had been punished by God with a contagious disease. The punishment given to Miriam, the complaining sister of Moses (Nm 12:9-10), to Gehazi the greedy servant of the prophet Elisha (II Kgs 5: 27) and to the proud king Uzziah (Chr 26:19) supported this Jewish belief. As a general rule, when a Jewish leper was healed (from any of the skin diseases considered as leprosy), he had to go to the local priest to have him confirm that the healed one was now clean and was permitted to mix with the general public.</span></span></span>



      Jesus rewards the trusting Faith of a humble leper: It is such a leper who has the courage to approach Jesus in public with trusting Faith in Jesus’ power to heal him. In all humility he kneels down and says to Jesus, *“Lord, if you will, you can make me clean.” *Jesus violates a social taboo by touching the leper, as he heals him with a single command, *“I will; be clean.”*</span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life message:</span> 1) We all need healing from our spiritual leprosy. Although we may not suffer from physical leprosy, we all suffer from the “spiritual leprosy” of sins. It is sin that we carry with us that keeps us unclean. Jesus, our Saviour, wants to heal us. Since Jesus is not afraid to touch our deepest impurities, and knows all of them better than we do, let us not try to hide them, nor fear to confess them in the Sacrament of Reconciliation. Just as the lepers cried out to Jesus for healing, let us also ask Jesus every night before we go to sleep to heal us from the spiritual leprosy of sins, and let us approach Jesus in the Sacrament of Reconciliation whenever we are in grave sin. </span></span></span>

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      The healing of the leper in the Gospel is a beautiful example of the right disposition in prayer. With his gesture of kneeling before Jesus, he recognises the divinity in Jesus and then he prays using the words, “if you will,” indicating that if it is God’s will that I be healed, let it be. Finally, he expresses his confidence that Jesus can heal him. We see here the recognition of the presence of God, respect for God’s will, and confidence in God’s power in the prayer of the leper. So, too, in our prayers, we need to be confident of God’s presence and power, and prioritise His will. Padre Pio would say, “Prayer is the best weapon we possess. It is the key that opens the heart of God.”</span></span></span>

    audio: null
    body: |
      Josemaria was a man who became a “master of Christian living” by reaching “the heights of contemplation with continuous prayer, constant mortification” and “exemplary docility to the movements of the Holy Spirit.”</span></span></span></span>



      It was at the early age of 16 that Josemaria felt called to some as yet unknown but important task. With characteristic faith he responded with the words of scripture: “Here I am Lord, because you have called me” (1 Sam 3:5). Ordained priest in 1925, he took up the study of civil law in Madrid in 1927, his apostolic zeal soon having him interacting with a cross-section of society—artists and academicians, labourers and intellectuals, army-men and clergymen alike. To strive for sanctity by way of living Christ’s own life among his people as enjoined by Baptism was the call that became evident in 1928 when making a retreat. Thus was born Opus Dei whose mission would be “to open up in the Church a new vocational path aimed at spreading the quest for holiness and the practice of the apostolate through sanctification of ordinary work in the world.”</span></span></span></span>



      With the rapid spread of Opus Dei came trials and hardships of every kind, financial difficulties assailing the worldwide expansion of apostolic works. Josemaria’s sense of humour, however, stood him in good stead. In 1954, he was miraculously cured of a serious form of diabetes that had troubled him for over a decade.</span></span></span></span>



      The heart of Josemaria’s message is: *Sanctify your daily life, and sanctify yourself in and through that daily life.*Beatified in 1992, he was finally canonized in 2002. </span></span></span></span>
