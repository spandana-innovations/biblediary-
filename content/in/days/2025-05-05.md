      Reflection: 1



      People came in search of Jesus because he had fed hem in abundance in the desert. Jesus told them not to limit their interest to earthly food that perishes but to seek food that endures to eternal life. It is true that people are more concerned about satisfying their earthly needs than about taking care of their eternal life. St Paul says, if you are raised with Christ and are no more earthlings, set your mind on the things that are above, not on earthly things (Col 3: 1-2). </span></span></span></span>



      Do we limit our interest to earthly things or do we focus our attention more on heavenly things? Are we interested in greater goods than perishable ones? Those who believe in Christ and follow him cannot be concerned only with what satisfies their hunger, but with higher and eternal goods and values. </span></span></span></span>



      *— Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP</span></span>*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel introduces Jesus’ famous discourse on the Holy Eucharist which emerged within a dialogue between Jesus and the Jews who had gone around the Lake and come to Capernaum searching for him. In answer to their question about his arrival, Jesus challenged them, saying that they were looking for him so they could get another free meal and that such meals would not satisfy them. He also instructed them to labour for food that would give them Eternal Life.



      Naturally, the Jews asked Jesus what they should do to get such a food. Since the Jews believed that the Torah was the “bread of life,” many may have thought that Jesus was instructing them to keep the Torah to attain Eternal Life. So, Jesus clarified that they had to do the work of God to attain eternal life; he told them that the “work of God” was not to work miracles for their own sake but to believe in Him as the Son of God, sent to give Eternal Life to those who believed in him. While regular food helps us to stay alive in this world, spiritual food sustains and develops our supernatural life, which will last forever in Heaven. This food, which only God can give us, consists mainly in the gift of Faith in Jesus and in the grace God gives us to live according to Jesus’ teaching. Through God’s infinite love, we are given in the Blessed Eucharist the very Author of these gifts, Jesus Christ, as nourishment for our souls.



      Life message: 1) Most of the time, we work for food which only nourishes the body. Jesus teaches that he is the Heavenly food, who nourishes the soul and gives us eternal life in union with God in Heaven. Hence, let us receive this Life-giving food both in the Holy Eucharist and in the Holy Scripture with proper preparation and reverence while repenting of our sins.

      Jesus asks the crowd not to search for food that perishes but for the food of eternal life. The exhortation is to set their minds on the greater good. Saint Paul would repeat this idea in his exhortation to seek the things that are above (Col 3:1). We have the choice between preoccupying barely our earthly life and perishable body and having our goals set on God and His kingdom. Think of the parable of the rich man whom Jesus would call a ‘fool’ for, he stored up goods for his future on earth, gamely forgetting to set right his life for the eternal good, whereas Zacchaeus was wise as he brought salvation to himself and his household through the conversion experience. Am I wise or foolish in the eyes of God?

      Very little is reliably known about St Angelo. His parents were Jews who were later converted to Christianity by a vision of Our Lady who announced to them that the long-awaited Messiah had already come and had redeemed his people. She also promised them two sons, and that they both would grow up as flourishing Olive trees on the heights of the Carmel — one as a patriarch and the other as a glorious martyr.



      Early on, the twins displayed great mental and spiritual gifts. At the age of 18, when they entered the Carmelite Order, they had already mastered Greek, Latin and Hebrew. After Angelo had spent five years on Mount Carmel as a hermit, Jesus appeared to him and bade him go to Sicily (Italy), where he would have the grace to offer the sacrifice of his life.



      Obedient to the call of the Lord, he promptly left Mt Carmel for Sicily. On his way and after his arrival in Sicily he succeeded in converting many by his teaching and miracles. At Palermo (Italy), about 200 Jews sought baptism as the result of his eloquence. Similar success was met with in Leocata, but there he aroused the fury of Berengarius whose wickedness and cruelty he had denounced.



      One day as he was preaching to a crowd, he was stabbed by a band of ruffians who attacked him under Berengarius’ influence. Mortally wounded, Angelo fell on his knees. Praying for his people, more especially for his murderers, he gave up his spirit.
