  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Matthew 17: 10–13"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today’s Gospel describes the warning and instruction given by Jesus to Peter, James and John as they were coming down the mountain after witnessing Jesus’ Transfiguration. Jesus forbade them to give any publicity to what they had seen, because people were expecting a conquering political messiah with Elijah as his forerunner, and a powerful reformer who would destroy evil and restore justice in the land for the messiah to rule.



      The Expected Messiah. Then Jesus indicated that he was the expected Messiah, and that John was the Elijah they had been waiting for. John’s mission was to prepare the way for the first coming of the Messiah, as Elijah’s mission would be to prepare the world for the Messiah’s second coming at the end of the world. The scribes misunderstood and taught that Elijah would come before the first coming of Jesus, the Messiah, and Jesus told his disciples that for those who were willing to believe it, John the Baptist had served as Elijah in announcing His own coming and preparing the people to receive Him. Jesus also reminded his disciples that he would fulfill his role as the Messiah not by gaining political power but by his suffering and death.



      Life messages:



      1) Let us accept Jesus as our Lord and Saviour, who became our Messiah through his death on the cross.



      2) We do so by cooperating with our Saviour in our eternal salvation, by obeying his commandment of love and by following the instructions given by the Church Jesus founded.

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      All those who expected the Messiah knew that Elijah’s coming must precede that of the Messiah. Malachi, the last prophet in the Bible, concludes his book saying, “I will send you the prophet Elijah before the day of the Lord.” Further he explains the role of Elijah. After four hundred years, John the Baptist appeared fulfilling the predicted role of Elijah. He was like a messenger (angel) at the first Passover who prepared the people to enter into the Promised Land, to the promised salvation. Elijah comes to set things in order, to call people to true repentance which would lead to reconciliation. Jesus says that Elijah did come before him. Now there is no reason for the disciples to doubt. In our lives too sometimes conveniently, we bring our own justifications to follow our decisions.

    saintName: "Saint John of the Cross - Confessor, Doctor of the Church (1542-1591)"
    ref: "Saint John of the Cross - Confessor, Doctor of the Church (1542-1591)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      John was born premature in 1942. Given the kind of upbringing fate provided, he obtained his education at a paupers’ school. After that he worked for seven years in a hospital, simultaneously frequenting a Jesuit school at Medina. At 21 he became a Carmelite and strictly followed the ancient unmitigated rule of the Order in all its austerity and was ordained a priest four years later.

      At the persuasion of Saint Teresa of Avila he did for the Carmelite Order of men what she was doing for the nuns. Thus the first reformed house for Discalced Friars was established at Duruelo on 28 November 1568, with the first three friars took their vows in “surpassing poverty, austerity and fervour”. Other foundations followed in rapid succession, and for five years John of the Cross, as he now called himself, was confessor to Saint Teresa and her nuns at Avila. The reform caused much internal strife and passionate disagreements which led to his imprisonment for nine months and mistreated and slandered until his miraculous escape. But it was precisely during his incarceration that he had his greatest spiritual illuminations.

      John died on 14 December 1591 and his incorrupt body was transferred to Segovia in 1592. Beatified in 1675, canonized in 1726 John of the Cross is known as the supreme “Mystic of the mystics”, the Church’s Doctor (since 1926) of mystical theology for his masterly exposition of Catholic mysticism in his “Ascent of Mount Carmel”, “The Dark Night of the Soul”, “The Spiritual Canticle”, and “The Living Flame of Love”.
