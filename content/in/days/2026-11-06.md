  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 16: 1-8"
    audio: null
    body: |
      In today’s Gospel, Jesus tells us the strange parable of a steward who was a rascal to teach us that serving God is a full-time job, not a part-time job or a spare-time hobby. Jesus also teaches us that we should use, in matters spiritual and eternal, the same ingenuity and planning which business people show in the business world. The parable challenges us to use our blessings — time, talents, health and wealth — wisely and shrewdly, so that they will count for our reward in eternity. We are on the right road only if we use our earthly wealth to attain our Heavenly goal. The parable: In the parable, Jesus tells us how the slave-steward of an absentee landlord ingeniously cheated his master by his unjust manipulation of the master’s business clients when the steward had been caught red-handed in misappropriating his master’s wealth. His tricks were intended to make him the friend of his master’s debtors and gave him the prospect of becoming rich by working for them (or blackmailing them?) when he was fired by his master from the stewardship.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages</span>: 1) We need to be faithful in the little things of life: As Saint John Chrysostom said, "*Faithfulness in little things is a big thing."* Our future opportunities in the eternal service of God largely depend on our stewardship in handling the little opportunities we have had on earth. As Mother Teresa used to recommend, *“Do little things with great love.”* 2) We have to act shrewdly, trusting in the power and assistance of God. Let us make use of our resources — like Hope in God’s justice, Faith in God’s assistance, and Trust in God’s grace, the Mass and the Sacraments as sources of Divine grace and the Holy Bible as the word of God for daily meditation. 3) Let us remember that as God’s stewards we need to be prepared to give an account of our lives at any time.



      *(Fr Tony: https://frtonyshomilies.com/)*
  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      In the Gospel, the steward suddenly realises his vulnerability and uncertain future as he faces losing his job. He isn’t strong enough for manual labour, and he’s ashamed to beg, so he tries to win favour through dishonest means. The master commends his cleverness but ultimately disapproves of his dishonesty by firing him. Jesus warns us: “The children of this world are shrewder than the children of light.” This reminds us to resist dishonest solutions in difficult times. Instead, we should stay alert, guarding against the world’s influence when we feel weak. The Gospel calls us to use our gifts and resources to build God’s Kingdom by living Christ’s values. St Paul urges us to “stand firm in the Lord” and not focus on temporary things, which don’t bring true joy or an eternal crown.
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Leonard of Limousin, Abbot, Confessor (- c.559)"
    ref: "Saint Leonard of Limousin, Abbot, Confessor (- c.559)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      A nobleman at the Frankish Court of Clovis I, St Leonard is said to have been converted to Christianity by St Remigius of Reims (France). He became a monk, preached the Gospel in Gascony, and founded the Abbey of Noblac, which he governed until his death. Esteemed highly by King Clovis, he was granted the rare privilege of releasing from prison whomever he chose.



      St Leonard’s was one of the most popular cults in southern Germany, Austria and Bohemia, especially in the Middle Ages, and innumerable favours were noted through his intercession.



      He is revered as the Patron of the sick, the insane, of women in childbirth, and of prisoners.
