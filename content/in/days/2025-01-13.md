  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mark 1:14–20"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today’s Gospel describes the beginning of Jesus’ preaching and healing ministry and the call of the apostles who were to continue that ministry. Jesus started public ministry immediately after John the Baptist was arrested. Following John’s pattern, Jesus, too, invited the listeners to repent as a preparation for believing in the Gospel, or the Good News, of the Kingdom of God. </span>



      *Repentance* means an about-face turn to God resulting in a change of mind, heart, behaviour, and life. It also means sorrow for having refused God’s love and a resolution to make amends. </span>



      *Believing*in the Gospel demands from the hearers a resolution to take Jesus’ words seriously, to translate them into action, and to put trust in Jesus’ authority. </span>



      Jesus preached the* Gospel, *or Good News, that God is a loving, forgiving, caring, and merciful Father Who wants to liberate us and save us from our sins through His Son, Jesus. According to Mark, Jesus selected four fishermen, Andrew and his brother Simon (later named Peter by Jesus), with James and his brother John, right from their fishing boats. Jesus wanted these ordinary, hard-working people as assistants in ministry because they would be very responsive instruments in the hands of God.</span>



      Life messages: 1) In order to be effective instruments in the hands of God and to continue Jesus’ preaching, healing and saving ministry, we, too, need to repent of our sins on a daily basis and to renew our lives by cooperating with God’s grace, relying on the power of God.</span>



      *(Fr Tony: https://frtonyshomilies.com/)*

    body: |
      Jesus’ call of the four disciples implies two things: *cost* and *commission*. Though he did not explicitly mention the cost, the disciples knew what it meant – their relationship with him would take priority over all other relationships. This involved leaving the security of their life’s comfort zones and taking an active part in the apostolic commission of fishing people for their Master. “While God’s grace is always bestowed freely, it is never bestowed cheaply,” says Dietrich Bonhoeffer in *The Cost of Discipleship.* Our response to Jesus’ call necessitates that we sacrifice all that we consider dear to us, including that which gives us safety and security. It is only then that we can take up the commission of bringing people to him (fishers of men).</span></span>
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Hilary of Poitiers - Bishop Confessor, Father and Doctor of the Church (c. 315-367)"
    ref: "Saint Hilary of Poitiers - Bishop Confessor, Father and Doctor of the Church (c. 315-367)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Saint Hilary hailed from a noble Gallo-Roman family living in Poitiers, then an important city in the western part of central France. His parents, both pagans, saw to it that he received a thorough education in all the profane sciences of the day, none of which could furnish him with a satisfactory answer as to the destiny of man, his quest finally leading him to study the Christian scriptures whose conception of God in turn led him to seek baptism.</span>



      The conversion of a man of his age [30 years] and stature naturally aroused much interest; he so zealously defended and propagated his new-found Faith, both by pen and by word of mouth, that, although married and the father of a growing daughter, was elected Bishop of Poitiers in 350.</span>



      The Church was at that time afflicted by the devastating heresy of Arius of Alexandria: a denial of the divinity of Christ, a concept that was gaining ground, threatening to make deep inroads also into Gaul. The Archbishop of Arles in Southern France, an avowed Arian, tried to get the Synod of Beziers (356) to ratify the faulty doctrine. Hilary went there to defend the truth but was prevented from doing so by the Arian majority and, shortly afterwards, was banished by the Arian Emperor Constantius to distant Phrygia in Asia Minor.</span>



      Recognized as one of the most outstanding and intrepid defenders of the Faith, Hilary used his three-year exile to study the Greek Fathers and to write his great thesis on the Trinity. In 359 he attended the Synod of Seleucia at the invitation of the Eastern Bishops and later went to Constantinople with the Synod’s delegates. But the Emperor persisted in his Arian viewpoint and at the suggestion of his advisers who were dismayed by Hilary’s persuasive powers had the bishop sent back to Gaul, where in 361 he made his triumphal re-entry into his See. </span>



      Hilary died in 367 or 368 and was proclaimed Doctor of the Church by Pope Pius IX in 1851.</span>
