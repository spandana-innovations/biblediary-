    body: |
      Today’s Gospel passage describes Jesus’ secret journey to Jerusalem to participate in the Feast of Tabernacles (Booths), amid rumors of his possible arrest. But Jesus courageously made his public appearance in the crowd in Jerusalem and started teaching in the Temple. Naturally, people started wondering why the authorities did not arrest him.



      Jesus’ Messianic claim and the Jewish reaction: Jesus made two unique and seemingly blasphemous claims. 1) He claimed that he was the Messiah, God’s Anointed One. 2) Jesus made the additional claim that only he knew God as He is because Jesus had come from God. By this claim, Jesus contradicted the belief of the Jews that they had the perfect and final revelation of God given through Moses and the prophets. In addition, Jesus’ claim to be the Messiah, and the “Son of Man” prophesied by Daniel, with exclusive and intimate knowledge of God was, they thought, nothing but blasphemy. The Jews argued that a mere carpenter-turned-wandering-preacher from Nazareth could not be the Messiah because nobody was supposed to know where the Messiah would come from. According to Jewish belief, the Messiah would emerge quite unexpectedly from Mount Olivet, cross the Kedron Valley, enter the city of Jerusalem, be anointed by Elijah the prophet, take possession of the City and the Temple and establish His Messianic kingdom.

      John in his gospel continually speaks of the “hour”. It is based on the biblical moment of time called “Kairos”. Kairos is the time of decision and confrontation. It happens when God displays His power and calls people to a decision. The hour for Jesus is the Cross, when he reveals the name of God as suffering, enduring love. For the world, the cross is a sign of destruction and rejection. For Jesus it is the supreme moment and symbol of glory. Lent is a *Kairos*time for each of us – a time of confrontation and decision. It is through our participation in the Cross of Jesus that we have the hope of the Resurrection.

      St Louise De Marillac, *Widow (1591 - 1660)*



      Louise, the daughter of a military courtier, was a well-educated and socially prominent Parisian woman. At 16 she felt a strong desire to join the Poor Clares, but poor health intervened and five years later she was married to Antoine Le Gras, a secretary to Queen Marie de Medicis, and bore him a son, Michel.



      With pious Antoine’s approval, she devoted all her free time to works of charity among the poor in Paris, though many considered it unbecoming of her social status. In 1619 she made the acquaintance of St Francis de Sales and St Vincent de Paul. When her husband died in 1625, Louise placed herself entirely under the latter Saint’s direction, and spent herself in superintending the various confraternities of charity which he had founded for the care of the needy.



      This is regarded as the real beginning of the Sisters of Charity; for their benefit St Vincent de Paul began his famous spiritual conferences in 1634 which continued for 25 years. For 12 years there was no written rule or constitution, and no official approval was sought for the new Order. At a time when it was unheard of for nuns not to be enclosed, the services of these Sisters were urgently needed in the slums and hospitals. St Vincent wanted the fear of God to be their enclosure, holy modesty their veil, the parish church their chapel, a hired room their cell, and charitable works their Office. As their habit the Sisters adopted the blue-gray dress and white linen cornette of the peasant women in the Isle de France.



      Louise de Marillac died on 15 March 1660, followed 6 months later by St Vincent. By then there were more than 40 houses in France. Today there are over 4,000 houses and some 40,000 Sisters in all parts of the world. The community is a lay one with only private vows, and the original rule and constitution have never been changed until recently.



      Beatified on 9 May 1920, Louise de Marillac was canonized on 11 March 1934, by Pope Pius XI and, on 10 February 1960, named Patroness of all Christian Social Workers.
