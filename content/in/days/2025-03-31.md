      Reflection: 2



      According to John’s Gospel, after facing rejection by his hometown, Nazareth, Jesus went to Jerusalem in Judea for the Passover feast. From Jerusalem, Jesus returned to Galilee and to his headquarters, Capernaum, where people received him as a miracle-working preacher and prophet. Today’s Gospel describes how Jesus did a long-distance (telepathic) healing for the dying son of an official employed in the royal court of king Herod. Jesus was preaching at Cana, twenty miles away from Capernaum. Herod’s officer came on horseback from Capernaum to Cana urgently seeking Jesus’ presence for the healing of his dying son. He was desperate enough to swallow his pride and make a public request to a wandering preacher for this healing. Jesus’ critical comment on the lack of belief in the Pharisees did not discourage the official. With expectant Faith he pleaded with Jesus, reminding him of the seriousness of the illness. So, Jesus told him, “*Go; your son will live.” *With trusting Faith in the assurance Jesus had given him, the officer rushed back toward his house. On the way there, he received the good news that his son had been healed at the very time Jesus gave his assurance. The officer was so grateful that he and the other members of his family accepted Jesus as the promised Messiah without fearing the ridicule of fellow-officers and friends.



      Life message: 1) Miraculous healings can take place in our lives, too, if we approach Jesus the healer with true humility, trusting Faith, earnest prayer and the willingness to surrender ourselves to God’s will. We have the unfailing word of Jesus to rely on, only that, nothing less.

    title: Reflection
    ref: null

    body: |
      On many occasions, Jesus healed the sick by placing his hands over them or by touching them. In today’s Gospel, we read about a distant healing performed by Jesus. To the official who requested Jesus to come down to heal his son who was at the point of death, Jesus said, “Go; your son will live”. The official believed in the words uttered by Jesus and went back home to find his son healed. There are at least a couple of other instances of distant healing by Jesus in the Bible. In Mark 7:24-30 and Matthew 15:21-28, we find the healing of the daughter of a Syrophoenician woman. In Matthew 8:5-13 and Luke 7:1-10, we find the centurion’s servant healed by Jesus. Distance is no barrier for Jesus. Let us learn to trust in his power.

    body: |
      St Daniel was a German merchant who, on his frequent trips to Venice, came to know and admire the Camaldolese monks at Murano. So enamoured was he of their way of life that he spent long periods of time with them and finally decided to live like a hermit in his own house, under their direction, and donned their habit.



      A deeply religious man, much given to prayer, he distributed nearly all his worldly possessions in order to alleviate the misery of the Venetian poor. He was murdered by robbers in 1411.
