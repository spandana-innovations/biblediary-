      Reflection: 1



      By her immediate and wholehearted acceptance of God’s plan, Mary consents to be the mother of the Saviour and opens the way for his birth. She represents the entire human race in welcoming God’s offer of salvation. The Lord is with Mary and through her He has come to be with all of us. By the powerful presence of God in her life, Mary conceives the Son of God, our saviour. She therefore has a unique role in the salvation of humankind. Mary and Joseph accepted God’s plan and shared in it wholeheartedly. By doing so, we can also become partners of God in working out His project of salvation.</span></span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel describes the story of the Annunciation, explaining how God began to keep the promise He had made to King David through the prophet Nathan, that David’s descendant would rule over the world as its Messiah. The Archangel Gabriel’s salutation to Mary: “*Hail, full of grace*,” reminds us of God’s words to Moses at the burning bush (Ex 3:12), and the angel’s salutation to Gideon, (Jgs 6:12). Mary is described as *“full of grace”*, filled with God’s favour and graciousness. She is to be the new Ark, a tent and temple. God will be in her, literally and physically, and thus she will be the greater House God promised to David. Mary’s believing question, “*How can this be, since I am a virgin*?” is natural. That is why Gabriel reminds Mary,”*For with God nothing will be impossible.”* God will “empower” her (“*the Spirit will come upon you*”) and “protect” her (“*overshadow you*”). Luke’s narrative points out that the Child will not only be a distant grandson of David — He will be God’s own Son. *“He will be great and will be called the Son of the Most High, and the Lord God will give to Him the throne of His ancestor David.”*Mary does not require confirmation but responds in Faith. She agrees to carry out the Word Gabriel has addressed to her.</span>



      <span class="subhead">Life messages</span> </span>



      1) We need to be humble instruments in the hand of God, trusting in His power and goodness. St. Augustine reminds us that God Who created us without our permission cannot save us without our active cooperation. Hence, let us cooperate in the fulfilment of God’s plan for us with Mary’s trusting Faith and humility. </span>



      2) Like Mary, who brought God to us as Jesus our Savior, it is our duty to carry Jesus everywhere and bring Jesus to the lives of others around us through love, mercy, forgiveness, and service. “Let the soul of Mary be in each one of you to magnify the Lord. Let the spirit of Mary be in each one to exult in Christ.” (St. Ambrose). </span>



      3) We should treasure these words of the Gospel and use them often, for example, by practicing the Christian custom of saying the Angelus every day and reflecting on the five Joyful Mysteries of the Rosary. (Navarre Bible Commentary).</span>

      ‘Giving up’ something precious in life is a hard challenge! But only by giving up human interests in favour of God’s choices can one find heavenly bliss! The Annunciation is a witness to this great aspect of “giving up”. Mary had been betrothed to Joseph. But the plan of God intervened at this joyful, undisturbed, serene phase of her life. Life was calling Mary to choose “giving up” – giving up her dreams and sharing in the magnificent plan of God’s Will. Today, we are called to “give up” – our will in favour of God’s Will; our sinful tendencies to allow holiness to overshadow us; our selfish desires to make space for the needs of others. In life’s journey, choosing God’s ways over ours makes way for heavenly joy.

    ref: "Saint Dominic of Silos - Confessor"
    audio: null
    body: |
      A peasant by birth, Dominic came to know God in the solitude of a shepherd’s work, drawing him as it did into monastic life whereby he could be alone with his Creator. As Prior of the Benedictine monks at San Millán de la Cogolla, his refusal to part with certain possessions of the monastery claimed by Garcia III of Navarre, earned him the king’s wrath by way of exile. Received very kindly by King Ferdinand I of Old Castille, he was entrusted with reforming and rebuilding the materially and spiritually decrepit abbey of Saint Sebastian at Silos.



      Under his inspiration, rising to the challenge the monastery produced some of the best Christian Spanish art in their illuminated manuscripts; the Mozarbic Rite (a variant of the Latin Rite) and the Visigothic script of ancient Spain were preserved, making the monastery a leading centre of learning and liturgy; innumerable miracles of healing were reported, too, the two storied Romanesque cloisters of the abbey remaining architectural gems of Spain.



      Thanks to Blessed Jane de Guzman who owed the birth of her son in 1170 to his intercession, Dominic is considered the Patron of pregnant women. The son, named Dominic in his honour, founded the illustrious Order of Friars Preachers. In fact, until the Spanish Revolution of 1931, it was the custom for the Abbot of Silos to bring and leave the staff of Saint Dominic by the bedside of the queen whenever she was known to be in labour until the successful conclusion of the delivery. Further, the miraculous redemption of prisoners from Moslem captivity in the 13th century through his intercession has made Dominic of Silos the Patron Saint of Prisoners as well.
