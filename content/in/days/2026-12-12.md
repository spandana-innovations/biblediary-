  - key: homily

    ref: "Mathew 17:10-13"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today’s Gospel describes the warning and instruction given by Jesus to Peter, James, and John as they were coming down the mountain after witnessing Jesus’ Transfiguration. Jesus forbade them to give any publicity to what they had seen, because people were expecting a conquering political Messiah with Elijah as his forerunner, and a powerful reformer who would destroy evil and restore justice in the land for the Messiah to rule.</span></span></span></span>



      The Expected Messiah. Then Jesus indicated that He was the expected Messiah, and that John was the “Elijah” they had been waiting for. John’s mission had been to prepare the way for the first coming of the Messiah, as Elijah’s mission would be to prepare the world for the Messiah’s second coming at the end of the world. The scribes misunderstood; they taught that Elijah would come before the first coming of the Messiah. But Jesus told the disciples that (for those who were willing to believe it), John the Baptist had served as Jesus’ Elijah in announcing and preparing the people to receive a Messiah, who would fulfill the Messianic mission not by political power, but by suffering and death.</span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life message: </span></span></span>1) Let us accept Jesus as our Lord and Saviour, Who became our Messiah by dying for us on the cross. We do so by cooperating with our Saviour in our eternal salvation, by obeying Jesus’ commandment of love, by following the instructions given by the Church Jesus founded and by transforming our suffering to redemptive suffering. </span></span></span></span>

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      The scribes refused to accept Jesus by asking the question: ‘Is this Jesus of Nazareth the Messiah or not?’ According to them, Elijah should come again before the Messiah comes and the Messiah should liberate the Jews from the colonisers and make the nation of the Jews great. The problem of the scribes was that of ‘recognition.’ Jesus proclaims that John the Baptist is the Elijah who was supposed to come, yet neither he nor John the Baptist worked for fulfilling the Jewish dream of a politically strong Jewish nation and so disappointed the Jewish audience. Jesus’ approach to establishing the reign of God was not by a demonstration of power, but through an outpouring of love. He strengthened the peoples’ hope for peace and redemption through his words and deeds. Only by accepting and living out the unconditional love of the Father through Jesus Christ, we would be able to establish God’s kingdom in this world.</span></span></span>

    ref: "Our Lady of Guadalupe"
    audio: null
    body: |
      The feast in honor of Our Lady of Guadalupe goes back to the 16th century. Chronicles of that period tell us the story.



      A poor Indian named Cuauhtlatohuac was baptized and given the name Juan Diego. He was a 57-year-old widower, and lived in a small village near Mexico City. On Saturday morning December 9, 1531, he was on his way to a nearby barrio to attend Mass in honor of Our Lady.



      Juan was walking by a hill called Tepeyac when he heard beautiful music like the warbling of birds. A radiant cloud appeared, and within it stood an Indian maiden dressed like an Aztec princess. The lady spoke to him in his own language and sent him to the bishop of Mexico, a Franciscan named Juan de Zumarraga. The bishop was to build a chapel in the place where the lady appeared.



      Eventually the bishop told Juan to have the lady give him a sign. About this same time Juan’s uncle became seriously ill. This led poor Juan to try to avoid the lady. Nevertheless the lady found Juan, assured him that his uncle would recover, and provided roses for Juan to carry to the bishop in his cape or tilma.



      On December 12, when Juan Diego opened his tilma in the bishop’s presence, the roses fell to the ground, and the bishop sank to his knees. On the tilma where the roses had been appeared an image of Mary exactly as she had appeared at the hill of Tepeyac.



      <span class="subhead">Reflection: </span>Mary’s appearance to Juan Diego as one of his people is a powerful reminder that Mary—and the God who sent her—accept all peoples. In the context of the sometimes rude and cruel treatment of the Indians by the Spaniards, the apparition was a rebuke to the Spaniards and an event of vast significance for the indigenous population. While a number of them had converted before this incident, they now came in droves.



      According to a contemporary chronicler, nine million Indians became Catholic in a very short time. In these days when we hear so much about God’s preferential option for the poor, Our Lady of Guadalupe cries out to us that God’s love for and identification with the poor is an age-old truth that stems from the Gospel itself.
