  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mark 10:32–45"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today St Mark reports one of several clashes between Jesus and his disciples, as they make their way to Jerusalem, the city where Jesus will be crucified. Jesus and his disciples are clearly on different wavelengths. The difference between them is expressed in the very different questions they ask of each other. The question that James and John ask Jesus focuses on glory, honour, status. The question Jesus asks them back focuses on the rejection and suffering that lies ahead for him. “Can you drink the cup that I must drink, or be baptized with the baptism with which I must be baptized?” This was referring to the cup of suffering and the baptism of fire.



      What James and John requested showed their interest in self-promotion. The response of Jesus showed his interest in self-giving. At the heart of being discipleship is self-giving love, becoming the servant of others, and this will often mean taking the way of the cross, as Jesus knew from his own experience. James and John, and all of us, are being called to follow the one who did not come to be served but to serve, whose purpose in life was not to promote himself but to empty himself for others. It is only in following this way that we will receive that share in Jesus’ glory that was the focus of James and John’s request.

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      “Cup” and “Baptism” are symbols of Jesus’ passion and death on the cross. James and John expressed their willingness to participate in the cup and baptism of Jesus. Not only James and John, but all his followers are reminded that when they are not united with him in the way of the cross, it marks a failure in Christian discipleship. The way of the cross implies self-sacrifice and service to others after the example of Jesus who always lived for others, never for himself. The challenge of the cross is never absent from the life of a Christian. Pope Francis rightly says, “When we journey without the cross, when we profess Christ without the Cross, we are not disciples of the Lord.”

      St Cyril Martyr (-251?)

      True to his mother’s training, Cyril when a still a child, would repeat the name of Christ at all times and confessed that the utterance of this name moved him strangely. His father, a heathen and an idolator, hated Christianity and would beat and revile him for the faith he professed. Young Cyril bore all with Christian joy and when he was driven away by his father from the shelter of his roof he said he had lost little and would receive a great recompense instead.

      It being a time of general persecution, the governor of Caesarea, learning about Cyril, had the child brought before him. The persecutors initially tried tempting him in various ways to apostatize and, later, even led him to the place of execution and showed him the horrible instruments of torture. But no threats could make him show the slightest sign of fear. The Governor pitying perhaps his tender years, offered him freedom and besought him to return to his home and inheritance. But the blessed youth replied: “I left home gladly, for I have a greater and a better reward which is waiting for me!”

      Cyril was then taken to the fires as if for execution but was brought out once again to be re-examined. Still, he remained filled with his unwavering heavenly desire and protested against the cruel delay of his executioners. Blandishments and added threats were of no avail in moving the little confessor, and the judge suffered him at last to win the eternal crown he craved for!
