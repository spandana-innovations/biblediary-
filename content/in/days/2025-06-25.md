      Reflection: 1



      False prophets deceive people and lead hem astray. Externally they appear friendly and helpful, but internally they are like wolves in sheep’s clothing. As a good tree can be recognised by its good fruit, a good prophet can be recognised by the quality of his life and the values he upholds. Likewise a false prophet can be identified by the false values he clings to. The trees that do not bear good fruit are fit only to be cut and thrown into the fire. What is essential is to do the will of the Father as Jesus has revealed to us. God will be pleased with us only if we use the gifts and talents He has gives us for the good of the community. The works we do, the fruits we produce, show what kind of people we are. The deciding factor is not the actions themselves but the intentions with which the actions are done. </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP</span></span>*Reflection: 2



      In today’s Gospel passage, taken from the Sermon on the Mount, Jesus gives his Church a warning against false prophets and their false doctrines. Jesus compares them to wolves in sheep’s clothing and tells us we can recognize them by observing the lives they lead and the doctrines they teach.



      False and true prophets: The Old Testament speaks of false prophets and how they mislead God’s people. Jer 23:9-40 is a classic example. The prophet condemns the false prophets of Baal. The Old Testament gives three signs of true prophets: a) they honour God and promote the worship of the one true God; b) they care for the poor; c) they fight for justice. Modern false prophets in the Church try to remove the cross from Christianity, dilute sin, and avoid teaching about God’s judgment while teaching that morality is relative, which God abhors: “*Woe to those who call evil good and good evil, who change darkness into light and light into darkness*” *(Is 5:20*). They try to separate the people of God from the Magisterium of the Church. But modern true prophets lead exemplary and righteous lives, obey God’s laws and the Church laws, and demonstrate the virtues of Faith, Hope, Charity, Justice, Prudence, Fortitude, and Temperance. In addition, they produce the fruits of the Holy Spirit. *“The fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control” (Gals 5:22-23).*The pre -Vatican II *Baltimore Catechism*expanded this passage from Galatians to Twelve Fruits: “*Charity, Joy, Peace, Patience*, *Benignity*[Kindness], *Goodness, Long-suffering* [Patience] *Mildness*[Gentleness], *Modesty, Continency, Chastity* [three effects of Self-Control].



      Life message: 1) As Christians, we participate in the prophetic role of Christ. Hence, we have the duty of leading others to Christ by our exemplary Christian lives.

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      Jesus warns against the deceitfulness of false prophets who lead people astray. They may appear to be true and harmless, like wolves in sheep’s clothing, but they inflict lasting damage on the lives of those they claim to teach and lead. Jesus also gives his disciples a very practical tip on how to distinguish false leaders from true ones: “You will know them by their fruits.” Just as a good tree abounds in good fruits, a true leader can be identified by his life and virtues, his actions and conversations. And a false one can also be identified by the type of life that the person leads. In my own life’s quests, am I guided by false prophets who lead me to destruction, or by true prophets who guide me to life and happiness?

      A native of Vercelli in Piedmont (Italy), St William was born of noble parentage, both of whom died while he was still a child. At 14 he gave away all his inheritance before setting out on a pilgrimage to the great shrine of St James at Compostela, Spain. Not content with covering the great distance barefoot, he girded his body with iron bands for harsher penance. In response to divine revelation on his return to Italy, he took up life as a hermit atop the lonely mountain between Nola and Benevento in southern Italy. With more and more men, drawn by his sanctity and miracles, placing themselves under his direction, desirous of following his austere life, he founded a monastery in 1119 and built a church dedicated in honour to Blessed Virgin.



      William therefore left them and, Under the munificent patronage of King Roger I of Naples, William erected a number of new monasteries and convents. True to his name, St William wore his faith like a “strong helmet” right until his death in 1142.



      The hermits of Monte Vergine were for some time widely diffused throughout southern Italy, but the Holy See joined them to the Cassinese Congregation of Primitive Observance during the last century.
