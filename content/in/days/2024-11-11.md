  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 17:1–6"
    audio: null
    body: |
      “Scandals will inevitably arise, but woe to him through whom they come.” With these words Christ warns His disciples about the greatest sin against charity. The word scandal is often misunderstood to simply mean shock or astonishment. The true meaning of scandal is to lead someone into sin. We lead others to think or act sinfully, uncharitably, speaking against our neighbour, against the Church, by impure talk and by urging one to do something sinful. We give scandal by our actions in various possible ways: by offering opportunities or occasions of sin, giving someone too much to drink, by immodest actions or dress. Parents give scandal to their children by neglecting their duties as Christians, missing Sunday Mass, not paying their debts, not correcting their children when they should. Scandal is an outrage against the Majesty of God. He calls His children to Himself. The scandal giver keeps God’s people from answering that call.



      (Pray Today’s Gospel, Bernard C. Mischke O.S.C. and Fritz Mischke O.S.C.)
  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      “Temptations to sin are sure to come, but woe to the one through whom they come!” Occasions for sin may come our way, regardless of our faith. It is our duty to correct the offender and never scandalize them by having a bad influence on anyone. We are called to help each other on our faith journey and encounter with God. In the First Reading, Saint Paul instructs Titus on how to appoint elders in every town. A great degree of faithfulness, goodness, purity, prudence, self-control and much more qualities and grace are required to truly live the faith and lead others to it. Saint Paul Challenges Christian leaders to communicate God’s words carefully and reliably. If our life corresponds to what we preach, no one can blame or contradict us. Then our presence in the world has significance, credible and acceptable. Only when we grow in deep faith, we can see the miracles that God works.

    saintName: "Saint Martin of Tours, Bishop Confessor (c.316-c.397)"
    ref: "Saint Martin of Tours, Bishop Confessor (c.316-c.397)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      At the time of St Martin’s birth, his father, a pagan, was a military tribune stationed in Pannonia (Hungary). Some years later, he was transferred to Pavia in Lombardy, where, at 15, Martin was recruited to the imperial cavalry at Amiens (Gaul).

      One day Martin passed a half-naked beggar, shivering in the cold. Moved with compassion, he split his cloak in two and gave one half to the poor man. Later that night, Christ reportedly appeared to him wearing the cloak. (The other half has long been preserved as “St Martin’s Cloak’” in the chapel of the Frankish Kings.) Not long after this famous incident, Martin received baptism, and, as soon as he was discharged from military service, became a disciple of St Hilary of Poitiers. In 361 with Hilary recalled by imperial decree, Martin once more returned to Poitiers. A great many monks gradually gathered around him there, and later formed the famous Benedictine abbey of Ligugé, the first Gallic monastery. St Martin would leave his cell from time to time to undertake missionary journeys through central and western Gaul where idolatry was still rampant among the rural folk. One day in 371, called into the city of Tours (in France) by a ruse, a highly reluctant Martin was forced by popular acclaim into becoming the new Bishop of the vacant See.Martin’s administration of his diocese was characterized by firmness and justice, and by a great zeal for the purity of the faith against the Priscillianist and Arian heresies, on account of which he made several journeys to Treves (Trier), then the Emperor’s residence.
