  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 1:39–56"
    audio: null
    body: |
      The mystery of the Incarnation comes to ordinary people living ordinary lives, who have the willingness to respond to God’s call and the openness and generosity to do God’s will. Luke, in today’s Gospel, tells us how two seemingly insignificant women met to celebrate the kindness and fidelity of God. In the Gospel, one definition of discipleship is to listen to God’s word and then carry it out. Mary did both, to become the most perfect disciple. The incident also shows us how sensitive Mary was to the needs of Elizabeth, her older cousin, who had miraculously become pregnant in her old age.



      Mary’s visit to Elizabeth. There is a saying, “One who is on fire cannot sit on a chair.” Mary, carrying Jesus and filled with the empowering fire of the Holy Spirit, hurried to the mountain country, a distance of perhaps 100 miles, where Elizabeth lived, thereby conveying the Holy Spirit to her cousin and her child. Like all good Jews, Mary was prompted in everything she did by her commitment to God’s word in her life.



      The paradox of blessedness. Blessedness confers on a person both the greatest joy and the greatest task in the world. Nowhere can we see the paradox better illustrated than in Mary’s life. Mary was granted the blessedness and privilege of being the mother of the Son of God. Yet, that very blessedness was to be a sword to pierce her heart: one day she would see her Son hanging on a cross. So, to be chosen by God is often both a crown of joy and a cross of sorrow. God does not choose us to give us a life of ease and comfort, but in order that we may respond to His love by accomplishing His purposes.



      <span class="subhead">*Life messages:*</span>



      1) We should recognize the real presence of Emmanuel (God Is with Us) and say “yes” to Him: The Visitation of Mary reminds us that, through his holy ministry, Christ continues to be present among his people. Let us recognize and appreciate the truth that the same Christ “dwells among us” in the Bible, in the Sacraments, in the praying community, and in our souls.



      2) We should convey Jesus to others as Mary did to Elizabeth. We can make a real difference in the lives of others today by carrying Jesus to them. For that, we must be filled with the spirit of Christ, allowing his rebirth within us. Then Jesus will enable us to share his love with all whom we encounter, offering them humble and committed service, unconditional forgiveness, and compassionate caring service.



      (Fr. Tony: https://frtonyshomilies.com/)

      Mary’s arduous journey to the house of Zechariah to help her cousin Elizabeth is a testimony to her sensitivity to the needs of others and her disposition of selfless service. Her intervention at Cana is another example of her concern for others. Mary thus anticipates the future lifestyle of her son who came not to be served but to serve. She proved that she is the first and the perfect disciple of Jesus because the first among his disciples should be the last of all and a servant of all. True service to others involves coming out of our comfort zones and sacrificing our time and resources. “Persons who describe themselves as Christians and who are unable to reach out to others, to go and meet them, are not totally Christian," cautions Pope Francis.

      Visitation of the Blessed Virgin Mary

      This feast commemorates the visit of the Virgin Mary, pregnant

      with the child Jesus, to her cousin Elizabeth, pregnant with John the Baptizer. It was at the Annunciation that Mary was informed of old Elizabeth’s pregnancy (Lk 1:36), prompting her to go “with haste” (Lk 1:39) to be of help to her cousin, thereby commencing her involvement in the work of redemption. What followed is awe-inspiring! At the sound of Mary’s greeting, the child in Elizabeth’s womb is sanctified and cleansed of original sin (cf. Lk 1:44) as foretold by the Angel Gabriel to Zechariah (cf. Lk 1:15) and in a leaping instant begins his mission of bearing witness to the light. Indeed, as David danced for joy before the Ark of the Covenant of old, John, the last of the prophets of the Old Testament, leaps for joy before Mary, the Ark of the New Covenant that brings God’s presence into the world! Then, “ . . . . filled with the Holy Spirit”, Elizabeth boldly declared Mary the most “blessed” among women, the Mother of God (cf. Lk 1:41-42) — the first formal acknowledgement as it were of the “Theotokos”!

      The feast of the Visitation was first heard of in 1263, when it was adopted by the Franciscans, its observance extended to the whole Church in 1389 by Pope Urban VI in supplication for the end of the Great Schism.

      St Mechtildis of Edelstetten

      Virgin (1125-1160)

      Mechtildis was the daughter of Count Berthold and Countess Sophia of Andechs, in modern Bavaria, Germany. The noble parents founded a Benedictine monastery on their estate at Diessen, Bavaria and had their daughter admitted there when she was but five years of age.

      In due course of time, Mechtildis professed the Benedictine Rule and went on to become Abbess at Diessen. In 1153, she was appointed Abbess at Edelstetten by the Bishop of Augsburg. Convinced of the need for urgent reform at the Abbey, Mechtildis went about her task in right earnest in the face of fierce opposition, even at the cost of having to expel some of the stubborn elements.

      Greatly revered for her mystical gifts and miracles, Mechtildis went to her reward on 31 May 1160.
