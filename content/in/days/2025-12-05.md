      Reflection: 1



      The two blind men followed Jesus not only physically but also in faith. They recognised him as the “Son of David”, that is, the Messiah, and asked for his mercy. They believed that Jesus was able to heal them of their blindness. Jesus touched their eyes and healed them. Though they were blind before, they had the hope of seeing, they believed in Jesus and sought his help. And in spite of being told to keep their healing secret, they were so joyful and thankful that they spread the fame of Jesus in the whole district.



      Do we seek Jesus only to get his favours or because we reccognise him as the Messiah and follow him with faith and love?



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel describes Jesus’ miraculous healing of two blind men who approached him with trusting Faith. Blindness was common in Palestine because of the intense glare of the eastern sun, clouds of unclean flies, and people’s ignorance of the effects of cleanliness and hygiene. The two blind men followed Jesus from the street all the way to the house Jesus entered, loudly expressing their confidence in the “Son of David” and requesting mercy. Jesus found in these men what was required for receiving a miracle, namely a strong and expectant Faith, an earnest desire for vision, and a sincere prayer for mercy. Although they were instructed not to tell anyone of their healing, as soon as they were healed, they immediately expressed their gratitude by bearing witness to Jesus’ healing power throughout the town.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span> We, too, need light and eyesight because we are often blind to the needs and expectations of those around us and even living with us. We are also often blind to the presence of Jesus living in us and in others, to the blessings God showers on us, and to the protection God gives us every day. Hence, let us pray for the spiritual eyesight to realize and experience the presence of Jesus in ourselves and others, and for the good will to do good to and for others.

      In life’s darkness, some succumb, some despair. The Gospel of the day unveils two blind men, resilient amidst shadows. Seeking hope amid dependence, they pursue Jesus, the Light, shunning despair. These two blind men take us through four stages of spiritual life: 1) A state of blindness, 2) A state of seeking, 3) The state of faith, 4) The stage of proclamation. Maybe many of us are in a state of blindness – of inferiority complex, of inner brokenness, of family crisis, of being rejected and not accepted etc. Our state of blindness should not become a hindrance to seeking the Lord. Our seeking the Lord needs to be supplemented greatly with the vitamin of Faith! Let our blindness fuel our quest for divine revelation amidst life’s shadows!

    body: |
      Born in 439, Saint Sabas was a young nobleman of Cappadocia, Turkey, who travelled to Jerusalem and after spending some years in the desert, settled down to an austere life under the guidance of Saint Euthemius. Near the brook Cedron he later established the great Laura Mar Saba, which still flourishes. Here, the monks lived in separate cells, somewhat like the Carthusians today. Six other houses also owe their foundation to his zeal.



      In 491 Sabas was ordained priest and three years later made archimandrate of all the hermit colonies in Palestine. By setting an example of scrupulous observance of the rule, he was able to bring about much needed reform in their discipline. He was also famous for his charity towards the poor. A valiant champion of orthodoxy, he twice travelled to Constantinople to influence the Emperor against the Monophysite, Nestorian and Origenist heresies, and as an old man of 90 interceded with Justinian on behalf of the Samaritan rebels and obtained their pardon.



      Saint Sabas died on 5 December 532, revered as one of the founders of Eastern monasticism. His relics, stolen by the Venetians, are still venerated in Saint Mark’s Basilica at Venice.
