      Reflection: 1



      Jesus teaches the disciples the importance of kind and caring fraternal correction and how it is to be done in the spirit of charity, without hurting the offender but helping him or her towards self-improvement. Christian communities should be places of holy and brotherly relationships. Christians must settle disputes among themselves amicably. When even a small number of believers in Jesus are gathered in harmony among themselves, the Lord himself is present with them, with his power and grace. He is Emmanuel, God with us, when we do his work, help the poor or pray as a united group. </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      The first part of today’s Gospel is one of the difficult passages in Matthew’s Gospel to interpret. Many Bible commentators think that Jesus never said these things, that probably they were a later addition by the Church because 1) there was no organized Church at that time, 2) Jesus never considered a sinner as a hopeless case, and 3) Jesus loved Gentiles and tax collectors.



      The real meaning: What Jesus actually meant was, “Do whatever you can to make the guilty person realize and confess his fault, thus helping him to repair the damage he or she has done to his or her personal and communal relationships.” Jesus seems to suggest the following steps to repair a broken personal relationship:



      1) One-on-one encounter: If you are sure that somebody has wronged you, tell him lovingly and politely that he has hurt you.



      2) The group encounter: If the first step does not work, meet him again in the company of two or three wise and honourable persons and try to make the culprit realize what he has done wrong.



      3) Parish encounter: If steps one and two do not work, bring his case to the pastor or to the parish council or the Christian fellowship.



      4) Leave him to Lord’s mercy: If the culprit remains stubborn, like a Gentile or proud tax collector, continue to pray for him and leave him to God’s mercy.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages: </span>



      1) Let us have the good will and generosity to accept our mistakes and ask pardon and forgiveness from the offended victim.



      2) Let us also learn to forgive and forget the offenses done against us

    title: Reflection

    body: |
      Jesus talks directly to his followers to tell us what our fellowship ought to be like. If a brother or sister in the faith hurts you, angers you, saddens you, or does you wrong in any way, you go and talk to him/her about it directly, one-on-one. Not only does this manner of working out difficulties lead to forgiveness, but it also does so in a graceful way. The offended party isn’t dragging the offender through the mud. If it gets worked out here, no one else needs to know. Right here, Jesus gives a clear blueprint for how our communities might be holy places where holy relationships might flourish. And, it’s something that we need to practice until it is so ingrained in our DNA that we can’t imagine living another way. After all, for Jesus, there isn’t another way.

    body: |
      St Hippolytus, whose name means “a horse turned loose”, was a learned priest in Rome and the most important theological writer of his time. He claimed to have been a disciple of St Irenaeus, while St Jerome refers to him as “a most holy and eloquent man”.



      At first, however, he was known for his “holier-than-thou” attitude towards the Church, unafraid of censuring even the Pope. He criticized Pope St Zephyrinus for not being prompt in detecting and denouncing heresy. Attacking the contemporary Trinitarian Modalism (an assertion that the three Persons of the Trinity are merely modes, or self-manifestations of one and the same God) and Patripassianism (a theory that God the Father himself suffered on the cross) he propounded a doctrine on the Logos that was close to subordinationism (viz., the Son and the Spirit merely proceed from the Father and are therefore subordinate to him).



      In 235, Hippolytus was banished to Sardinia by Emperor Maximus. Here, sharing his exile was none other than Pope Pontianus himself, who, during his reign (230-235), had held a synod which confirmed the excommunication of Origen in Alexandria.



      The election of St Anterus ended the schism but his near instant martyrdom was followed by the election of Pope St Fabian (r.236-250) who brought the mortal remains of Hippolytus and Pontianus to Rome and accorded them a solemn burial — to Hippolytus in the cemetery of the Via Tiburtina and Pontianus in the crypt of St Callistus.



      <span class="subhead">Reflection:</span>*“Master teacher of humankind, the Church is enlightened by the Holy Spirit. She has two enemies: sin and ignorance. She does not reject discovery, knowledge, the natural and divine sciences, but rather examines, adopts and uplifts these so that they can make a precious contribution to her teaching”*(Blessed James Alberione).
