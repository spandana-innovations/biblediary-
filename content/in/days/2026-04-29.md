      Reflection: 1



      Most of the Jews did not believe in Jesus, and even those among hem who believed in his did not acknowledge him for fear of the Pharisees. And so, as a summary discourse, Jesus restated his challenge. For us too, to believe in Jesus Christ is to accept him and his entire teaching. His words come to us in the midst of so many concerns, and often we do not feel the urge to believe his words. When we disobey or neglect his word, it is actually Jesus we are rejecting. And to reject Jesus is to reject the Father who has sent him. To see Jesus is to see the Father. Our eternity depends on our rejection or acceptance of his word. Are we faithful to the word Jesus has spoken to us? Do we express through our deeds and attitudes the love and mercy of God our father.



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel text, taken from John’s Gospel, is a passage from the last public discourse of Jesus before his arrest and crucifixion. The main ideas in the passage are 1) Jesus’ relationship with the Father; Jesus’ role as the Light and Life of the world; and the criteria for His final judgment of us – Heaven or Hell 1) Jesus teaches us that he is one with the Father and he is the image of his invisible Father. Because He is one with the Father, the Father speaks through him and operates through him. Hence, those who accept Jesus and his message accept God the Father’s message. 2) Jesus claims that he is the Light and Life of the world. Psalm 27 exclaims, “*The Lord is my Light and my salvation!”* As Light, Jesus removes the darkness of evil from the world and from our souls, shows us the correct way to go in life, and gives us the warmth of his sharing, sacrificial love. As Light, God’s word enables those with *eyes of Faith* to perceive the hidden truths of God’s Kingdom. As the Life of the world, Jesus, by his words, produces the very *Life of God* within those who receive these words with Faith. 3) We are rewarded or punished eternally based on whether or not we accept Jesus and his teachings and, if we have chosen Jesus, whether or not we live our lives accordingly.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span> 1) As Christians, our duty is to reflect and radiate the light of Jesus in the darkness of evil around us by acts of sharing love, kindness, forgiveness, and humble service. 2) Let us ask for the strength of the Holy Spirit to choose Christ and his ideals every day and to reject everything contrary to Christ’s teachings.

      Jesus shows us that His mission is fully in conformity with the will of the Father, doing only what He was sent to do. Similarly, in Acts, the apostles faithfully follow the promptings of the Holy Spirit, setting out to spread the Gospel even in the face of challenges. Their faithfulness allowed the mission to flourish and gain new believers. These readings call us to reflect on our own commitment to the mission entrusted to us by God. Are we, too, listening for God’s Spirit and acting according to His will? Saint Catherine of Siena wisely said, “If you are what you should be, you will set the whole world on fire.” By faithfully living out our vocation and responding to the Holy Spirit’s promptings, we can bring light to others, just as Jesus and the apostles did, allowing God’s mission to grow through us.

    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Catherine of Siena - Virgin and Doctor of the Church (1347-1380)"
    ref: "Saint Catherine of Siena - Virgin and Doctor of the Church (1347-1380)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Catherine, the youngest of the 25 children of the Benincasa family of wool dyers in Siena, Tuscany (Italy), when still young, was favoured with celestial visions and began to practise severe austerities. At 16 she became a Dominican tertiary, lived for three years like an anchoress in her own home, and heroically undertook the care of advanced cases of leprosy and cancer which no one else would touch and, though meeting with nothing but abuse, succeeded in bringing about the sufferers’ conversion.



      Subsisting for long periods solely on the reception of the Eucharist and praying incessantly for sinners who refused to pray for themselves. At 28, she received the sacred stigmata, but in response to her pleadings to God, these remained outwardly invisible while she was alive. The most famous of her writings is her *Dialogue* in which the Father converses with her on the spiritual life.



      Catherine was instrumental in having Pope Gregory XI lift the interdict over Florence and finally return from Avignon to Rome in 1377. At the request of Gregory’s successor Urban VI she spent the last six years of her life in Rome, offering the sacrifice of her life for the unity and renewal of the Church. She was barely 33 when she died three long months after having had a seizure.



      Proclaimed saint in 1461 and Doctor of the Church in 1970, she is the Patron Saint of Italy, and is invoked against fire, pestilence and headaches.
