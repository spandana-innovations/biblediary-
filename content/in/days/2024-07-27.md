  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Matthew 13:24-30"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Poet Yeats wrote:“Things fall apart; the centre cannot hold; mere anarchy is loosed upon the world; the blood-dimmed tide is loosed and everywhere the ceremony of innocence is drowned.” How rightly he pictured the presence of evil in the world! However, not just evil, but also good, in fact, more good can be found in the world, as in a field where weeds grow with the wheat (Mt 13:25). But, why does God allow evil people to thrive with the good ones? Because, every sinner is potentially a holy person. By not eliminating evil from human lives, God gives every bad one time to be converted and to find his or her destiny in the end. The Israelites promised to abide by the laws God gave them through Moses. But at every turn of their spiritual journey, they violated them. Yet, God continued to care for them, for he knew they were in the right direction, in spite of their repeated unfaithfulness. We must not become impatient with ourselves. When a small child learning to walk falls down, it is not discouraged but picks itself up and tries again and again until it has mastered the art of walking. So too, if you fall, pick yourself up and try again.

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today’s parable of the wheat and the weeds tells us not to be hasty in our judgement of other people as bad, because our compassionate God patiently waits for the conversion of his ‘bad people’. Since we are all a combination of wheat and weeds, we should not consider anyone as a wicked person. As J.T. Adams says, “There is so much good in the worst of us, and so much bad in the best of us, that it ill behoves any of us to find fault with the rest of us”. We know of many ‘bad people ‘who made a ‘U’ turn with God’s grace. As God awaits repentant sinners to return to Him for mercy, we too must have patience and be kind to them for God’s time to transform them. Let’s live as healthy and exemplary wheat in God’s field, treating the weeds with compassion, and leaving the judgement to God alone.
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "St Innocent I - Pope, Confessor ( - 417)"
    ref: "St Innocent I - Pope, Confessor ( - 417)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Elected three days after the death of Pope Anastasius (27 December 401), this energetic and gifted Pope ruled the Church for 16 years. Innocent obtained from Emperor Honorius a drastic decree against the Manichaeans, Montanists and Priscillianists. While he was unsuccessfully trying to arrange peace terms at Ravenna between the Western Roman Emperor and Alaric the Goth, the latter captured and sacked Rome in 410.

      St Innocent did much to strengthen ecclesiastical discipline, decreed that Roman customs should be the general norm, and that “all ecclesiastical matters throughout the world are, by divine right, to be referred to the Holy See”. His zeal for the welfare of the Church embraced both the West and the East, but he was unable to get St John Chrysostom reinstated at Constantinople because the Eastern Emperor favoured the usurper Theophilus. St Innocent’s death on 12 March 417 was mourned deeply by the entire Church.
