  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mathew 1: 1-16. 18-23"
    audio: null
    body: |
      The feast is the birthday celebration of the mother of Jesus, our Heavenly Mother and the Mother of the Church. It is the birthday of an ordinary woman who was chosen to become the mother of an extraordinary Divine Child. The Church celebrates the death day of a saint as his/her feast day, considering it his/her “birthday in Heaven.” The three exceptions are Jesus’ birthday (Christmas), Mary’s birthday (September 8), and John the Baptist’s birthday (June 24). Mary’s birthday is celebrated because of her Immaculate Conception. John the Baptist, in Elizabeth’s womb, was filled with the Holy Spirit during Mary’s visitation of Elizabeth. We honor Mary because God *has done great things for her* (Luke 1:49), a) by choosing her as the mother of Jesus His Son, b) by filling her with His Holy Spirit twice, c) by making her the embodiment of all virtues (“*full of grace”),* and our Heavenly Mother and d) by allowing her to become the most active participant with Christ, her son, in our redemption.</span></span></span></span>



      The readings: (Mi 5:1-4; Rom 8:28-30; Mt 1:1-16, 18-23). </span></span>Romans 1:3 states that Mary was a descendant of David, and Matthew’s genealogy in today’s Gospel also supports this truth.</span></span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life Messages:</span></span></span> 1) Let us, as Mary’s children, give a suitable birthday gift to our Heavenly Mother. Every mother wants her children to inherit and acquire all her good qualities. Hence, the best birthday gift to Mary is for us to become holy children of a Holy Mother.</span></span></span></span></span>

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      The Nativity of the Blessed Virgin Mary celebrates the birth of the one chosen to bear Jesus, bringing the hope of salvation to the world. Mary’s birth marks a pivotal moment in God’s plan, as she was destined to be the Mother of God, full of grace and prepared from the beginning for this role. Through Mary, we see the mystery of God’s love unfolding in a humble and faithful life, devoted to His will. This feast reminds us that God works through ordinary lives to accomplish extraordinary things. Mary’s openness to God’s grace allows the light of Christ to enter the world. By reflecting on Mary’s birth, we are invited to consider our own openness to God’s call and to trust that, like Mary, we are loved and chosen to bring His light to others. At the same time, let us all make greater efforts to promote the dignity of all mothers and protect all women. As Pope Francis says, “To hurt a woman is to insult God, who from a woman took on our humanity. He did not do it through an angel; nor did he come directly; he did it through a woman.”

    audio: null
    body: |
      The Church regards the day of a saint’s death a “birthday”, and rightly so! For it was on that day that the elect ended his/her life on earth to be born anew to the real life in heaven. There is, however, an exception made whereby the Church celebrates the natural birth of three persons, viz., Jesus, Mary and John the Baptist.</span></span></span></span>



      The birth of a child is always a joyful event in the family. Indeed, as Rabindranath Tagore says, “Every child comes into the world with the assurance that God is not yet fed up with the human race.” Today we celebrate the birth a woman who came as “the dawn of hope and salvation to the whole world”. </span></span></span></span>



      “Having been destined to become the Mother of the Eternal Word, this Child was enriched with so great a grace that even at the moment of her Immaculate Conception she exceeded all the Saints and Angels in sanctity, for she was given a higher order of grace, which corresponded to her dignity of Mother of God,” exults St Alphonsus de Liguori.</span></span></span></span>



      Mary, the new Eve, is the Mother of the Church and a mother to each one of us. She is the delight of the Father, the joy of the Son and the faithful spouse of the Holy Spirit who would descend on her to form Christ.</span></span></span></span>



      Today’s feast, with its origin in the Orient, first celebrated in Jerusalem in the fifth century, and introduced in Rome in the eighth, helped fix the date of the “Immaculate Conception” at 8 December.</span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Reflection:</span></span></span> *“Today the Virgin is born, tended and formed, and prepared for her role as Mother of God who is the universal King of the ages”* (St Andrew of Crete).</span></span></span></span>
