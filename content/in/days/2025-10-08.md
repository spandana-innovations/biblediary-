    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      As devout Jews, the disciples of Jesus used to pray at specified times. But by observing how Jesus prayed they discovered a new kind of in intimacy with God. They requested him to teach them to pray. And he taught them the Lord’s Prayer. It is the model of perfect prayer. Jesus wants his disciples to pray approaching God as a loving and caring Father. They should hold His name holy and do His will, ask pardon from Him, pray for their daily nourishment and for protection from evil. Though we can pray for other intentions also, as some of the prayers in the Bible show, we should include all these elements in our prayer. </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2The context: The disciples were fascinated by watching their Master Jesus at prayer. They knew that John the Baptist had taught his disciples how to pray. In response to the request made by one of the apostles, Jesus taught the beautiful prayer, *Our Father.* The*Catechism of the Catholic Church* teaches that the Our Father “is truly the summary of the whole Gospel” (CCC #2761).



      The great mystical Doctor of the Church Saint Teresa of Ávila gave this advice while praying the Lord’s Prayer: “Much more is accomplished by a single word of the Our Father said, now and then, from our heart, than by the whole prayer repeated many times in haste and without attention.” And Saint Thérèse of Lisieux said that the “Our Father” was one of the prayers she prayed when she felt so spiritually barren that she could not summon up a single worthwhile thought.



      A prayer in two parts: In the first part of the prayer, we address God, lovingly acknowledging Him as our Heavenly Father, praising and worshipping Him. Then we ask Him that His Holy Will may be done by us in our lives on earth as perfectly as it is done in Heaven. In the second part, we ask our Father’s blessings on our present time (daily bread), our past (forgiveness of sins) and our future (protection against the tempter and his temptations). In this part we also invite the Triune God into our lives. We bring in 1) God the Father, the Provider, by asking for daily bread, 2) God the Son, our Savior, by asking forgiveness for our sins and 3) God the Holy Spirit, our Guide, Advocate, Comforter, and Illuminator, by asking for protection and deliverance from evil.



      Special stress on spirit of forgiveness: In this prayer, Jesus instructs us to ask for forgiveness from others for our offenses and to give unconditional forgiveness to others for their offenses against us as a condition for receiving God’s forgiveness ourselves.

      “Lord, teach us to pray” is the fervent plea of one of the disciples of Jesus; he is certainly voicing the sentiments of all those present with him and others too. They see Jesus praying, in deep encounters with his Father. Saint Luke’s Gospel is also referred to as the ‘Gospel of Prayer’ because ‘Jesus at prayer’ is highlighted several times. The disciples are also eager to pray the way Jesus does. Jesus teaches them what we commonly call today ‘the Lord’s Prayer’ or the ‘Our Father’. The prayer has all the dimensions of what a perfect prayer should be: of praise and petition, of a personal relationship with God and at the same time, having the courage to forgive others.

      S</span></span></span>ergius and Bacchus were said to have been officers of the Roman army on the Syrian frontier; Sergius being described as commandant of the recruits’ school and Bacchus as his subaltern. They were personal favourites of the Emperor Maximian, until one day when he went into the temple of Jupiter to offer sacrifice he noticed that they did not enter with him.</span></span></span>



      Ordered to take part in the sacrifice, both of them refused and professed their faith in Christ, much to the dismay of the Emperor. They were soon stripped of their arms and ranks and instead paraded through the streets, dressed in women’s clothing. But none of these humiliations and threats could change their hearts.</span></span></span>



      They were then dispatched to Risafe in Mesopotamia, where the governor had them scourged so severely that St Bacchus died under the lash. His body was thrown out on the highway, where vultures protected it from being attacked by dogs—an incident recorded about several other martyrs. St Sergius was made to walk a long distance wearing shoes with nails thrust through into his feet, and was then beheaded.</span></span></span>



      In 431, Alexander, Metropolitan of Hierapolis, restored and blessed the church over the grave of St Sergius, whose walls in the middle of the sixth century were plated with silver. The church at Risafe was one of the most famous in the East, and Sergius and Bacchus became the heavenly protectors of the Byzantine army, with the two Theodores, Demetrius, Procopius and George.</span></span></span>
