      Jesus is speaking to his apostles after the Last Supper.



      Scripture lessons: In this Last Supper discourse, Jesus explains his Divine origin and his relationship to God his Father in clear terms without using any metaphors. The apostles acknowledge that they understand the Divinity of Jesus. But Jesus prophesies that they will soon desert him and seek their own safety, while he will be arrested, brought to trial and crucified. Our Faith is tested every day when we live in a world filled with agnostics, atheists, and pleasure-seekers who see true believers as superstitious people and hate them. That is why Jesus gave his apostles and all his future disciples the assurance of the anointing of the Holy Spirit Who would strengthen them and guide them. The Second Vatican Council teaches in connection with this passage: “The Lord Jesus who said `Be of good cheer, I have overcome the world’ (John 16:33), did not by these words promise complete victory to his Church in this world. This sacred Council rejoices that the earth which has been sown with the seed of the Gospel is now bringing forth fruit in many places under the guidance of the Spirit of the Lord, who is filling the world” (Presbyterorum Ordinis, 22).



      <span class="subhead">*Life messages:*</span>



      1) Our Faith is firmly rooted in the Divinity of Jesus demonstrated by his Messianic miracles, most of which were foretold by the prophets. The Resurrection of Jesus is the Miracle of miracles proving Jesus’ Divinity beyond doubt.



      2) We need to get our daily infusion of spiritual strength by recognizing the presence of God – the Father, the Son, Jesus, and the Holy Spirit — living within us wherever we are, and by communicating with our indwelling God in prayer.

      The Gospel text of today presents Jesus as absolutely fearless despite all odds being stacked against him. The conviction that he was doing his Father’s will and the belief that “the Father is with me” made him fearless. He exhorted his disciples and his future followers to take courage in spite of the tribulations they would face because he would definitively defeat all the forces of evil through his resurrection. The victory of Christ empowers them to accept suffering, hatred and persecution in the world with courage and serenity as Jesus did. Open wide the doors to Christ!”

      Our Lady of Fatima

      On 13 May 1917, in that third year of the First World War which was to cost eight million lives, important events were taking place simultaneously — in Russia, communistic, atheistic Bolshevism was being organized under the leadership of Lenin and Trotsky who had just arrived in St Petersburg; in the neighbourhood of the little Portuguese town of Fatima, the Mother of God herself deigned to appear in the first of six apparitions to three children, to make through them one final appeal to our materialistic, war-torn world.

      “In a spirit of sacrifice to our Lord, are you willing to accept everything he may send you as reparation for the sins by which his majesty is offended, as a means for converting all sinners, and as atonement for the blasphemies offered to the Immaculate Heart of Mary?” A simple “yes” being their reply, she urged: “Then pray the Rosary often and devoutly, for the peace of the world!” “Recite the Rosary daily, and after the Gloria Patri of each decade add: O my Jesus, forgive us our sins! Save us from the fires of hell! Lead all souls to heaven, especially those who most need thy mercy!”

      And on 13 October, she declared: “I am the Lady of the Holy Rosary! I have come to ask all humankind to do penance and to ask pardon for its sins! Do not add to the countless outrages by which our Lord is already so offended, especially by sins of impurity.”

      St Mary Dominica Mazzarello

      Virgin (1837-1881)

      When the Salesian Congregation, founded in 1859, was firmly established, the Blessed Virgin urged Don Bosco to extend to girls the good work his Salesians were doing for the poor boys. Shortly after, the Saint had a providential encounter with Fr Pestarino, the saintly Parish Priest of Mornese, a small vine-clad village in Piedmont, near Geneva, Italy. Fr Pestarino introduced him to a flourishing sodality of girls he himself had founded in his parish, viz., the Pious Union of the Daughters of Mary Immaculate. This sodality proved to be the cradle of the Daughters of Mary Help of Christians.

      The head and heart of this group was Mary Mazzarello. Born at Mornese on 9 May 1837, into a humble, God-fearing peasant family, she was brought up in an atmosphere of honesty and simplicity. Cheerfulness, gentleness, earnestness and thoroughness were the outstanding traits of her character, and she had very early shown unmistakable signs of deep insight into things Godly. Tender love for Jesus in the Blessed Sacrament, and for his Blessed Mother, were the distinguishing marks of her piety.

      While nursing the sick during an epidemic, Mary contracted Typhoid. Her robust health thus impaired, Mary realized that she would never again be fit for strenuous work. An inspiration flashed across her mind — she would learn dress-making, and then, together with her friend Petronilla, set up a workroom where young girls would come to learn the art of loving God in and through sewing. The idea germinated. The two friends entered upon their new venture, resolved that every stitch would be an act of love for God.

      As the enterprise took shape, Mary Mazzarello had a clear vision of her mission in life. As she walked along a country lane one day, she beheld a spacious building peopled with young girls and Nuns. It had never stood there before! Her bewilderment increased when she heard a voice saying: “I entrust them all to you!”

      Soon enough, the “workroom” became home to some motherless girls, and five more sodalists asked to share in the good work that was being done. Next came a festive oratory, to which the girls of Mornese were irresistibly drawn, much to the relief of their mothers and that of Fr Pestarino. Thus Mary and her little band wended their way in the direction Don Bosco himself was guided.

      The incorporation of the group into the Salesian family took place when, on 5 August 1872, Don Bosco gave Mary and 14 other members, the Religious Habit and the Rules he had drawn up for them, and called them the Daughters of Mary Help of Christians. The very next day Sr Mary Mazzarello was compelled to accept the superiorship of the new Congregation which she went on to govern with rare prudence and wisdom till her death in 1881. To her Don Bosco was God’s mouthpiece and so sought and closely followed his guidance, even to the minutest detail. Making his aims and spirit, in all their native charm, her very own, she bequeathed the same to her daughters to the end of time.

      Mary Mazzarello was canonized in 1951. Today the Daughters of Mary Help of Christians, known also as Salesian Sisters, number 16,543 in 1,272 institutions all over the world.
