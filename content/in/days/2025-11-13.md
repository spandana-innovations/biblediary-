  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 17:20–25"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      Jesus says that the kingdom of God comes not in any spectacular way but quietly. In fact it has already come and is at work through the preaching and miracles of Jesus. Those who believe the Good News of Jesus already enjoy this kingdom. And the second coming of Christ will light up the entire world, and all will be aware of his return. We should always be prepared for him so that his return will not catch us off guard. At the same time, Jesus asserts that he must first go through his Passion and death before he rises in glory. When sufferings come, we should not fall into despair but deepen our faith and hope in Jesus.



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      The Jews believed that the sudden and unexpected arrival of the promised Messiah would be accompanied by special signs. They also believed that he would be a political Messiah, who would rule Israel forever after overthrowing all other rulers. Hence, they asked Jesus about the signs accompanying his arrival as the Messiah – if he were the Messiah.



      Jesus’ reply: Jesus replied that the kingdom of God was already *within them,* and that was the greatest messianic sign. The Greek word we translate as “within” means both *within you* and *among you*. Considering the kingdom of God as within you, we are to understand that the Messiah is going to rule the hearts and minds of individuals, creating a revolution in human hearts and converting them from stony hearts to Spirit-filled loving, merciful, and compassionate hearts. Considering the kingdom of God as among you, we are to understand that God Himself is present among His people in the Person of Jesus of Nazareth, representing the Kingdom of God and doing God’s will in the most perfect way possible. Jesus also warned the Pharisees not to search for the Messiah anywhere else. He would appear again in Jesus’ Second Coming, quite unexpectedly, and as unmistakably as a flash of lightning that “lights up the sky from one side to the other.”



      <span class="subhead">Life message</span>: Let us be Kingdom people by allowing Jesus the true Messiah to have complete control of our lives. Let us allow Him to rule our lives by giving priority to him in all our actions.

      In Luke 17:20-25, Jesus talks about the Kingdom of God. He envisions a world where justice, love, and compassion prevail. Jesus embodies these values. He says the Kingdom is here among us; we just need spiritual insight to see it. The Kingdom is not some distant, unattainable concept. It is already present in our midst through the teachings and actions of Jesus. We don’t need to search far and wide; what we require is a spiritual perspective to recognise and experience the Kingdom of God right where we are. When we face suffering, we often complain and miss the opportunity for personal growth and a deeper connection with God’s vision for our lives. How can we see and experience God’s Kingdom in our everyday lives, even during tough times?
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Stanislaus Kostka, Confessor"
    ref: "Saint Stanislaus Kostka, Confessor"
    audio: null
    body: |
      The second of 7 children, Stanislaus hailed from high Polish nobility. At 14 he and his elder brother Paul were enrolled in the newly opened Jesuit College at Vienna. During their three years in Vienna the brothers lived for a time in the house of a Lutheran, where Stanislaus grew rapidly in piety and religious fervour. Paul, however, became so violently antagonistic toward his “hermit” brother that the latter, under continued nervous strain, finally fell ill. Worse, living in a Protestant household he was even denied the Sacraments, but he saw angels, attended by St Barbara, bring him Holy Communion one day. Our Lady too appeared to him and, advising him to become a Jesuit, completely cured him.



      Given his father’s stout objections, the Jesuits in Vienna dared not accept Stanislaus! So he quietly slipped out of the house early one morning, changed into peasant’s clothing in the nearby woods, and set out on foot for Augsburg, some 450 miles away in Germany, where he met St Peter Canisius, the German Provincial. From there he was sent on to Rome with recommendation—another 800 miles on foot!—where he was accepted by the General, St Francis Borgia. His delicate constitution notwithstanding, he practised all manner of penance as a Novice, and God bestowed numerous supernatural favours on him.



      In the 10th month of his novitiate, Stanislaus took critically ill and, five days later, i.e., on the feast of Our Lady’s Assumption, succumbed to fever.



      Stanislaus Kostka, canonized in 1726, is the Patron of novices in certain houses of formation and is also invoked against palpitation and in dangerous illnesses, especially against dying without the Viaticum.
