      Reflection: 1



      Today’s Gospel shows that the birth of John the Baptist was an act of God’s mercy and an occasion for his parents and neighbours for rejoicing. Both his parents said his name was John, in accordance with what the angel had told Zachariah. From the time the angel met him in the Temple till the child was named, Zachariah was unable to speak, because he initially did not believe the angel’s message. But both he and his wife lived a life of faith and prayer. They waited patiently and with hope for the fulfilment of God’s promise communicated by the angel. Faithfulness, prayer, patience and hope will lead us in the end to rejoice in God’s favour.



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel describes the birth and naming of St John the Baptist, the last Old Testament prophet. He was given the mission of heralding the promised Messiah and of preparing the Chosen People to welcome that Messiah by preaching to them repentance and the renewal of life. John was born to the priest, Zechariah and his wife, Elizabeth in their old age. Today’s Gospel passage describes John’s birth, Circumcision, and Naming ceremony.



      A miraculous birth and an event of double joy: His elderly parents rejoiced in John’s birth, as he was a gift from God in their old age. Since the child was a boy, all their neighbors rejoiced with them, and the village musicians celebrated the birth by playing their joyful music. The Naming followed the baby’s Circumcision, and Elizabeth insisted that the child should be named John (which means “the Lord is gracious”), the name given him by the Archangel Gabriel when he spoke to Zechariah. Appealed to by the gathered people, the mute Zechariah approved that name by writing, “His name is John.” At that action of obedient surrender to the Lord God, the priest’s speech was restored, and he loudly proclaimed the praises of God for blessing him with a son and Israel with her Deliverer, whose herald his son would be.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span>



      1) We need to pray for our parents and be thankful to them for the gift of life, the training, and discipline they have given us, and the love and affection they have lavished on us. Let us ask God’s pardon if we are, or were, ungrateful to them, do/did not take proper care of them in their illness or old age or ever inflicted pain on them.



      2) We need to remember and pray for our godparents who sponsored us in Baptism, which made us children of God, brothers and sisters of Jesus, heirs of Heaven, and members of Jesus’ Mystical Body, the Church.



      3) We should have the courage of our Christian convictions as John the Baptist did, and we should become heralds of Christ as the Baptist was, by our transparent Christian lives.

      In our spiritual journey, there are moments when we find ourselves in a “mute” mode with God. The couple of the day – Zechariah and Elizabeth – underwent this “mute” experience. Though advanced in age, and living a life of prayer and faithfulness, they were not blessed with the gift of a child. But then came the precious moment of divine revelation that ‘his name is John’ when fresh joy radiated their lives! We are taught an important lesson: “God’s delays are certainly not His denials!” In our “mute” moments, let’s remember the oft-heard truth about three ways that God answers our prayers: “Yes” to our desires, “Wait” for His perfect timing, or “No” for His greater plans. Let us be patient in faith, hope and love in our ‘mute’ moments so that we may experience the gracious moment that says, “his name is John!”

      Thorlák Thórhallsson is one of the three saints venerated in Iceland, the other two being Saint Jon Ogmundson and Blessed Guthmund the Good. The Vikings came in the 8th and 9th centuries, and by the beginning of the 10th century the country was divided into two dioceses, Skálholt and Holar.



      Born in 1133, Thorlák was ordained deacon at fifteen and a priest at eighteen. He was sent to England and France for further studies where he became ‘Canon Regular of Saint Augustine’ and, full of reforming zeal, returned to Iceland in 1161, a time of clerical laxity in Iceland. Thorlák devoted much time and energy to study and ministry. When a certain heirless farmer died, he left Thorlák land for a monastery. Here Thorlák established a monastery of Canons Regular, with himself as Abbot.



      In 1178, as Bishop of Skálholt, Thorlák set into motion diocesan reforms in right earnest, striving to improve spiritual standards and ecclesial discipline. Encouraging others to a holy life by personal example, he imposed the observance of clerical celibacy, put a stop to lay interference in Church affairs and attempted to do away with simony that was then rampant, despite stiff opposition and with the support of only his Metropolitan, Archbishop Saint Eystein of Norway who had consecrated him.



      After fifteen years as bishop, Thorlák, well into his 60th year, resolved to resign his bishopric and return to monastic life but death intervened on 23 December 1193. Within five years, however, in recognition of his work and virtues, he was canonized by the althing, i.e., the local assembly of bishops, the absence of papal validity notwithstanding.



      <span class="subhead">Reflection: </span>“The bishop, in the spirit of Jesus Christ, is a father to all, and has a unique goal in his diocese: to shepherd the flock entrusted to him by the Holy See, to watch over them with word, example, deeds, the sacraments and government till they reach the eternal sheepfold in heaven” (Blessed James Alberione).
