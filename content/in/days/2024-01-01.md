    body: |
      How wonderful it is to begin the New Year with the words of blessing on our lips from the First Reading of the day, “May the Lord bless you and keep you…”! As we celebrate the Solemnity of Mary, Mother of God, we are reminded of the fact that everyone needs a mother to mediate our beginning in human form. The Gospel Reading presents the reactions of Mary and the shepherds at the Birth of Jesus. The shepherds express their joy externally whereas Mary treasured up and pondered over the events in her life. The shepherds come, see and verify what had been told them and to share the news with others. They depart with praise and glory to God. Mary is the first believer and her life was deeply connected with the life of Jesus. We shall follow Mary and the shepherds to deepen our faith and to share it with everyone!
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Mary, Mother of God"
    ref: "Mary, Mother of God"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Our attention, focused at Christmas on Jesus, the Incarnate Word of God, today centres on Mary, his Virgin Mother. The Church’s affirmation of Mary as Mother of God implies not that Jesus’ divine nature comes from Mary but that his human nature was formed from her humanity, the two natures uniting in her womb in the second person of the Blessed Trinity. Being Son of God, it follows that Mary is the Mother of God.



      Paul’s assertion, “God sent the spirit of His Son into our hearts, crying out ‘Abba, Father!’” (Gal 4:6) helps us realize that Mary is also the Mother of us all having been made so at the foot of the cross by Jesus himself.



      The title, “Mother of God”, dates back to the 3rd/4th century, when its Greek form, “Theotokos” (God-bearer) became the pivotal point of the Church’s teaching on the Incarnation. The Ephesian Council confirmed the title in 431 and Pius XI, on Christmas Day 1931, through Lux Veritatis, enjoined the celebration of its feast on the whole Western Church.



      In Fulgens Corona (1953), Pius XII avers that from the title “Mother of God” flow “all the privileges and graces with which her soul and life were adorned in such an extraordinary manner and measure.” Paul VI’s Marialis Cultus declared that this feast commemorates Mary’s role in the mystery of salvation and exalts the singular dignity this mystery brings to the “Holy Mother through whom we were found worthy to receive the Author of Life”.



      <span class="subhead">Reflection:</span> “The divine maternity is the pinnacle of her greatness, the culminating point to which all the privileges of our Mother converge and from which they radiate. . . . Whoever understands her maternity, has understood Mary” (Joseph Marie Perrin, OP).
