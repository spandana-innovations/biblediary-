      Reflection: 1



      After they met the angel at the tomb of Jesus, the women were running with great joy to tell his disciples when the Risen Jesus suddenly met them. They worshipped him, and he told them to go and tell his disciples to go to Galilee where they would meet him. The guards at the tomb of Jesus were bribed by the chief priests to spread the story that the body of Jesus was stolen by his disciples.



      The Risen Lord let himself be seen by those who loved him and were looking for him. He was not visible to the others, but he appeared several times to his disciples. The Christian community, including all -- its leaders, every man and woman – should proclaim the Resurrection and bear witness to it by the way they live, in spite of obstacles, opposition and persecution. For this purpose the Spirit of the Lord works with them and empowers them.



      *— Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel describes how the two Marys who had been at the foot of the cross – Mary Magdalene and “the other Mary” (sister or cousin of Jesus’ mother) – went to the tomb of Jesus early on Sunday morning. They were shocked at seeing an open tomb without Jesus’ body in it. As they ran back with fear and joy to report the news to the apostles, the risen Jesus greeted them and told them to inform the apostles that he would meet them in Galilee. The guards, too, went to the chief priests to report the Resurrection of Jesus. But they were promptly silenced by the Temple authorities who bribed them to spread the false news that Jesus’ body had been stolen by his disciples.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages: </span>



      1) The Resurrection of Christ is the most sublime and foundational truth of our Faith. It is the presence of the risen Lord everywhere that gives meaning to our worship and prayers, because Jesus is present everywhere to hear our prayers and grant our petitions.



      2) The Risen Lord is present in the Holy Eucharist, in the Bible, in the praying assembly, and in believing Christians, so we need never be out of contact with him.



      3) Let us renew this fundamental belief of our Faith every day and let us recognize and serve the risen Christ in everyone around us, thus becoming the Marys, “apostles to the apostles”.

    body: |
      Today, we celebrate the joy of the resurrection and bear witness to the transformative power of the risen Lord in our lives and the world. In today’s gospel passage, the women who witnessed the angel at the tomb and later encountered the risen Jesus provided testimony that confirmed the reality of His resurrection. Their firsthand accounts served as credible and powerful evidence. The women were eyewitnesses to the angel’s message and their encounter with the risen Jesus. Their testimony was based on what they had personally seen and experienced, making it a compelling and reliable account. Today’s gospel passage offers a rich context for reflection on the resurrection of Jesus. It highlights the emotional impact of encountering the risen Christ, the power of truth, the importance of testimony, and the challenges of opposition and rejection.

    audio: null
    body: |
      Born in Aosta in the Italian Alps, Saint Anselm came from the noble Lombard family. After the death of his mother, Ermenberga, a pious Burgundian woman, he fled home on account of his father’s violence and harshness. In 1060 he took the Benedictine habit at Bec (Normandy), where his illustrious countryman Blessed Lanfranc had started his famous school. Outstanding for his learning, he was within a span of three years made Prior and, after another 15 years, Abbot.



      In 1093 he was made Archbishop of Canterbury, in the hope that he would tackle the encroachments of King William the Red. Reconciliation was effected in 1107, when the king relinquished his claim to investing Bishops and Abbots.



      Gentle and self-effacing by nature, Anselm was unyielding on matters of principle. A man of fearless zeal, he strongly denounced the slave trade and staunchly defended the *Filioque* (“and from the Son”), a doctrine that indicated matter-of-factly that the Holy Spirit proceeded from the Father *and* the Son.



      His thinking as evident in his philosophical and theological works, the *Monologium* and the *Proslogium*, with the ontological argument for the existence of God as also *Cur Deus Homo* (Why God Became Man), a dialogue with a non-Christian have had such a deep and abiding influence upon the Church’s philosophy and theology as to earn him the titles of “Father of Scholasticism” and “Doctor of the Universal Church”. Anselm died at Canterbury in 1109.
