  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Matthew 5:38–42"
    audio: null
    body: |
      During their captivity in Egypt, the Jews became familiar with the crude tribal law of retaliation called Lex Talionis (=Tit-for-Tat) given by the ancient lawmaker Hammurabi during the period 2285-2242 BC. In the Sermon on the Mount, Jesus rejects even the concession of milder retaliation allowed by Moses. In its place, Jesus gives a new law of love and grace – and no retaliation.



      “An eye for an eye and a tooth for a tooth.”Moses instructed the Israelitesto follow tit-for-tat retaliation, rather than to wreak total destruction upon their enemies. That is, instead of mutilating or murdering all the members of the offender’s family or tribe, they should discover, then punish by an equal mutilation or harm, only the offender. Later, a milder version of this law was substituted. It demanded monetary compensation, as decided by a judge, in place of physical punishment. Moses also gave the Israelites several laws commanding merciful treatment for the enemy if he also was a Jew (e.g., Lv 19:18).



      The true Christian reaction: For Jesus, retaliation, or even limited vengeance, has no place in the Christian life. Jesus illustrates the Christian approach by giving three examples:



      1)Turn to him the other cheek:Striking someone on the right cheek (with the right hand), requires striking with the back of one’s hand, and, according to Jewish concepts, the blow inflicts more insult than pain. Jesus instructs his followers to forgive the insult gracefully and convert the offender.



      2) “Let him have your cloak as well.” Jesus instructs his followers that they should show more responsibility and a greater sense of duty than to fight over possessions.



      3) “Go with him two miles.” A Christian has the duty of responding, even to seemingly unjust demands by helping or serving gracefully not grudgingly.



      (Fr. Tony: https://frtonyshomilies.com/)
  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      Revenge and retaliation are natural human reactions when we are hurt. Jesus asks his followers to be radically different by refraining from revenge and retaliation. By saying this, he is not legitimizing some new law, but preferring a deeper application to the attitude of our hearts. The essence of his teaching is that his followers should overcome evil with good. Our God is gracious and merciful and He expects His followers to be the same. Being children of God, we have to refuse to stoop to the level of those who do us wrong. When we retaliate to the who wrong us, we join them in their evil and become like them.

      St Gregory Barbarigo (Bishop, Confessor) (1625 -1697)

      Gregory studied at Padua before entering the diplomatic service. While in Munster, as secretary to the representative of the Venetian republic he met the Papal Legate, Fabio Chigi, who encouraged him to become a priest. Ordained in 1655, he was soon called to Rome as Referendary for the two Signatures (supreme tribunals of the Holy See).

      Consecrated Bishop of Bergamo in 1658, he entered the city carrying with him the Acta Ecclesiae Vediolanensis. In 1660 he was created Cardinal and, in 1664, nominated bishop of Padua, where he died in 1697.

      The pains he took to fulfil his duties as pastor of souls in his two dioceses is almost unsurpassed. When he took charge of his diocese, it was badly neglected—there were only 30 students in the seminary which was housed in a small dilapidated building; the 300 parishes of the diocese, counting about 250,000 faithful, were ravaged by ignorance. He transferred the seminary to the former monastery of Vanzo, where he was able to gather 100 students, recruiting them himself during his pastoral visits; he set his clergy an example, undertaking to teach catechism to the villagers and to visit all the parishes in his diocese, even the most inaccessible ones, at least twice during his term as bishop, imposing upon himself severe penance, fasting, sacrifice and prayer for the good of his flock. He even restored the University. Within a short span of time, the number of schools doubled, the number of teachers rising five-fold!
