      With today, Palm Sunday, begins the great week of the liturgical year. In the Holy Week we commemorate the chief mysteries of our divine faith and contemplate the words and deeds of the Lord in the last days of his earthly life. Today we celebrate the joyful entrance of Jesus into Jerusalem to accomplish the great mystery of redemption through his Suffering, Death and Resurrection.</span></span></span></span>



      In the simplicity of faith, let us join the procession of the children of Jerusalem. With great joy and enthusiasm they welcomed Christ into the holy city. Let us also acclaim honour and praise Christ as our King and Lord. Let us welcome him into our hearts and homes so that he may rule and govern our lives.</span></span></span></span>



      Jesus is the King who comes not to dominate, but to serve and to give his life as a ransom for the human race. Therefore, after the joyful procession, today’s liturgy passes to the story of his Passion.</span></span></span></span>



      The Passion of Jesus is the supreme sign of the love and mercy of the Father. Having seen the drama of so much love and so much suffering, the crowd went back beating their breast. In the story of the</span></span></span></span>



      Passion the divinity of Jesus remains hidden; instead, the suffering servant is manifested in all his pain and misery.</span></span></span></span>



      During this Holy Week we shall be repeatedly reminded of what God has done and is continuing to do for us. We can only beat our breast in humble contrition, for we are unworthy of the infinite love of God.</span></span></span></span>



      *(Excerpts from: Fr Dr George Kaitholil, Feast Day Homilies, ST PAULS, Mumbai, India)</span></span>*</span></span>

      A summary of human life can be experienced if one were to accompany Jesus from Palm Sunday to Easter. On Palm Sunday, Jesus is apparently at the height of his popularity after having received a rousing welcome because of his fame as a wonder-working teacher. As we enter into Holy Week, everything changes to betrayal, torture and death. The admiring throng and the hosannas are things of the past. Even being a participant in the Last Supper does not prevent one from betraying Jesus. The man dying on the cross like a common criminal does not in any way appear like the Messiah who was acclaimed just a few days earlier. How did Jesus go through this ordeal? In his most trying moments of crisis, he turns first, not for human consolation, but to God. Jesus’ story can be our story.
