      Reflection: 1



      The Jews had been praying to God to condemn the evil world and to punish the wicked. But God loves the world and sends His own Son to be crucified in order to save the world. Here the world is the object of God’s love. The “world” means the whole creation of which humanity is the crown, and God loves all and wants all to be saved. God has sent His Son to save the humankind if they willingly turn to Him.



      Today we celebrate the solemnity of the Most Holy Trinity. The mystery of the trinity is difficult to grasp, but is beautiful and easy to experience. The Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit are spoken of in different places in the Gospel and in the Letters. The sacraments are celebrated and our prayers are made invoking the Holy Trinity. The Holy Trinity is the most sublime model of unity, love and sharing. Our families should reflect, in their own way, the love and oneness of the Trinity, like a mirror.



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s feast invites us to live in the awareness of the presence of the Triune God within us: The Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. The mystery of the Holy Trinity, a doctrine enunciated by the ecumenical councils of Nicaea and Constantinople, is one of the fundamental doctrines of Christianity and the greatest mystery of our Faith, namely, that there are Three Divine Persons, sharing the same Divine Nature in one God. “There is one God, who has three Persons, Father, Son and Holy Spirit. Each Person is God, yet there is still only one God” (CCC #234, #253-256). We have the Father Who is the Creator, the Son Who is the Redeemer and the Holy Spirit Who is the Sanctifier, the Counsellor. The doctrine of Three Persons in one God, co-equal and co-eternal in Divinity yet distinct in Person, is not explicitly spelt out in the Bible. Even the very word “Trinity” is not found in the Bible. But the doctrine of the Trinity underlies all major Christian feasts, including Christmas, the Epiphany, Good Friday, Easter, the Ascension and Pentecost. All the official prayers of the Church, including the Holy Mass and the Sacraments, begin with an address to the Holy Trinity: “*In the Name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit.*” We are baptized, absolved of our sins, and anointed in the name of the Blessed Trinity. Throughout the world, when Church bells ring three times a day, Christians are being invited to pray to God the Father (the Provider); God the Son (the Saviour); and God the Holy Spirit (the Sanctifier), giving glory to the Triune God for the Incarnation of the Son and our Redemption through His suffering and death, as we pray the Angelus, or in the Easter Season, the *Regina Coeli*. We bless ourselves with the Sign of the Cross, invoking the Name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, and we conclude our prayers glorifying the Holy Trinity, saying “*Glory be to the Father and to the Son and to the Holy Spirit*….” Today’s readings convey the fundamental mystery that the Triune God reaches out to people with love, seeking the deepest communion with them.



      Life messages: 1)We need to respect ourselves and respect others. Our living belief in the presence of the Triune God within us should help us to esteem ourselves as God’s holy dwelling place, to behave well in His holy presence, and to lead purer and holier lives, practicing acts of justice and charity. This Triune Presence should also encourage us to respect and honour others as “Temples of the Holy Spirit.”



       We need to be aware of God as the Source of our strength and courage. Our awareness and conviction of the presence of God within us give us the strength to face the manifold problems of life with Christian courage. It was such a conviction that prompted the early Christian martyrs being taken to their execution to shout the heroic prayer of Faith from the Psalms: *“The Lord of hosts is with us, the God of Jacob is our refuge” (Ps 46*:7,11*).*



      3) We need to see the Trinity as the model for our Christian families: We are created in love to be a community of loving persons, just as the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit Are One in Love. From the day of our Baptism, we have belonged to the One God, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. How privileged we are to grow up in such a beautiful Family! Hence, let us turn to the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit in prayer every day. We belong to the Family of the Triune God. The love, unity, and joy in the relationship among the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit should be the supreme model of our relationships within our Christian families. Our families become truly Christian when we live in a relationship of love with God and with others.



      St. Francis Xavier’s favourite prayer was: “Most Holy Trinity, who live in me, I praise You, I worship You, I adore You, and I love You.” May the Son lead us to the Father through the Spirit, to live with the Triune God forever and ever. Amen.

      The human body that looks as a single unit is an assembly of a number of vital organs which work in coordination. The human body consists of 79 organs like the heart, brain, kidneys, liver, and lungs. Besides, the human body consists of a trillion cells which are considered the fundamental units of life. Everything that surrounds us and works harmoniously actually consists of so many parts which work in union. The congregation that comes to attend the Sunday Mass in the parish church can be called a single unit but consists of so many individual members. Today we celebrate the feast of the Holy Trinity. God is One, but in God, there are different Persons: the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. The harmonious existence of ‘Three Persons in God’ is the mystery of the Holy Trinity. Yet this is the reality and we put our faith and trust in the Trinity. Harmony is something we attempt to achieve in our personal and social lives. Harmonious life will bring success and fruitfulness to life. The Holy Trinity is the prime example perfect unity and harmony.

    saintName: "Visitation of the Blessed Virgin Mary"
    ref: "Visitation of the Blessed Virgin Mary"
    audio: null
    body: |
      This feast commemorates the visit of the Virgin Mary, pregnant with the child Jesus, to her cousin Elizabeth, pregnant with John the Baptizer. It was at the Annunciation that Mary was informed of old Elizabeth’s pregnancy (Lk 1:36), prompting her to go “with haste” (Lk 1:39) to be of help to her cousin, thereby commencing her involvement in the work of redemption. What followed is awe-inspiring! At the sound of Mary’s greeting, the child in Elizabeth’s womb is sanctified and cleansed of original sin (cf. Lk 1:44) as foretold by the Angel Gabriel to Zechariah (cf. Lk 1:15) and in a leaping instant begins his mission of bearing witness to the light. Indeed, as David danced for joy before the Ark of the Covenant of old, John, the last of the prophets of the Old Testament, leaps for joy before Mary, the Ark of the New Covenant that brings God’s presence into the world! Then, “ . . . . filled with the Holy Spirit”, Elizabeth boldly declared Mary the most “blessed” among women, the Mother of God (cf. Lk 1:41-42) — the first formal acknowledgement as it were of the “Theotokos”!



      The feast of the Visitation was first heard of in 1263, when it was adopted by the Franciscans, its observance extended to the whole Church in 1389 by Pope Urban VI in supplication for the end of the Great Schism.
