      Reflection: 1



      Christianity is not simply a set of dogmas or well built churches; it is the life of people who follow Jesus and bear witness to him. Bearing witness to him can sometimes lead us to the shedding of our blood, that is, martyrdom. The Christians who understand the way of Jesus and follow it understand martyrdom and its value. And the Spirit dwelling in them gives then the strength to bear witness to Christ even through martyrdom. </span></span></span></span>



      *— Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP</span></span>Reflection: 2</span>*</span>



      In his final discourse with the apostles at the Last Supper, Jesus assured them that he would not desert them. Instead, a powerful Divine Helper, the Holy Spirit, would come to them from Jesus and the Father in order to guide them and to strengthen them.



      The role of the Holy Spirit as outlined in today’s Gospel: 1) As the Counsellor or Paraclete or Advocate, the Holy Spirit would coach, defend, and strengthen the apostles in their sufferings and persecution and would guide them during their trials before the civil authorities. 2) As the Spirit of Truth, He would bear witness to Jesus and enable the apostles to bear witness to Christ heroically before the pagans. The Holy Spirit would give them an experiential knowledge of Jesus and an in-depth knowledge of Jesus’ teachings. *"The mission of the Church is carried out by means of that activity through which, in obedience to Christ’s command and moved by the grace and love of the Holy Spirit, the Church makes itself fully present to all men and peoples in order to lead them to the Faith, freedom, and peace of Christ by the example of its life and preaching, by the Sacraments and other means of grace" (Vatican II Decree, Ad Gentes 5).* Then Jesus foretells the nature of the persecution: 1) Excommunicating Jesus’ followers from synagogues; 2) Establishing the murder of Jesus’ followers (“heretics”), as a religious duty in defense of Judaism and, so, pleasing to Yahweh.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages: </span>



      1) As the Divine Advocate, the Holy Spirit living within us continues to help us bear witness to Christ by assisting us to live transparent Christian lives.



      2) He also gives us courage and perseverance when we meet adversities and challenges.



      3) As the Divine Teacher, the Holy Spirit, through our daily study of the Bible, helps us to know Jesus thoroughly, to love him personally, and to experience him intimately, so that we may live the ideals of Christ and convey them to others through our genuine Christian lives.

      The message of Pope Francis for the 2021 Diocesan World Youth Day was to courageously bear witness to Jesus Christ, in the footsteps of Saint Paul. The Pope has defined Christianity on several occasions not as a school of ideas or a collection of beautiful temples, but as a living people who follow Jesus and bear witness to him. This witness sometimes ends up giving one’s life as is evident in the lives of martyrs. A Christian who does not take the dimension of martyrdom seriously does not understand the road that Jesus has indicated. It is through the gift of the Spirit that we can face the trials that come in our lives. The Spirit gives us the strength to face martyrdom as we bear witness to our faith.

    saintName: "St Philip Neri, Confessor (c.1515-1595)"
    ref: "St Philip Neri, Confessor (c.1515-1595)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Giving up his commercial activities at the age of 26, Philip Neri went to Rome to seek his own sanctification and that of his fellowmen. He led a disciplined life of prayer and penance and studied philosophy and theology; he frequently visited the Seven Churches—a 12-mile pilgrimage which he would perform on foot after sundown, often spending entire nights in the catacomb of St Sebastian. In 1548 he founded a confraternity to care for poor pilgrims and started the monthly adoration of the Blessed Sacrament.



      Ordained priest in 1551, he went on to become an outstanding confessor and counsellor, combining as he did popularity with deep piety against the background of a corrupt Rome and a disinterested clergy. He would celebrate Mass with loving delight and devote his afternoons to engaging young men and boys in talks, games or visits to the Seven Churches, bringing home to the minds of people spiritual truths in the most unusual but efficacious ways. From 1564, several of his followers became priests and settled down with him at the Church of San Giovanni de Fiorentini, praying and eating in common, celebrating the Eucharist and preaching regularly. This sowed the seed of what was to become known as the “Oratory”, whose distinctive feature was the regular afternoon session of informal talks held in a little room above the Church concerning spiritual matters.



      An outstanding mystic of the Counter-Reformation, Philip passed away on the Feast of Corpus Christi, 1595 and was canonized in 1622.
