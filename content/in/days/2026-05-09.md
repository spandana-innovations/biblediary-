  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "John 15: 18-21"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      After giving his disciples the commandment of love, Jesus tells them that the world hates them because they are not of the world. Hatred is the true nature of the world, that is to say, the people who oppose God and His revelation. They hate those who recognise the revelation in God’s Son, Jesus. This happens because the world is under the influence of Satan, the power of evil, who instigate others to persecute the followers of Christ. The Jews and the Roman Empire considered Christian faith as a threat, and so they persecuted and killed Christians in large numbers. Jesus himself has been the first to be persecuted and killed by evil forces. If we decide to live an authentic Christian life, we must be prepared to face opposition and suffering. To remain faithful to Christ in and through suffering, we need his strength, for which we must pray every day.



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      In today’s Gospel passage, taken from the Last Supper discourse, Jesus warns his apostles of what they can expect from a world which ignores God and His teaching. They will be hated and persecuted as Jesus was. But there can be no compromise between Christ’s disciples and the followers of the powers of darkness. The term “world” in today’s Gospel passage means people who are hostile towards God and opposed to His will. They represent an evil society which *“callsevil good and good evil”* (Is 5:20). Such a society will hate Christ and his teachings because Christian teaching exposes the evil of society and its false and dangerous doctrines. Since the Church Jesus established stands for truth, morality, and justice, it does not support the modern “dictatorship of relativism.” The modern world hates and ridicules everything Christian through its liberal, agnostic and atheistic media.



      <span class="subhead">Life message:</span> Let us ask the Holy Spirit for the courage of our Christian convictions to believe and practice what Jesus taught and what Jesus continues to teach through the Church.

      The discourse moves from love to hatred. These two stand in opposition. The disciples will experience hatred, rejection, persecution, and death because the world has hated both Jesus and His Father. The reason why the world hates the Father and the Son is that the world does not accept that Jesus comes from God. The world has its own ideas about who the Christ should be and how one should relate to God. The world’s expectation does not match with the claims Jesus makes; hence it hates Him. If the disciples accept the worldview of the opponents of Jesus, then the world would love them too. Thankfully, the disciples have decided to remain with Jesus; hence the world hates them too and persecutes them. What is our choice? Worldview or Jesus’ view?

    saintName: "Saint Peter Nolasco Confessor (c.1182-1256)"
    ref: "Saint Peter Nolasco Confessor (c.1182-1256)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Of noble birth in southern France near Toulouse, St Peter Nolasco, who was canonized in 1628 by Pope Urban VIII, was from his youth known for his sincere piety and his great charity towards the poor and the unfortunate. He took part, for some time, in the crusade against the Albigenses before migrating to Spain, where he was appointed tutor to the young King, James of Aragon and formed an intimate friendship with St Raymond of Penyafort, then a canon at Barcelona in Spain. In 1218, Our Lady directed him in a vision to found a new religious order specifically for the redemption of the Christian captives, many of whom, on account of the brutality experienced at the hands of the cruel Moors, were giving up their faith in despair.



      The first monks of the new Order which had the active backing of the King and of St Raymond, were some noblemen who belonged to a charitable confraternity in Barcelona. They now came to be known as the Mercedarians, or members of the Order of Our Lady of Mercy, membership in which was of three kinds: Knight, Priest and Lay Brother; their vows included, besides the three regular ones, a fourth one, viz., to go into infidel territory and if need be to become a hostage in Moorish hands in order to ransom the faithful. For many this ended in martyrdom.



      Peter, who remained a layman, was “Commander-General” until his resignation in 1249. His mission as “Ransomer” took him repeatedly into Moorish territory (including Africa), in order to arrange for the ransom of Christian prisoners, whereby, when he died in 1256, a total of 2,718 slaves had been liberated. His work was carried on later at such a pace that by 1632 the number had swelled to 490,736!
