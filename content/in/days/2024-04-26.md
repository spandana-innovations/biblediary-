    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "John 14:1–6"

    body: |
      Jesus consoles his apostles who are sad and disheartened at the prospect of his arrest and crucifixion by assuring them that he is going to prepare an everlasting accommodation for them in his Father’s house in Heaven. He gives them the assurance that he will come back to take them to their Heavenly abodes. It is then that Thomas says to Jesus, *“Lord, we do not know where you are going; how can we know the way?”*Jesus answers Thomas’ question with, *“I am the Way, and the Truth, and the Life; no one comes to the Father, but by me.”*</span></span></span>



      Jesus the Way, the Truth, and the Life: The basic doctrine of Judaism is that Yahweh is the Way, the Truth, and the Life. Hence, Jesus is making the revolutionary claim that he is equivalent to Yahweh. Jesus declares that he is the safest and surest way to God, thus discrediting the notions that all religions are equally sure ways to reach God, and that no organized religion, but only living a good life of sharing love, is necessary to reach God. Jesus is the Way which he calls narrow, for it is the way of loving, sacrificial service. Jesus is the Truth who revealed truths about God and God’s relationship with man in his teaching. Jesus also taught moral truths by demonstrating them in his life. Jesus is the Life because he himself shares the Eternal Life of God, and because He shares his Divine Life with his disciples through the Word of God and the Sacraments.</span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">*Life messages: </span></span></span>*</span>



      We should share the Divine Life of God by making use of the means Jesus established in his Church: </span></span></span>



      a) by actively participating in the Eucharistic celebration and properly receiving the Body and Blood of Christ in Holy Communion; </span></span></span>



      b) by the worthy reception of the other Sacraments; </span></span></span>



      c) by the meditative daily reading of the Word of God; </span></span></span>



      d) by following the guidance of the life-giving Spirit of God, living in the Church and within us; </span></span></span>



      e) by communicating with God the Source of Life, in personal and family prayers and </span></span></span>



      f) by going to God to be reconciled with Him daily, repenting of our sins, and by receiving the Sacrament of Reconciliation, at a minimum, when we are in mortal sin (so that we can receive Him in the Eucharist), by forgiving others who offend us, and by asking God’s forgiveness of our own sins. </span></span></span>

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null

    body: |
      We all experience a natural fear of death. It is not easy to think of our own death or that of our dear ones. Today’s gospel passage clearly tells us why we should not fear death but should on the contrary wait for it with eager expectation. Death is not an end but is a door to enter a new life. Jesus reassures us that there are many rooms in his Father’s house. He promises to come back and take us with him. But often we behave like the disciples who have not yet come to the realization that Jesus is the way to the Father. What should I do to overcome my fear of death? The only way is to surrender myself to Jesus and eagerly long to be with him.</span></span></span></span>

      Saint Anacletus</span></span> - *Pope Martyr (-c 88)*</span></span></span></span>



      It is now generally conceded that the name Anacletus (or Anencletus), which is given in all the ancient lists of Popes from the second to the fourth century was probably abbreviated to Cletus. It was later erroneously believed that Cletus and Anacletus were two distinct figures, and they were therefore commemorated on 26 April and 13 July respectively. </span></span></span></span>



      Anacletus succeeded Saint Linus to the papal throne about the year 76 and reigned for twelve years. The 6th century *Liber Pontificalis* states that he had a small chapel or “Memoria” erected over the resting place of the Prince of the Apostles atop Vatican Hill and another one over the tomb of Saint Paul on the property of the matron Lucina on the Ostian Way. Aside from this, little is known of the reign of this second successor of Saint Peter.</span></span></span></span>



      Anacletus was believed to have been martyred under the cruel Emperor Domitian, the “Nero-come-to-life-again”. </span></span></span></span>
