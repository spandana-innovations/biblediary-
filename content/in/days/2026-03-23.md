    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      Jesus is the merciful judge who does not approve sin but forgives the sinner. He reveals himself as the merciful judge and Saviour. He exposes the hypocrisy of the Pharisees in observing the Law to the letter but not fulfilling its spirit. They had the habit of condemning others and not acknowledging their own faults. They brought the adulterous woman to Jesus to test him so as to find some charge to bring against him. Jesus knew their intention and made them fall in their own trap. None of them could claim to be free from sin. At the reply of Jesus they were confronted with their own guilt, and in shame hey left the place. While not approving her sin, Jesus does not condemn the adulterous woman but tells her not to sin again. Sinners as we all are, let us put our trust in Jesus and ask him for forgiveness and the grace to sin no more.



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      The Jewish civil and criminal code considered three grave sins as punishable by death, namely idolatry, murder, and adultery. The Law (Lv 18:20; 20:10; Dt 22:22), prescribes death for both the married woman and her partner if they are caught in adultery. If the guilty woman is betrothed, and she and her partner are within the City, both are to be taken outside the City gate and stoned to death (Dt 22-23). In both cases they have violated God’s sixth commandment and have destroyed the fidelity and unity of marriage. In today’s Gospel, we find the Pharisees preparing a trap for Jesus by bringing to him a woman caught in adultery. The trap and the escape: If Jesus consented to her death by strangulation or stoning, he would be violating the Roman law which forbade killing by private citizens. In addition, he would lose his reputation as a merciful rabbi. If Jesus said “no,” he would violate the Mosaic Law. Hence, Jesus ingeniously escaped from the trap by leaving the judgment to the conscience of the accusers. St. Augustine puts Jesus’ stand as follows: “Let this woman be punished, but not by sinners; let the law be applied, but not by its transgressors.” Jesus condemned sin, but not the sinner. He gave her a stern warning not to sin anymore, but showed her mercy, compassion and forgiveness.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span> 1) We too should learn to hate sin and love the sinners showing them mercy and compassion, sympathy, and acceptance, and leading them to noble ways by our own exemplary lives. 2) We should show mercy and compassion to those who sin because we ourselves are sinners in need of God’s forgiveness. 3) We have no right to judge others because we often commit the very faults we condemn, we are often partial and prejudiced in our judgment, and we do not know the circumstances which led someone to sin.

      Life often shatters us, leaving us broken and empty. Yet, in those darkest moments, God extends His hand, offering renewal and hope. The story of the woman ‘caught’ in adultery reminds us of His boundless mercy and justice. While the world may judge, humiliate, and condemn, the wisdom of God is greater than all human craftiness and cunningness! The accusers left in silence, convicted by their own faults, while Jesus, the only One truly without sin, showed compassion instead of casting a stone. This encounter is a call for us to pick up the broken pieces of our life, and begin again, filling our lives with the hope only God provides. Let’s trust, begin anew, and find strength in His grace.

    audio: null
    body: |
      St Turibius Alfonso de Mogrovejo was a young professor of law at the great University of Salamanca (Spain) when King Philip II, recognizing his outstanding talents and deep piety, appointed him president of the court of the Inquisition at Granada in 1575. In 1580 he was designated Archbishop of Peru. Duly ordained and consecrated, though much against his own wishes, he arrived in Peru in 1581.



      His 26 years in the See of Lima were filled with unbelievable apostolic labours, and his self-denial and limitless energies brought about the eradication of the numerous evils he encountered upon his arrival, his most famous battle cry being, “Christ said: ‘I am the truth’; he did not say: ‘I am the custom’!” It is estimated that he travelled throughout his vast archdiocese — over 50,000 miles! — almost always on foot, everywhere baptizing and confirming the natives, in all nearly half a million souls, including Sts Rose of Lima and Martin de Porres! Innumerable were the schools, hospitals, convents and chapels which his burning zeal saw constructed, and to him is also due the foundation of the first American seminary, at Lima, in 1591. He died on 23 March 1606 and was canonized by Pope Benedict XIII in 1726.
