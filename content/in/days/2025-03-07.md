      Reflection: 1



      This short and apparently simple text has a profound meaning. John the Baptist was the forerunner of Jesus; he came to prepare the people to welcome Jesus. He fasted and also taught his disciples to do so. Now Jesus has come, the bridegroom has arrived, and at the wedding feast there is no place for fasting. But some of the disciples of John failed t recognize Jesus as the bridegroom – the expected Messiah – and so they continued to fast. They ask Jesus why his disciples did not fast while they themselves and the Pharisees fasted. In his answer to them Jesus makes it clear that with his coming the wedding feast has begun. </span></span></span></span>



      Can the wedding guests mourn while the bridegroom is with them? Jesus also predicts that he will be taken away, arrested, tortured and killed, and then they will fast, that is, participate in spirit in the suffering of Jesus. We do this in a very special way on Good Friday through the liturgical services and through fasting and abstinence. However, this fasting is not a perpetual mourning, for commemoration of the passion and death of Jesus is soon followed by the celebration of his Resurrection. </span></span></span></span>



      *— Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP</span></span>*</span></span>



      Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel passage gives Jesus’ reply to the question asked by a few disciples of John the Baptist about fasting and feasting. Prayer, fasting and almsgiving were the three-cardinal works of Jewish religious life. Hence, John’s disciples wanted to know why they and the Pharisees fasted, while Jesus’ disciples were seen feasting with him and never fasting.



      Jesus’ reply: Jesus responded to their sincere question using three metaphors: the metaphor of the “children of the bridal chamber,” the metaphor of patching torn cloth and the metaphor of wineskins (Mark 2:18-20; Luke 5:33-35). In today’s Gospel passage taken from Matthew, Jesus compares his disciples with the children of the bridal chamber. These people were selected friends of the bridegroom who feasted in the company of the bride and groom during a week of honeymoon. Nobody expected them to fast. Jesus declares that his disciples will fast when he, the bridegroom, is taken away from them. One of the fruits of the Spirit is joy, and it is mentioned next after love in St Paul’s list, “love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness and self-control” (Gal. 5:22). Hence, we are to welcome the joys of Christian life as well as the crosses it offers us. The Fathers of the Church interpret the image of the bridegroom and bride as referring to Christ and his Church. Some explain it topologically: as long as the Spouse is with us, we are not able to mourn; but when by our sin we turn from *Jesus,* then is the time for tears and fasting. Yet others apply the words of Christ to the Holy Eucharist. The parable does not condemn the strictness of John nor does it condemn fasting. The disciples of Christ kept the fasts prescribed by the Law, but they did ignore those imposed by the Pharisees.



      Life messages:



      1) Fasting reduces the excessive accumulation of fat in our soul in the form of evil tendencies and evil habits (= spiritual obesity). In addition, fasting gives us additional moral and spiritual strength. It offers us more time to be with God in prayer. It encourages us to share our food and goods with the needy. We fast so as to share in the sufferings of the Body of Christ (Col 1:24).



      2) We need to be adjustable Christians with open and elastic minds and hearts: The Holy Spirit, working actively in the Church and guiding the teaching authority in the Church, enables the Church to have new visions, new ideas, new adaptations and new ways of worship in the place of old ones. So, we should have the generosity and good will to follow the teachings of the Church.

      The existence of a community of the disciples of John the Baptist itself is questionable. John was the precursor of the Messiah. He pointed to Jesus and said to his disciples, “Here is the Lamb of God” and they followed him. Yet some of the disciples of John the Baptist remained as a community on their own, not perceiving Jesus as the Messiah. They did not recognize the Bridegroom in Jesus and continued to fast and wait. Our religious activities can sometimes lead us to ritualism. Fasting is not an end in itself, but it is a means to spiritual discovery and spiritual enlightenment. It should not deteriorate into a meaningless ritual.</span>

    body: |
      The record of the passion of St Perpetua, her slave St Felicity and their companions is among the most ancient, reliable hagiological histories that have come down to us.</span>



      During the persecutions of Septimius Severus, five catechumens were arrested. They were Vibia Perpetua, a noblewoman of 22 and her infant, Felicity, a pregnant slave, Revocatus, Satwininus, Secundulus and later Saturus their Catechist. While still under arrest they were baptized. Perpetua’s father besought her to apostatize, because she was his favourite child. She, however, had the vision of a golden ladder guarded by the dragon and strewn with hooks and weapons that made difficult the ascent. But, following the example of Saturus, she trod over the dragon and, climbing the ladder, reached a beautiful place where a tall man in the garment of a shepherd welcomed her and offered her curds and milk. This was the premonition that she was to suffer and die.</span>



      Perpetua, Felicity and their companions were all condemned and ordered to be thrown before the wild beasts, at the garrison games festival in honour of Ceasar Geta. On the day of their triumph, they were flogged and were beheaded by the gladiators.</span>
