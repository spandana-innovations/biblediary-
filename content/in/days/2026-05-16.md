      Reflection: 1



      Though the presence of the Holy Spirit the disciples will be close to the Father who is one with Jesus. Therefore we should pray in the name of Jesus, and whatever we ask in his name will surely be granted by the Father. In order to pray in the name of Jesus we must first be united with him by living according to his teachings. In our prayer we should ask mainly for whatever will deepen our understanding of eternal life and whatever will bring glory to the Father. Let us seek to deepen our relationship with God through prayer. Now that Jesus is leaving the world and returning to the Father, he assures us that God loves us. The name of Jesus is powerful and the Father is eager to grant our requests because we love Jesus and believe in him. </span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Jesus used parables and metaphors, both in teaching the general public and in explaining teachings to the apostles. Today’s Gospel passage is taken from Jesus’ last discourse with his disciples at their Last Passover Supper together. Here, too, Jesus uses metaphors of a vine and its branches and the simile of a woman giving birth. Now Jesus tells them that he is going to tell them about God, his Father, in plain language. Jesus explains the mystery of his Incarnation in plain language saying, *“I came from the Father and have come into the world; again, I am leaving the world and going to the Father."* Then Jesus corrects the Jewish misconception of God, his Father, as a judging and punishing God, telling the apostles that God the Father is a loving and forgiving Father, to Whom they can pray directly (“Abba!”) in Jesus’ name, and that their prayers will be granted because the Father knows that they love His Son, Jesus, and believe in His Divinity. To pray in Jesus’ Name is not a magic formula or password. It means that we come to God the Father in the merit and righteousness of His Son, Jesus. In other words, I come to the Father depending on the perfect merit of Jesus which gives me standing before the Father. It also means that I pray to Father as Jesus’ representative, asking that God the Father’s will be done and that His name be glorified. In other words, praying in Jesus’ name means praying with Jesus’ authority and asking God the Father to act upon our prayers because we come in the name of His Son, Jesus. This is the pattern of prayer in the Liturgy. The Eucharistic prayer is invariably addressed to the Father, *“through Him (Jesus), with Him and in Him, in the unity of the Holy Spirit”* All our prayer has the pattern of the Trinity stamped on it. This does not mean that we should never pray to anyone but the Father. We are free to pray to Jesus, Mary, and the saints, but always in the full knowledge that the Father is the ultimate Recipient of all prayer – just as the sea receives every stream.</span></span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life message:</span></span></span> 1) God our Father is a loving, merciful and providing God who wants His children to approach Him directly and through His Son and our only mediator, Jesus. 2) Hence, let us make our prayers of adoration, praise, thanksgiving, contrition, and petition more effective and fruitful by offering them to God our Father through His Son Jesus Christ, in union with the Holy Spirit.</span></span></span></span></span>

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      Prayer is a powerful means that God has given us to enable us to be in touch with Him. Christians firmly believe that their prayers will be heard and answered. However, Jesus introduces a new notion about prayer in His discourse. He promises that whatever the disciples ask of the Father in His Name will be obtained. Jesus also mentions that until then the disciples have not asked anything in His Name, but soon, they will begin to do so. They can rest assured that whatever they ask will be granted to them and their joy will be complete. This does not rule out the possibility of directly approaching God in prayer. The reason is that the Father loves the disciples because they have loved Jesus and believed that He came from God. We have two very important reasons to make our petitions known: there is power in the Name of Jesus and the Father is willing to grant our petitions because we love His Son. Hence, let us pray and pray constantly.</span></span></span>

    saintName: "Saint Simon Stock, Confessor (c.1165 -1265)"
    ref: "Saint Simon Stock, Confessor (c.1165 -1265)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      This native of Kent, England (born at Ayelsford), received his nickname, “Simon of the Stock” from the legend that at the age of 12 he started living as a hermit in the hollow of a great oak tree and continued to do so for 63 years! He was known as an itinerant preacher, until he joined the Carmelite Order in 1240 soon after its introduction into England, driven out of Jerusalem by the Moslems. </span></span>



      In 1247 he was elected 6th General of the Order at whose spread he laboured with much zeal and success despite his advanced age. Houses were opened for the training of young Carmelites in the great University cities of the time, viz., Cambridge, Oxford, Paris and Bologna; the rule of the Order was also modified so as to make the former hermits into mendicant friars. An ancient Carmelite tradition relates that Our Lady appeared to St Simon on 16 July 1251, and presented him with the Brown Scapular to which she attached the well-known privilege, i.e., “This shall be the privilege for you and for all Carmelites, that anyone dying in this habit, shall be saved.” Thus to him is ascribed the spread of the scapular devotion.</span></span>



      Simon Stock died on 16 May 1265 at Bordeaux. Though never formally canonized, his relics have long been venerated as those of a saint in the Cathedral at Bordeaux and in the Carmelite Monastery at Aylesford, England.</span></span>
