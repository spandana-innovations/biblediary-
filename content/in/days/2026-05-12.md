    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      Jesus continues to explain to his disciples what the Holy Spirit will do. In God there is interaction between the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit. Right from the day of Pentecost, the Spirit acts in the Church and reveals Himself as the Spirit of Christ. He will prove to the world its errors: its basic sin of refusing to believe in Jesus; its sin of violating righteousness by judging and condemning Jesus who is innocent and holy. Even though the world killed Jesus, he is still present among us through the Holy Spirit. Jesus rose from the dead and ascended into heaven, but has not left us alone. By his death and resurrection he has ultimately defeated Satan, the prince of the world. </span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      In today’s Gospel, Jesus tries to console his sad and disheartened disciples at the Last Supper, for they are at a loss, hearing the news of their master’s imminent departure. So, he assures them that they will not be left alone. He will send the Holy Spirit upon them as a friend, guide, consoler, and teacher. Then Jesus explains the three different roles of the Holy Spirit in their lives. First, He will convince the world about the seriousness of sin. Thus, the Holy Spirit will lead us to repent of our sins and seek forgiveness from Jesus. The Divine Advocate will demonstrate that not believing in Jesus is the real sin. It is the Holy Spirit Who would prick the hearts of the Jews on the day of Pentecost, convicting them of their sin of crucifying their Messiah. In the same way, He convicts us of wrongdoing and convinces us of God’s truth. Second, the Spirit convinces us of the righteousness of Christ, which means that that Jesus was right in his teachings and promises, as proved by God His Father Who granted him Resurrection and Ascension into Heaven. Although Jesus was condemned to death, it was actually Satan, the ruler of this world, who was condemned through Jesus’ death. Third, the Holy Spirit gives us the inner and unshakable conviction that we shall all stand before the judgment seat of God. When we heed God’s judgments, we find true peace, joy, and reconciliation with God.</span></span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life message</span></span></span>: We need to allow the Holy Spirit to do what He wishes in and through our lives so that He may release us from the grip of sin and set us ablaze with the fire of God’s love. </span></span></span></span></span>

      Jesus assigns a very important role to the *Paraclete* whom we understand as the Holy Spirit. As He returns to the Father, He announces that the *Paraclete* will take His place and assist the disciples. The *Paraclete* will also show the world its error with regard to righteousness. That is, Jesus will be shown to have been righteous because Jesus’ death is actually His return to the Father. The *Paraclete* will illumine the disciples to see the error of the world about judgement. What is implied here is that the actual error of the world consists in its refusal to accept Jesus as the Messiah sent by God. It is to be understood that the world is under the influence of its master, Satan. Hence, it is not Jesus who is being judged but rather the ruler of this world (Satan). Through His resurrection and glorification, Jesus has won ultimate victory over Satan and his followers.</span></span></span>

    body: |
      The veneration of Sts Nereus, Achilleus and Domitilla is very ancient, going back to the 4th century. It was on the occasion of their festival, which was observed with some solemnity in Rome 200 years later, that Pope St Gregory the Great delivered his 28th homily— “These saints, before whom we are assembled, despised the world and trampled it underfoot when it offered them riches and pleasures”. The church in which he spoke was built over their tomb in the cemetery of Domitilla, on the Via Ardeatina.</span></span></span></span>



      Nereus and Achilleus were members of the Praetorian Guard, the elite corps of soldiers entrusted with guarding the very person of the Roman emperor. According to an inscription found in the tomb over their remains in 1874 — in the family vault in the cemetery of St Domitilla, in the underground church built by Pope St Siricius in 390 — they were ordered by Emperor Domitian to slay his niece Flavia Domitilla on account of her allegiance to the Christian Faith. But moved by her example they became Christians themselves and refused to carry out the order. They were thus banished to Ponza and later, under the reign of the Emperor Trajan, to the island of Terrecina. Here they were martyred — Nereus and Achilleus were beheaded and Domitilla, whose banishment St Jerome describes as one, long, martyrdom, was burnt alive. </span></span></span></span>






      St Pancras *Martyr (A.D. -304)*</span></span></span></span>



      In the persecution under Diocletian this 14 year-old saint, inspired by the example and words of his uncle, Denys, who was in bonds for the faith, offered himself voluntarily as a martyr. Supposed to have been a Syrian or Phrygian orphan who was brought up by Denys, together with whom he was converted to Christianity, he defended boldly before the Emperor the cause of the Christians.</span></span></span></span>



      He was beheaded by the sword on the Via Aurelia in Rome and his body was buried in the cemetery of Calepodius which afterwards took his name. And about the year 508 Pope Symmachius built a basilica over his tomb near the Potra de San Pauerazion.</span></span></span></span>



      In the 7th century, Pope St Vitalian sent some relics of St Pancras to England and within a short period of time, the young saint became very popular. The first church erected in Canterbury by St Augustine was dedicated to him. There is also a railway station in London named after him. It was under his invocation that St Gregory the Great had previously built a monastery for Benedictines.</span></span></span></span>
