      Reflection: 1



      Jesus tells us the parable of the sower who sows the seed; when it is grown and ready, he reaps the harvest. Some seeds fall on unsuitable soil and do not produce anything. The kingdom of God, represented by the seed, will grow and produce fruit if he person who receives the seed is fertile soil, that is, if he or she is well disposed. The seed, sown in our hearts, will produce fruit depending on how well our hearts are prepared for it. This calls for a conversion, a change of heart, from which begins the transformation of the whole person. The sower is generous enough to sow he seed in all hearts. Preparing ourselves as a fruitful seed ground is our responsibility. </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      In today’s Gospel passage, we learn that Christ’s message comes to us like a seed seeking to find a place to rest and grow. The seed – the message - is simple. It’s good. But what about the ground? What kind of reception will it receive?



      Rocky ground. I know a man (divorced) who has tried to raise his four kids the best way possible. He is a highly educated man, very successful in his career, and earns more money than he needs. But when it comes to his children, they have all but rioted against him. He was too old fashioned. He was too stuck in his ways. He wasn’t exciting enough for them, at least not as exciting as the dads on TV. He kept beating to a very different drum: education, morals, education, morals, education, morals.



      Suffice it to say, the seed this man threw fell on really rocky ground. Actually, it was choked before it even hit rock bottom.



      But kids grow up and have to move out. They need to find a job and pay their bills. Today, this man’s “children” know exactly what he was talking about. Life is tough because it is very fair. You get what you put into it. Nothing more and nothing less. If you screw up, then YOU screwed up.



      After years of waste, his kids are going back to school to earn a degree. They are finally putting their “house” in order by cleaning up their act. But their mistakes took their toll and sucked the best years out of their lives. They are starting to build what they should have built ten years ago. They have wasted their life on rocky ground.



      At the start, I said this man tried his best. I didn’t say he did the best. He wasn’t careful in monitoring his children’s relationships. He wasn’t careful in controlling his children’s settings. He wasn’t vigilant and proactive in what they heard, read and saw. His divorce cost him his marriage and his children. It cost him another set of eyes, ears and hands.



      News report after news report continue to highlight the loss of faith among teens. What they fail to do is correlate this “loss of faith” with a loss of faith in humanity; that is, an increase in absent fathers and mothers; an increase in divorce and separation; the loss of personal morals and dignity; the increase in promiscuity and drug use; the loss of a loving, stable and solid environment.



      Kids today are scared. And they take their fear and act on it: they rebel.



      The Pope is in Brazil to knock gently at the door. The teens that are there welcome him. The teens that are not there are scared of him. Why? Because all they have ever known is the darkness of sin and the disillusion of falsehoods. All they have ever had is a rough and rocky life.



      If they only knew the calm and soothing voice of Jesus Christ.



      *(Fr Alfonse - fralfonse.blogspot.com)*

      “A sower went out to sow.” The seeds the farmer sows in various types of soil are of the same quality, but the yield is of varying measures. Even if the seed is of the best quality, the yield would depend on the quality and the surroundings of the soil. Every farmer knows that only fertile soil with enough moisture and sunshine yields abundant crops. God sows His word in human hearts. The presence of the evil one and his temptations are a reality that can snatch away the seed of goodness from us. There is also the possibility of a poor, shallow and uninspiring heart that does not help the Word of God to grow and produce fruit. Again, the worldly forces of wealth and all its allurements can make it impossible to focus on God’s Word and thus cause it to disappear in favour of worldly attractions. A heart that is clean and free of all external influences and distractions receives the Word of God and produces abundant fruits. How disposed am I to the Word of God and its growth and fruitfulness in my life?

    audio: null
    body: |
      Bridget (or Brigitta), brought up by an aunt after the untimely demise of her devout parents, lived her life to the full first as a married woman and then as a religious. Married when barely 13 she lost her husband, Ulf Gudmarsson, in 1344, after 28 years of a happy marriage that bore them four sons and four daughters, of whom, Catherine of Sweden is a saint.



      Her penitential exercises and prophetic visions constantly on the rise since the early age of seven, the Passion of Christ marked the centre of her faith that expressed itself in charity. In response to one of her numerous “revelations”, Bridget founded the double Order of the Holy Redeemer (for men and women, with separate enclosures for each) at Vadstena in 1346, with generous endowments from the king. There were, in imitation of Christ’s 72 disciples (12 Apostles and Saint Paul), 4 Deacons, 8 Lay Brothers, and in a nearby convent 60 Nuns, who by their prayers were to sustain the preaching apostolate of the priests. In keeping with what her name denotes, viz. courage and strength, she did not fear to reprove the king himself, besides princes, priests and popes, for dereliction of duty and morals.



      During her Holy Land pilgrimage in 1372, she was blessed with four months of near continuous visions of the Lord’s life and Passion. These began with the young maiden Mary in her parents’ home, while in the humble cave at Bethlehem she witnessed Our Lady kneeling enthralled before her newborn Infant and exclaiming: “Be welcome, my Lord and my Son!”



      Bridget was canonized in 1391.
