    body: |
      Reflection:1



      The disciples of John the Baptist were confused because Jesus who was baptized by John in the Jordan was himself now baptizing. But John presented himself as a friend of Jesus who rejoiced to hear his voice. He told his disciples, “He must increase, but I must decrease.” He knew that Jesus came from above and ranked above him. In fact John came to prepare the people to welcome Jesus. John did not seek any honour, acclaim or messianic title. He was a powerful preacher and prophet, but humble and self-effacing. Do we look at those who do better than we as threats, or are we happy to cooperate with them?



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel passage shows the loveliness of John the Baptist’s humility. John was responding to his disciples who complained that many among them were deserting John to join the new preacher, Jesus, whom John had baptized.



      John’s explanation: John told them plainly who he really was and what his mission was. He told them that he was only a forerunner of the Messiah and that his mission was to prepare a people for the Messiah by preaching repentance. He was challenging his hearers to receive the baptism of repentance as their first step in renewing their lives, so they could welcome Jesus the Messiah into their lives. John explained further that his role was to be the “friend of the Bridegroom” (*shoshben*); the Bridegroom was Jesus. As the *shoshben* arranges the meeting of the bride and groom, arranges the details of the wedding, presides over the wedding, guards the bridal chamber and leaves happily, John prepared the Bride, namely, the Jewish nation, for receiving her Bridegroom, Jesus the Messiah, by baptizing the people who were willing to repent and then baptizing Jesus and introducing Jesus to the people as the “Lamb of God.”



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span> 1) Our mission, as St. Francis de Sales puts it, is to “bloom where we are planted.” God has given a unique mission to each one of us, and we are expected to accomplish that unique mission by receiving God’s strength through the various means Jesus has instituted in his Church. *“No one can receive anything except what has been given from Heaven.”*2)True humility and trusting Faith in God are necessary for us to accomplish our life’s mission by using God’s freely given gifts.

    title: Reflection
    ref: null

    body: |
      Today’s Gospel reading highlights missionary companionship rather than competition. John the Baptist does not see Jesus as a rival in his mission, although his disciples find it strange that the latter has been gaining more popularity and fan following. Despite all human expectations, John continues to offer persistent and bold witness to Jesus, presenting Him as the messianic bridegroom. As a friend of the groom, John is neither envious of Jesus nor upset in any way but rejoices that his mission has been accomplished. Competition focuses on rivalry, striving to outdo, outperform or surpass others in achieving goals, status, or success, while companionship is centred on building relationships based on trust, support, empathy, and mutual respect. How do I perceive my colleagues or fellow workers in the workplace?
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Agatho Thaumaturgus - Pope Confessor (-681)"
    ref: "Saint Agatho Thaumaturgus - Pope Confessor (-681)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      A native of Sicily (Italy), Agatho had been married for some 20 years before he decided to renounce the world and become a Benedictine monk in Palermo and later in Rome. Tradition has it that he was already a centenarian when he ascended the Papal throne in 678. He was particularly beloved for his gentleness and unfailing charity. The most important event of his three-year reign was the VI Ecumenical Council at Constantinople (680-681), in preparation for which he had a number of synods held throughout the West. His three legates presided at the Council, but he apologized for their poor Greek as due to the barbarous times in which they lived, it was more important to keep the Faith than to study foreign languages. Agatho’s dogmatic encyclical against the Monothelite heresy was received by the Council with great joy as expressing the true Catholic Faith—“Peter spoke through Agatho”—but the Pope had already died when the Council’s decrees in condemnation of Monothelitism entered Rome.



      Saint Agatho has been given the title “Thaumaturgus” because of the numerous miracles and wonders which he worked. He is also known to have sent skilled choir masters to England to reintroduce the Roman chant there.
