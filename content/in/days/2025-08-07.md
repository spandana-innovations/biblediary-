      Reflection: 1



      Peter is the first among the Apostles to profess faith in Jesus Christ, Son of the living God. It was not mere human insight but the Father Himself who revealed this to Peter. As He did to Peter, the Father has revealed to us also the real identity of Jesus. And Jesus appoints Peter the rock foundation of his Church and gives him authority to bind and to unbind on earth with effect in heaven. However, when Jesus mentions his coming passion and death, Peter rebukes him, moved by mere human consideration, thus becoming an obstacle in the mission of Jesus. Later on Peter realized his mistake. He still stands, together with the other Apostles, as the visible foundation of the Church, the community of Christ’s followers. As the Church is both physical and spiritual, human elements may interfere in the Church, but She is always guided by the Father’s revelation. </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel passage is the first of the three times when Jesus foretold His passion, death and Resurrection. The passage consists of two sections, the Messianic confession of Peter and Jesus’ prophecy of a swiftly approaching passion and death.



      Jesus as the Christ, our Lord and Savior:Today’s Gospel explains the basis of our Faith as the acceptance of Jesus as the Christ, our Lord and Savior. It also tells us that Christ Jesus became our Savior by His suffering, death, and Resurrection. This famous profession of Faith by Peter took place at Caesarea Philippi, at present called Banias, twenty-five miles northeast of the Sea of Galilee. Jesus realized that if the apostles did not know Jesus’ real identity, then the whole of the entire Messianic ministry, suffering, death, and Resurrection would be useless. Hence, the Teacher decided to ask a question in two parts. 1) “What is the public opinion about Me?” 2) “What is your personal opinion?” Their answer to the first question was: *“Some say John the Baptist, others Elijah, still others Jeremiah or one of the prophets.”*Peter volunteered to answer the second question saying*: “You are the Christ (Messiah), the Son of the living God.*” Jesus confirmed Peter’s insight as a special revelation from God. *“No mere man has revealed this to you, but my Heavenly Father.”*



      <span class="subhead">Life messages</span>



      1) Let us experience Jesus as our Lord and Savior and surrender our life to God We experience Jesus as personal Savior by listening to God’s Word through the daily, meditative reading of the Bible, by talking to Him through daily, personal, and family prayers, frequenting Holy Mass and offering Jesus our lives on the altar, being reconciled with God every night, asking His pardon and forgiveness for our sins and receiving the Sacrament of Reconciliation whenever we are in mortal sin) and by receiving the strengthening power of God’s grace through the sacraments, especially the Holy Eucharist.



      2) The next step is the surrender of our lives to Jesus by rendering humble and loving service to others with the strong conviction that Jesus is present in every person.

      In today’s gospel reading, Jesus’ real identity is revealed and proclaimed, for He was not only for the Jews, and his people, but for the whole of humanity. We also see Peter and his two personalities. At first, he shows great insight and perceptiveness. He calls Jesus “the Son of the Living God”. And Jesus responded by calling him the Rock on which his Church would be built. However, Peter reproaches Jesus for mentioning his impending suffering and death. Jesus makes a swift turnaround from praise to rebuke. He chastises Peter for being an obstacle to his mission. We reflect today on how we are like Peter in many ways. Like Peter, Jesus Christ, my saviour, has held out his hand to save me from drowning in sorrow, selfishness, anger, pride, greed, and even denying God’s existence. This sense of the impossible becoming possible would not exist without the rock of my spiritual and physical Church community.

      One of the most violent persecutions faced by the Church was the one begun by Emperor Decius in 250 and continued by his successor, Valerian, whose first edict (257) forbade the believers to ever hold assemblies. Thus, incessantly hunted, Pope Sixtus II, who had succeeded Pope Stephen I in August 257, would meet with his clergy for the Eucharist in the catacombs (underground caverns which doubled as cemeteries). On 6 August 258 they were found in the cemetery of Praetextatus on the Appian Way. Sixtus, together with four of his deacons, Saints Januarius, Vincent, Magnus and Stephen were seized and beheaded. Another deacon, St Lawrence was martyred 4 days later. Sixtus was buried in the nearby cemetery of St Callistus.



      Persecution apart, in the first and only year of his pontificate, he was faced with a controversy about the validity of Baptism by heretics. He shared the view of the North African bishops who, under St Cyprian, denied recognition of such baptism. However, apparently influenced by Dynosius of Alexandria, he adopted a tolerant attitude as regards the divergent rebaptism policies of the Eastern Churches, even going to the extent of deputing envoys to discuss the matter with Cyprian at Carthage.



      The most highly venerated pope martyred after St Peter, St Sixtus II is commemorated in the Roman Canon of the Mass.
