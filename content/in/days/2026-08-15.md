      Three Questions answered: Q 1: Do Catholics worship Mary? Fact 1:</span></span> Catholics don’t worship or adore Mary because we worship only God, and Mary is not God. Fact 2: We venerate her, honor her, and love her as Jesus’ mother and our Heavenly Mother.</span></span></span></span></span>



      Q 2: Why do Catholics venerate Mary? </span></span>Mary herself gives the reason in her “Magnificat” recorded in Luke (1:48-49): 48: “*For he has looked upon his handmaid’s lowliness; behold, from now on will all ages call me blessed. 49: The Mighty One has done great things for me, and Holy is his Name.*</span></span>*”</span></span>*</span></span></span>



      1) God has honored Mary in four ways, and we honor her because God honored her:</span></span></span></span></span>



      a) He chose her as the mother of His Son, Jesus Christ the Messiah.</span></span></span></span></span>



      b) In preparation for this role, God made her “Full of grace” by her Immaculate Conception.</span></span></span></span></span>



      c) He anointed her twice with His Holy Spirit: at the Annunciation and at Pentecost, making her the most Spirit -filled of all women.</span></span></span></span></span>



      d) God allowed her to participate actively in Christ’s suffering and death, suffering in soul all Jesus suffered in body.</span></span></span></span></span>



      2) Mary is our Heavenly Mother, given to us by Jesus from the cross.</span></span></span></span></span>



      3) Mary is our role model for all virtues, particularly, love, fidelity, humility, obedience, surrender to the will of God, and patience.</span></span></span></span></span>



      Q 3: Why do we believe that Mary was taken to Heaven after her death and burial? (“</span></span>Assumption” means, after her death, Mary was taken into Heaven, both body and soul. The word Assumption comes from the Latin verb “*assumere*”, meaning “to take to oneself.” Our Lord, Jesus Christ took Mary home to himself where he is. It was on November 1, 1950, that, through the Apostolic Constitution *Munificentimus Deus,* Pope Pius XII officially declared the Assumption as a Dogma of Catholic Faith, giving the following reasons:</span></span></span></span></span>



      1) Uninterrupted tradition in the Catholic Church starting from the first century AD. (The first trace of belief in the Virgin’s Assumption can be found in the apocryphal second-to-third century AD accounts entitled *Transitus Mariae *[Latin: “The Crossing Over of

      Mary”].</span></span></span></span></span>



      2) The feast is found in all the ancient liturgies</span></span></span></span></span>



      3) The belief in the assumption of Mary is taught by all early Fathers of the Church, e.g., Origen (died AD 253), St. Jerome (died AD 419) and St. Augustine (died AD 430).</span></span></span></span></span>



      4) Negative evidence: Mary’s tomb was never reported or venerated.</span></span></span></span></span>



      5) Old Testament evidence of corporal assumption of Enoch (Gn 5: 24) and Elijah (2 Kgs 2:1).</span></span></span></span></span>



      6) Theological reasons: her Immaculate Conception and sinless life.</span></span></span></span></span>



      Life messages: 1</span></span>) We are challenged to keep ourselves pure and holy children of a Holy Mother. 2) We are challenged to accept total liberation from all our bondages. 3) We are assured of our resurrection and given the inspiration to face pain, suffering, despair, disappointment and temptations as Mary did.</span></span></span></span></span>

    body: |
      The Assumption of the Blessed Virgin Mary celebrates Mary being taken body and soul into heavenly glory, a testament to God’s promise of eternal life. In Luke 1:39-46, Mary visits Elizabeth, who proclaims her as “blessed among women” for her faith and for being sinless and immaculate from the moment of conception. Mary responds with the Magnificat, glorifying God’s greatness and faithfulness. This reveals Mary as the model disciple, whose life reflects total surrender to God’s will. Her Assumption is the culmination of her intimate union with God, beginning with her immaculate conception and her fiat at the Annunciation. It is a sign of hope for all believers, showing that faithfulness leads to eternal life. The Assumption also invites us to magnify the Lord in our lives, trusting in His promises. Like Mary, we are called to live in purity, humility, gratitude, and joy, anticipating the fulfillment of God’s plan in our journey to Him.</span></span></span>

      Lack of explicit mention of the privilege of the Assumption as such in the NT notwithstanding, tradition and sound theological reasoning indicate its implicit revelation in the scriptures. St Germanus of Constantinople (d. 733) wrote that Mary’s virginal body is an altogether holy and chaste dwelling for God and can never know dissolution into dust. </span></span></span></span>



      “Mary conceived without detriment to her virginal modesty, brought forth her Son without pain, passed hence without decay, according to the word of the angel, or rather God speaking by the angel, that she might be shown to be full, not half-full, of grace,” wrote Pope Alexander III (1159-1181). In 1568, Pope Pius V made the feast of the Assumption a holy day for the entire church. </span></span></span></span>



      Since Christ, the Way, the Truth and the Life himself has said, “Where I am, there shall my servant be also” (Jn 12:26), how can his own Mother not share his dwelling place? Consequently, it was only after he had sought and obtained from the world’s bishops a formal assessment as regards the sentiments of the people in each diocese that Pope Pius XII defined the dogma of the Assumption as a doctrine of faith in his Encyclical, *Munificentissimus Deus*, on 1 November 1950. Rightly then, on this feast, the greatest of Mary’s feasts, the Church Universal with one voice joins Mary in exulting: “Holy His Name!”</span></span></span></span>
