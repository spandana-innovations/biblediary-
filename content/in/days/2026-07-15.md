  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mathew 11: 25-27"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Jesus knew that ordinary people with large, sensitive hearts, rather than proud intellectuals like the Scribes and the Pharisees, were able accept the “Good News” he preached. Such people would inherit Heaven rather than the “learned” and the “wise” who prided themselves on their intellectual achievements. Hence, in the first part of today’s Gospel Jesus prays loudly, joyfully thanking God his Father and praising Him for revealing Himself to the simple-hearted, thus condemning intellectual pride. A person who is full of self-centeredness fails to perceive supernatural things as real.</span></span></span></span></span>



      Jesus’ unique claim to be God’s perfect Reflection: *“*</span></span>*No one really knows the Father except the Son, and him to whom the Son wishes to reveal Him” (Mt 11:27). </span></span>*The claim that Jesus alone can reveal God to men forms the centre of the Christian Faith. John records Jesus’ claim in different words which He spoke at the Last Supper: *“He who has seen me has seen the Father”* (Jn 14:9). What Jesus says is this: “If you want to see what God is like, if you want to see the mind of God, the heart of God, the nature of God, if you want to see God’s whole attitude to men–look at Me!”</span></span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life message</span></span></span>: We need to learn how to know and love God better by studying Jesus’ revelation about God his Father. We do this by daily reading the Holy Bible, especially the Gospels, by meditating on the passages read and by applying them to our lives. The more we study the Bible, the more we learn about the Triune God, and especially about Jesus our Savior. This knowledge will help us to love Jesus more, experience his presence in our daily lives, see his face in everyone around us, and surrender our lives to Jesus by rendering humble service to everyone around us. </span></span></span></span></span>

      In the verses prior to today’s Gospel text, Jesus denounced the Galilean towns of Chorazin, Bethsaida, and Capernaum, which rejected His call to repentance. This foreshadows the rejection of Jesus by most of Israel. However, there were some who accepted Jesus, and He thanked the Father for these “little children”. These little children were His own disciples who were uneducated common men, sinners, the poor and tax collectors who were not knowledgeable but open to Jesus’ message and His call to repentance. They imbibed His teachings and put them into practice in their day-to-day lives. Since they were unpretentious and humble enough to accept their limitations and dependence on God, the Father filled them with faith and knowledge of Jesus. The divine praxis is very clear: “Whoever exalts himself will be humbled, and whoever humbles himself will be exalted” (Mt 23:12).</span></span></span>

    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Bonaventure, Bishop, Confessor, Doctor of the Church (c.1218-1274)"
    ref: "Saint Bonaventure, Bishop, Confessor, Doctor of the Church (c.1218-1274)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      At the age of four, Giovanni was cured of a grave illness through the intercession of Saint Francis d’Assisi. He was named “Bonaventure” — a name which variably means “good venture”, “good attempt” or “happy arrival” — when, in c 1243, he entered the Order of Friars Minor founded by Francis d’Assisi. In 1257 he received his doctorate and earlier, the same year, though hardly 39, he was elected Minister General, an office he held through 16 difficult years. He prudently endeavoured to curb the extremists, both among the Spirituals (or Observants), who insisted on the literal observance of Saint Francis’ original rule, and the Conventuals (or *Relaxati*), who favoured certain mitigations and relaxations.</span></span></span></span>



      In 1263 Bonaventure ordered that a bell be rung and the Hail Mary recited at nightfall in each monastery in honour of the Annunciation which was popularly believed to have taken place at that hour — a pious practice that the Angelus seems to have taken its origin from. In 1273, he reluctantly became Cardinal Bishop of Albano, and when the emissaries of the Pope, Gregory X, arrived at his monastery near Florence, they found him washing dishes and were told to hang the Cardinal’s hat on a nearby tree until he had finished! </span></span></span></span>



      Saint Bonaventure always attached the utmost importance to ‘preaching’ and seized every opportunity, be it with his friars, the clergy, the common people, Popes or Kings, to expound the Word of God, in which too he attained great fame.</span></span></span></span>



      Canonized in 1482, he was declared a doctor of the church in 1588.</span></span></span></span>
