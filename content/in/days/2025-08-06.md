      Reflection: 1



      In the Transfiguration, Jesus allowed his disciples Peter, John and James to see for a brief moment his glory, thus strengthening them to bear persecution bravely. Peter wanted to make here tents as it was good to be there, but the pilgrimage on earth should go on till our total union with God in heaven. The voice of the Father who said Jesus was his beloved Son said that again, adding that they must listen to him. The entire event takes place in the context of prayer. Jesus is he Father’s definitive Word to the entire human race. We should listen to him, accept his teaching, love it and live it so that we may reach where he has reached. Let us pray to the Father to make His Son’s voice resound in our hearts day by day and reshape our lives.



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2The Transfiguration of the Lord



      In the Transfiguration story in today’s Gospel, Jesus is revealed as a glorious figure, superior to Moses and Elijah. The primary purpose of Jesus’ Transfiguration was to allow him to consult his Heavenly Father and ascertain His plan for His Son’s suffering, death and Resurrection. The secondary aim was to make his chosen disciples aware of his Divine glory, so that they might discard their worldly ambitions and dreams of a conquering, political Messiah and might be strengthened in their time of trial*.* On the mountain, Jesus is identified by the Heavenly Voice as the Son of God. Thus, the Transfiguration narrative is a Christophany, that is, a manifestation or revelation of who Jesus really is. Describing Jesus’ Transfiguration, the Gospel gives us a glimpse of the Heavenly glory awaiting those who do God’s will by putting their trusting Faith in Him.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span>



      1) The “transfiguration” in the Holy Mass is the source of our strength: In each Holy Mass, the bread and wine we offer on the altar become “transfigured” – more properly, transubstantiated — into the living body and blood of the crucified, risen and glorified Jesus. Just as Jesus’ Transfiguration strengthened the Apostles in their time of trial, each Holy Mass should be our source of Heavenly strength against temptations, and our renewal of Christian living.



      2) Each time we receive one of the Sacraments, we are transformed: For example, Baptism transforms us into sons and daughters of God and heirs of Heaven. Confirmation makes us temples of the Holy Spirit and warriors of God. By the Sacrament of Reconciliation, God brings back the sinner to the path of holiness.



      3)A message of encouragement and hope: In moments of doubt and during our dark moments of despair and hopelessness, the thought of our future transfiguration in Heaven will help us to reach out to God and to listen to His consoling words: *“This is My beloved Son; listen to Him*.” Let us share the glory of His Transfiguration with all we encounter.

      How does today’s Gospel touch our lives? The disciples’ experience of Jesus’ transfiguration was an invitation for them to transform themselves too. The goal of our life is to be one with God. How is that transformation to take place? “This is my Son, my Chosen One; listen to him!” By listening to Jesus and living by his teachings in life. By integrating his will into my vision of life even if it is hard. It means having total trust in walking his way, which will bring me into full union with God, the source of all Truth, Love, Happiness, and Peace. Today’s Gospel reveals a paradox of our Christian faith: we belong here and we do not belong here. We are to find God in this world, but this is not our permanent home. We are pilgrims on a journey to a place of total union with our God of Truth and Love. However, it is easy to get obsessed with things on the way, but life goes on and it is important to know where it is headed.

    ref: "The Transfiguration of Our Lord"
    audio: null
    body: |
      While living among his disciples, Jesus had often spoken to them of his kingdom, and of his second coming in glory—the *parousia* at end-time. But, about a year before his passion, in order to strengthen them in their faith in the kingdom and of his glory therein, he gave them a wonderful vision, an experience that was to prepare them the better for what was to come.



      He took three of his closest disciples, Peter, James and John atop a high mountain—traditionally referred to as Mount Tabor in Galilee, which is some 2000 ft above Lake Tiberias. There they saw him suddenly transfigured, i.e., his face brilliantly illuminated sun-like and his clothes dazzling a glistening white, while in conversation with him appeared Moses and Elijah.



      The Transfiguration was thus a turning point in which Jesus was not only seen revealed firsthand as the long-awaited Messiah, but was acknowledged as the beloved and only begotten Son of God by the Father. For Peter, James and John, it was a foretaste of the very essence of his redemptive work that would translate itself into the Resurrection, the basis of which is suffering and death. Indeed, the transfiguration prefigures the everlasting enthronement of Christ. It is this glorification that the Church celebrates today.
