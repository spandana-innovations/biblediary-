  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mark 4:35-41"
    audio: null
    body: |
      The thrust of the first reading is God taming the turbulent sea. And in the Gospel reading Jesus stills the stormy sea. Both instances reveal God’s majesty and dominion over mighty waters. The former silences Job, leaving him awe-stricken. In the latter, the disciples are amazed and ask, “Who can this be?” God brings order from chaos.



      The apostles and Jesus are caught up in the same situation of a storm-tossed rough sea. Jesus is soundly asleep while the agitated disciples are terrified. Why two contrasting responses to the same situation? Jesus trusts in the power of His Father who has dominion over the waters and furious seas. So Jesus can relax and sleep, even amidst surging waves. Anxiety and agitation are the response of the disciples. The reason for this negative response is found in the query of Jesus: “Why are you so frightened? How is it that you have no faith?” Jesus has faith. The disciples have no faith. Hence the contrasting responses. The stormy sea is the symbol of our life on earth. Seldom do we enjoy peace and tranquillity. For different reasons, there are storms raging at different levels of our personality. Whatever may cause a tsunami in us – finance, sickness, betrayal, etc. – we find ourselves often in a hopeless and helpless situation.



      The Word of God assures us: the Lord is with and for us in any and every situation. And He is the master of every situation. Have faith in Him. The psalmist trustingly acknowledges that he can face any situation with the help of the Lord. St Paul encourages: “When I am weak, then I am strong, and I can do everything in Him who strengthens me.”



      (Philip John SSP & Premdas SSP, New Horizon Homilies, ST PAULS, Mumbai, India)
  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      The storms in the Gospel resonate the storms we face in our life. Some storms seem to arise from nowhere and take us by surprise. Other storms build and brew as we watch. Whatever it be, these are painful and pressing experiences of life. Many a time, God allows trials, difficulties and storms to come into our lives because these serve as tests and opportunities to prove our trust in God. The storms can make us trust God or see Him as less than He is which makes us react: ‘Don’t you care about us?’ It reveals to us whether we live by fear or by faith. Jesus’ words – ‘Why are you so afraid?’ – are more about us than the circumstances of our lives. Storms may continue to happen but with faith we can face them. Faith does not change the storm but it changes us. It allows us to see and know that God, the master of the storm, is there with us, and He is in full control of the storms and of our life.

      St Joseph Cafasso (Confessor) (1811-1860)

      Born of deeply religious parents at Castelnuovo, Italy, Joseph Cafasso was, as a boy called the “little saint”, and though poor, his talents, industry and piety saw him ordained priest at the early age of 22. He was made prefect, then professor of moral theology, and finally rector of the seminary of Turin. Fr Cafasso, the “Pearl of the Italian clergy”, though small of stature and somewhat deformed, had been the source of blessing not only for the clergy and people of Turin, but the whole world as the spiritual director of the great apostle of charity, St John Bosco. Cafasso had the confidence of all, young and old, rich and poor, even as clergy, bishops, princes and, indeed, all classes of people benefited from his ministrations in the confessional and his Retreats.

      He evinced keen interest in the work of St Joseph Cottolengo, the indefatigable advocate of the orphans, the poor and the distressed of all kinds and founder of the “Little House of Divine Providence”.

      Joseph Cafasso, who died in 1860, was beatified in 1895 and canonized in 1947.
