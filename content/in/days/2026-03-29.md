      Reflection: 1



      Pilate was interrogating Jesus. He asked, “Are you the king of the Jews,” and Jesus agreed. Though the Jewish leaders accused him of many things, he did not reply to a single charge. Pilate said to the Jews that he had nothing to do with this holy man. But, stirred by their leaders, the crowds asked Pilate to release Barabbas and to crucify Jesus. Pilate washed his hands and claimed to be innocent of the blood of Jesus. Then he released Barabbas and handed over Jesus to be crucified. The Roman soldiers stripped Jesus, crowned him with thorns, tortured and made him carry his cross, and crucified him. Pilate wrote on the cross of Jesus, “This is Jesus, the king of the Jews.” On the cross, Jesus surrendered his spirit to the Father and died. The soldiers who saw the manifestations that took place as Jesus died, said, “Truly this man was the Son of God.”



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      We see how a conversation on the cross changed life from cross to eternity.



      Bad thief to Lord Jesus: “Are you not the Messiah? Save yourself and us!” Good thief to bad thief: “Do you not fear God, since you are under the same sentence of condemnation? And we indeed have been condemned justly, for we are getting what we deserve for our deeds, but this man has done nothing wrong.” Good thief to Lord Jesus: “Jesus, remember me when you come into your kingdom.” Lord Jesus to the good thief: “Truly I tell you, today you will be with me in Paradise.”



      When the bad thief ridiculed our Lord Jesus, our Lord was helpless.

      The good thief recognized our Jesus and fought for him to his best, though he was hanging on the cross. He defended our Lord Jesus, not with a sword but with words of faith.

      And what did our Lord Jesus offer in return? – “Today, you will be with me in Paradise”. Our Lord Jesus replies with gratitude. “Today” not “tomorrow” or not “after purgatory” – This token of thanks from Jesus was offered “Today”. Jesus does not delay his thanks!!



      What do we understand from this conversation? We don’t need a perfect past to stand for Jesus.

      We don’t need a pulpit or a position. Our greatest charity will be a simple task done during our pain. We are always in a position to help others.



      So if you are hanging from a metaphorical cross- be it illness, failure, poverty, loneliness or regret – remember: that’s exactly where grace can enter. Stand up for Jesus hanging from that cross. Paradise is too close for those who stand for Christ!!



      Testimony: “I asked my brother why he is maintaining his friendship with one of friend, who leads life in bad ways. Unable to tolerate him, his wife had left him. His parents ignored him. He was thrown out of his job too. He is even a difficult person to be with. Why are you

      still giving so much importance to him? My brother replied, ‘If I too reject him, then he will have no one in the world. Where will he go?” I realized that God shows His unconditional love through His people.”



      Once Mother Teresa came to know about a family of 8 children who were starving for several days. So she took rice, went to their home and gave it to their mother. The mother of the children immediately split the rice into two portions and gave one portion of the rice to their

      neighbouring family. She said “Like us, they were also starving”. She remembered her neighbour in her poverty too.



      When the good thief pleaded with Jesus, “Lord, remember me”, our Lord remembered how the good thief remembered him and defended him during his trouble.

      It is easy for our Jesus to remember us, when we remember others.

      Palm Sunday marks the powerful beginning of the Holy Week, inviting us to journey alongside Jesus with renewed faith, hope, and love. As we hold our palms – symbols of both triumph and sacrifice – we celebrate Christ’s entry into Jerusalem as the Prince of Peace, not with the pomp of earthly power but with the humility of a servant. ‘No Cross, no crown! No wounds, no blessings!’ These palms, fresh and green, remind us of our commitment to Him. Yet, in days, they will dry, urging us to keep our spirit alive even when faced with trials. To make this a truly ‘holy’ week, let us engage in daily prayer, read Scripture, and reflect on the Passion of Christ. We need to receive the Sacrament of Reconciliation to mend our relationship with God and others, fostering forgiveness and healing. We also need to reach out to those in need to deepen our solidarity with the sufferings of Christ. Are we prepared to leave the safety of the crowd and climb Calvary? Today as our hearts echo, ‘Hosanna,’ let us remain faithful to our Crucified Lord as we prepare to proclaim, ‘Alleluia! He is risen!’

      The early history of St Rupert was formerly very obscure; and there has been considerable difference of opinion as to the date at which he actually flourished and died. According to the most reliable sources he was a Frank, though Colgan claims that he was an Irishman whose Gaelic name was Robertach. It is affirmed with certainty now that he was the son of St Bertha of Lorraine and that he was consecrated a bishop at Worms before he began his missionary activities.



      About the year 697, together with several of his companions, Rupert presented himself before Duke Theodo, without whose permission nothing could be done. Theodo not only made them welcome, but also consented to listen to their preaching and to receive instruction. Rupert confirmed his preaching with miracles. The duke’s conversion and baptism was followed by that of many of the nobles as well as of many of the subjects, without much opposition having been met with.



      He made the old ruined town of Juvavum, which the duke had given him, his headquarters. Renaming it Salzburg, he built there a church and a monastery with a school which were dedicated to St Peter. Rupert had been ably seconded by his companions, three of whom, Vitalis, Chuniald and Gislar, were later declared Saints.



      He also admitted his sister (or niece), St Erentrudis, into the nunnery which he built at Nonnberg and of which she became the first abbess. He died at Salzburg (Austria) probably about the year 710 or 718. St Rupert’s feast is observed in Ireland as well as in Austria and Bavaria.
