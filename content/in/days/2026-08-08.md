  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mathew 17: 14-20"
    audio: null
    body: |
      When Jesus came down from the mountain of the Transfiguration along with Peter, John and James, the father of an epileptic son knelt before Jesus and asked Jesus to heal his son. The father’s complaint was that the other nine disciples of Jesus waiting for their master to come down from the mountain could not heal the boy.



      The healing: After expressing impatience with the crowd for their lack of Faith, Jesus rebuked the demon and cast him out of the boy. Later when the apostles asked Jesus why they had been unable to do the same although they had been given the power of exorcism, Jesus pointed out their lack of Faith. Jesus said further that even a small amount of Faith would enable them to do great things. *Faith moving mountains* was a Jewish phrase meaning that God can remove all difficulties for one who places his trusting Faith in Him.



      <span class="subhead">Life message:</span> We will be able to solve our problems and many of the problems of our fellow-human beings if we place our trusting Faith in God’s power and goodness and ask for His strength in prayer.

  - key: reflection

    ref: null

    body: |
      Most of us say we have faith in God. Often, we fail to apply the faith we have to our daily living. We begin to be agitated, upset, and fearful when some problems confront us because we tend to rely on ourselves rather than on God. Faith, even the size of a “mustard seed” can produce great results, Jesus reminds us. To have such faith that grows strong, we must cultivate it. Mother Teresa once said, “Has your faith grown? If you do not pray, your faith will leave you.” Prayer helps us grow in faith as it turns our attention to Jesus, and not on ourselves. Prayer helps us focus on His promises, not on our feelings. Let us pray: Renew us, Lord, by the power of Your Spirit, and give us faith, even if it’s only the size of a mustard seed.

    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "St Dominic Guzman, Confessor (c.1170-1221)"
    ref: "St Dominic Guzman, Confessor (c.1170-1221)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      The name Domingo (Spanish) means “I belong to God”. In time this founder of the great Order of Preachers (Dominicans) was to prove the truth of its meaning by the joy that radiated from his mystical union with God.



      Dominic studied at Palencia and, in 1196, joined the Canons Regular (Augstinian Friars) at the Cathedral of Osma. He led a disciplined life of prayer and penance. In 1203, passing through southern France whilst on a trip to Denmark with Bishop Diego of Osma (whom he had succeeded as Prior), he was appalled at the confusion created in the minds of the faithful by the preaching of the Albigenses. The Albigenses—or Cathars, “the pure”—claimed to be Christians but held the heteredox belief that flesh and all matter was evil, that the spirit was of God, and that flesh and spirit were therefore in permanent conflict. Further, they denied the Incarnation and rejected the sacraments. That apart, the Albigenses lived in admirable personal austerity, inveighing continually against the worldliness and wealth of the Church and its Prelates.



      Dominic rose to the occasion. He established a convent of nuns at Prouille with a group of women converted from the heresy, and with his group of friar-preachers, was welcomed into Toulouse by Bishop Foulques who established them as *“diocesan preachers”* in 1215. Subsequent to the Lenten sermons Dominic preached before Innocent III and his court, he was appointed Papal theologian, an office ever since held by a Dominican Priest.



      Dominic was canonized in 1234.



      <span class="subhead">Reflection:</span> *“A man who governs his passions is master of the world”*(St Dominic).
