  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      In today’s Gospel we see how God protects Jesus when Herod brutally massacred the children of his age. Matthew emphasizes Jesus’ solidarity with Israel. Leaving all that they had in their home town, they had to live like refugees in Egypt. The Son of God had experienced the miserable life of a refugee family: no identity, but poverty, anxious about future, ill-treatment, vulnerability, slavery, injustice and so on. For Joseph and Mary, life with the Son of God was not easy. They endured everything because of the living presence of the “Emmanuel.” Rachel weeps for her descendants in Ramah, where she is buried, because they are taken into exile from there. God heard her cry. A saviour like Moses is born to save her children. Our sufferings are the visible signs of “Emmanuel” in our lives.

    ref: "Holy Innocents (Martyrs)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Feeling greatly threatened by the very existence of the newborn King whom the Magi had come his way to worship, Herod had ordered the slaughter of all male children, under two years of age, in Bethlehem and its environs (Mt 2:16), in the vain hope of getting rid of Jesus. </span></span></span></span></span>



      Of Herod Emperor Augustus had said that he would much rather be Herod’s pig than his son, because he refused to eat pork even as he slew his own offspring, his cruelty stemming from his acute insecurity! In 35 BC Herod had his brother-in-law killed; in 34, his brother Joseph; in 29, his wife Mariamne and her mother, Alexandra; in 25, yet another brother-in-law, Costobar; in time, two of his own sons named Alexander and Aristobulus; finally, in 4, barely four days before he died himself, another son named Antipater. </span></span></span></span></span>



      Before all these atrocities of Herod, the massacre of the innocents that left numerous parents disconsolate appeared too insignificant to historians like Josephus to merit any mention at all. But the Church venerates them as martyrs because they died not only for Christ but also instead of Christ. The shedding of blood was for them like a baptism that earned them the divine life. In medieval times their commemoration was known as “Childermas”, i.e., Children’s Mass. </span></span></span></span></span>



      This dreadful Herodian has a parallel in an equally heinous crime being committed worldwide today, of which Mother Teresa says, “The greatest destroyer of peace in the world today is abortion.” As Mother Church affirms: “From the moment of its conception life must be guarded with the greatest care, while abortion and infanticide are unspeakable crimes” (Gaudium et Spes, No. 51).</span></span></span></span></span>
