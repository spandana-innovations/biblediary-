      St Luke the Evangelist</span></span> is the author of the third Gospel, written before the year 63 A.D.</span></span> He is also author of the Acts of the Apostles. </span></span>He was well </span></span>educated as a physician, </span></span>very skilful in painting and some Greeks speak of him leaving many pictures of Jesus and Mary. Luke was a very close companion of St Paul during his second and third missionary journeys. While in Jerusalem, he frequently visited Paul in prison, accurately recorded and interpreted with the utmost fidelity the oral preaching and catechizing of the great missionary St Paul with regard to the life of Christ. His Gospel is particularly valuable for the stress it lays on Christian purity, poverty and joyousness. </span></span>He was the only evangelist to incorporate the personal testimony of the Blessed Virgin Mary, whose role in Christ's life emerges most clearly in his Gospel.</span></span> In his Gospel, he stressed particularly that God’s mercy and forgiveness is extended universally to both Jews and the Gentiles and that is shown in the parables of the Good Samaritan and the Prodigal Son. His symbol is the ox, the animal for sacrifice, because he begins his Gospel with the history of Zechariah, the priest offering sacrifice to God. </span></span></span></span>

      The feast of Saint Luke invites us to introspect into our way of being a missionary. Luke was impressed by the compassion of Jesus for the poor, the marginalized and women. He was writing the Gospel of Jesus for his Gentile Christian community. He was sensitive to their feelings and therefore avoided details which would offend them. He upheld the Good Samaritan and the Samaritan Leper and the widows in his time to show that the kingdom of God belongs to them. He believed that there will be greater joy in Heaven for the repentant sinner than for the righteous as he narrated the story of the Prodigal Son. He emphasized that Jesus came for all especially the last, the lost and the least. *If I were to write the Gospel of Jesus, what will I include in it?*</span></span></span></span>

      St Luke Evangelist (-c.74)



      St Luke was a native of Antioch, the capital of Syria. A physician by profession (cfr. Col 4:14), he was consulted by St Paul during an illness on his 2nd great missionary journey, and converted by him to the Faith. Himself a gentile, he thenceforth associated himself with the great Apostle of the Gentiles, and while Paul was preaching in Thessalonica, Athens and Corinth, and during his 3-year stay at Ephesus, Luke carried on the work of Evangelization in Philippi. He returned with Paul to Jerusalem, and visited him frequently during his imprisonment at Caesarea; it was probably at that time that Luke wrote his extensive Gospel which Sts Jerome and John Chrysostom called St Paul’s Gospel. He is rightly said to have been “illumined by St Paul”, for he recorded and interpreted with utmost fidelity the oral preaching and catechizing of the great missionary. He is also credited with having painted a portrait of the Virgin Mary.</span></span></span>



      Being highly educated, Luke could write in polished, elegant Greek, and he related the events to contemporary history. In his endeavour to furnish an historical background for the faith of the educated Greek converts of the young Church, he particularly stressed the fact that God’s mercy and forgiveness extended universally both to the Jews and the Gentiles who believed in him. Thus, the parables of the Good Samaritan and the Prodigal Son, for instance, appear only in his Gospel.</span></span></span>



      St Luke is the Patron of physicians, artists, brewers, butchers, glass-workers and notaries.</span></span></span>

      St Peter of Alcantara </span>*Confessor (1499-1562)</span>*</span></span>



      Of noble parentage, Peter, at 16, distributed his fortune among the poor and joined the Franciscan friars of the Strict Observance at Alcantara in Spain. He was sent to found a new monastery near Badajoz and was ordained priest in 1524. Then began his wonderful career of successful preaching, filled with tender human sympathy, especially for the poor. </span></span></span>



      Made Provincial in 1538, he resigned three years later when his emphasis on the penitential aspects of the life of St Francis d’Assisi, founder of the Franciscans, met with stiff opposition. Consequently, together with St John of Avila, he retired into the mountains of Portugal as a hermit; but his widespread fame for sanctity caused other friars to follow his example, and several small communities of hermits gradually sprung up. For these reformed discalced “Alcantarine” Congregations he wrote a rule of hitherto unheard-of austerity and poverty, which St Teresa described as “incomprehensible to the human mind”. His own cell, for instance, was so small that he could never lie down and died on his knees aged 63 and was canonized in 1669. As for food, he partook of some only every third day. This Alcantarine reform spread rapidly, particularly in Spain.</span></span></span>



      His beautiful treatise on prayer *Tratado de la oración y meditación* has been translated into many languages and was frequently used by St Francis de Sales. </span></span></span>
