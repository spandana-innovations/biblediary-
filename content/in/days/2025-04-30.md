      Jesus affirms that God so loved the world that He gave His only Son to save the world. God did not condemn the sinful world but sent His Son to make reparation for its evil deeds. The Son, Jesus, has come as the light of the world but people loved darkness because their works were evil. </span></span></span></span>



      God’s boundless love is open to welcome every repentant sinner who turns away from darkness to light. Those who do not believe in Jesus remain in spiritual darkness and away from God. Through His Son God saves all human beings if they willingly accept him, but those who refuse him continue under the influence of evil. The disciples of Jesus are those who believe him and his testimony about God’s plan of salvation. </span></span></span></span>

      Reflection: 2



      Jesus explained God’s plan of salvation to Nicodemus by declaring that the story of Moses and the brazen serpent was a sign pointing to the Good News that God would show His love for mankind by subjecting His own Son to suffering and death in order to save them all: *“For God so loved the world that He gave His only Son, so that everyone who believes in him might not perish but might have eternal life” (Jn 3:16).*This is the summary of the Gospel message of salvation through Christ Jesus. This is the Good News in the Gospels.



      Today’s Gospel passage teaches us that our salvation is the free gift of a merciful God, given to us through Jesus, His Son. It explains that Jesus, the Incarnate Son of God, became the agent of God’s salvation, not just for one sinful nation, but for the sins of the whole world from the beginning through the end. Through *Jn 3:16*, the Gospel teaches us that God has expressed His love, mercy, and compassion for us by giving His only Son for our Salvation. This tells us that the initiative in all Salvation is God’s love for man. St. Augustine of Hippo describes a dream message received by his mother, Monica, who prayed and wept unceasingly, fearing Augustine would be damned because of the life he was leading. This message convinced her that she had to live with him, not cut him off as she had been doing, for God still loved him even in his present condition. Augustine’s example also explains to us the universality of the love of God. God’s motive is Love and God’s objective is Salvation. Those who actually receive eternal life must believe in the Son and express that love in deeds.



      <span class="subhead">Life message: </span>



      1) We need to respond to God’s love for us by loving and serving Him in others in whom He dwells. God’s love for us is unconditional, universal, forgiving, and merciful. Let us make an earnest attempt to include these qualities in sharing our love with others during this Easter season. “In the evening of life, you will be examined on love,” said St. John of the Cross

      Today’s gospel passage encapsulates the core message of the Gospel. “God so loved the world, that He gave his only begotten Son…” (Jn 3:16). It speaks of God’s boundless love and the gift of eternal life through faith in Christ. It is a choice between light and darkness. We are challenged to embrace God’s love, respond to His offer of salvation, and walk in the truth, allowing our lives to bear witness to His transformative grace. Those who believe in Christ come into the light. They have their deeds exposed in the presence of God. Unbelief, on the other hand, results in remaining in spiritual darkness and separation from God. It’s a state of spiritual alienation where individuals are estranged from the source of life and love.

    saintName: "Saint Pius V - Pope, Confessor (1504-1572)"
    ref: "Saint Pius V - Pope, Confessor (1504-1572)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Born in Piedmont (Italy) of an impoverished family of Bolognese nobility, Antonio Ghislieri entered the Dominicans at 14. Ordained to the priesthood at 24, he devoted himself to the teaching of philosophy and theology for 16 years. A man of solid piety, austerity and self-discipline, he passed long nights in prayer.



      Made Bishop of Sutri in 1556, Ghislieri succeeded Pius IV as Pius V in 1566. The Papal court underwent reform, with Cardinals being urged to curb their luxurious lifestyle and Bishops required to found seminaries and to reside in their Sees; Church music and liturgical worship underwent reform. To the Mass were added the *Domine non sum dignus* (“Lord, I am not worthy”), the *Confiteor* (“I confess”), and the blessing, all and sundry coming to feel the Pope’s influence in carrying out the decisions of the great Council of Trent that had concluded in 1563.



      Politically, Pius V worked ceaselessly to bring about a union of the Christian Princes of Europe against the threatening Turks. The faithful were urged to do special penances for this intention. Finally, the Turkish attack on Cyprus brought the forces of Venice, Genoa, Spain and the Holy See together under Don Juan of Austria who, as commander-in-chief, leaving behind all soldiers of known evil lives, won the great naval victory of Lepanto in 1571, in memory of which was instituted the feast of Our Lady of Victory—later changed to the feast of the Holy Rosary—and entered the invocation, “Help of Christians” in the Litany of Loreto.



      Pius V, whose mortal remains were moved from Saint Peter’s to Saint Mary Major’s in 1588 by Sixtus V, was canonized in 1712.
