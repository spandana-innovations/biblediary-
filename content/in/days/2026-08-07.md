    body: |
      After Peter made his famous declaration of Faith in Jesus as God and Messiah, Jesus plainly warned his disciples about his approaching suffering, death and Resurrection. But the apostles were unwilling to accept such a fate for their Master. Peter even took Jesus aside and prayed, “*God forbid, Lord! This must never happen to you!” *It was after Peter’s protest (and Jesus abrupt response), that Jesus declared the three conditions of the discipleship which he expects from his followers. We hear them in today’s Gospel.</span></span></span></span></span>



      Conditions of Christian discipleship</span></span>: 1) Deny yourself 2) Take up your cross. 3) Follow Me.</span></span></span></span></span>



      Denying oneself</span></span> means cleansing the heart of all bad habits and evil tendencies and evicting the self, with its selfish thoughts and desires from one’s heart. It also means enthroning God in one’s cleansed heart and surrendering one’s life to God’s service by serving others.</span></span></span></span></span>



      Carrying the cross</span></span> means willingly accepting the pain involved in serving others sacrificially. It also means spending our time, talents, wealth, and health for others until it hurts us. Cross-bearing is also our sacrificial sharing of God’s blessings with others. Further, it includes our doing penance to make reparation for our sins and to grow in self-control. Carrying one’s cross becomes easier and more meritorious when we accept life’s crosses as loving gifts given by a loving Father. The comparison of our light crosses with the heavy crosses given to others should make us grateful. Finally, we should carry our crosses, praying for Heavenly strength.</span></span></span></span></span>



      “Follow Me”</span></span> means the disciple should be ever ready to obey as Jesus directs him or her through his words in the Bible and through the teaching authority he instituted in the Church.</span></span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life message:</span></span></span> We need to love the cross, wear the cross, and transform the God-given crosses of our life into the instruments of our salvation by working with the guidance of the Holy Spirit.</span></span></span></span></span>

    body: |
      Jesus is calling us to a radical discipleship. In urging us to take up our cross and follow Him, Jesus is inviting us to embrace our lives as it unfolds each day: welcoming the hardships, challenges, and even persecutions that may come our way as we strive to follow the path of Jesus. It is not easy or comfortable to be a disciple of Jesus. It requires us to deny ourselves, to let go of our selfish desires; it also means to place the will of God and the needs of others before our own. It means being willing to endure hardship, opposition, and even rejection for the sake of the Gospel. Taking up the cross and following Jesus is not just for our salvation alone. It is about striving to create a better world, one that reflects God’s values of love, compassion and justice.</span></span></span>

      St Sixtus II and Companions, *Pope and martyr, martyrs*</span>* (- c.258)</span></span>*</span></span></span>



      One of the most violent persecutions faced by the Church was the one begun by Emperor Decius in 250 and continued by his successor, Valerian, whose first edict (257) forbade the believers to ever hold assemblies. Thus, incessantly hunted, Pope Sixtus II, who had succeeded Pope Stephen I in August 257, would meet with his clergy for the Eucharist in the catacombs (underground caverns which doubled as cemeteries). On 6 August 258 they were found in the cemetery of Praetextatus on the Appian Way. Sixtus, together with four of his deacons, Saints Januarius, Vincent, Magnus and Stephen were seized and beheaded. Another deacon, St Lawrence was martyred 4 days later. Sixtus was buried in the nearby cemetery of St Callistus.</span></span></span></span>



      Persecution apart, in the first and only year of his pontificate, he was faced with a controversy about the validity of Baptism by heretics. He shared the view of the North African bishops who, under St Cyprian, denied recognition of such baptism. However, apparently influenced by Dynosius of Alexandria, he adopted a tolerant attitude as regards the divergent rebaptism policies of the Eastern Churches, even going to the extent of deputing envoys to discuss the matter with Cyprian at Carthage.</span></span></span></span>



      The most highly venerated pope martyred after St Peter, St Sixtus II is commemorated in the Roman Canon of the Mass.</span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Reflection:</span></span></span>* “Pray that we may think of immortality rather than death. . . . We know that soldiers of Christ are properly not killed, but crowned” </span></span>*(St Cyprian).</span></span></span></span>






      St Cajetan, *Confessor (1480-1547)*</span></span></span></span>



      Even as a youth, Cajetan was known as “the Saint”. At 24 he obtained his doctorate in secular and canon law and was made a protonotary apostolic at the court of Julius II. In Rome after his ordination on 30 September 1516, he joined the Congregation of the Love of God, a group devoted to piety and charity and re-animated it with his youthful enthusiasm; he laboured with special zeal at promoting frequent Communion among the laity. In 1522, he renounced his wealth and ecclesiastical dignities, and founded a hospital for incurables in his native Vicenza. Before long he was the leader in all spiritual and charitable undertakings in Venice and Rome. </span></span></span></span>



      In 1524 he founded the first Congregation of Clerks Regular, who came to be popularly called Theatines after Bishop John Caraffa of Theate who was its first Superior (later Pope Paul IV). The Theatine Priest combined the monastic spirit with an active ministry and, by setting an example of pure and holy living, sought to improve the scholarship, morality, preaching and religious training among Priests. </span></span></span></span>



      The Theatine Order grew quietly and slowly, but made a substantial contribution to the work of Church reform. In time, the *monte de pietu* (Mountain/Fund of piety) which Cajetan founded as a non-profit credit organization went on to become the Bank of Naples.</span></span></span></span>



      Cajetan was canonized in 1671.</span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Reflection:</span></span></span> *“Sterile tears shed over contemporary evils give neither glory to God nor benefit to men. No! “Go and do likewise” — be like the Samaritan, who was not satisfied to look at the man who had been wounded and robbed, but helped him, brought him to the inn and paid his expenses”*(Blessed James Alberione).</span></span></span></span>
