  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Matthew 15:29-37"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Jesus was a tremendous crowd puller, not as a politicians of today but as a healer and a miracle worker. Particularly, it is this ministry of healing attracted a large number of people, from different walks of life, to follow him. The great events of today’s Gospel reading, both the healing of various types of broken people and the feeding of four thousand were an invitation to believe in the power of Jesus. In fact, they all came just to listen to him, he felt compassion for them and so He did not want send them away hungry. While the disciples say, “where will we find enough food and that too, in this desert to feed such a big crowd’’, Jesus remains undettered by the scarcity of the place and the circumstances. He falls back on the resources of the people. With the resources at hand, however meagre, he feeds the multitude. We cannot and should not look upon them as our slaves but in the spirit of self-help, we must build on their resources as Jesus did in today’s Gospel, which is the true charity. And so, true charity means recognizing the dignity and the rights of our neighbour.



       *(Fr Michael Raj ssp)*

    body: |
      Jesus’ power and care for His people is seen in the Gospel. He heals the multitudes, acts out of compassion and provides before they ask. Here we see, in action, the message sent to John the Baptist which confirms that He is the Messiah (11:4-5). Isaiah’s prophesy (61:1-4) is fulfilled in the person of Jesus. However, they fail to recognize in Him the Messiah. Jesus told His disciples “I have compassion for the crowd.” Though Jesus had already multiplied bread, they failed to put their trust in His power. They could only think from the human point of view, buying bread. Sometimes we, too, like the disciples, fail to believe and put our trust in Him and, instead try to solve the problems with our own strength.

    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint John Damascene - Confessor, Father and Doctor of the Church (c. 650 – c. 753)"
    ref: "Saint John Damascene - Confessor, Father and Doctor of the Church (c. 650 – c. 753)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      John Damascene was the son of a fervent Christian who, as grand vizier (chief financial officer) in Damascus, the then capital of the Arabian empire, used his great influence and wealth to purchase the liberty of numerous Christian slaves being sold by Arab raiders. In 699 he thus redeemed a devout and erudite Sicilian named Cosmas and entrusted to him the education of his growing son, John. The latter proved to have a brilliant mind, and was, on his father’s death, appointed chief counsellor of the city of Damascus but soon resigned his post on account of the hostility of Caliph Abdul Melek towards the Christian Faith.

      In c. 730, with Cosmas, John became a monk at Mar Saba near Jerusalem and was ordained priest by Patriarch John V of Jerusalem where he taught, preached and counselled in matters of faith and doctrine. When, in 726, the Byzantine Emperor Leo III promulgated his first decree against the veneration of images, John led the faithful in disregarding the command.

      Saint John Damascene who was proclaimed doctor of the church by Pope Leo XIII in 1890, is best known for his great oratorial skills and his important theological writings. His famous “Fountain of Wisdom” is an invaluable compilation of ancient Church traditions and the theological opinions of the great ecclesiastical writers up to his time constitute the earliest “Summa Theologica” and have been held in the highest esteem both by the Greek and the Roman Churches.
