  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mark 12:28b-34"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Jesus’ answer to the scribe’s question “Which commandment is the first of all?” in Mark 12:28, challenges us to take four practical steps to know God better and love Him more. Let’s dive in. First, “Love the Lord your God with all your heart,” means God wants us to love Him with everything inside us. This is, loving God passionately. When a person is passionate about something, it drives him. It defines how he lives. They make all their decisions around that passion. If we are passionate about our faith, we do our jobs and raise our families in ways that reflect Biblical principles and priorities rather than the principles and priorities of society. Second, “Love the Lord your God with all your mind,” means God wants us to love him with our brains. This is loving God thoughtfully. The Christian faith is a reasonable one – one that thinking people should be able to embrace. It’s not a blind faith at all. 223 Third, “Love the Lord your God with all your strength,” means God wants us to love Him with our gifts and abilities. This is loving God practically. God wants us to use our time, talents and treasure for the His glory and for the benefit of our brothers and sisters. Fourth, “Love your neighbour as yourself,” means God wants us to love Him by loving others. This is loving God socially. Treat other people the way you want to be treated. If we want someone to be our friend, we must first be a friend to them.



      *(Kindle Your Spirit, Fr John Pichappilly)*
  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today’s Gospel, introduces the most important commandments: to love God with all our heart, mind and strength, and to love our neighbour as ourselves. When there is a burning love for God in our heart, it drives us to love others who are God’s children. When we experience a boundless love of God, with due reverence and gratitude, we become channels of His saving love to people around. In the First Reading we see the commandment to love God with our heart, soul and might. The Second Reading teaches that unlike other priests, Jesus who was holy and blameless, made a love offering to God once and for all for the sake of the salvation of the whole world. It was the supreme example of love of God and neighbour. Our life is meant to imitate the Lord and reach out to others who are in captivity of various kinds. Can we, like Jesus, offer our lives as a sacrifice for the sins of the world? This is the best way to participate in the sacrificial offering of Jesus. Even a small act of love can be a great deal for a needy neighbour. Let us cultivate true love of God and love for His people in our hearts.
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Martin De Porres, Confessor (1579 -1639)"
    ref: "Saint Martin De Porres, Confessor (1579 -1639)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Martin was born in Lima, Peru, the child of a free love union. His father, Don Juan de Porres, a knight of Alcantara, Spain, abandoned his mother (a freed negro woman from Panama) after the birth of his son. Growing up in utter poverty, he cultivated an immense love for his fellow poor and the afflicted. At 12 the “little mulatto”, as he was wont to call himself, was apprenticed to a surgeon and soon came to be loved by the inmates of the city hospitals and prisons among whom he distributed a part of his meagre wages.

      At 15, Martin became an Oblate of the Dominicans, and such was his charity, obedience and humility, that after nine years the Friars insisted upon his becoming a lay brother. He spent many night hours in spiritual reading, prayer and penitential exercises. Though he tried to conceal the miraculous favours which God bestowed upon him, he soon came to be regarded as a saint, an angel of peace and of compassion; he was able to heal the sick by his mere touch, by his prayers, by the Sign of the Cross; he had the gift of prophecy and invisibility; he could read people’s hearts and understand unexpressed desires even at great distances. At certain times God permitted the friars to see Martin accompanied by angels or enveloped in light during his ecstasy.

      Twenty-five years after his death his body was found to have remained supple and incorrupt.

      Canonized in 1962, St Martin de Porres is called the patron of social justice.
