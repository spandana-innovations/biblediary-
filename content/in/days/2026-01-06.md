    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      The Apostles requested Jesus to send the crowd away to get food for themselves. They in fact showed concern but were not kind enough nor did they trust in the power of Jesus. Jesus had compassion on the crowd because they were like sheep without a shepherd, with no one to take care of their needs. Jesus nourished the hungry crowd with the word of God and with bead and fish by working the miracle of the multiplication of bread and fish. This shows that he is the Messiah, the true shepherd. Like Jesus, the Church and all its members should reach out to the poor and console and care for them. If we surrender to him out poor and little possessions and abilities, he will multiply them and make them abundant and return them to us for our benefit and that of so many others. Jesus can do what we think is impossible. Do we trust him enough and willingly cooperate with him? </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel describes Jesus’ miraculous feeding of a great multitude. The story is told in all four Gospels and serves as Jesus’ way of introducing to those present a merciful and providing God. Through God’s power, Elijah gave the widow, each day, just enough to meet her needs (1 Kgs 17:13-16). Jesus, on the other hand, gives generously and abundantly. This miraculous feeding was meant to remind people of God’s provision of manna in the wilderness and to prefigure the true Heavenly Bread, which Jesus would offer His followers, for Jesus performed this miracle just before promising the Sacrament of the Eucharist for our spiritual feeding.</span></span></span>



      While teaching, Jesus took pity on the growing physical hunger of those listening, and challenged the apostles to feed them. But they had with them only five loaves of bread and two dried fish. Jesus took these, said a prayer of blessing, broke them, and asked the apostles to distribute them till the hungry people were fully satisfied. Since it was mid-April, springtime in Israel, the people could sit comfortably on green grass in their groups of hundreds and fifties. After serving a generous meal which satisfied all, the Apostles collected twelve wicker baskets of leftover bread and fish pieces, a vivid proof and reminder of God’s generosity in giving and a warning to all of us not to waste God’s blessings.</span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life messages</span>: 1) We may not be able to feed the hungry millions in the world, but today’s Gospel challenges us to do our humble share in alleviating hunger and poverty in our neighbourhood. God will amplify our little contributions and reward our good will and generosity. 2) We need to be thankful to God for miraculously giving us our daily spiritual bread in the Holy Eucharist.</span></span></span>

      In today’s Gospel, Jesus feeds the five thousand and reveals that He is the compassionate Good Shepherd. Moved by the people’s physical hunger, He reaches out and provides for them. In this miracle, we see the divine capacity to meet human needs, both physical and spiritual, despite limited resources. In God’s hands, even the smallest provision becomes abundant. The act of Jesus taking, blessing and breaking the bread, and giving it to the crowds can be seen as a foreshadowing of the Eucharist, through which He nourishes each and every believer. Furthermore, it points to the eschatological banquet, where God’s blessings are meant for all and where everyone is invited. How do I nourish the hungry world around me today? </span></span>
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Blessed André Bessette - Religious (1845-1937)"
    ref: "Blessed André Bessette - Religious (1845-1937)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      The eighth of twelve children of French parentage in Quebec, Canada, André was orphaned at twelve. In his youth he worked in the U.S.A. at various jobs - farmhand, shoemaker, baker, but failed grossly at everything!</span></span></span></span>



      At 25, his admission to the Holy Cross Brothers in Canada lapsed after a year’s novitiate on account of ill health which, incidentally, had assailed him right from birth, depriving him of regular schooling, leaving him unlettered all his life. Finally admitted at the instance of the Bishop, he was appointed doorkeeper at the Notre Dame College in Montreal, apart from janitor, sacristan, infirmarian, launderer, barber etc. He once humorously said, “When I joined this community, the superiors showed me the door, and I remained 40 years!”</span></span></span></span>



      Helpful by nature, effective as counsellor and unassumingly pious, he spent long nights on his knees in prayer in his room near the door. On his windowsill facing Mount Royal stood a little statue of Saint Joseph his devotion to whom was so profound that he obtained healing for many by rubbing them lightly with oil taken from the lamp burning before him, suspicion and skepticism giving way to awe as the pile of crutches, canes and braces grew! “Saint Joseph cures, not I!” </span></span></span></span>



      André’s desire to build a magnificent chapel in the saint’s honour was fulfilled by saving the nickels and dimes earned from haircuts to college students. The walls of the chapel, built atop a steep hill, having come up by 1931, money ran out!</span></span> “Put a statue of Saint Joseph in the middle. If he wants a roof over his head, he’ll get it”, commented someone, and get it he did! The Oratory on Mount Royal, which took 50 years to build, now houses André’s mortal remains. </span></span></span></span>



      André Bessette, who died aged 92, was beatified by Pope Saint John Paul II in 1982. </span></span></span></span>
