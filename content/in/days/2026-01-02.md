      Reflection: 1



      On being asked who he is, John the Baptist rejects all the great titles people attribute to him. He says plainly that he is not the expected Messiah, or Elijah or a prophet. He quotes Prophet Isaiah and says he is the one crying in the wilderness, “Make straight the way of the Lord.” He says he is only baptizing with water, calling people to repentance. But there is one among the people who is so great that he is not even worthy to untie his sandals. John knows exactly who he is and what his mission is. He does not claim to be greater than he is, though he could have easily done so as people hold him in great esteem. John’s humility, sincerity and faithfulness to Christ are models for us to follow.</span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      The news reached the central Jewish religious authorities in Jerusalem that one John, the son of a Jewish priest, was preaching repentance and renewal of life to the Jews and inviting them to receive the baptism of repentance meant only for Gentiles. Hence, the Sanhedrin sent a delegation of experts to Bethany on the eastern bank of river Jordan (different from the Bethany near Jerusalem, where Lazarus lived), to discover whether John was claiming to be the expected Messiah or his forerunner Elijah, the prophet, and to ask why he encouraged the Chosen People to receive the baptism of repentance.</span></span></span>



      John frankly declared in all humility that he was not Elijah nor the expected Messiah nor even one of the Old Testament prophets reincarnated. Later, Jesus referred to him as “a lamp “*He was a burning and shining lamp, and you were willing to rejoice for a while in his light* (Jn 5:35). In the spiritual life, the ideal is to become invisible, and our role as Christians is to become salt, yeast, grain, and light. But John claimed that he was the forerunner of the real Messiah, and that his mission was to prepare the lives of the Jews to receive the expected Messiah and to bear witness to him when he should appear in public. John also explained to them that he was baptizing the Jews with water because they must be made holy through repenting of their sins and renewing their lives if they were to receive the most Holy Messiah in their midst.</span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span> </span></span></span>



      1) As Catholic Christians, we believe in the coming of Jesus our Lord and Saviour on our altars during each Eucharistic celebration. Hence, we, too, need to repent of our sins and ask God’s pardon and forgiveness on a daily basis if we wish to receive Jesus into our hearts and lives sacramentally. </span></span></span>



      2) We, too, need to renew our lives with the help of our Lord Jesus living within us, together with the Father and the Holy Spirit, so that He may radiate His love, forgiveness, and mercy to all around us. 3) We too need to practice the true humility of John the Baptist.</span></span></span>

      Today’s Gospel reading focuses on John’s testimony, correcting misconceptions about who he is and what he is doing. He rejects all suggested messianic titles and points to the one who is coming to fulfil God’s promises. The personal witness of John the Baptist invites us to learn to witness to Christ in our daily lives and to avoid seeking the limelight for ourselves. We should not take Christ’s place but allow Him to shine through our words and actions. Often, we tend to attract people to ourselves so they can see us shining; however, we are called to be lights so that others can see Christ and come closer to Him through us.</span></span>

      (1) Saint Basil the Great</span></span> - *Bishop Confessor, Father and Doctor of the Universal Church (330-379)*</span></span></span></span>



      SaintBasil comes from a wealthy and holy family, for the Church honours as saints his Parents, Basil the Elder, and his mother, Emelia (daughter of a Martyr), his brothers, Gregory of Nyssa and Peter of Sebaste, his sister, Macrina the younger, as well as both the parents of his father. </span></span></span></span>



      Well versed in the letters, rhetoric, philosophy, astronomy, geometry and medicine at Athens, he was influenced by Macrina to turn his mind to spiritual pursuits. </span></span>Ordained Priest in 364 by Archbishop Eusebius of Caesarea, he was in 370 elected to succeed the archbishop to whom, by his sanctity, learning and administrative skill, he had become indispensable. “One led the people while the other led the leader” says Gregory, his friend and biographer.</span></span></span></span>



      After visiting the flourishing colonies of monks and hermits across Syria, Palestine, Mesopotamia and Egypt, he led the eremitical life for 5 years before founding a monastic colony on his family property in Pontus. His famous ‘Rule of Saint Basil’ (*manual labour*, *study*and *prayer*) gained rapid acceptance, earning him the title, ‘Father of Eastern Monasticism’, his intrepid opposition to the Arianizing efforts of Emperor Valens ensuring that Capadocia remained Catholic.</span></span></span></span>



       The faithful observance of the Canons by clergy and laity alike rendered the diocese a model to all even as his concern for the destitute led Basil to establish facilities for the care of the sick poor, the helpless aged, ostracized lepers and the homeless and the technical training of the unskilled; all work supported by an aroused social responsibility of the wealthy, Basil himself practised the Christian precept of ascetic poverty, spending his entire material inheritance on the starving population during a famine.</span></span></span></span>



      Outstanding among the doctrinal works of this patron of Russia and of hospital administrators are his treatise on the Holy Spirit and the Eucharistic Liturgy he formulated.</span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Reflection:</span></span></span> *“I read the Gospel then and realized that the great means for attaining perfection lies in selling one’s goods and sharing them with the poor, in dropping all anxieties about this life, and refusing to allow the soul to be turned aside by the love of any earthly thing”* (Saint Basil the Great).</span></span></span></span>



      (2) Saint Gregory Nazianzen</span></span> - *Bishop Confessor, Father and Doctor of the Universal Church (c.330-389/390)*</span></span></span></span>



      Like Saint Basil, Gregory was also born into a family of Saints: his father was Gregory the elder, Bishop of Nazianzus, his mother, Nonna, his brother, Caesarius and his sister, Gorgonia. When studying at Caesarea he formed an intimate friendship with Basil. Later studies took him to Caesarea in Palestine, to Alexandria, Egypt, and finally to Athens, where he rejoined Basil in studying rhetoric. At 31, both he and Basil decided to serve God’s as hermits. But a few years later his father insisted on ordaining him a priest. The great treatise which the young Gregory then wrote on the priestly office was to become an inspiration to Saints John Chrysostom, Gregory the Great and countless others.</span></span></span></span>



      Eleven years later, Basil, who had meanwhile become his Metropolitan, consecrated him bishop against his will, but Gregory, considering it a semi-political move, left his diocese soon after and returned to Nazianzus to assist his ageing father.</span></span></span></span>



      In 379, called to Constantinople, the stronghold of the Arian heresy, by a small remnant of faithful Catholics, Gregory, by his great sanctity and eloquence brought about a genuine rebirth of the Faith. His remarkable discourses on the Trinity in the light of the Nicene Creed earned him the title “the Divine”, which the Greek Church has given to only one other Saint, viz., John the Evangelist. One of Gregory’s eminent pupils at this time was Saint Jerome.</span></span></span></span>



      In 380, Emperor Theodosius exiled and replaced the Arian Patriarch with Gregory. However, his gentle and highly sensitive nature, coupled with his continually poor health, caused him to resign from this new but stormy posting after only a few months before finally retiring to Nazianzus, where he died.</span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Reflection:</span></span></span> *“Our (Basil’s and mine) single object and ambition was virtue, and a life of hope in the blessings that are to come; we wanted to withdraw from this world before we departed from it. With this end in view we ordered our lives and all our actions. We followed the guidance of God’s law and spurred each other on to virtue”*(Saint Gregory Nazianzen).</span></span></span></span>
