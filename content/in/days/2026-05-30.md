  - key: homily

    ref: "Mark 11: 27- 33"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      The Jewish authorities want to trap Jesus into an assertion that he has divine authority, so that they can cook up charges of blasphemy against him. But they themselves get trapped. The counter question Jesus asks about the source of John the Baptist’s authority places them in a dilemma. And to save their face, they feign ignorance. Their gesture is right, but their intention is wrong. The ingenuity and scheming of the human and anti-kingdom agents serve, against their will, to highlight the divine origin of Jesus and his authority. No one can defeat the programme of the kingdom of Jesus. Jesus invites us to examine the purity of our intention, to live under the authority of God and to do good, with good and pure intention. </span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      After casting out animal merchants and money- changers from the Temple immediately after the Palm Sunday procession, Jesus started teaching in the Temple courts. Hence, the chief priests and elders of the people approached Jesus questioning his authority for entering the city in a triumphal procession, for allowing the children to acclaim him, for curing the sick, for casting out merchants and moneychangers, and for teaching in the Temple area. This was a trap. If Jesus claimed that his authority was Divine as the Messiah, they would arrest him as a blasphemer. If he claimed that his authority was human, he would be arrested as a mad zealot damaging the people’s simple Faith in the Temple and what it stood for. Since Jesus did not want to risk his life and mission a few days ahead of time, he silenced them by asking a challenging counter-question about John the Baptist and his message – was this from God or man? Was this Divine or human?</span></span></span></span></span>



      If they answered that it was Divine, the questioners would be asked to explain why they did not accept John’s message and his witness-bearing that Jesus was the Messiah. If they answered that it was human, they would have to face the anger of the crowd who had accepted John as a prophet. Hence, they kept silent, opting for a shameful self-humiliation.</span></span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life message: </span></span></span>In religious matters we should not ask whether our stand for Christ is safe or useful. Instead, we need to stand for Truth with the courage of our Christian convictions even if it costs our life. </span></span></span></span></span>

      “By what authority are you doing these things” or “who gave you this authority to do them?” Although Jesus did not give a clear and direct answer to the question, we can straightaway give the answer: God gave Him this authority. Bringing John the Baptist into the picture is a clever answer that Jesus posed to them, and the authorities have no answer to it. John the Baptist was a popular prophet in the area around the Jordan River. He proclaimed the coming kingdom of God and the need to prepare for the divine judgement that will accompany it. John was known and the people were going to him. The authorities, who knew about John and his ministry, want to maintain their power which they derive from the Temple. Jesus and John are Kingdom-centred. The authorities who came to trap Jesus got themselves trapped.</span></span></span>

    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Joan of Arc, Virgin,  Martyr (1412-1431)"
    ref: "Saint Joan of Arc, Virgin,  Martyr (1412-1431)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      The “Maid of Orleans” and liberator of France, Jean d’Arc was a native of Domremy in Lorraine. The youngest of five children of a poor peasant farmer named Jacques, she was from her 13th year conscious of supernatural “voices” directing her. Three years later these voices (Sts Michael, Margaret and Catherine) insistently urged her to lead the French King’s armies to victory over the English, just when their complete defeat seemed imminent — “It is God who commands it!”</span></span></span></span>



      In the ensuing winter, however, many military advantages were lost through procrastination and political jealousies, and when Joan was captured at Compiegne by the Burgandians, the urgrateful Charles abandoned her to her fate — she was sold by her captors to the English, who, in order to rid themselves of one who inspired their troops with superstitious terror, had her condemned for witchery by subservient prelates and judges. In 1431, just 19, she was burned alive at the stake at Rouen. A generation later, a re-trial of her case, at the request of her family, established Joan’s complete innocence. </span></span></span></span>



      Joan of Arc was canonized in 1920 and is the Patron of France.</span></span></span></span>
