    ref: "John 3: 16-21"

    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      The Jews have been praying to God to condemn the evil world and to punish the wicked. But God sends His own Son to be crucified in order to save the world. Hence the world is the object of God’s love. Here the world means the whole of creation of which humanity is the crown, and God loves and wants all to be saved. In another sense, the world refers to human beings who deliberately and willfully stand against God and are under the influence of evil. Evil continues to influence all who refuse to acknowledge the truth of Jesus. The Father has send Jesus His Son to save all those who accept him and believe in him. God reveals in Jesus the depth of His love for us. To choose Jesus is to choose life. Those who reject Jesus are judging and condemning themselves and excluding themselves from light and life. </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Jesus explained God’s plan of salvation to Nicodemus by declaring that the story of Moses and the brazen serpent was a sign pointing to the Good News that God would show His love for mankind by subjecting His own Son to suffering and death in order to save them all: *“For God so loved the world that He gave His only Son, so that everyone who believes in him might not perish but might have eternal life” (Jn 3:16).*This is the summary of the Gospel message of salvation through Christ Jesus. This is the Good News in the Gospels.</span></span></span></span></span>



      Today’s Gospel passage teaches us that our salvation is the free gift of a merciful God, given to us through Jesus, His Son. It explains that Jesus, the Incarnate Son of God, became the agent of God’s salvation, not just for one sinful nation, but for the sins of the whole world from the beginning through the end. Through *Jn 3:16*, the Gospel teaches us that God has expressed His love, mercy, and compassion for us by giving His Only Son for our Salvation. This tells us that the initiative in all Salvation is God’s love for man. St. Augustine of Hippo describes a dream message received by his mother, Monica, who prayed and wept unceasingly, fearing Augustine would be damned because of the life he was leading. This message convinced her that she had to live with him, not cut him off as she had been doing, for God still loved him even in his present condition. Augustine’s example also explains to us the universality of the love of God. God’s motive is Love and God’s objective is Salvation. Those who actually receive eternal life must believe in the Son and express that love in deeds.</span></span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life message:</span></span></span> 1) We need to respond to God’s love for us by loving and serving Him in others in whom He dwells. God’s love for us is unconditional, universal, forgiving, and merciful. Let us make an earnest attempt to include these qualities in sharing our love with others during this Easter season. “In the evening of life you will be examined on love,” said St. John of the Cross [*Dichos, 64, note 595, CCC 1022; Sayings of Light and Love, #57*in *The Collected Works of St. John of the Cross*, trans. Kieran Kavanaugh OCD and Otilio Rodrigues, OCD Institute of Carmelite Studies, (Washington, DC: ICS Publications, 1979, p,672*)*.] — What St. John of the Cross means by “love” is love expressed in deeds. </span></span></span></span></span>

      “God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son.” These words from today’s Gospel reveal the depth of God’s love for us through Jesus, His greatest gift. God’s love has no limits and conditions; Jesus came not to judge us, but to save us and offer us eternal life. When we truly believe in Him and make His love the centre of our lives, we experience the new life in its fullness – one that is filled with hope, purpose, and peace. Jesus is the light that guides us out of darkness, helping us see the truth and live in freedom. By walking in his light, we grow closer to God and receive the strength to live in love and goodness. Choosing Jesus means choosing life, both now and forever. Let us embrace this gift, allowing his love to transform us and bring us the abundant, eternal life that only He can give.</span></span></span>

    saintName: "Saint Benedict Joseph Labre - Confessor (1748-1783)"
    ref: "Saint Benedict Joseph Labre - Confessor (1748-1783)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      The eldest of 15 children of a well-to-do peasant of Amettes, near Boulogne in France, Benedict studied for some years with an uncle who was the Vicar of Erin until he realized that he lacked a priestly vocation and felt called to the cloister. However, he was successively turned away by the Carthusians, the Trappists and the Cistercians, by which time he was 22. </span></span></span></span>



      With a pilgrimage to Rome began his unique life as “the holy tramp”—13 years of continuous pilgrimage to the great shrines of; maintaining a strict fast and abstinence, he begged along the way, sharing the little he received by way of food and alms with the poor and the needy. His baggage comprised only his New Testament, Breviary, “The Imitation of Christ” and some books of prayer. Wrapped in prayer and peace, he remained perennially cheerful. </span></span></span></span>



      Seven years of pilgrimages later, after becoming a Franciscan tertiary, he stayed on in Rome but, having no cell or fixed abode, he took his modicum of sleep amidst the ruins of the Colosseum where, in the dead of night, he would implore God’s mercy with outstretched arms before the Stations of the Cross. He frequented churches that held the 40 Hours’ devotion and spent hours in ecstasy, thus becoming known as the “beggar of perpetual adoration”. </span></span></span></span>



      When he died during Holy Week 1783, aged 35, such crowds poured into Santa Maria dei Monti where he “lay in state” that the Easter Mass could not be sung and the military was called in to maintain order. The hundred-and-thirty-six miraculous cures attributed to his intercession within the first three months lead to his canonization in 1883.</span></span></span></span>
