  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 10:38–42"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Jesus and the apostles are invited to the home of Martha and Mary. Lazarus, their brother, takes the apostles out to look at the cattle and the crops, but Jesus is tired. He has been doing all the work, while the apostles are standing around learning. In the house we have nervous, energetic Martha: rush, rush, get a big banquet on the table, blow at the fire, stir the soup, clank platters and plates, check the meat in the pan. Is Mary lazy by not helping her sister? “Not at all,” says Jesus. Mary lives by the Spirit, and Jesus declares this to be of first importance. He is not, however, condemning Martha’s hospitality. It’s genuine, it’s good, and Jesus knows Martha’s faith. But there is a greater kind of hospitality, and Mary has found it. It is “listening to the Lord,” opening not only our house but our mind and heart to Jesus. </span></span>



      *(Pray Today’s Gospel, Bernard C. Mischke O.S.C. and Fritz Mischke O.S.C.)*</span></span>

    body: |
      The account of Jesus’ visit to Martha and Mary brings to the fore two contrasting characters: a) Martha welcomes Jesus as a guest *(atitthi devo bhava),* Mary welcomes him as a teacher *(Guru devo bhava).* b) Martha is the scattered personality (exterior), Mary is centred personality (interior). c) Martha wants to feed Jesus with earthly food but Mary wants to be fed by Jesus’ presence, his love and wisdom. d) Martha is action oriented. Mary chooses to be a contemplative. e) Martha loves words, Mary loves silence. f) Martha is busy with too many things. Mary has chosen one necessary thing which will not be taken away from her. g) Martha is restless and complaining whereas Mary is quiet and peaceful. *Which of these two characters can I identify myself with?* </span></span></span></span>

    audio: null
    body: |
      Sergius and Bacchus were said to have been officers of the Roman army on the Syrian frontier; Sergius being described as commandant of the recruits’ school and Bacchus as his subaltern. They were personal favourites of the Emperor Maximian, until one day when he went into the temple of Jupiter to offer sacrifice he noticed that they did not enter with him.</span></span></span>



      Ordered to take part in the sacrifice, both of them refused and professed their faith in Christ, much to the dismay of the Emperor. They were soon stripped of their arms and ranks and instead paraded through the streets, dressed in women’s clothing. But none of these humiliations and threats could change their hearts.</span></span></span>



      They were then dispatched to Risafe in Mesopotamia, where the governor had them scourged so severely that St Bacchus died under the lash. His body was thrown out on the highway, where vultures protected it from being attacked by dogs—an incident recorded about several other martyrs. St Sergius was made to walk a long distance wearing shoes with nails thrust through into his feet, and was then beheaded.</span></span></span>



      In 431, Alexander, Metropolitan of Hierapolis, restored and blessed the church over the grave of St Sergius, whose walls in the middle of the sixth century were plated with silver. The church at Risafe was one of the most famous in the East, and Sergius and Bacchus became the heavenly protectors of the Byzantine army, with the two Theodores, Demetrius, Procopius and George.</span></span></span>
