      Reflection: 1



      Jesus went up the mountain, a place away from the crowds, a place of prayer. There he called the persons of his choice, and they willingly went with him. He chose them and they accepted his choice. From them he chose twelve to be his closest friends and helpers, and named them Apostles. They were to carry on his mission, expand it and bring it to various parts of the world. They were ordinary simple people without any outstanding qualities. They had their own positive and negative points. </span></span></span></span>



      Jesus chose the Twelve not because of their merits, but because of his loving plan. They were to be with him and to go and preach the Gospel, bringing Jesus with them to very many people. He shared with them his own authority to heal and even to cast out demons. Jesus chooses the weak and makes them strong. Their loyalty to him is the one thing required of them. </span></span></span></span>



      Jesus trained the Twelve by his word and example, instruction and correction. There were occasions when their weaknesses prevailed, as in the case of Peter who denied him, and of the others who ran away from the scene of the crucifixion. But, except for Judas Iscariot who betrayed him, they all repented and returned to Jesus. </span></span></span></span>



      None of us is equal to the task Jesus gives us. But let us willingly and thankfully accept his mission, trusting that he will empower us and work with us. The Lord is with us; we should take care to be with him always.</span></span></span></span>

      Reflection: 2</span></span> </span></span></span></span>



      Today’s Gospel passage gives a short account of the call and mission of the Apostles. Jesus is the first missionary, sent by his Father with the “Good News” that God, his Father, is a loving, merciful, and forgiving Father Who wants to save everyone through His Son Jesus. Today’s Gospel describes how Jesus selects and empowers twelve future missionaries as apostles, giving them his own mission along with a share of his power to preach, and to heal the sick as proof of the truth of their message. Then, Jesus sends them in pairs to the Jewish towns and villages as heralds, to prepare the people to receive the Good News.</span>



      Special features: Jesus selected very ordinary people, most of them hard-working fishermen with no social status, learning, or political influence, because he was sure that they would be very effective instruments in God’s hands. It was a strange mixture of people. Matthew was a hated tax-collector for a foreign power, while Simon the Cananaean was a Zealot and fanatical nationalist who belonged to a militant group determined to destroy Roman rule by any means. The others were mostly professional fishermen with a lot of good will, patience and stamina. At first it was only their admiration and love for Jesus that united them. Jesus selected them after a night of prayer and gave them his own powers of healing and exorcism and his own mission of preaching the “Kingdom of God.”</span>



      <span class="subhead">Life message:</span> 1) As Christians, we have the same mission that Jesus entrusted to his apostles: to proclaim the word of God to all the world. We fulfill this mission primarily by living out Jesus’ teachings and by promoting and helping the world-wide missionary activities of the Church with prayer, moral support, and financial aid.</span>

      The twelve Apostles constitute a special group within the broader community of disciples. This group was not famous or qualified as per the world’s standards. There was nothing commendable about their resumé. They were all different: different personalities, backgrounds, skills, strengths and weaknesses. Yet they were all chosen by Jesus uniquely and specially. When it comes to answering Jesus’ call to you, you need not be a pious and ideal Christian. You need not have all the skills and qualifications to be at the Lord’s work. You just have to be *willing*. After all, Jesus will empower and equip you for his purpose because he is in control of your calling. </span>

    ref: "Saint Francis de Sales - Bishop Confessor Doctor of the Church (1567-1622)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      The eldest of thirteen children, Francis de Sales was born to aristocrats Francis de Boisy and Frances de Sionnaz at the Sales castle France. At 25 he obtained his doctorate in law at Padua, but, declining a senatorship and giving up law, proceeded to become a priest.



      The province of Le Chablais on the south shore of Lake Geneva, under the Protestant city of Berne for fifty years, had just been re-conquered by the Duke of Savoy, a Catholic. Francis and his cousin, the Canon Louis de Sales, volunteered to win its Calvinist inhabitants back to the Faith. He set forth the Church’s principles in the famous “Controversies”, hand-copied leaflets of which he would slip under house-doors or paste up on walls. No wonder he was declared patron of writers/journalists by Pius XI in 1923!



      People who heard him preach were converted as much by his gracious attitude as by his lucid arguments. Francis succeeded in converting over 70,000 Calvinists and cherishing the joy of seeing all churches re-opened to Catholic worship. His ministrations though were not without risks. At 32, Francis had signally so impressed Pope Clement VIII, who personally examined him in the presence of his Cardinals, that he was appointed to succeed Bishop de Granier in 1602.



      As bishop, Francis drastically simplified his lifestyle so as to enable him the better to succour the poor and the needy. He reformed religious communities, formulated simple language catechetical instruction for both young and old, and held annual synods for his clergy on whom he particularly impressed the value of short, straightforward and unadorned sermons.



      Voluminous correspondence apart, he made time to write masterpieces of spiritual guidance like *Introduction to the Devout Life*for the benefit of the laity, and *Treatise on the Love of God* and *Spiritual Conferences*, both addressed to the Visitation sisters that he and Saint Jane Frances de Chantal had founded in 1610.



      Beatified in 1662 and canonized in 1665 Francis was declared Doctor of the Church in 1877.
