  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mark 1:14-20"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Jesus, the Rabbi from Nazareth calls his disciples with a note of urgency. It is because Jesus brings good news that God has entered human history. He invites humans to participate in the task of proclaiming the reign of God. This entails repentance and immediate decision to follow him.



      Mark describes Jesus ‘coming as the “time of fulfilment”. He has in mind the prophecies of old which spoke of a time when God would definitely intervene in human history and straighten them by changing their hearts. But this appointed time is also a time for people to decide totally for God. There is no neutrality, neither can one postpone the decision. Thus there is a note of urgency in his invitation.



      The saints are persons who responded positively and decisively for the kingdom of God. Once they heard his voice, they immediately answered him with a yes, much like the fishermen of today’s gospel.



      (Fr George C. Mathew, SSP)
  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      The book of the prophet Jonah gives us the story of a nationalist who did not want to preach conversion among his enemies. Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian empire and a city known for its evil ways. But Jonah finally reached the city announcing the impending danger and called everyone to repentance. They heeded to his preaching, put on sackcloth and fasted. Their action had the desired effect of God showing them mercy. When there is sincerity of heart and humility to change our ways, God’s blessings in our lives will be abundant. In the Gospel Jesus begins his own ministry of announcing the imminent coming of the Kingdom calling people to repentance which his forerunner John the Baptist had begun. He then calls his disciples to be his companions in accomplishing his mission, to be fishers of men. The concept of God’s Kingdom reflects a promise and expectation of an ideal situation as reflected also in Zechariah 14:9. The call to repent denotes a sense of being sorry and consequent change of heart. To believe essentially means to have an attitude of trust because the one being trusted is faithful. Jesus’ invitation to repent and believe in the Good News refers to having faith in him. The urgency of our response calls for an immediate action like the first disciples who left everything and followed Jesus.
