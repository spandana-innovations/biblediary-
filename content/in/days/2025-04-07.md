      Reflection: 1



      The Pharisees were angry with Jesus for telling them the truth about his own divine origin. Jesus is the light of the world. He is the light of truth that brings to us the knowledge of the Father. Anyone who follows Jesus will not walk in the darkness of ignorance, superstition and evil. The declaration Jesus makes about himself is entirely true because he comes from the Father, knows the Father and knows himself. It is the Father who sent him. Jesus is not merely a teacher or prophet, but the Son of God who became man. The more we open ourselves to receive light from Jesus, the more we shall be able to give light to others, leading them to him who is the light of the world. Therefore, faith coupled with loving obedience is the only way we should respond to Jesus. That will benefit us as well as others. </span></span></span></span>

      Reflection: 2



      It was the first night of the Jewish Festival of the Tabernacles (Festival of Booths), and Jesus was standing in the Court of the Women in the Temple courtyard in Jerusalem. It was evening, and Jesus was facing the huge blaze of light from the two colossal golden lamp stands, on which, as was customary for this evening, hung a multitude of lamps, lighted after the evening sacrifice. The light brightened the whole city of Jerusalem (Navarre Bible). Suddenly Jesus made the controversial claim, “I am the Light of the World,” which was saying that, like Yahweh, Jesus was the Light of the world. This claim was seen by the scribes and Pharisees as his making himself equal to God (blasphemy), for Jews associated the word light with God. *The Lord is my Light (Psalm 27:1). The Lord will be your everlasting Light (Isaiah 60:19). When I sit in darkness, the Lord will be a Light to me (Micah 7:8).*The Jewish reaction: The Jews challenged Jesus’ claim as one being made without two witnesses and hence baseless. But Jesus argued that he had another witness, God his Father, and, hence, that his claim was valid. He further declared that their counterargument was wrong because of their ignorance of who Jesus really was as the Son of God.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages: </span>



      1) We believe in Jesus’ claims because his miracles and his Resurrection from the dead prove that Jesus is God.



      2) Every Christian needs to receive the Light from Jesus and reflect and radiate that Light in the form of love, kindness, mercy, forgiveness, and humble service.

      Jesus’ declaration, “I am the light of the world,” carries profound implications for our life. It asserts that he is not merely a prophet, teacher, or moral leader but the divine, eternal God incarnate. He is one with the Father, as he consistently emphasizes throughout the Gospel of John. The passage invites us to embrace and follow Jesus, the light and the source of spiritual illumination and truth. We are challenged to follow him, allowing his light to dispel the darkness of sin and ignorance in our lives. It is important to recognize God’s testimony in Jesus to respond with faith and obedience.

    body: |
      John Baptist de la Salle was made a canon of the cathedral of Reims at age 16 and ordained priest at 27. A model of piety and humility, he was gradually drawn through his love for the poor, into what was to become his life-work.



      At that time Jansenism—whose twin focus was the denial of man’s ability to resist temptation and the rejection of the doctrine that Christ died for all men—had taken firm root in France; the constant foreign and internal wars had brought people famine, ruin and untold suffering; education was neglected, ignorance and vice abounding. Faced with such desperate conditions, John de la Salle rejoiced at being asked to help found two free schools for the poor. He took personal and active interest in the teachers and their methods, encouraged them, housed and fed them under his own roof, thus guaranteeing them against excessive expenditure which might have consumed their meagre earnings.



      To John the education of the poor was an urgent need. The Institute of the Brothers of Christian Schools, founded about 1680 on simplicity, mortification and absolute reliance upon Divine Providence, finally blossomed into the flourishing and worldwide religious congregation it is today. There were to be no priests in his institute but only brothers, their lives fully consecrated as “educators of the mind” and “apostles of the soul”. Above all he insisted on the teaching of the Faith in Catholic Schools and the use of the vernacular instead of Latin in school texts, so that through the pupils’ reading ability the parents at home might also receive an education in Christian doctrine and morals.



      His writings embrace the fundamentals of scientific pedagogy, Christian behaviour and manners, Christian doctrine, treatises on the religious life, method of mental prayer and numerous meditations — all simple, direct and vigorous in approach.



      John Baptist de la Salle died in 1719, was canonized in 1900, and declared patron of all teachers in 1950.
