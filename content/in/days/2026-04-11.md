    ref: "Mark 16: 9-15"

    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      The Gospel text summarises the appearances of the risen Jesus and his ascension. More especially it points out that the outcome of the Resurrection is the universal mission of the Church. Witnessing to Jesus risen from the dead and now seated with the Father is the task and the mission of the Church. Like the early Christians, we too may not always succeed in fulfilling this duty as we ought to; fear and doubt may prevent us from doing this. The risen Jesus continues to challenge us to bear witness to him in the whole world. Though the Apostles initially did not believe, later they believed and boldly proclaimed the Good News of Jesus amidst difficulties and oppositions. Jesus did not abandon them or disqualify them; he helped them to accept and proclaim the truth.



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel mentions the three appearances of the risen Lord and Jesus’ entrusting to the apostles their mission to preach his Good News: *“Go into the whole world and proclaim the Gospel to every creature.”* (Mk 16:15). According to Mark (in the longer ending), Jesus first appeared to Mary Magdalene. But Jesus’ apostles did not believe her. Later, he appeared to two disciples during their trip to Emmaus. When they had returned to Jerusalem and while they were explaining their experience, Jesus appeared to the eleven apostles gathered there. After gently chiding them for their unbelief and hard-heartedness, Jesus gave them his preaching mission or the *“universal Apostolic mandate.”*



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span> 1) The preaching and witnessing mission of Jesus applies especially to the successors of the Twelve Apostles, namely, the Bishops in communion with Peter’s successor, the Pope. 2) However, Vatican II declared in the Decree on the Apostolate of the Laity (*Apostolicum Actuositatem),* that the whole *“Church was founded to spread the Kingdom of Christ over all the earth for the glory of God the Father, to make all men partakers in Redemption and Salvation” (AA, 2).“On all Christians, accordingly, rests the noble obligation of working to bring all men throughout the whole world to hear and accept the Divine message of salvation” (AA, 3).(Fr Tony: https://frtonyshomilies.com/)*

    title: Reflection
    ref: null

    body: |
      Today’s Gospel contrasts the effects of belief and unbelief in the resurrection of Jesus. Mary Magdalene’s belief drives her to share the good news, becoming the first to proclaim Jesus’ resurrection. This faith transforms her, giving her courage to move beyond her past and her comfort zone. Similarly, the apostles’ belief leads them to preach boldly, despite challenges, guiding others towards God’s love. In contrast, the apostles’ initial unbelief – and the religious leaders’ resistance – highlights how unbelief can close us off to grace. When we doubt God’s promises, we miss out on the fullness of truth and blessings He wants to give us. Jesus calls us, like He did His disciples, to believe and share His message. As we grow in faith, He empowers us to move beyond our fears, share His love, and experience the peace and joy that come with trusting in His resurrection.

    saintName: "Saint Stanislaus - Bishop Martyr (1030-1079)"
    ref: "Saint Stanislaus - Bishop Martyr (1030-1079)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Saint Stanislaus was born in Szczepanow near Krakow on 26 July 1030. His parents, members of the Polish nobility, were devout Catholics. Following their death Stanislaus distributed his inheritance among the poor. Not long after his ordination, he was appointed canon and preacher at the cathedral of Krakow, and then Vicar General.



      Humble to the core, he accepted his election as Bishop of Krakow in 1072 only upon the express command of Pope Alexander II. An exemplary shepherd, he stood fearlessly for right and justice, never hesitating to denounce vice even in high places. His inability to sway King Boleslas II of Poland from the scandal he was causing by the cruel treatment of his subjects and by his unbridled lust to the excommunication of the king and the suspension of all public services in the cathedral, he himself retiring to the chapel of Saint Michael in a suburb of Krakow.



      The king was furious and, in 1079, sent some of his knights to kill the holy bishop. But when they did not dare to carry out the command, he himself burst into the chapel where Stanislaus was celebrating Mass and split his head with his sword. As a result, Pope Gregory VII laid all Poland under an interdict. Consequently, in 1081, Boleslas was dethroned and exiled, and two years later died in Hungary as a penitent with the Benedictines.



      Stanislaus, whose body was enshrined in the main altar of the Cathedral in 1089 by his successor, was canonized in 1253 at Assisi, the first Polish saint ever and principal patron of Poland.
