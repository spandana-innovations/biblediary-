  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mathew 12: 1-8"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today’s Gospel passage gives us Jesus’ teaching on the purpose of the Sabbath and on its proper observance. This was his response to a criticism and a silly accusation made by Pharisees against his disciples who, on a Sabbath, to satisfy their hunger, plucked ears of grain from a field and ate the grains after removing the husks by rubbing the grains between their palms and blowing away the chaff. The Pharisees accused them of violating Sabbath laws by performing three items of work forbidden on Sabbath, namely, harvesting, threshing, and winnowing.</span></span></span></span></span>



      Counter-arguments:</span></span> Jesus gives three counterarguments from Holy Scripture defending his apostles. (1) Basic human needs, like hunger, take precedence over Divine worship and Sabbath observance. Jesus cites from the Scripture the example of the hungry David and his selected soldiers. They approached Ahimelech, the priest of Nob, who gave them for food the “offering bread” which only the priests were allowed to eat (1 Sm 21:1-6). (2) No law can stand against Divine worship. That is why the priests were not considered as violating Sabbath laws although they did the work of preparing two rams for sacrifice in the Temple (Nm 28:9-10). (3) God desires that we practice mercy: Jesus quotes the prophet Hosea to tell the accusers God’s words: “*I want mercy, not sacrifice”(Hos 6:6).*</span></span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span></span></span> Like the Jewish Sabbath, the Christian Sunday is to be 1) a day for rest and refreshment with members of the family; 2) a day for thanksgiving and the recharging of spiritual batteries through participation in the Eucharistic celebration for Catholics and through worship service for the Non-Catholics; 3) a day parents should use for teaching religious Faith and Bible lessons to their children; 4) a day for doing works of charity in the neighbourhood and in the parish; 5) a day for socializing with family members, neighbours, and fellow parishioners. </span></span></span></span></span>

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      The disciples of Jesus were accused of breaking the Sabbath by the Pharisees when they plucked ears of grain to eat because they were hungry. Jesus defended them with four convincing arguments. He asserted that genuine human need takes precedence over the law by citing the example of King David, who satisfied his and his companions’ hunger with the bread of the Presence reserved for the priests, and by quoting Hosea 6:6, which states that the Lord prefers mercy to the fulfilment of ritual laws. Since Jesus came not to abolish the law but to fulfil it, He reinterpreted the law and restored the true meaning of the Sabbath intended by God. In the implementation of laws and administrative policies, we can be law-centric or human-centric. The way of a Christian ought to be human-centric, for that was the way of Jesus.</span></span></span>

      Fearing lest the distraction of earthly honour separate or distance him from what is more noble or godly, Alexius, by divine inspiration, decided to renounce the world. On his wedding night, with his bride’s consent, he secretly left Rome, and giving away to the poor in the far East all that he had with him, spent several years at the gate of Our Lady’s church in that city, living on alms, until an image of Mary spoke and revealed his holiness to the people as “the man of God”. Thereupon he fled back home; but his father, failing to recognize him, received him as a beggar and employed him, allotting a corner under the staircase where he lived for seventeen years, unknown, bearing patiently the demeaning attitude of his own father, spouse and slaves. </span></span></span></span>
