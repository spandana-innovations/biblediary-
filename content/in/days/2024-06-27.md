    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Matthew 7:21–29"
    audio: null
    body: |
      For the past couple of weeks we have been reading from the Sermon on the Mount and today we have read the final section of that great Sermon. There are three activities that followers of Jesus engage in, speaking, listening and doing; and all three are important. When we gather for public prayer we speak; we call on Jesus as “Lord, Lord.” Then both at public worship and at times of private prayer we listen; we hear the word of the Lord and let it sink into our hearts. Speaking and listening will always be central to the life of a disciple. However, Jesus adds that unless our speaking and our listening overflow into good actions their value is undermined.



      It is not enough to say “Lord, Lord,” – for we must also do the will of the Father in heaven. It is not enough to listen to the words of Jesus, we need to act on them. We must act in harmony with what we say and what we hear. When our speaking to God and our listening to his word bear fruit in good works, imitating the life of Jesus, then our lives are solidly grounded, like a house built on rock. Today’s gospel promises that if our words to God and God’s words to us shape our attitudes and behaviour, we can more easily withstand any storms that come our way in life.

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today’s gospel passage is the final section of the Sermon on the Mount which ends with a warning about preachers and hearers. The problem with preachers is that they claim to know and follow God, but their outward profession does not correspond to their life. They end up serving themselves rather than doing the will of God. The second category is of hearers who are receptive to God, but God’s will doesn’t get reflected in their actual lives. They are like people who build their houses without solid foundation. Jesus wants us to be doers of God’s will, based on His word. Am I merely a preacher and a hearer only, or a doer as well? If we are doers, then we will be able to withstand the rain and flood which represent any kind of crises in life, even the final judgement.

      St Cyril of Alexandria (Bishop, Confessor and Doctor of the Church) (370 - 444)

      A native of Alexandria, Cyril was sent by his uncle, Theophilus, the Patriarch, to the monks in the Egyptian desert to be trained as an ascetic. In 403, he accompanied Theophilus to Constantinople to hold his “Synod of the Oak”.

      Succeeding Theophilus, Cyril immediately undertook two drastic measures: the closing of all Novatian churches so as to protect his flock from the wrongful denial of Novatianism concerning the power of the Church to absolve those who had lapsed into idolatry during persecution.

      In 428, Cyril effectively countered the Nestorian heresy of denying the fact that Mary was the Mother of Jesus both as God incarnate and of his human nature with the use of the term Theotokos (“God-bearer”, i.e., Mother of God). Pope St Celestine I commissioned Cyril to warn Nestorius to recant lest he be excommunicated. Finally, the Council of Ephesus, in 431, deposed and excommunicated Nestorius.

      Cyril’s remaining years were devoted to further defending in numerous writings this Catholic doctrine of the dignity of Mary as the Mother of God and of the hypostatic union of the two perfect and distinct natures in Christ, the divine and the human, by which “the Divine Person suffered in his human nature”. He also wrote a book against Julian the Apostate. St Cyril was declared Doctor of the Universal Church in 1882.
