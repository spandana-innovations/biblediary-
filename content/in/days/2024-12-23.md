  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      The whole hill country of Judea marvelled at the birth of John the Baptist. Zachariah and Elizabeth are now blessed with a child in their old age. But the supernatural phenomena do not end with the birth of John. Now there is something quite extraordinary about the naming of the new-born child. John is the name chosen by Elizabeth and approved by Zechariah. John means YHWH is gracious. The child’s birth proves how gracious God is to the old couple as well as to the people of God as a whole. To be childless was considered a curse and a punishment from God. But now it has changed into a situation of joy for them. John’s birth also proves that God is gracious to Israel and to the whole world because John is the one to announce the coming of the Messiah, the Saviour of the world. He is sent ahead to prepare the way for the Messiah and to proclaim that the Kingdom of God is at hand. No wonder, the whole country of Judea treasured the joyful moment in their hearts saying, “What then will this child be?” When we open our hearts to God’s plan, miracles do take place in our lives. Am I open to God’s plan? Do I believe that God’s hand is at work at every moment and event in my life, starting with my conception in the womb of my mother?



      *(Fr Joe Eruppakkatt ssp)*

      Today’s Gospel passage sets before us a beautiful family. Zechariah and Elizabeth live the purpose of God at the creation of man and woman in the beginning. Zechariah had shared his God experience including the name suggested for the child by the angel to his wife. Therefore, she is able to speak for her husband the name of the child is “John.” She is courageous enough to place her opinion even when all suggest otherwise. The standards others set are human standards and conditioned by customs, cultures and practices. Sometimes God’s standards do not match worldly standards. At times even if the God of surprises acts through us powerfully, we remain passive for fear of others and their opinions. Let us make ourselves available for the surprises of God.

    ref: "Saint John of Kanty - Confessor (1390 -1473)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Born on 23 June 1390 to pious country parents, John was surnamed after his birthplace in the diocese of Cracow, Poland. Having done his Master’s in liberal arts from the University of Cracow in 1417, he served as rector of the school of the Templars of Miechów, later teaching at the school of Philosophy where he was appointed dean. After obtaining his Master’s in Theology, he taught Theology the rest of his life.

      In parish ministry, he endeared himself to all by his amiability and humility, and above all by his particularly outstanding generosity toward the poor; whatever he possessed in cash and kind ever at their disposal, he often returned home barefoot having given a beggar the very shoes he was wearing! In a spirit of penance he ate frugally and slept sparingly and on the floor. Deeply sensitive to human ingratitude and offences committed against God, he offered Mass daily for the appeasement of heavenly justice.

      During his lifetime he made four pilgrimages to Rome on foot and one to the Holy Land in the hope of being martyred there at the hands of the Turks. On one such pilgrimage he was waylaid. Hardly had the robbers let him proceed on his assurance that they had taken everything he had when, scrupulous to the core, he suddenly remembered some coins sewn into his clothing and, calling them back, offered them that sum also. Astounded, they promptly restored to him all that they had taken from him!

      John passed away on Christmas Eve 1473, aged 83 years, noted more for his sanctity than for the brilliance and originality of his teaching. Beatified in 1690, he was declared patron of Poland and Lithuania in 1737 and canonized in 1767.
