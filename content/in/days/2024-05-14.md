  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "John 15:9–17"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today’s Gospel is dedicated to the commandment of love. Out of love the Father sends the Son. The Son brings this current of love to human beings. This calls for a reverse movement of love, too: from us to Christ and from Christ to the Father, so that the circle of love is complete. The response of obedience, which is the way to remain in the love of Christ, is the essential nucleus of Christian faith and true discipleship. Similarly, the believers in Christ, we, should love one another as Christ loved us, that is to say, in a self-sacrificing manner. To give one’s life for friends is the supreme proof of love. It is important to note here that Jesus calls us, believers and disciples, his friends, for the simple reason that he has chosen us to be his friends and loved us to the very end. “You did not choose me, I chose you” says he.



      The relationship between Jesus and his disciples is characterised by love and friendship. The privilege of having seen the Risen Lord is personal and cannot be transmitted. But the experience of the faith of the Apostles and the power that the Lord conferred on them must be communicated to others who are called to be their successors. To celebrate this feast is to renew our contact with our origins in the Apostolic Church. The Apostles share with us their Easter witnessing. As he chose Matthias and the other Apostles, the Lord has chosen us also to be his friends, and made known to us everything he learnt from the Father. He has commanded us to love one another and commissioned us to bear fruit. If we fulfil this mission faithfully, we too shall be admitted to the company of the Apostles and saints in the joy and glory of eternal life. Let us be thankful to God that He has chosen us, among millions, to share in the faith of the Apostles and in their destiny.



      (George Kaitholil SSP, Feastday Homilies, ST PAULS, Page no. 144-146).

    body: |
      The First Reading of today describes how Matthias was chosen to take the place of Judas among the Apostles. The reading also indicates that Matthias was witness to Jesus’ ministry, death, resurrection and ascension. Besides, there exists an ancient tradition that he was one among the seventy-two disciples whom Jesus sent ahead of him to the cities and villages of Israel. He certainly had a profound and personal relationship with Jesus. It is a necessary qualification for anyone who wants to walk in the footsteps of the Apostles to become a witness to Jesus. They must establish a personal relationship with Jesus and enter into intimate communion with him to the extent of being called “friends” by him. “An apostle is one who is sent, but even before that he is an ‘expert’ on Jesus”, reminds Benedict XVI.

      St Matthias Apostle and Martyr (1st century)

      St Matthias had been one of our Lord’s disciples from the time of his Baptism in the Jordan, and, after the Ascension of Our Lord, he was chosen by lot to fill the place left vacant among the 12 Apostles through the betrayal and suicide of Judas Iscariot. That is all we know with certainty about this saint from the first chapter of the Acts of the Apostles (1:23-26): “And they (apostles) appointed two, Joseph, called Barsabbas, who was surnamed Justus, and Matthias. And praying, they said: You, Lord, who know the hearts of all men, show which of these two you have chosen, to take the place of this ministry and apostleship, from which Judas has by transgression fallen, that he might go to his own place. And they gave them lots, and the lot fell upon Matthias, and he was numbered with the eleven apostles”.

      According to one tradition, he preached Christianity in Judaea (modern Israel) and Ethiopia where he was crucified, but others hold that he was beheaded at Jerusalem.

      St Matthias is patron of tailors, carpenters and reformed alcoholics, and is invoked against smallpox.
