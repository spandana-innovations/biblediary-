    ref: "John 3:7b–15"

    body: |
      Reflection: 1






      Faith in the Son of Man is an essential condition to have eternal life. Those who look up to Jesus who was lifted up on the cross with faith and trust will have eternal life. By God’s command Moses lifted up a bronze serpent on a pole in the desert so that those who were bitten by a venomous serpent could be healed by looking at it. In our usual way of thinking a serpent is associated with poison, or with evil as in the case of the serpent that deceived our first parents. But the serpent that Moses lifted up was an anti-venom that could save life. It prefigured Jesus who would be lifted up on the cross so that those who look at him with faith, trust and hope could be saved from damnation and receive eternal life. Contemplating the crucified saviour leads us to repentance, conversion and salvation.



      *— Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel is the continuation of the night visit of Nicodemus with Jesus. Nicodemus was a rich Jewish rabbi and one of the seventy members of Sanhedrin. He wanted to clarify whether the obeying of the Mosaic Law and the offering of prescribed sacrifices were enough for one’s eternal salvation. But Jesus used the occasion as a teachable moment, showing Nicodemus the necessity for a spiritual rebirth through the action of the Holy Spirit by means of the water of Baptism as an essential condition for one’s salvation.



      Jesus teaches Nicodemus the effects the Holy Spirit produces in the souls of the baptized. We know the presence, force, and direction of wind by its effects. It is so with the Holy Spirit, the Divine "Breath" (pneuma), given us in Baptism. In Hebrew and Aramaic, the scholars tell us, the same word pneuma means “spirit,” “breath,” and “wind.” We do not know how the Holy Spirit comes to penetrate one’s heart. But He makes His presence felt by the change in the conduct of one who receives Him. Jesus further explains that he himself comes from Heaven, and, hence, his teaching is credible. Then, by comparing how God saved the snake-bitten Israelites through the symbol of the bronze serpent, Jesus tells Nicodemus that “the Son of Man” is going to save mankind by his death on the cross.



      Life message: We need to adjust our lives, recognizing and making full use of the presence of the Holy Spirit in our lives:



      1) We need to begin every day by asking for His Divine strength and guidance and end every day by asking His pardon and forgiveness for our sins.



      2) We need, as well, to pray for His daily anointing and for His gifts, fruits, and charisms so that we may live as children of God.



      3) We also need to throw open the shutters and let the Spirit enter the narrow caves in which we bury ourselves.

      The essence of Jesus’ advice to Nicodemus is that the Holy Spirit’s role in spiritual rebirth is transformative. When someone is “born again” or “born from above,” it involves a radical and profound change at the core of his/her being. This change is not something that can be manufactured or manipulated by human effort; it is a supernatural work of the Holy Spirit. It involves conviction of sin, repentance, and a new life in Christ. It is through our faith in the Son of Man who was lifted on the cross that we are offered eternal life and salvation. We need to examine our faith, recognising that belief in Jesus Christ as our Saviour is the pathway to eternal life.

    saintName: "Saint Catherine of Siena - Virgin and Doctor of the Church (1347-1380)"
    ref: "Saint Catherine of Siena - Virgin and Doctor of the Church (1347-1380)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Catherine, the youngest of the 25 children of the Benincasa family of wool dyers in Siena, Tuscany (Italy), when still young, was favoured with celestial visions and began to practise severe austerities. At 16 she became a Dominican tertiary, lived for three years like an anchoress in her own home, and heroically undertook the care of advanced cases of leprosy and cancer which no one else would touch and, though meeting with nothing but abuse, succeeded in bringing about the sufferers’ conversion.



      Subsisting for long periods solely on the reception of the Eucharist and praying incessantly for sinners who refused to pray for themselves. At 28, she received the sacred stigmata, but in response to her pleadings to God, these remained outwardly invisible while she was alive. The most famous of her writings is her *Dialogue* in which the Father converses with her on the spiritual life.



      Catherine was instrumental in having Pope Gregory XI lift the interdict over Florence and finally return from Avignon to Rome in 1377. At the request of Gregory’s successor Urban VI she spent the last six years of her life in Rome, offering the sacrifice of her life for the unity and renewal of the Church. She was barely 33 when she died three long months after having had a seizure.



      Proclaimed saint in 1461 and Doctor of the Church in 1970, she is the Patron Saint of Italy, and is invoked against fire, pestilence and headaches.
