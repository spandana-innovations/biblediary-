    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 2: 22-40"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Family harmony</span></span></span></span></span>



      This feast might set too high an ideal for families, if the homilist lauds the virtues of the Holy Family, without also showing that they had to face many issues not unlike those of our own households. Fortunately, the Gospel tells us something quite different about the childhood of Jesus, in a down-to-earth way.</span></span></span>



      Even though we honour them as the Holy Family, it does not mean they never had problems to face, as every family must. Just as every Christian has a share in the cross of Christ, the Holy Family also experienced the cross in their shared life. To mention just a few examples, we can imagine how misunderstood both Mary and Joseph must have been about the conception of Jesus before they came to live together. Joseph was even planning to divorce Mary privately before being assured that it was the work of God. Nine months later, the birthplace of Jesus was an animal shelter, since no better lodgings could be found.</span></span></span>



      Today’s Gospel from St Matthew shows how the little family had to flee as refugees to Egypt because the child Jesus’ life was in danger from king Herod, in much the same way as refugees from war-torn countries have to flee to save their lives. This prompts us to be more aware of, and show more empathy towards, those refugees in our own day who seek a safer life among us than they had in the countries from which they fled.</span></span></span>



      St Luke has another story to illustrate what trials were faced by Mary and Joseph, in trying to understand the development of Jesus as a young person. When he was twelve, they were shocked to lose him for three days and then had to deal with the unsatisfactory explanation that he “had to be about his Father’s business.” Still, he returned with them to Nazareth and was subject to them, in the quiet rhythm of family life in their village. We do not hear of Joseph any more after that so we presume that he had died before Jesus began his public ministry. Then too, the public life of Jesus must have taken its toll on Mary. In the Temple when he was an infant, old Simeon had predicted that a sword of sorrow would pierce Mary’s soul. How she must have been pained to hear his enemies say that Jesus was a glutton and a drunkard, a friend of tax-collectors and sinners, and at the end, when Mary watched her son die in public disgrace, on the cross.</span></span></span>



      What sustained the family of Nazareth through all of these trials and crosses? What holds families together in times of difficulty is love and trust. Whenever families are happy, it is where love and respect are highly prized among them. We pray for an outpouring of those qualities in our families today. A major threat to family life nowadays is that we don’t spend enough time together, in friendly conversation. We are so busy working, socialising, using our electronic gadgets or watching TV that we have no time to talk to each other.</span></span></span>



      A barrister, a busy career woman, was living just ten kilometres from her old, widowed father. But months often passed between her visits to him. When her father texted to ask when she might bring his grandchildren to visit him, she listed lots of reasons that kept her from doing so, court schedules, meetings, new clients, etc. Her father then asked her, sadly, ‘When I die, will you come to my funeral?’ The daughter was indignant. “Dad, how can you ask me that? Of course, I’ll be there!” He smiled and said, “Aah! Then please forget my funeral and come to visit me now. I need you now more than I will then.” Message understood – and the daughter began to visit him regularly after that.</span></span></span>

    body: |
      When we celebrate the Feast of the Holy Family, we think of our own family, which is the foundation of our lives. In our homes, we have the picture of the Holy Family in Nazareth: Joseph works in his carpenter’s workshop, the young Jesus helps him, and Mary sits gently at the spinning wheel. A picture of harmony meant to encourage people to live in peace and harmony. But today, the place of work and of children’s education is no longer our homes. This affects the harmony of families, and modern gadgets add to the woes. Increasingly, parents too are neglecting the religious formation of children. What modern families could learn from the Holy Family is the manner in which each of them respected and accepted one another. Each of them knew the God-given responsibility and joyfully accepted and remained faithful to it. </span></span></span>
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Feast of the Holy Family of Jesus, Mary, and Joseph"
    ref: "Feast of the Holy Family of Jesus, Mary, and Joseph"
    audio: null
    body: |
      The Feast of the Holy Family of Jesus, Mary, and Joseph is celebrated by the Catholic Church on the Sunday within the Octave of Christmas, or on 30 December when no Sunday falls within the Octave. Instituted to highlight the importance of family life, the feast presents the Holy Family of Nazareth as the model for all Christian families.



      The Holy Family lived a simple and hidden life in Nazareth. Jesus, the Son of God, chose to grow within a human family, under the care of Mary, His mother, and Joseph, His foster father. Sacred Scripture tells us that Jesus was obedient to them and grew in wisdom, age, and favor before God and people (cf. Lk 2:52). Mary is remembered for her faithful acceptance of God’s will and her deep spirit of contemplation, while Joseph is honored for his obedience, humility, and protective care of the Child and His mother.



      Though chosen for a unique role in salvation history, the Holy Family was not spared from hardship. They experienced poverty, the threat of violence, and the flight into Egypt. Their life reflects trust in God amid uncertainty and faithful perseverance in daily responsibilities.



      This feast emphasizes the dignity of family life and the home as the first place of faith formation. It reminds the faithful that love, prayer, mutual respect, and fidelity are central to Christian living. By honoring the Holy Family, the Church encourages families to seek holiness in ordinary life and to place God at the center of their relationships.
