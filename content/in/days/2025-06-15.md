    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "John 16:12-15"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      Jesus promises the Apostles that the Holy Spirit would come and guide them into all the truth. The Spirit will not teach anything different or more than what Jesus has taught. He will make the teachings of Jesus more deeply understood by the believers. What Jesus has taught is not his own; he received it from the Father. Therefore it is evident that the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit are in perfect communion among themselves. What one teaches or does belongs to the other also. The Holy Trinity is one God in three Persons. The love that exists among the divine Persons is poured into our hearts by the Holy Spirit. The triune God has made His dwelling within us.



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      The mystery of the most Holy Trinity is a basic doctrine of Faith in Christianity, understandable not with our heads but with our hearts. It teaches us that there are three distinct Persons in one God, sharing the same Divine Nature, co-equal and co-eternal. Our mind cannot grasp this doctrine which teaches that 1+1+1 = 1 and not 3. But we believe in this Mystery because Jesus, who is God, taught it clearly, the Evangelists recorded it, the Fathers of the Church tried to explain it, and the Councils of Nicaea and Constantinople defined it as a dogma of Christian Faith. (Add a pertinent anecdote).



      Importance in Christian life: 1) All prayers in the Church begin in the Name of the Holy Trinity and end glorifying the Trinity. 2) All Sacraments are administered (we are baptized, confirmed, anointed, our sins are forgiven, our marriage is blessed, and our Bishops, priests and deacons are ordained) in the name of the Holy Trinity. 3) When Church bells ring thrice daily, they remind us to give glory to the Holy Trinity for the Incarnation of Jesus and His Redemption of all of us. 4) We bless ourselves, and the priest blesses us, in the Name of the Holy Trinity.



      Biblical basis: There are only vague and hidden references to the Trinity in the Old Testament. But the New Testament gives clear teachings on the Holy Trinity.



      1) At the Annunciation, God the Father sends His angel to Mary, God the Holy Spirit comes upon her, the Power of the Most High overshadows her, and God the Son becomes Incarnate in her womb.



      2) At the baptism of Jesus, when the Son receives baptism from John the Baptist, the Father’s Voice is heard, and the Holy Spirit appears as a Dove and descends upon Jesus.



      3) At the Ascension, Jesus gives the missionary command to his disciples to baptize those who believe, in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit.



      4) John’s account: In John’s Gospel, chapters 15–18, we have a detailed account of Jesus’ teaching of the role of each Person of the Holy Trinity: a) God the Father creates and provides for His creatures. b) God the Son redeems us and reconciles us with God. c) God the Holy Spirit sanctifies us, strengthens us, teaches us, and guides us to God.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages: </span>



      1) Let us respect ourselves and others because everyone is the temple of the Holy Spirit where all Three Persons of the Holy Trinity abide.



      2) Let us have the firm conviction that the Trinitarian God abides in us, that He is the Source of our hope, courage and strength, and that He is our final destination.



      3) Let us practice the Trinitarian relationship of love and unity in the family relationships of father, mother, and children because by Baptism we become children of God and members of God’s Trinitarian family.



      4) Let us practice the *I–God–my neighbour* vertical and horizontal Trinitarian relationships in this life by loving God.

    body: |
      The Holy Trinity of Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, one God in three divine persons, united in perfect love, is revealed to us in their love for us humans. We can know and experience the Trinity insofar as they reveal themselves to us in love. In the Second Reading of today, Saint Paul reveals this truth powerfully, saying, “God’s love has been poured into our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us.” Perfect love and unity exist between the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit, and the same love has been poured into human hearts by the Spirit. We are made partakers of the love of the Trinity. In other words, the Holy Trinity exists and resides in each of us as God is love and that love has been poured into our hearts. Jesus reveals the same in the Gospel of John, “If anyone loves me, he will keep my word, and my Father will love him, and we will come to him and make our home with him” (Jn 14:23). In today’s Gospel, Christ, the Second Person of the Holy Trinity introduces the Holy Spirit and the Father thus: “When the Spirit of truth comes, he will guide you into all the truth… All that the Father has is mine; therefore I said that he will take what is mine and declare it to you.” Like the Holy Trinity, we strive daily to live in love, unity, and truthfulness, being aware that the Trinity has made its home within us. We have a Trinitarian God and we are a Trinitarian people.

    ref: "St Germaine Cousin (Virgin) (c.1579 - 1601)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      The daughter of a peasant named Laurent Cousin in Pibrac near Toulouse, France, Germaine was born with a deformed hand and suffered from *scrofula* (swelling of the glands). While still little, her mother died, and when her father remarried, her stepmother treated her with the utmost cruelty and insisted on her being ostracized, compelling her to sleep with the sheep in the stable. God rewarded the humility and patience with which she bore all manner of suffering and neglect, by granting her a marvellous sense of his presence, and a deep love for the Holy Eucharist and the Blessed Virgin.



      Germaine used to assemble the village children and teach them to love Jesus and Mary; from her scanty allowance of food which consisted of leftovers from the family table she managed to distribute portions to those who appeared to be in still greater misery than herself.



      In reparation for the sacrileges of which the heretics in neighbouring towns were guilty, Germaine subjected herself to additional austerities; people were just beginning to realize her deep-rooted sanctity when God called her to himself in her 22nd year. She was canonized by Pope Pius IX on 29 June 1867. Every year an annual pilgrimage takes place on 15 June to the church in Pibrac where her incorrupt body still rests.
