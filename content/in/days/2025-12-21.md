      Reflection: 1



      Mary, betrothed to Joseph, conceived without human intervention, by the power of the Holy Spirit. Joseph, a just man, was confused, but did not want to put Mary to shame. What happened to her was God’s work, and He took initiative to clarify it to Joseph. An angel of the Lord told him in a dream how Mary conceived, and asked him to take her as his wife, and to name her son Jesus, for he would save his people from their sins. Joseph obeyed the angel’s command. Thus he became the legal father of Jesus, and made him a descendant of David. Joseph embraced God’s will and obeyed it perfectly, for he was a righteous man. He was privileged to live in the closest relationship with Mary and Jesus, the Son of God. These three holy persons were associated in a unique way in their obedience to the will of the Father. Let us pray Jesus, Mary and Joseph to help us to be in the best relationship with God.</span></span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Loving, responsive obedience to God, as modelled for us in the Gospel by St. Joseph, is the central theme of today’s readings, with special emphasis on the Virgin Birth of Jesus.</span>



      Scripture lessons summarized: </span>



      In the first reading, the prophet Isaiah gives a sign from God to King Ahaz of Judah: *“Behold, a virgin shall conceive and bear a son, and his name shall be called Emmanuel” (Is 7:14). *Matthew considers this prophecy as one of the most descriptive and definite prophecies foretelling the future Messianic King, the Christ, who is to be born as a descendant of David. </span>



      The Refrain for today’s Responsorial Psalm (Ps 24), “Let the Lord enter; He is King of Glory” reminds us that we must choose to let Jesus enter our hearts to rule our lives, for God never forces us to receive His gifts. In the second reading, Paul asserts that Jesus was a descendant of David and thus the Messiah: “*from David according to the flesh,”* become Incarnate as Jesus, but was revealed and established by the Father as Son of God in power by his Resurrection from the dead. Then Paul provides a sweeping summary of God’s mighty acts in history through Jesus Christ. </span>



      Today’s Gospel, from Matthew, focuses on the person and role of Joseph. For Jesus to fulfil the Messianic prophecy given by Isaiah, Joseph had to, and freely did, accept Jesus as his son, making Jesus a legal descendant of David because Joseph was a descendant of David. Hence, Matthew makes it clear that Jesus was not the biological child of Joseph. But because Joseph was the husband of Mary at the time Jesus was born, Jesus was legally the son of Joseph and thus a descendant of David.</span>



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span> </span>



      1) Like Joseph, we need to trust in God, listen to Him and be faithful. Although we may face financial problems, job insecurity, tensions in the family, and health concerns, let us try to be like St. Joseph, trusting and faithful. Instead of relying on our own schemes to get us through life, let us trust in God and be strengthened by talking to Him in fervent prayer and by listening to Him speaking through the Bible. </span>



      2) We need to experience* Emmanuel* in our lives and change the world: The Good News and the consoling message of Christmas is that the Child Jesus still waits today to step into our hearts – your heart and mine – and to change us and the world around us by the beauty of God’s love, kindness, mercy, and compassion. Let us take some time to welcome the Christ Child into our hearts and lives this week, so that God may change our world of miseries with the beauty of that love. </span>



      3) Do we have any gift for our “Birthday Boy?” Let us check to see if Jesus is on *our* list this Christmas and if we have a special gift in mind for him. A heart filled with love for God and our fellow-human beings is the birthday gift which Jesus really wants from us. Hence, let us prepare our heart for Jesus, filling it with love, mercy, compassion, and forgiveness on this Christmas and every day of our lives. </span>



      4) Let us be a Christmas gift to others: The greatest gift we can give to those we love, is to have faith in them, believe in their dreams and try to help them realize them. We need to believe in the dreams of our husband, wife, children, parents, heroes, leaders and friends, then try our best to help them realize those dreams.</span>

      St Joseph’s virtues shine as a guiding light for us. He is the epitome of swift obedience. Choosing God’s desires over personal comforts, he teaches us that true holiness lies in embracing the Divine Will. We observe that Joseph was able to give top priority to doing what God wanted him to do. He knew that in God’s plan, Jesus and Mary belonged to him. So he safeguarded them – treasuring them close to his heart. Holiness consists in doing what God wants – when and where He wants. Joseph was privileged to share the most intimate physical presence with the two holiest persons of this world – Jesus, the Son of God and Mary, the Immaculate Mother. And yet, he contented himself to be the “silent hard-worker.” May the greatness of St Joseph inspire us to live in total commitment.
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Peter Canisius - Confessor, Doctor of the Church"
    ref: "Saint Peter Canisius - Confessor, Doctor of the Church"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Ordained Priest at 25 (1546), at age 26 Peter Canisius assisted Cardinal-Bishop of Augsburg as Theologian at the Council of Trent. After making his solemn profession as a Jesuit in 1549—the first German to do so!—he taught Theology at the University of Vienna, preached in the cathedral and at the court of Ferdinand I, and laboured in every way to restore true Catholic doctrine, for the Church in Vienna was in a deplorable state due to acute shortage of priests.</span>



      He kept himself free (in contrast to the Protestants) from all narrow-mindedness and personal vituperation, his exemplary humility, moderation and tact, making him the most influential leader of the Counter Reformation in those lands, the “Second Apostle of Germany”.</span>



      He corresponded enormously with Saints Charles Borromeo, Philip Neri and Francis de Sales, and was in demand as adviser to pope, bishop, king, prince and commoner alike. He zealously promoted Rosary sodalities, encouraged the publication of good books, and founded some 30 schools and Papal seminaries in order to form an instructed laity and a devout clergy. Thousands were won back to the Faith and crowds thronged his confessional and the Masses he celebrated.</span>



      His ascetic and homiletic writings aside he is most famous for his Triple Catechism—for children, young people and adults—containing a lucid treatise on Roman Catholic dogma, which even in his lifetime saw 200 editions in 12 languages.</span>



      Peter died in 1597, was beatified in 1864, and was canonized and declared Doctor of the Church in 1925.</span>
