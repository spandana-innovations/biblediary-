      Reflection: 1



      The tension between the Jews and Jesus goes on relentlessly. When Jesus raised Lazarus from the dead, Many Jews had come with Mary and Martha and saw what Jesus did, and believed in him. Some of them reported to the Pharisees what Jesus had done. The Pharisees were scared that Jesus was performing many signs and everyone would believe in him, and the Romans would come and subjugate their nation. Caiaphas the high priest prophesied that Jesus would die for the nation and to gather into one the scattered children of God, thus indicating the saving death of Jesus. That day they began planning to put Jesus to death. God brings good even out of human resistance and hostility, and fulfils His purpose. Jesus who raised Lazarus from the dead is the life-giver; he cannot be destroyed even by death. How deep is our faith, and how faithfully do we follow him?



      *— Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel gives the reaction of the High Priest and his associates when the news about Jesus’ raising of Lazarus reached their ears. They convened a joint council of priests and Pharisees to discuss the reaction of the people in favour of Jesus. (Lectio Divina = In order to be able to understand this reaction of one part of the population, it is necessary to become aware that half of the population of Jerusalem depended completely on the Temple so as to be able to live and to survive. Because of this, it would have been difficult for them to support an unknown prophet from Galilee who criticized the Temple and the religious authorities. This also explains why some even were ready to inform the authorities. They were afraid of the Romans, because in the past it had been shown many times, by the Roman invasions in the year 64 before Christ until the time of Jesus, that the Romans repressed with great violence any attempt at popular rebellion. (Cf. Acts 5:35-37). In the case of Jesus, the Roman reaction could have led to the loss of everything, even of the Temple and of the privileged position of the priests). Caiaphas held the High Priesthood from the year 18 to the year 36 AD. It was the popular belief that when a High Priest asked for God’s counsel for the nation of Israel, God would speak through him. God used Caiaphas to prophesy the redemptive death of Jesus for the whole world when Caiaphas said: *“It is expedient for you that One Man should die instead of the people, so that the whole nation may not perish.”* Here, Caiaphas’ words have two meanings: 1) Caiaphas meant he wanted to put Christ to death on the pretext that that would ensure the political peace and survival of Israel. 2) The Holy Spirit meant that the new Israel, the Kingdom of God, the Church, would be founded through the death of Christ on the Cross, and that His death would save not only Israel but “all the children of God who are scattered abroad.” The prophets had already announced that the future assembly of Israelites faithful to God would form the new people of Israel. These prophecies were fulfilled by the death of Christ, Who, when raised up on the cross, drew and gathered together the true people of God, composed of all believers, whether Israelites or not.)

      Today’s gospel passage invites us to contemplate the significance of Jesus’ miraculous signs, the diversity of human responses to his ministry, and the sovereignty of God’s plan. God’s sovereign plan has been revealed in the unfolding events surrounding Lazarus’ resurrection and the growing opposition to Jesus. We see how God’s purposes are accomplished, even in the face of human resistance, and that His ultimate plan is centred on the redemptive work of Jesus Christ on the cross. Let us reflect on our faith and willingness to follow Christ, even when it requires surrendering our desires for power and control.

    saintName: "Saint Gemma Galgani - Virgin (1878-1903)"
    ref: "Saint Gemma Galgani - Virgin (1878-1903)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Gemma was the fourth of 8 children whose parents Enrico and Aurelia, were extremely devout. Aurelia died in 1886, having planted in Gemma the seeds of her devotion to Our Lord’s Passion. Unable to express her emotions about the moment of intimacy between her soul and Jesus at her First Holy Communion at 9, Gemma did make good her resolve to be “always good.”



      Gemma loved solitude and quiet, and was always most reserved in talking. One day, after she had received Holy Communion, Our Lord said to her: “Gemma, I expect you on Calvary, that Mount towards which you have been led.” After a long, painful illness patiently borne, she made a vow of chastity and in 1899, Saint Gabriel appeared to her, promising to make the novena in honour of Saint Margaret Mary with her, which he did each night. After a good confession and receiving Holy Communion, she arose completely cured. Her attempt at nun-hood was thwarted by the condition of her health.



      Gemma received the sacred “stigmata” on the vigil of the Feast of the Sacred Heart in 1899 apart from experiencing other phenomena of the Passion, such as the sweat of blood, the scourging, the crowning with thorns, etc. Under the spiritual direction of the Passionist Fathers, Gemma, who was also blessed with the gift of prophecy apart from ecstacies, visions and revelations, came to be widely known for her sanctity and many favours were obtained through her prayers.



      Gemma died in 1903, having offered herself as a sacrifice for sinners, particularly for unworthy priests. She was canonized in 1940.
