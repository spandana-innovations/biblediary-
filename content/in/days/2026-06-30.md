  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mathew 8: 23-27"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Matthew’s emphasis on Jesus’ wondrous works helps him to reveal Jesus’ true Messianic identity. The role of God in calming the storms of life is the central theme of today’s Gospel. By describing the miracle, Matthew also assures his first-century believers that nothing can harm the Church as long as the risen Lord is with them. The incident reminds us today to keep Jesus in our life’s boat and to seek his help in the storms of life.</span></span></span>



      The storm: The Sea of Galilee is a lake thirteen miles long from north to south and eight miles broad from east to west at its widest. It is notorious for its sudden storms. When a cold wind blows from the west, the valleys, gullies, and hills act like gigantic funnels compressing the storms and letting them rush down to the lake to create violent waves. Unable to control their boat or their fears, the disciples wake Jesus up, accusing him of disregarding their safety. Jesus’ response is immediate. At once he rises and rebukes the winds and the sea, and instantly there is total calm. Only then does Jesus gently chide his terrified and now astonished disciples for the smallness of their Faith.</span></span></span></span></span>



      Life message: 1) </span></span>We need to welcome Jesus into the boat of our life to calm the storms we face. All of us are making a journey across the sea of time to the shore of eternity, and it is natural that we all will experience different types of violent storms occasionally in our lives: physical storms, emotional storms, and spiritual storms. We face storms of sorrow, doubts, anxiety, worries, temptations, and passion. Only Jesus can give us real peace in the storm of sorrow or console us for the loss of our dear ones. When the storms of doubt seek to uproot the very foundations of the Faith, Jesus is there to still that storm, revealing to us his Divinity and the authority behind the words of Holy Scripture. He gives us peace in the storms of anxiety and worries about ourselves, about the unknown future, and about those we love. Jesus also calms the storms of passion in people who have hot hearts and blazing tempers. </span></span></span></span></span>



      *(Fr Tony: https://frtonyshomilies.com/)*

    title: Reflection
    ref: null

    body: |
      The calming of the sea by Jesus illustrates how He can bring calm to our lives amidst the turmoil we face on our earthly journey. Sometimes, God shows us that He’s in control by putting us in situations we can’t control. It’s important to remember that “I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me” (Phil 4:13). Even the worst enemy we fear in life can be overcome by God’s power. To experience this power, we need to have faith. Jesus wants us to move from having “little faith” to having “great faith”. This kind of faith enables us to earnestly pray to the Lord from our hearts. As Mahatma Gandhi once said, “It is better in prayer to have a heart without words than words without heart.”</span></span></span>
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "First Martyrs of the Church at Rome (64 - 314)"
    ref: "First Martyrs of the Church at Rome (64 - 314)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      In the early years of the Church, persecutions kept breaking out like rash across the vast Roman Empire. But given the present situation, we hardly give those terrible persecutions so much as a thought. One of the first major persecutions began under Nero in the year 64 A.D. and was followed by one under Domitian in 95 A.D. In 107 A.D., Trajan tested the faith, hope and love of the first Christians with another terrible ordeal by fire, prison and the sword. In 135 A.D., Hadrian followed his example and Marcus Aurelius followed suit in 180 A.D. In 222 A.D., Septimus Severus did his best to shake the constancy of the early Christians, followed by Decius in 250 A.D. and Valerian in 257 A.D., the last attempt being that of Diocletian in 303 A.D.</span></span></span></span>



      The Church today offersus this Commemoration of the First Martyrs to help us always bear in mind that the blood of martyrs is first and foremost the seed of faith; secondly, to have us prove, as they did, that our faith is a living, vibrant reality; and thirdly, consider the possibility of someday being called upon to bear witness as they did—to our faith and our hope, to our love for God and for our fellowmen, as so many of our contemporaries here in India itself and elsewhere have done.</span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Reflection:</span></span></span>* “To die for the truth is not to die merely for one’s faith, or one’s country, it is to die for the world. The blood of martyrs is shed in confirmation of the noblest claim — the claim to feed upon immortal truth, to walk with God, and be divinely free” </span></span>*(Cowper).</span></span></span></span>
