  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mark 10:1–12"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Jesus presents God’s vision of marriage, as found in the book of Genesis, involving a man and a woman coming together and giving themselves to each other for life, so that their two lives become one life. In Jesus’ own day there were reservations about this vision of married life. Some would have seen it as too idealistic and not taking sufficient account of the reality of people’s lives. Yet, Jesus reiterates the teaching of the book of Genesis. There is resistance to this vision today as well of course, and some would want to define marriage in much broader and looser terms.



      Marriages break down, we all know that. I expect many of us here in the church will have family members whose marriages have not lasted. Yet, we need to hold on to the vision that Jesus gives us in today’s gospel as God’s ideal for marriage. Marriage is a relationship of love between a man and a woman that reflects God’s loving relationship with his people. The love of husband and wife in marriage is a special expression of Christ’s love for his church, although not the only one. That vision is worth fighting for, especially in a culture that is often unsympathetic to it. It is worth fighting for because it is true to and worthy off what is best and deepest in people.



      (Association of Catholic Priests: Homily Resources).
  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      The Law of Moses permitted a man to divorce his wife if “he has found some indecency in her” (Deut 24:1). Some rabbis interpreted the phrase “some indecency” so liberally that divorce for trivial reasons was not uncommon. Jesus categorically told them that Moses permitted divorce because of their “hardness of heart” and it must be understood as a concession and not as a permanent principle. He was telling them that they were focusing on what is permitted by the law and not on the real will of God. Jesus is critical of the human tendency to look for exceptions and excuses and escape from responsibilities. He is also opposed to the lax interpretation of rules and laws which defeats their intended purpose.

      Our Lady Help of Christians

      I promise,” St Bridget once heard our Lord say to his Immaculate Mother, Mary, “‘to hear graciously all who ask a favour of me in your name, even if they are sinners — provided that they have the will to amend their ways.” It was at the foot of the cross, that Mary was officially, formally commissioned by the Lord himself to take care of all the redeemed— “Woman, behold thy son” (Jn 19:26).

      Pope Pius VII was imprisoned by Napoleon in 1808, but after the battle of Leipzig the 74-year-old Pontiff was once more set free, and in 1814 he returned to Rome in triumph. After Napoleon’s “Hundred Days” had been brought to an end in 1815 by the allied victory at Waterloo, Pius VII instituted today’s feast for the Papal States in thanksgiving to God and the Blessed Virgin, and to commemorate the anniversary of his return from Napoleonic captivity.

      The devotion to Mary under the title “Help of Christians” was actually fostered and propularized by Don Bosco who himself had a very deep devotion to Mary Help of Christians. He even erected a Basilica in her honour under that title in Turin in 1868 and later, in 1872, went on to found the Salesian Sisters under her patronage, viz. the Daughters of Mary Help of Christians).

      Reflection: “May the Blessed Virgin help us to be faithful to the teachings of the Church and obtain for us the grace of perseverance in serving God on this earth, so as to join her one day in the kingdom of heavenly glory” (St John Bosco).
