  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Matthew 8:28-34"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      By his divine power Jesus cast out demons from two men and liberates them from the evil spirits. With his permission the demons went into a large number of pigs which then rushed into the sea and were lost. The villagers begged Jesus to leave the place; rather than believe in him, they were worried about safeguarding their material possessions. They were afraid Jesus would cast out more demons and send them to other pigs or other domestic animals and they would lose them! They did not appreciate the values of Jesus and the spiritual freedom and dignity he gave, but were selfish and attached to earthly possessions. Do we follow the spiritual values taught by Jesus, or do we follow earthly and material values opposed to the values of Jesus?



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel episode demonstrates Jesus’ power over the devil in a Gentile town called Gadara (Matthew) or Gerasa (Mark and Luke) of Decapolis, east of the Jordan. Two (in Mark and Luke, one), possessed men came out of a tomb-filled desolate place. The possessing demons, recognizing Jesus as the Son of God, begged that Jesus send them into a herd of swine. In Mark’s and Luke’s version of the incident, the possessed man’s demons named themselves Legion (6000), indicating their number. Jesus did as the evil spirits requested, the then-possessed swine ran down the slope, and they drowned in the sea. The frightened people of the city asked Jesus to leave their city. The people considered their swine more precious than the possessed men, and the liberation given to these men from evil spirits did not matter to them. If we have a selfish or materialistic outlook, we fail to appreciate the value of Divine things and push God out of our lives, begging Him to go away as these people did.



      Life messages*:* Come out of your tombs:



      1) Jesus is calling us to come out of the tombs. Our tombs are the closed-in, sealed-off areas of our hearts where Life in the Spirit of God has died because we have not allowed Jesus to minister to us through others. When we are sealed off from God, we are lonely. We try to satisfy our inner emptiness by filling our lives with money, promiscuity, addictions, or workaholism.



      2) Jesus, the liberator, is ready to free us from the tombs of our evil addictions and habits. Let us go to him and receive his love, that we may experience the joy and freedom of the children of God.

    saintName: "Sts. Processus and Martinian, Martyrs (c. -100 -)"
    ref: "Sts. Processus and Martinian, Martyrs (c. -100 -)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Though about their life nothing much is known, the veneration in which these two martyrs were held by the Christians of Rome goes beyond the 4th century. However, according to Roman Martyrology, they were

      warders of the Mametine prison and were converted along with 40 others by the teachings and miracles of two prisoners, Sts Peter and Paul. The warders offered the apostles their liberty. It is said that a sudden flow of water miraculously sprang up in the prison thus enabling Peter to baptize them. Paulinus, the officer in charge, tried to persuade both Processus and Martinian to denounce Christianity. Finding them firm in their resolve to persevere in their new faith they were subjected to cruel torture. They boldly declined to offer incense on the altar of Jupiter, and all through their suffering their only utterance was “Blessed be the name of the Lord!” until they were finally slain by the sword. They were buried by a woman called Lucina, on her own property.



      In the 4th century a Basilica was built over their tomb, where, in the course of time many miracles and healings took place drawing great crowds. At the beginning of the 9th century St Pascal I had their

      relics transferred to St Peter’s where an altar was built in their honour.
