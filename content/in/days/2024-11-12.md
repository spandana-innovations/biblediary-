    body: |
      The words of Jesus here addressed to the apostles seem very hard, almost severe. Jesus is telling them that even when they have done all that He asks they should still consider themselves useless servants.</span></span></span></span>How can you explain these words of Jesus in light of His great love and understanding? We know that Luke’s is the Gospel of total dedication.</span></span></span></span>We are reminded here in this short parable that we must be unceasing in our labours for Christ. Who are we to judge or to criticize the Lord? It is His privilege to ask much of us, to make demands. We simply are to live out His Will, to do the work He asks of us. The emphasis of the short parable is dedication – work when the Lord asks it, rest when He so decides. We really can never stop and relax with the idea that we have done enough. That decision is up to God.</span></span></span></span>St Paul understood this kind of total dedication well. He wasn’t looking for any special praise or reward. In his first letter to the Corinthians he writes, “Yet preaching the Gospel is not the subject of a boast: I am under compulsion and have no choice. I am ruined If I do</span></span></span></span>not preach it” (1 Cor 9:16).</span></span></span></span>These words of Jesus were addressed to His apostles. They had just told Him their need for an increase of faith, if they were to follow Him in all His demands. Jesus simply answers with His demand for total dedication as if to say, “If you trust in me, do all I ask, your faith in me will grow; it will be sufficient.</span></span></span></span>We are the present-day apostles. Jesus expects total dedication from us, too. We cannot rest on our laurels. We have done nothing unusual in His service, even if we have done what He asks of each of us. We ought simply to work for the kingdom and put our trust in Him.</span></span></span></span>



      Jesus, we are useless servants. We have done no more than our duty. Yet we trust in You to increase our faith and our love.</span></span></span></span>

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      The Gospel today explains how faithful slaves accomplish the services entrusted to them and humbly submit their works as duty and responsibility, claiming nothing. In the same way, reverential fear of God is the key to faithfulness. Faithfulness to God is our first obligation in all that we are called to do, in the service of the Gospel. We are assigned to live in the world as responsible stewards of God, upright and exemplary. We have nothing to boast of; rather we graciously acknowledge all we do as our mere responsibility. Saint Paul asks Titus to teach believers old and young, men and women who are expected to live in accordance with the sound doctrines of the Church and thus to live a blameless life in the world having all virtues proper to one’s role. Leaders are expected to be ideal in all this. Our integrity and seriousness towards the precepts of God shall be the hallmark of our life. Christian leadership is a call to servanthood, following the example of Jesus our dear master who served humanity unreservedly. A life well lived has longstanding impact and influence than any teaching. There is no limit for being good and doing the best one can. Let us desire to live a life based on God’s precepts and give values of true Christian faith to the world. </span></span></span></span>

    saintName: "Saint Josaphat Kunsevich, Bishop Martyr (1580 -1623)"
    ref: "Saint Josaphat Kunsevich, Bishop Martyr (1580 -1623)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Saint Josaphat was born in Lithuania at a time when the Ruthenian Church, having separated from Rome, had fallen on evil days. Named John Kunseyvc at birth, he was a lad of 15 when the promising “Union of Brest-Litowski” withdrew some ten million Christians from allegiance to the Orthodox Patriarch of Constantinople and reunited them to Rome, a step which was to a large extent to become void by persecution in Russia and Poland.Ordained priest in 1609, Josaphat proved to be a great influence for good in the reform of his Order and the regeneration of the clergy. He rose rapidly to be Abbot, Coadjutor and finally, at 38, Archbishop of Polozk, which was one of the Sees where rivalry between theCatholic and Orthodox Clergy was in sharp evidence.His personal life was one of austerity and mortification. In the constant strife between the Uniates and Schismatics, he showed indefatigable zeal for converting souls to union with Rome. He entreated, preached, heard confessions, not only in the churches but in the hospitals, prisons and in the fields. As a result, personages of importance got converted by this “Thief of Souls”! Indeed, so fruitful were Josaphat’s endeavours that the hatred of the schismatics reached the point where, in 1623, they finally martyred him and threw his mangled body into a river. Consequently, the conversions marked a steady rise, not excluding his murderers! Canonized in 1867, Josaphat became the first Easterner to have been formally raised to the honours of the altar by Rome.
