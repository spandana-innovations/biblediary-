  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "John 1:43–51"
    audio: null
    body: |
      In today’s Gospel of John (John 1:43-51), Nathanael, also called Bartholomew or “son of Tholomay,” is introduced as a friend of Philip. He is described as initially being skeptical about the Messiah coming from Nazareth, saying: “Can anything good come out of Nazareth?” But he accepts Philip’s invitation to meet Jesus. Jesus welcomes him saying, “Behold, an Israelite indeed, in whom is no guile!” Jesus’ comment, “Before Philip called you, when you were under the fig tree, I saw you,” is probably based on a Jewish figure of speech referring to studying the Torah. Nathanael immediately recognizes Jesus as “the Son of God” and “the King of Israel.” Nathanael reappears at the end of John’s Gospel (Jn 21:2) as one of the disciples to whom Jesus appeared at the Sea of Tiberius after his resurrection from the tomb. The Gospels thus present Bartholomew as a man with no malice and a lover of Torah with openness to truth and readiness to accept the truth. Nathanael was the first Apostle to make an explicit confession of Faith in Jesus as the Messiah and as the Son of God.



      Life message: Let us pray for the grace to love the word of God as Bartholomew did and to accept the teaching of the Bible and the Church with open heart and open mind without pride or prejudice.



      (Fr Tony: https://frtonyshomilies.com/)

      The demand to love one another is a continuous action required of the members of the Church. Both the law and the Gospel, call believers to a life free from sin and filled with love. When life and love are absent, the salvation of God in Christ cannot be enjoyed. Every Christian is called to abide in God and remain in the light by obeying the love command of Jesus. Compassionate and efficacious love is the guarantee that we abide in God and remain in His light. The Gospel narrates the call of Philip and Nathanael. Jesus has insight beyond that of the prophets. He is the revealer to whom and through whom God communicates. The one who recognizes the identity of Jesus and bears witness to him is a true follower like Philip or Nathanael.

    body: |
      We have here the first and probably the best known case of a Stylite, i.e., a “pillar-hermit”. An oriental ascetic who passed long years perched atop a high column, Saint Simon was born of well-to-do peasants near the northern border of Syria. At 15 he entered a monastery, where his self-imposed austerity was found to be so extreme that his brethren considered him unsuited to community life, and he thenceforth lived as a hermit.

      All through the Season of Lent he would remain in a standing position and would partake of neither food nor drink. His advice and spiritual counsel were sought by innumerable people, even by the Emperor Theodosius and the Empress Endocia. Many were the miraculous cures wrought by his prayers. But in order to escape the importunities of relic-seeking admirers, he had a column built on a hill near the boundary between Syria and Cilicia, at first only some ten feet high, but eventually raised to sixty feet; and on its unsheltered top he spent the remaining 36 years of his life. The space was only about three feet in diameter, so that he could stand, kneel or sit, but never lie down. From here he would preach to the faithful on feast days and give counsel to all who ascended to him by means of a rope ladder. Some of his letters are still extant.
