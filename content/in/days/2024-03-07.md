  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 11:14–23"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today’s Gospel passage gives the crushing reply of Jesus to the Scribes’ slanderous explanation of Jesus’ miracle, namely, that Jesus expelled devils by using the assistance of the leader of devils, *Beelzebul*.



      Jesus refutes the false allegation raised by the Scribes against him with four counterarguments.



      1) A house divided against itself will perish, and a country engaged in civil war will be ruined. Hence, Satan will not fight against Satan by helping Jesus to expel his coworkers.



      2) If Jesus is collaborating with Satan to exorcise minor demons, one must admit that the Jewish exorcists are doing the same.



      3) Jesus claims that he is using the power of his Heavenly Father to evict devils, just as “*when a strong man, fully armed,*[the devil]*guards his own palace, his goods are in peace,”* he[the devil] can be routed when “*one stronger than he”*[Jesus, using the power of God]*assails him*[the devil]*and overcomes him*[the devil],*he*[Jesus]*takes away his*[the devil’s]*armor in which he*[the devil]*trusted, and divides his*[the devil’s]*spoil.”*



      4) Finally, Jesus delivers a crushing blow to his accusers as described in Mark’s Gospel (Mk 3:22-30), warning them that by telling blatant lies they are blaspheming against the Holy Spirit; their sins are unforgivable because they will not repent and ask for forgiveness.






      <span class="subhead">Life messages: </span>



      1) We can be influenced by the evil spirit if we listen to him and follow him.



      2) Hence, we have to keep our souls daily cleansed and filled with the Spirit of God, leaving no space for the evil spirit to enter our souls.



      3) If we disregard and disobey God’s word, we open the door to the power of sin and to Satan’s deception and control by failing to guard our five senses properly.

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      Most people consider a “demon” as a red horned creature with a long tail, based on mythology and artistic imagination. Catholic tradition has held that the devils are fallen angels. When Jesus meets the “possessed” man, and drives out the demon, he is immediately accused of being in league with Satan. "He casts out demons by Beelzebul, the prince of demons”. Jesus' response is logical: if Satan is divided against himself, how will his kingdom stand? Jesus’ power is to unite and gather while that of Satan is to divide and scatter. The real nature of Satan (devil) is to scatter and to divide as the word “diabolical” conveys. As Jesus is always the voice of unity, Satan stands for division.

      Sts Perpetua & Felicity, *Martyrs ( -203)*



      The record of the passion of St Perpetua, her slave St Felicity and their companions is among the most ancient, reliable hagiological histories that have come down to us.



      During the persecutions of Septimius Severus, five catechumens were arrested. They were Vibia Perpetua, a noblewoman of 22 and her infant, Felicity, a pregnant slave, Revocatus, Satwininus, Secundulus and later Saturus their Catechist. While still under arrest they were baptized. Perpetua’s father besought her to apostatize, because she was his favourite child. She, however, had the vision of a golden ladder guarded by the dragon and strewn with hooks and weapons that made difficult the ascent. But, following the example of Saturus, she trod over the dragon and, climbing the ladder, reached a beautiful place where a tall man in the garment of a shepherd welcomed her and offered her curds and milk. This was the premonition that she was to suffer and die.



      Perpetua, Felicity and their companions were all condemned and ordered to be thrown before the wild beasts, at the garrison games festival in honour of Ceasar Geta. On the day of their triumph, they were flogged and were beheaded by the gladiators.
