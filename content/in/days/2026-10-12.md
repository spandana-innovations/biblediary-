  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 11: 29-32"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Since there had been many false prophets and false messiahs in the past, and since their pride and prejudice did not permit them to see the Messiah in a carpenter-from-Nazareth-turned-wandering-preacher, the Jewish religious leaders demanded that Jesus should show some “Messianic” signs and miracles taken from their list. They would not accept that Jesus’ numerous miraculous healings were the Messianic signs foretold by the prophets.



      Jesus’ negative response: Calling them an apostate generation who refused to believe in their own prophets and denied the hand of God in the miracles he worked, Jesus warned these religious leaders that they would be condemned on the Day of Judgment by the people of Nineveh and by the Queen of Sheba from the South. This is one of the instances in which Jesus held up Gentiles as models of Faith and goodness (other examples: The Canaanite woman in Mt 15, the centurion in Lk 7, the Good Samaritan story in Luke 10; etc.). The pagan Ninevites heard the voice of the Lord God in the prophet Jonah, repented, and were spared. The Queen of Sheba recognized God’s Wisdom in King Solomon, and she travelled to Israel to receive more of it. Nevertheless, Jesus gave the religious leaders challenging him, “the sign of Jonah.” It was the undeniable Messianic sign of his own Resurrection from the tomb on the third day after his death, just as Jonah had spent three days in the belly of the giant fish before finally going to Nineveh to accomplish the mission God had originally given him.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span> We need to recognize God-given signs in our lives: 1) *Each Sacrament*in the Church is an external sign representing God’s grace. 2) On the altar we re-present Christ’s sacrifice on the cross using liturgical signs and prayers – The Sacrifice and Sacrament of the Eucharist. 3) Everyone living with us or working with us is a sign of God’s living presence in our midst, inviting us to love and honour him or her as God’s child and the living Temple of the Holy Spirit. 4) All world events and all the events in our lives are signs of God’s care and protection for us, His children. 5) The Holy Bible is a sign of God communicating His message to us every day. So, let us learn from these God-given signs instead of looking for signs in weeping Madonna’s, bleeding crucifixes and daily messages of visionaries.



      *(Fr Tony: https://frtonyshomilies.com/)*
  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      When people asked Jesus for a miraculous sign, He replied that only the “sign of Jonah” would be given. Jesus compared His generation to the people of Nineveh, who repented at Jonah’s message. Unlike those who waited for signs, the people of Nineveh responded immediately to God’s call. Jesus challenges us to do the same – not to wait for miracles but to listen to His words and turn to God in faith and repentance. We have the wisdom and teachings of Jesus to guide us. Will we respond with faith or turn away from Him? St Paul reminds us, “For freedom Christ has set us free. Stand firm, therefore, and do not submit again to the yoke of slavery” (Gal 5:1). We are challenged to stand firm in faith, free ourselves from sin, and deepen our commitment to following Jesus.
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "St Carlo Acutis"
    ref: "St Carlo Acutis"
    audio: null
    body: |
      St Carlo Acutis (1991–2006) is often called “God’s influencer” for the digital age. Born in London and raised in Milan, Carlo lived a short life, but one filled with deep faith, joy, and purpose. From a very young age, he showed an extraordinary love for the Eucharist, calling it his “highway to heaven.” Daily Mass, Eucharistic adoration, and the Rosary shaped his life and guided his choices.



      What makes Carlo especially inspiring is that he was an ordinary teenager in many ways. He loved computers, video games, football, and spending time with friends. Yet, he used his technological skills for an extraordinary mission. Carlo created a website documenting Eucharistic miracles from around the world, believing that technology could be a powerful tool for evangelization. His work continues to inspire faith across continents.



      Carlo’s holiness was expressed not only in prayer but also in charity. He was deeply sensitive to the poor, often giving his pocket money to those in need and defending classmates who were bullied. His kindness, humility, and simplicity made him a silent witness to Christ among his peers.



      At the age of fifteen, Carlo was diagnosed with an aggressive form of leukemia. He accepted his suffering with remarkable serenity, offering it for the Pope and for the Church. His final words reflected his faith: “I am happy because I have lived my life without wasting a minute on things that do not please God.”



      Canonized in 2024, St Carlo Acutis reminds us that holiness is possible today—online and offline. His life encourages young people to use their gifts, especially digital media, to bring Christ to the world, and to find true happiness in the Eucharist.
