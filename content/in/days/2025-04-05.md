      Reflection: 1



      Different people had different opinions about Jesus. Some thought he was a prophet, others said he was the Christ. Some were prejudiced against him. The officers sent by the chief priests and the Pharisees were impressed by the words of Jesus and came back to their masters saying, “No one ever spoke like this man.” But those masters condemned them. Nicodemus, one of the Pharisees, said to them that the law does not propose to judge a man without first giving him a hearing. Still, the others told him that according to Scripture no prophet arises from Galilee. </span></span></span></span>



      Different opinions and beliefs about Jesus continue even today. We need to have an honest look at our own beliefs and opinions about him. Today’s Gospel text shows how the religious leaders of the time of Jesus were blinded by prejudices against him. This is a challenge to all of us to be open to the work of God and to approach Jesus with faith and humility.</span></span></span></span>



      *— Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP</span></span>*</span></span>



      Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel describes the reaction of the people, the Pharisees, the Temple police, and Nicodemus, the Sanhedrin member, to Jesus’ public claim to be the promised *Messiah* and the “*Son of Man”*prophesied by Daniel.



      The common people say that Jesus probably is the expected Messiah because of His authoritative teaching and authentic miracles. But the Pharisees, the Scribes, and the Jewish priests can only see Jesus as a Galilean from Nazareth, and they argue from Scripture that the real Messiah must be born in David’s family in Bethlehem. The Temple police, whom they have sent to arrest Jesus, report that they have not arrested Jesus because, “nobody ever spoke like Him.” They are impressed by Jesus’ wisdom and authoritative teaching. Nicodemus, a prominent member of the supreme council of the Jews defends Jesus, demanding that the Sanhedrin give Jesus a fair trial before they have Him punished for blasphemy on unfounded claims.



      Life messages: 1) We believe in Jesus’ teachings, based on His authority as God. We believe in the Sacred Scriptures based on the teaching authority Jesus gave to Peter and his successors. Since Jesus and His Apostles believed in the Old Testament Books as the inspired word of God, and since the Church teaches the same thing, we, too, believe them to be the real word of God, and we follow the instructions given in the Bible. Hence, we need to follow the Bible as the guide of our Christian life and accept the traditional interpretation given to the word of God by the teaching authority in the Church.



      2) Today we Christians are also “a sign of contradiction,” as Simeon described Jesus, because we are different and challenging when we stand for Christ and choose His teachings while others reject them. We need to have, and act on, the courage of our Christian convictions.

  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Vincent Ferrer - Confessor (1350-1419)"
    ref: "Saint Vincent Ferrer - Confessor (1350-1419)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Born in Valencia to parents of noble lineage, Vincent completed his philosophy at age 14 and joined the Dominicans at 17. In 1384 he became professor at the cathedral school, and in 1391 adviser to King John I of Aragon and confessor to the queen. On the election of Cardinal Piedro de Luna as Pope Benedict XIII at avignon, Vincent became his Grand Penitentiary and confessor, and in 1399 *legate a latere Christi* with full powers to preach penance and prepare the faithful for God’s judgement.



      Poorly clad and travelling mostly on foot throughout France, Switzerland, Italy, Spain, Germany, England, Scotland and Ireland, he drew huge throngs of people with his well-prepared sermons that no church was found adequate enough to accommodate! Stupendous miracles of healing marked his progress. He evidently had the gift of tongues, for in each of these countries, with all their different languages, he steadily succeeded in touching the hearts of his hearers and awakening their consciences, frequently pausing to enable his hearers to recover from weeping. In Spain alone he brought 25,000 Jews and 8,000 Moors into the Church, in northern Italy and southeastern France thousands of Waldensians and Cathari, too; unlettered peasants came closer to the Church.



      Blessed with the gift of prophecy, Vincent once singled out in a crowd in Alessandria (northwestern Italy) the youthful Saint Bernardine of Siena who was to later evangelize his native Italy. An indication of the esteem in which Vincent was held throughout Spain can be gauged from the fact that in 1409 he was appointed one of the judges to decide upon the successor to the throne, Ferdinand of Castile owing his election to his influence.



      To Vincent goes the credit for the dramatic end to the 36-year long schismatic disharmony in the Church. Having failed to urge the antipope Benedict XIII, first in 1408 and then in 1415, to resign in favour of Clement VII (living at Avignon, France), Vincent mounted the pulpit before a huge assembly presided over by Benedict himself and him. Abandoned by his supporters Benedict instantly fled!



      Vincent’s entire life was one of great austerity. He slept little and prayed much, fasting rigorously. He died in 1419 while evangelizing Brittany and was canonized in 1455.
