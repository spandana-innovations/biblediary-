  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 10: 38-42"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Today’s readings are about hospitality and the necessity of listening to God before acting. Jesus welcomed and tended to the needs of all, reflecting in His actions the very hospitality of God. All four Gospels recount Jesus’ welcoming and feeding of the multitudes of people who came to hear the Good News. The Gospel passage describes how Martha, a true child of Abraham, wanted to extend the traditional generous hospitality of her people to Jesus, the true Messiah, by preparing an elaborate meal, while her sister Mary spent her time in talking and listening to Jesus.



      Jesus’ advice: The episode is also intended to teach us where we should place our priorities. Presenting Martha as a dynamo of action and Mary as a true listener to the word of God, today’s Gospel invites us to serve others with Martha’s diligence, after recharging our spiritual batteries every day by prayer, listening to God and talking to God as Mary did. We are able to minister truly to the needs of others only after welcoming God’s words into our hearts, minds, and living.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span> 1) We need to recharge our spiritual batteries: Without the “fuel” of prayer, silence, and communion with God, service can become a crushing responsibility, a burden rather than a vocation, an annoyed grumbling rather than a response to the invitation of God.



      2) We need listening Martha’s and serving Marys: Martha has become a symbol of action-oriented, responsible people who get the job done. Our world needs such men, women, boys and girls who get the job done. This is certainly true in the Church where we need the active cooperation of many parishioners in its various ministries.



      *(Fr Tony: https://frtonyshomilies.com/)*
  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      “Mary has chosen the good portion, which will not be taken away from her.” Why was Mary’s choice considered “good”, and does that mean Martha’s choice was bad? Jesus surely valued Martha’s active service and hospitality. However, He may not have appreciated her attitude of complaining and finding fault with her sister. Mary chose the “better portion” by sitting at the Lord’s feet and listening to His teaching. She focused on the Word of God, savouring it deeply. She chose something lasting, something “that will not be taken away”. Mary was not distracted by other things, but gave her total attention to the Lord. In our busy lives, filled with distractions, do we take time to “sit at the Lord’s feet and listen to his teaching?” According to Pope Francis, “Mary teaches us the need for that contemplative spirit that is not idleness but the gift of being totally attentive to the Lord” (*Angelus Address, July 21, 2013*). While service and caring for others are important, the Word of God is what truly lasts. God’s Word leads us to union with Him and is essential for eternal life.

    ref: "Saint Bruno (Abbot Confessor) (c.1030 -1101)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Bruno Hartenfaust was ordained priest at 25, and received a canonry at Cologne but was recalled by the Bishop of Rheims to assist in directing his former school.



      In 1075 he was appointed Chancellor and Canon-theologian for the diocese. But after the death of his friend, Bishop Gervais, the See was occupied by the worldly and violent Manasses I who, when later suspended, fled for his life in a popular uprising. Bruno and his six companions thereupon carried out a vow made some years before to leave the world and embrace the monastic life in the Alpine solitude of the Chartreuse given to them by St Hugh, Bishop of Grenoble, commencing in 1084 a life of silence, prayer, labour, study, mortification, and poverty. That was the beginning of the Eremitical Order of Carthusians.



      Bruno, however, was to spend only six years at the Chartreuse, for Pope Urban II, a former pupil at Reims, called him to Rome as his adviser and confessor with the aim of continuing the reform of the clergy initiated by Pope St Gregory VII; but faced with the anti-pope, Guibert, and the military advances of Emperor Henry IV, Bruno repeatedly tried to persuade Urban to let him return to the solitary life, but Urban wished him to remain within call, and so had a new monastery founded in Calabria, where Bruno died in 1101. His Grande Chartreuse continued to grow until it controlled some 250 monasteries.



      <span class="subhead">Reflection:</span> *“In the midst of the battle which we fight for God, we enjoy peace which the world does not know”*(St Bruno).
