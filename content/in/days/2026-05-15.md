      Reflection: 1



      Jesus indicates to his disciples his coming death and warns them of a period of his absence which will make them sorrowful. He also promises them his return which will bring them lasting joy. When Jesus returns they will rejoice and no one will take their joy away. This assurance of Jesus gives us great hope and courage. Even though we may have to pass through pain and suffering, the final victory will be ours and we shall rejoice in the Lord forever. The only condition is that we should live as faithful disciples of Jesus. Let us every day desire this grace deeply and pray for it. </span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      After foretelling his imminent departure following the Last Supper, Jesus tried to boost the morale of his sad, dispirited disciples. First, he consoled them, promising them to send his Holy Spirit as their Paraclete — Consoler, Guide and Advocate (Attorney). Then, Jesus assured them that his absence would only be temporary.</span></span></span></span></span>



      Contrast between present sorrows and future glory: Jesus compares the temporary pain, sufferings, and persecutions of his disciples to the passing, though intense, labor-pains of a woman giving birth to her child. The moment she hears the cry of her child and sees his or her face she forgets all her pain. In the same way, the "other-worldly" joy waiting for his disciples will transcend all types of earthly joys.</span></span></span></span></span>



      Life messages: 1) Let us see our pains and suffering as God’s means to strengthen our will and form our character. 2) The conviction of the temporary nature of our suffering and of the glory waiting for us if we accept pain and suffering graciously, converting them into willing acts of reparation for sins, will help us to face them heroically. </span></span></span></span></span>

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      It is common experience that death of a loved one brings sadness and sorrow. As Jesus senses that His death is approaching, He explains that the disciples will lament and weep, but the world will rejoice because it welcomes His death. Addressing His disciples, Jesus indicates that His death will bring renewal in the Israelite society for which He has been sent. But the way to this process involves pain and suffering culminating in His death. As for the disciples, they are sad at the thought of losing their beloved Master. But Jesus promises them that their weeping will be turned into joy at His resurrection. Jesus then brings in the example of a woman in childbirth who suffers pain when the hour for her childbirth approaches, but then she will experience joy that a new person has been born into the world. Similarly, the disciples are now sad at His coming suffering, but He will see them again and then they will rejoice. Their joy, Jesus says, will remain with them and nobody will be able to take that joy away.</span></span></span>

      St. Isidore was born at Madrid, Spain, in the latter half of the 12th century. For the greater part of his life he was employed as a laborer on a farm outside the city. Many marvelous happenings accompanied his lifelong work in the fields and continued long after his holy death. He was favored with celestial visions and, it is said, the angels sometimes helped him in his work in the fields. St. Isidore was canonized in 1622.</span></span>



      He is the patron of farmers.</span>
