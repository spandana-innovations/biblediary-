  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "John 10:22–30"
    audio: null
    body: |
      The works I do in my Father’s name give witness in my favour. Jesus reveals Himself in terms of action. His self-giving poverty is action – a living, moving, performing generous love. And so Jesus becomes known to us throughout history, as acting – alive in His living Church, in His Sacraments, in His acting members.



      Jesus does not accept any external trappings of kingship: no crown, robes, throne, retinue or palace. These are static things, showpieces of one who desires to be a king rather than to act like a king. Jesus acts: He is voluntarily poor because that is the effect of love in action; it is the result of what He does.



      Jesus manifests Himself in actions; we must manifest Him in actions, too. The real Christian is Christ endlessly searching for people in need of help. Not a mere kindness done here or there to the needy upon whom we happen to stumble, but the urgent, unflagging search of love.



      (Bernard C. Mischke PSC & Fritz Mishcke OSC, *Pray Today’s Gospel,* ST PAULS, Mumbai, India)

      We come across two distinct groups of people in today’s gospel: the Jews who do not believe in Jesus and others whom Jesus describes as ‘my sheep.’ They listen to him, believe in him and follow him. The first group still doubts about Jesus’ identity. So they are asking Jesus to tell them plainly whether he is the Christ or not. For his part, Jesus asks them to look at the works that he performs in his Father’s name and then believe in him. Am I among those who believe that Jesus is the Christ, the anointed one sent by the Father? If I believe in him, do I listen to him speaking to me in different ways and respond to him in a way befitting his followers?

      Saint Adalbert of Prague - *Bishop martyr (956-997)*



      Christened Vojtech at birth in the princely Slavnik family of Libice in East Bohemia, Adalbert was trained in theology at Magdeburg under its first Archbishop, Saint Adalbert, whose name he eventually took at his “confirmation”.



       The first Czech occupant of the See of Prague, Adalbert was a man of vibrant energy and zeal and, much given to austerity, strove to reform the clergy, suppress abuses of various kinds and eliminate pagan practices, as also to spread Christianity throughout Bohemia and neighbouring Hungary. However, distressed by the superficial attitude evident towards the Faith, Adalbert left Prague in 982 for Rome, where he became a Benedictine monk.



      Persuaded by the Pope he returned to Prague in 992; but disillusioned with the lack of change in the situation, he left Prague again in 995 only to learn of the brutal massacre of the Slavnik family and its supporters that followed. Now refused permission to return to his See, he obtained Papal release from his episcopal obligations and dedicated himself entirely to missionary activity, becoming a close friend and trusted adviser of the Roman Emperor, Otto III, on matters concerning the progressive Christianization of the Slavs.



      True to the meaning of his baptismal name, Adalbert was to truly prove “a consolation to the army” of Jesus Christ. Invited by the Polish king, Boleslas I the Great, he set about organizing missionary work among the heathen Prussians on the Baltic coast where he was martyred in 997. His body, ransomed by Boleslas, was buried at Gniezno in Poland and later transferred to Prague in 1039.
