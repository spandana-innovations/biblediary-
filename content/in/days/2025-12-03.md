      Reflection: 1



      Jesus sends seventy-two disciples on a mission to the places he himself was going to visit. The number seventy-two represents all the anions of the world, and their mission is a foreshadow of the Church’s mission to the whole world. Jesus tells the seventy-two about the urgency of preaching the kingdom; it is an urgent and pressing mission and no time should be wasted in other activities. He also warns them of the hostility they are going to face. He foretells them that the Galilean towns that refused to understand the signs and miracles Jesus worked in them, were going to have a tragic end. Jesus also assures the seventy-two that to hear them or to reject them is the same as to hear or reject Jesus himself and the Father who sent him. How do we welcome the teaching of the disciples of Jesus, and what do we do to help others to welcome it?</span></span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      The Decapolis was a loose federation of ten cities with a mixed population of Jews and Gentiles. Jesus took six months to travel to the Decapolis via Tyre and Sidon and to return to Galilee. The healing and feeding described in today’s Gospel took place on a hill near the Sea of Galilee after Jesus’ return from the Decapolis. Jesus healed the lame, the maimed, the blind, and the dumb, then, expressed His pity for the hungry multitude to the apostles. When they asked Jesus where they were supposed to get enough bread to feed this enormous crowd, He asked how many loaves they had. They told Him, “*Seven, and a few small fish.”* Jesus had the crowd sit, then took the loaves and fish, gave thanks to God for them, broke them and gave them to the apostles to give to the people. After the crowd had eaten their fill, the Apostles, again at Jesus’ command, collected the broken pieces; they filled seven baskets with the fragments.</span>

      St Francis Xavier, the Patron Saint of Missions, exemplifies unparalleled faith and zeal. His life inspires us to ignite the world with the fire of Faith, Love, and Hope. On this great Feast day, Jesus commissions His disciples, reminding them to preach by their lives. “Preach as you go,” a phrase often overlooked, carries profound meaning. It calls for making every action a proclamation of God’s love and joy. The life of St Francis Xavier is a strong statement that ‘glorifying God is not confined to specific places or situations but extends to wherever we are.’ Let us echo St Francis Xavier’s fervour by glorifying God in our daily lives, fostering deep faith and trust wherever we go.
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Francis Xavier - Confessor"
    ref: "Saint Francis Xavier - Confessor"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Born on 7 April 1506 to Don Juan de Jasu and Doña Maria de Azpilcueta y Aznárez, Francis Xavier was one of the six who, with Ignatius de Loyola, took their vows as the first Jesuits on 15 August 1534. Ordained priest on 24 June 1537, he celebrated his first mass after a rigorous 40-day fast and spiritual preparation. He was Ignatius’ secretary in Rome when he was sent by King John III of Portugal to the East Indies. At only a day’s notice, Francis set out with the Portuguese ambassador for Lisbon. The 11,000-mile sea voyage having wintered at Mozambique, he landed in Goa, the centre of Portuguese activity in the East, on 6 May 1542 and, learning the local language, nursed the sick, preached, and taught catechism, before proceeding to the Fishery Coast and baptizing the pearl fishers.



      In 1544 Francis proceeded to Ceylon, thence to Malacca and the Spice Islands and, some believe, even the Philippines. Despite language constraints and Buddhist opposition in Japan where he had landed on 15 August 1549, his preaching, accompanied by numerous miracles, was so effective that within some 40 years, the faithful in Japan numbered 400,000!



      Francis succumbed to fever on 3 December 1552 during a fruitless three month-long wait on the Sancian island of Canton for Chinese pirates who, for a tidy sum, were to smuggle him into China. In 1553, his body, found incorrupt and fresh after over two months of burial, is venerated till today in the Bom Jesus Basilica in Old Goa, even as his right arm, severed at the elbow at the instance of the then Jesuit General Claudio Acquaviva, lies enshrined in the Church of the Gesù in Rome.



      Beatified in 1619 and canonized in 1622, Francis has been named Apostle of the Indies and patron of foreign missions.
