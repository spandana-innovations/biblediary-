  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mathew 11: 16-19"
    audio: null
    body: |
      The message of John the Baptist and the message of Jesus fell on deaf ears and met with stiff resistance from the self-righteous Scribes and the Pharisees because of their jealousy, prejudice and spiritual blindness. Hence, they attributed the austerities of John the Baptist to the devil and identified Jesus’ table fellowship with sinners as gluttony, evidence contraindicating any messianic possibility. In today’s Gospel, Jesus compares these Scribes and Pharisees to irresponsible street-children.



      Dog-in-the-manger attitude: Jesus compares the attitude of the Scribes and the Pharisees to that of street-children who want to entertain themselves by playing wedding and funeral songs. They divide themselves into two groups. But when one group proposes to sing wedding songs and asks the other group to dance, the second group will propose funeral songs and ask the first group to carry one of them on their shoulders as they act out a funeral procession. In the end both groups will be frustrated. Jesus states that the Scribes and Pharisees act exactly like these irresponsible, immature children because of their pride and prejudice. Jesus criticizes the unbelieving Jews for not listening either to John the Baptist, who preached a message of austerity and repentance, or to Jesus, who preached the good news of love, mercy and salvation.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span> 1) Jesus’ parable about disappointed playmates challenges us to examine whether we are buffet Catholics with selective hearing, so that we hear only what we want to hear. Jesus’ message of the kingdom of God is *good news* and it produces true joy and spiritual freedom for those who will listen, but it is also a warning for those who refuse to listen and close their minds. *Hearing* the Gospel implies the total acceptance and assimilation of what we hear and the incorporation of it into our daily lives. Like the generation of Jesus’ time, our age is marked by indifference and contempt, especially in regard to the things of heaven. Indifference dulls our ears to God’s voice and to the good news of the Gospel. Only the humble of heart can find joy and favour in God’s grace.

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      In today’s Gospel reading, Jesus is pointing out the ‘divided-tongue’ of the people whose speech is unserious, moody, and inconsistent. They criticise both the ‘ascetic’ life of John the Baptist as well as the ‘life-enhancing’ approach of Jesus. Their approach to life seems to be based on ‘convenience’ and they behave according to their moods. There is a lot of ambiguity in what people speak. But wisdom demands just the opposite kind of behaviour. It is based on facts, experiences, convictions, and commitments. Today in our society too, ambiguity has become the identity of many of us. A man could be a very ‘successful’ teacher, but at the same time an alcoholic and a very irresponsible parent. The disciples of Jesus cannot ‘say’ and ‘preach’ one thing and live in ways that are opposed to such speeches and preaching. Let us pray to the Lord to increase our faith and strengthen our commitment.

    saintName: "Saint Damasus I - Pope, Confessor (c.304-384)"
    ref: "Saint Damasus I - Pope, Confessor (c.304-384)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Saint Damasus was elected Pope on 1 October 366 to succeed Saint Liberius under whom he had served as a deacon. Hailed by Saint Jerome as “an incomparable man”, “a Virgin Doctor of a Virgin Church”, he was equally famed for his personal holiness and learning as for his great zeal for the purity of the faith and his love of the poor.



      The first to term Rome the ‘Apostolic See’, Damasus emphasized at the Roman Council of 382 that the ecclesiastical supremacy of Rome was based not on the decree of any council but on the words of Christ himself. Saint Jerome attended the synod and stayed on as confidential secretary and friend to Damasus who, in turn, encouraged him in his biblical studies, commissioning him to prepare the Vulgate version of the Bible.



      Also the first Pope to send an apostolic vicar to Eastern Illyricum (Greece) to counter the jurisdictional claims of the patriarchate of Constantinople, Damasus duly enforced the imperial edict that prohibited Roman clergy from soliciting legacies and gifts from widows and orphans and another that confirmed the Pope’s right to decide on all matters ecclesiastical.



      His reverence for the Martyrs led him to discover, restore and redecorate their tombs and the catacombs. We owe much of our knowledge about the early Martyrs to the numerous marble inscriptions composed by him and which are still extant. Damasus, who is also called the Father of the Roman Mass whose ancient liturgy he revised and shortened and who introduced the use of Latin, died on 11 December 384.
