  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mathew 18: 15-20"
    audio: null
    body: |
      The first part of today’s portion of Matthew’s Gospel is one of the passages many have found difficult to interpret. Many Bible commentators think that Jesus never said these things, that probably they were a later addition by the Church because 1) there was no organized Church at that time, 2) Jesus never considered a sinner as a hopeless case, and 3) Jesus loved Gentiles and tax collectors.</span></span></span></span></span>



      The real meaning: What Jesus actually meant was, “Do whatever you can to make the guilty person realize and confess his fault, thus helping him to repair the damage he or she has done to his or her personal and communal relationships.” Jesus seems to suggest the following steps to repair a broken personal relationship: 1) One-on-one encounter: If you are sure that somebody has wronged you, tell him lovingly and politely that he has hurt you. 2) The group encounter: If the first step does not work, meet him again in the company of two or three wise and honourable persons and try to make the culprit realize what he has done wrong. 3) Parish encounter: If steps one and two do not work, bring his case to the pastor or to the parish council or the Christian fellowship. 4) Leave him to Lord’s mercy: If the culprit remains stubborn, like a Gentile or proud tax collector, continue to pray for him and leave him to God’s mercy.</span></span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span></span></span> 1) Let us have the good will and generosity to accept our mistakes and ask pardon and forgiveness from the offended victim. 2) Let us also learn to forgive and forget the offenses done against us.</span></span></span></span></span>



      *(Fr Tony: https://frtonyshomilies.com/)*
  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      God created us to live in unity with Him and with one another. In the Gospel of today, Jesus is instructing His disciples and us to live in love. The three-step process of reconciliation is to restore the offender to the community. Considering Jesus’ unrelenting compassion, He teaches that the Church should never give up on anyone, even a hardened sinner. The pastoral concern of the community for a member who lives in sin never completely exhausts. The Church, the community gathered in God’s name, is invested with His saving presence and power. We are the delight of God’s heart, and all His works – His compassion, His discipline – are intended to lead us back to Him. And Jesus assures us that whenever the Church gathers, even when only two or three of its members are present, He is with us.</span></span></span>

      H</span></span></span></span>ailing from the royal family of King Laoghaire, St Murtagh is reputed to have been the first Bishop of Killala, Ireland, appointed by St Patrick. It seems probable, that he lived at a later date, for in the life of St Cormac, it is stated that the harbour of Killala was blessed in turn by Patrick, Brigid, Colmcille (St Columba), Kenny and Murtagh, which doesn’t sound, according to some, that St Patrick and Murtagh were contemporaries.</span></span></span></span>



      Furthermore, he is stated to have met St Colmcille at Ballysodare, near Silago, after Colmcille’s conference with the Irish king at Drumkeith in 575.</span></span></span></span>



      St Murtagh’s feast is kept throughout Ireland. </span></span></span></span>
