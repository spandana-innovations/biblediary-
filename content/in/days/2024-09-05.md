  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 5:1–11"
    audio: null
    body: |
      The scene is the Sea of Galilee (Gennesaret in Greek and Tiberius in Latin). The story of the miraculous catch of fish described in today’s Gospel is similar to the post-Resurrection appearance of Jesus recounted in John 21:4-14. It is one of the “epiphany-call stories” which direct our attention to the fact that Jesus had his own criteria for selecting people to be his apostles. The reading challenges us to examine our own personal calls to conversion and discipleship.</span></span></span></span>



      The miraculous catch followed by the call: After teaching the crowd from a seat in the boat of Simon, Jesus said to him “Put out into the deep and let down your nets for a catch.” And Simon answered, “Master, we toiled all night and took nothing! But at your word I will let down the nets.” Simon and his companions were stunned by the biggest catch of their lives. This event led Simon to acknowledge his unworthiness, as a sinner, even to stand before the Divine Presence of Jesus. Jesus was so impressed by Simon’s obedience and confession of unworthiness that he immediately invited Simon, Andrew, James and John to be his disciples and so to “catch men” instead of fish.</span></span></span></span>



      Life Messages: </span></span></span></span>



      1)</span></span> Our encounter with the holiness of God needs to lead us to recognize our sinfulness. The Good News of today’s Gospel is that our sinfulness — our pride and self-centeredness – does not repel God. That is why we offer this Mass asking God’s pardon and forgiveness, and why we receive Jesus in Holy Communion only after acknowledging our unworthiness.</span></span></span></span>



      2) With Jesus, the seemingly impossible becomes possible. Today’s Gospel passage tells us an important truth about how God works in and through us for His glory. God chooses ordinary people – people like you and me – as His ambassadors. He uses the ordinary circumstances of our daily lives and our responses.</span></span></span></span>

    body: |
      In the familiar Gospel passage of today, Jesus performs a great nature miracle of the catch of fish. But his success should be measured not in/by the catch of fish, but rather, by the fact that Peter and others will be called to accompany him in the world-wide mission of ‘catching people.’ Simon Peter and his companions must have been shocked by what had happened at the one word of Jesus! This was possible because Peter believed in the word of Jesus and let down the nets. Do we believe in the word of Jesus enough to let go of our ego, our self-inflated image, our attitude, etc.? If, and only, when we rise above our ego that the word of Jesus will take effect, making a miraculous catch possible. </span></span></span></span>

    saintName: "Saint Teresa of Kolkata -(Religious, Founder) (1910-1997)"
    ref: "Saint Teresa of Kolkata -(Religious, Founder) (1910-1997)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Teresa was born in Skopje, Yugoslavia and Christened Agnes Gonxha Bojaxhiu. The youngest of three children who lost their father in their childhood, she joined Loreto Abbey in Ireland at 18, taking the name “Teresa”, choosing St Therese of Lisieux as her patron. Five months later, i.e., in 1929, she came to Calcutta (now Kolkata), where she taught for sixteen years at St Mary’s School, Entally and mastered the local language, Bengali.</span></span></span></span>



      In 1946 she got what she referred to as “the call within the call”, viz., “to be God’s love in action to the poorest of the poor”. In 1948, clad in a coarse blue-bordered white sari, a crucifix pinned at her shoulder and equipped with a 6-month intensive training course in ‘nursing’, she nursed the sick and the dying with utmost tenderness, lovingly cleansed their maggot-infested wounds, washed their naked bodies, cradled them into the Lord’s peace the way only someone divinely possessed could. </span></span></span></span>



      In 1952, she started “Nirmal Hriday” (pure heart), a home for dying destitute, followed by the opening of “Shishu Bhavan” for children; in 1957, “Shanti Nagar” for lepers; in 1963, a parallel institute for men, the “Missionary Brothers of Charity”. Winner of the Nobel Peace Prize (1979) and the Bharat Ratna (1980), Teresa walked with royalty without losing the common touch and was comfortable with Pope, politician and pauper alike. </span></span></span></span>



      She was canonized in 2016.</span></span></span></span>
