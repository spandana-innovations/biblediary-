  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Matthew 10:17–22"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Jesus predicts persecution for His followers: “Brother will hand over brother to death; children will turn against parents and have them put to death. You will be hated by all on account of me. But whoever holds out till the end will escape death.” Jesus means that the faithful ones will escape eternal death, since He has already predicted bodily death for

      many of His witnesses.



      St Stephen was one such witness who understood the spirit of Jesus to the full. Christlike giving – Christmas giving – meant for him the gift of his very life. At first sight it seemed he received no earthly gift in return. His persecutors stopped their ears so as not to hear his final testimony to God’s love.



      But once Stephen has offered that greatest Christmas gift – “Lord Jesus, receive my spirit” – the power of God is at work. How Stephen must have jumped with joy in heaven when he saw the Lord’s return gift on earth, a gift he hadn’t expected, a gift worth all he had given, a gift worth his blood! One of the radical young men that stood by cheering and holding coats when Stephen was stoned to death was a young Pharisee named Saul. He had been so filled with hate at what Stephen said, that he took it as a cue for a long rampage of Christian arrests. Saul became such a famous persecutor that when he came down the street, all Christians automatically locked their doors.



      And now see what the Lord has given Stephen in return for his life: the life of a converted Saul, a Paul who becomes the world’s greatest missionary for Jesus.



      You see we never know who our friends are, or who our friends in the future may be.



      To Your strong hands my spirit, O Saviour, I commend:

      The next day of the birth of Jesus, the Church keeps the feast of Saint Stephen, who became the first martyr. This reminds us that if one wants to follow Jesus, s/he should be willing and ready to embrace all kinds of persecutions, even death. Stephen was a man “full of grace and power” and he continued the works of the Lord. Saul, in whose presence Stephen was killed, felt that he was doing the will of God by protecting the old Law. The old wineskin could not contain the new wine. However, later, when he received the grace and power of the risen Lord, he was able to hold the new wine in the new wineskin. This transformation helped him to become a powerful emissary for the Lord. Witness of life is more powerful than words.
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Stephen, Proto-Martyr - (-c. 36)"
    ref: "Saint Stephen, Proto-Martyr - (-c. 36)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Within a year of Jesus’ death and resurrection the young Church at Jerusalem already numbered over 5000, and the Apostles felt the need to delegate some of their duties—like preaching, baptizing, and the fair distribution of alms—to ‘deacons’ (helpers). The first of the seven to be ordained for this purpose was Stephen, “a man full of faith and the Holy Spirit”.

      Stephen, “full of grace and fortitude”, performed “great wonders and signs” in the name of Jesus. A pupil of the great Gamaliel, he preached fearlessly in the “synagogue of the Freedmen” (descendants of Jews who had been carried off to Rome as children in 63 B.C. and there freed), and in the Synagogues of the Hellenist Jews from Cyrene, Alexandria, Cilicia (Beirut) and Asia, who preferred to worship by themselves. He insisted that Jesus came to save all and not merely the “Chosen People”, and that the Church was separate and distinct from the synagogue, reproving the Pharisees for their obstinacy that prevented them from recognizing the long awaited Messiah and, instead, crucifying him.

      Accused of blasphemy before the Sanhedrin, his fiery and eloquent defence of the Church, his enumeration of God’s past mercies toward Israel and the latter’s ingratitude, and finally his declaration that he saw “the heavens opened and the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God”, so raised their fury that they dragged him outside the city and stoned him to death. All through the fracas, Stephen’s thoughts, as a true follower of Jesus, were for his persecutors—“Lord, lay not this sin to their charge.” He earned himself the distinction of becoming the first martyr of the fledgling Church, exclaiming, “Lord Jesus, receive my spirit!” thus living up to the meaning of his name which, in Greek, signifies “crown”!
