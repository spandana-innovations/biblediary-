      Reflection: 1



      Jesus chooses and appoints his twelve Apostles. They symbolize the twelve tribes of Israel and stand for the new people of God Jesus is establishing. They were to be detached from earthly possessions, they were to depend on God, and be courageous to face opposition. The Father sends His Son to the earth to establish His kingdom, and the Son sends his Apostles to continue his work and to be leaders of the new people of God. Their mission, at that moment, was limited only to Israel, but after the Resurrection of Jesus it is extended to all nations. Jesus empowers the Apostles and gives them authority to cast out demons, to heal the sick and to proclaim that the kingdom of heaven is near. Every Christian has to share in this apostolic task by works of charity, living example and spoken word. How and to what extent are we doing this task? </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel passage gives a short account of the call and mission of the apostles. The first missionary was sent to this world when God the Father dispatched His only-begotten Son, incarnate as Jesus of Nazareth, into this world with the “Good News” that God is a loving, merciful, and forgiving Father Who wants to save everyone through His Son Jesus. Today’s Gospel describes how this first missionary selected and empowered twelve future missionaries as apostles, sending them to the Jewish towns and villages as heralds to announce the Good News that God was keeping His promises now.



      Special features: Jesus selected very ordinary people, most of them hard-working fishermen with no social status, learning, or political influence, because he was sure that they would be very effective instruments in God’s hands. It was a strange mix of people: Matthew was a hated tax collector for a foreign power, while Simon the Cananaean was a Zealot — a fanatical nationalist determined to destroy Roman rule by any means; the others were mostly professional fishermen with a lot of good will, patience and stamina. It was only their admiration and love for Jesus that united them. Jesus selected them after a night of prayer and gave them a share in his Divine powers of healing and exorcism with the mission to announce the coming of the “kingdom of God” in an immediate visit to them by “the One Who is to come”



      Life message: 1) As Christians, we have the same mission that Jesus entrusted to the apostles. We fulfil this mission by proclaiming the word of God, primarily by our living out of Jesus’ teachings, and by promoting and helping world-wide missionary activities of the Church with prayer, moral support, and financial aid.

      Jesus in today’s gospel, chooses the twelve apostles and sends them out on a mission. He first of all empowers the apostles by giving them the authority to announce the Good News and to cast demons. In this way, he enhances their human resourcefulness with divine power. Secondly, he pragmatically sharpens the goals of the apostles by telling them whom they should approach first in announcing the Good News. Like the Apostles, we too are endowed with the mission of proclaiming the Good News and empowered by His Spirit to work for the kingdom of God as envisioned by Jesus. Are we ready to collaborate with Jesus in his mission?

    audio: null
    body: |
      Of the 19 priests and religious who were hanged on account of the Faith by Calvinists in Gorkum, Holland, the martyrology indicates that 12 were hanged specifically because of their belief in the Eucharist and the primacy of the Pope. They are: Nicholas Pieck, Jerome Weerden, Leonard Vechel, Nicholas Janssen, Godfrey van Duynen, John van Oosterwyk, John van Hoornaer, Adrian van Hilvarenbeck, James Lacops, Andrew Wontera, Antony van Willehad (90 years old) and Nicasius van Heeze.



      While James Lacops was hanged from a ladder, the rest were hanged from the beams. After their death, their bodies were cast into 2 ditches where they lay till 1616 when, during a truce between Spain and the United Provinces, they were translated to the Franciscan church in Brussels, Belgium. They were canonized on 29 June 1867 by Pope Pius IX.
