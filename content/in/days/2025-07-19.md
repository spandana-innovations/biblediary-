    ref: "Matthew 12:14–21"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      The Pharisees were plotting to get rid of Jesus while many people followed him and were healed by him. Though the Pharisees held on to legalism, they stood against justice, truth and mercy which Jesus was practicing and preaching. But the crowd did not follow them; they followed Jesus because in him they found hope and new life, health and happiness. In today’s Gospel, St Mathew points out that this was in fulfilment of the message God gave through Prophet Isaiah (Is 42:1-4), stating how the chosen one would behave, how he would be meek and peaceful, how he would bring justice to victory, and how all nations will put their hope in him.






      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      To retain the privilege of being God’s elect people, called to be his very own, we need to practise kindness, compassion and a healthy humility. Micah saw his people needing deep reform. Instead of sleeping at night, they now “work out evil on their couches” coveting fields and seizing them, cheating others of their inheritance. Land-hungry people were reversing the purpose of the exodus. God’s people were meant to live in a homeland where each family passed its property from one generation to the next (Lev 25:3-28). But many were reducing others again to slavery. As punishment, gentiles will seize all the land for themselves. As in other prophecies, we note how foreigners have a role in God’s plans.



      Matthew quotes Isaiah about the Suffering Servant. The purpose of Jesus is well described by this passage, written during the Babylonian exile. Its message was rejected in his own day, as its attitude towards the gentiles seemed too mild, even hopeful for their salvation. Jesus is described as. “my servant whom I have chosen, my loved one in whom I delight. He will not contend nor cry out... The bruised reed he will not crush. In his name the gentiles will find hope.” If we disregard our neighbour in time of sickness and trouble, we do not deserve the name of Christian, for, like Jesus we are called to cure and heal, quietly, without ostentation. We cannot disregard the outsider without being called to account by God.



      As we open our hearts to people of mixed ancestry, according to the example of Jesus, we will be apostles of hope, proclaiming hope, not just for others but also for ourselves. In many ways, others can teach us how to be God’s chosen people.

      We encounter two kinds of people in today’s gospel: The Pharisees, who were plotting to get Jesus out of their lives, and a large crowd of people who followed Jesus and whom he had healed. The first group promotes conspiracy, hatred, destruction, death and annihilation of the innocent and the killing of Truth because they could not withstand the presence of the Truth, that is, Jesus. The second group follows Jesus with open hearts and Jesus gives them new life and hope. They accept and live by the Truth. What would we choose if we were offered life and death, truth and falsehood, destruction and promotion of life?
  - key: saint
    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Sts. Justa and Rufina, Virgins & Martyrs (A.D. - 287)"
    ref: "Sts. Justa and Rufina, Virgins & Martyrs (A.D. - 287)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Sts Justa and Rufina were two Christian women at Seville in Spain who maintained themselves by selling earthenware. But they refused to sell vessels for the use of heathen ceremonies so as not to concur in idolatrous superstitions. Infuriated, the heathens broke up their stock-in-trade. Justa and Rufina retaliated by overthrowing the image of a false goddess, whereupon, they were impeached before the Governor.



      The Prefect commanded them to renounce their Faith and offer sacrifice to the very goddess whose image they had overthrown. They politely declined, but boldly confessed Christ as their Lord. As a result, the Prefect ordered that they be stretched on the rack and their sides torn with hooks. An idol was placed near the rack with incense that if they should offer sacrifice they should be released, but their fidelity could not be shaken. Consequently, Justa died on the rack while Rufina was strangled to death and their bodies burnt.



      The two saints are greatly venerated in Spain and their names doubtless represent historical martyrs in that place.
