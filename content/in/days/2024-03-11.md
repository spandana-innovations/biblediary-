  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "John 4:43–54"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Faith is one of the greatest virtues of Christian life. Faith in God brings transformation and shapes the entire Personality. It helps a person to live a good life and helps to realize salvation. Faith is not belief in particular doctrine or creed. Faith is all about trust – absolute trust. We say we believe in God or in Jesus, but do we believe in what God promises us?



      Prophet Isaiah deliberately alludes to the dawn of a ‘New Creation’ for those who believe and live by it. The old will be forgotten and for the one who believes, is already living in this New Age –“the kingdom of God is at hand” ( Mk 1:25). Part of the new experience of liberation from the past, weeping and crying for help will never again be heard in the streets of Jerusalem. “The sinner” or ‘faithless’ is out of place here, in this New Creation. Prophet Isaiah assured that the one who believe would experience everything anew such as peace, security and prosperity in life.



      In Today’s Gospel reading, the royal officer and the entire family is able to experience that Newness of life because of their faith. The officer not simple believes but he goes away having full confident that his son will be well again. This is what we called faith in action. It’s the faith of the officer that dragged him to Jesus. His faith in the words of Jesus brings new life to his son. Jesus doesn’t need to be present there physically to cure the son but his words from distance suffices. In the healing of the boy, the fullness of God is available to them through Jesus. In faith, the officer was able to see God in flesh and blood, in the person of Jesus. Let us earnestly ask God, to strengthen our faith so as to experience the Newness of live in our day-to-day lives and proclaim the same faith to others.



      *(Fr Francis Nganingmi A.S.)*

    body: |
      One of the most common identities given by the gospels for Jesus is “Healer”. There is no recorded instance of Jesus ever turning away anyone who sought a cure, either for oneself or for a loved one. His healings take a variety of forms. Sometimes they are instantaneous and as a result of a command, as it happens in today’s gospel, sometimes gradual. But in every instance, he wants the recipient to open themselves to the word of God by a transformed life. Countless people experience abundant blessings through a command uttered by Jesus. The response to this healing is expressed by the official who comes to believe in Jesus together with his whole household. This is the meaning of faith: accepting Jesus as Lord and Saviour, and living by his teachings.

      St Eulogius of Cordoba, *Martyr (c.818 - 859)*



      St Eulogius was the son of a noble senatorial family of Cordoba in southern Spain. An avid reader and student, he was much esteemed for his holy life, his great eloquence and for his profound humility.



      The Saracen rulers had tolerated the Christian religion during the first 130 years of their Spanish occupation, provided the faithful kept their heavy tax payments. But in 850 there began a period of persecution during which many were martyred for the faith. St Eulogius did much, by spoken and written word, to encourage the persecuted and imprisoned Christians. His three important works, viz., “Exhortation to Maryrdom”, which encouraged two Christian girls, Flora and Mary, threatened with slavery, “Memorial of the Saints” which relates the trials of the Martyrs, and “Apologia” which defended martyrs seeking death by proclaiming their faith, had a profound and widespread effect, keeping many from apostatizing.



      When the Archbishop of Toledo died in 858, St Eulogius was chosen successor, but he was arrested just when he was due for his consecration, for daring to defend a Saracen maiden, St Leocritia, who had abandoned Islam for the Catholic faith. In his great zeal, the holy Priest even endeavoured to convert his Arab judge by pointing out to him the errors of Mohammed’s doctrine, thus literally inviting death—he was beheaded on 11 March 859.
