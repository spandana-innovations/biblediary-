      Reflection: 1



      We must make a necessary choice between God and money. Greed for money or luxury is a false god, hat is, Mammon, which leads us to follow false values. God is our heavenly Father who takes care of all our needs; if we trust Him, we shall be free from worry. Divine Providence for us challenges us to care for our fellow humans. If we truly trust in God and depend on Him, our hearts will be free to seek God’s kingdom and His justice; and He will provide for all that we need. God who takes care of birds that do not sow or reap, and makes the lily of the field so beautiful, is much more interested in the welfare of His children who trust in Him. </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP</span></span>*Reflection: 2



      Today’s passage from the Sermon on the Mount instructs us to serve God alone as our Master and to avoid worries and anxiety by placing our trusting Faith in the providence and care of a loving God and by living one day at a time in God’s presence, doing His will and praying for and deriving strength from Him.



      Impossibility of serving two opposed masters: Man’s ultimate goal and Master is God and not material possessions. We cannot serve both at the same time. Material possessions should not replace God and become gods. They are given to us to be used as means to reach our ultimate goal, especially by sharing them with the needy.



      Jesus’ arguments against unnecessary worries:



      1) Unnecessary worries and anxiety cause spiritual, physical, and mental damages. a) Worries and anxiety cause the spiritual disease of sin when, like pagans and atheists, we do not trust in the goodness and providential care of a loving heavenly Father. b) Worries and anxieties cause physical diseases like hypertension, heart problems, respiratory diseases, insomnia, and rheumatic diseases. c) They also cause mental diseases like depression, phobias, obsessive-compulsive disorders and many others.



      2) In nature, other creatures (like birds), work hard for their daily food, but they do not worry about tomorrow’s food. 3) Worry is useless because we cannot increase even an inch of our height or a day of our lives by hours of worrying.



      Life Messages: How to avoid worry: 1) Trust in the providence of a loving God. 2) Acquire the art of living one day at a time without worrying over the dead past, the living present, or the unknown future. 3) Seek God’s kingdom by doing His will every day and live a righteous life obeying God’s law.

      Today’s gospel passage focuses on the theme, of ‘anxiety versus trust’. Anxiety is one of the most common sicknesses across the spectrum.     saintName: "St Aloysius Gonzaga (Confessor) (1568-1591)"
    ref: "St Aloysius Gonzaga (Confessor) (1568-1591)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Aloysius, the eldest son of the Marquis Ferdinand of Gonzaga, Duke of Mantua, Prince of the Roman Empire, was expected to become a great statesman and soldier. At the age of eight he was sent with his brother to spend two years at the court of Francesco de Medici in Florence (Italy). But deeply absorbed in matters spiritual he took a vow of perpetual chastity and, at age 12, received his First Communion from St Charles Borromeo, a relative, who was then Archbishop of Milan. At 17 years, he entered the Jesuit novitiate.



      “I am a twisted piece of iron, and have entered religion in order to get straightened out!” he insisted. Yet, his motto was: “I was born for greater things”.



      In his 22nd year he had a premonition of his approaching death and turned over his theological and spiritual notes to his rector as the only material things to which he was still attached. When a great famine broke out in Italy in 1590, followed by pestilence, the Jesuits opened an emergency hospital, and Aloysius solicited alms and tended the sick. But in March 1591 he himself took ill and succumbed three months later, aged barely 23, with St Robert Bellarmine spending the whole of the last night by his side. He was canonized in 1726, and in 1729 declared the special protector and patron of youth, especially of young Catholic students.
