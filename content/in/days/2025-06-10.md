      Reflection: 1



      Jesus asks us, his disciples, to function like salt and light in the world. His followers are to be visible to all on account of their committed lives; they will enlighten the world; they will help people to live healthy and holy lives – as salt preserves and purifies what is mixed with it. Like a city built on a hill, the Church must be seen as a community that lives and communicates around the values taught by Christ. A community that fails to act this way is like salt which has lost its taste; it is of no use. It is essential that the followers of Christ mix with others and influence them to be pure and holy, sharing with them the precious lessons of wisdom learned from Christ. Every Christian has a missionary role in life, as part of the Church. </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP</span></span>*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel passage is taken from the Sermon on the Mountwhere, using two simple metaphors, Jesus outlines the role of Christians in this world.



      The salt of the earth: In the time of Jesus, salt was connected in people’s minds with four special qualities.



      i) Salt was equated with purity because it was white and it came from the purest of all things, the sun and the sea. Hence, it was the common ingredient in the sacrifices offered to God by the Jews and pagans.



      ii) Salt was the commonest of all preservatives in the ancient world. It was used to prevent putrefaction of meat, fish and fruits in pickles.



      iii) Salt lends flavour to food items (Job 6:6). Salt was used to season food.



      iv) Salt was also used sprinkled on charcoal or dried horse dung to keep fire burning evenly in an oven for a longer time in Israel.



      The light of the world:



      i) light makes things visible in darkness.



      ii) A lamp or flashlight helps us to walk or work in darkness.



      iii) Light gives us warning, telling us to halt when there is danger ahead.



      iv) Light is a source for warmth and heat.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages: </span>



      1) As the salt of the earth, the Christian must be an example of purity, holding aloft the standard of absolute purity in speech, in conduct, and even in thought.



      2) As the salt of the earth, the Christian must have a certain antiseptic influence on life and society, defeating corruption, injustice and impurity and making it easier for others to be good.



      3) As salt, we have to preserve Christian faith, cultural values and moral principles. As salt, we have to improve the tone of society (“season” it), preserve Faith, and extend the fire of the Holy Spirit through evangelization efforts.



      4) As light of the world Christians are expected to reflect the Light borrowed from Christ and radiate that light in the form of love, kindness, mercy, forgiveness, and humble service.



      Thus, the task of Christians is to be the salt of society, preserving, reconciling, adding flavour, giving meaning where there is no meaning, and giving hope where there is no hope. Every Christian also has to reflect the light borrowed from Christ and radiate around him or her that light in the form of love, kindness, mercy, forgiveness, and humble service.

    title: Reflection
    ref: null

    body: |
      Salt and light are two commodities Jesus used to portray the image of how his followers should behave and live in the world. Salt is not useful to itself. Its usefulness comes to the fore when it is mixed with other objects. When joined to food, it gives taste, preserves and purifies. In the same way, the followers of Christ are called to exist for others, sharing with them the joy and beauty of Christian life, being an agent of purification from sin and evil, and preserving and protecting the teachings and values of Christ. In the same way, light is used to brighten places and help others to see objects. It is difficult to imagine a place without light, either natural or artificial. The followers of Christ help enlighten the hearts and minds of others with the wisdom and knowledge of Christ. At the same time, a lamp without oil and salt without saltiness is useless. In the same way, a Christian without the spirit of Christ is useless and worthless.

    saintName: "St Getulius and Companions (Martyrs) (c.120)"
    ref: "St Getulius and Companions (Martyrs) (c.120)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Getulius, the husband of St Symphorosa, had been an officer in the Roman army under Trajan and Hadrian. Upon his conversion to the Christian faith, he resigned his commission and withdrew to his estate in the Sabine Hills, near Tivoli (Italy).



      Here he lived in seclusion, surrounded only by a little band of Christians whom he taught and supported. He was one day engaged in instructing some of his people when he was surprised by a visit from Cerealis, the imperial vicar, who had been sent to arrest him. Incidentally, Cerealis himself was won over to the Faith as a result of conversations with Getulius and with his brother Amantius who, though an ardent believer, still remained a tribune in the Roman army.



      As soon as the Emperor learned of what had taken place, he ordered the Consul Licinius to apprehend the three men and to condemn them to death unless they consented to abjure their faith. They were brought before the Consul who ordered them to renounce their faith. But they all made a bold confession, whereupon they were imprisoned for 27 days at Tivoli and subjected to diverse forms of torture—to no avail. They were finally beheaded or burnt at the stake on the Via Salaria. With them perished another Christian, named Primitivus. The relics of these Saints were buried by St Symphorosa in an arenarium upon her estate.
