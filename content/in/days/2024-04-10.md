    ref: "John 3:16–21"

    body: |
      Jesus explained God’s plan of salvation to Nicodemus by declaring that the story of Moses and the brazen serpent was a sign pointing to the Good News that God would show His love for mankind by subjecting His own Son to suffering and death in order to save them all: *“For God so loved the world that He gave His only Son, so that everyone who believes in him might not perish but might have eternal life” (Jn 3:16). *This is the summary of the Gospel message of salvation through Christ Jesus. This is the Good News in the Gospels.</span></span></span>



      Today’s Gospel passage teaches us that our salvation is the free gift of a merciful God, given to us through Jesus, His Son. It explains that Jesus, the Incarnate Son of God, became the agent of God’s salvation, not just for one sinful nation, but for the sins of the whole world from the beginning through the end. Through *Jn 3:16*, the Gospel teaches us that God has expressed His love, mercy, and compassion for us by giving His only Son for our Salvation. This tells us that the initiative in all Salvation is God’s love for man. St. Augustine of Hippo describes a dream message received by his mother, Monica, who prayed and wept unceasingly, fearing Augustine would be damned because of the life he was leading. This message convinced her that she had to live with him, not cut him off as she had been doing, for God still loved him even in his present condition. Augustine’s example also explains to us the universality of the love of God. God’s motive is Love and God’s objective is Salvation. Those who actually receive eternal life must believe in the Son and express that love in deeds.</span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">*Life message:*</span></span></span></span>



       We need to respond to God’s love for us by loving and serving Him in others in whom He dwells. God’s love for us is unconditional, universal, forgiving, and merciful. Let us make an earnest attempt to include these qualities in sharing our love with others during this Easter season. “In the evening of life you will be examined on love,” said St. John of the Cross</span></span></span>

      No human being can live happily and grow normally without having the experience of being loved by significant others in one’s life. God has been communicating His unconditional love for us, humans, ever since the human race came into existence on this planet. As Christians we believe that the love of God is fully manifested in the person of Jesus, through his incarnation, his words, deeds and especially through his death on the cross and his resurrection. The depth of God’s love for the world and for each human person is clearly mentioned in today’s Gospel when it says, ‘God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son....’ Let us consciously accept this love of God into our hearts today and give an adequate response of love in return.</span></span></span></span>

      Saint Fulbert</span></span> - *Bishop Confessor (c 960-1029)*</span></span></span></span>



      Born in the Picardy region of France, Saint Fulbert studied at the Benedictine abbey at Reims where he had as his instructor the famous Gerbert of Aurillac, who in 999 was to become Pope Sylvester II. Fulbert was the founder of the cathedral school at Chartres, which before long became the most renowned institution of its kind in France, attracting as he did students even from Germany, Italy and England. In appreciation of his wisdom and learning, his pupils styled him the “Venerable Socrates”.</span></span></span></span>



      For the last 22 years of his life he was Bishop of Chartres and as such laboured at the reform of the clergy and against the practice of assigning church benefices to laymen; he did not hesitate to even rebuke some of his fellow Bishops for their worldly-minded, military adventures. A personal friend of Saint Odilo of Cluny, of Blessed Richard of Saint Vannes, and of Saint Abbo of Fleury, he wholeheartedly endorsed their great monastic reforms.</span></span></span></span>



      Saint Fulbert is also hailed as a poet. He composed a number of hymns to Our Lady, for whom he had a special devotion, and introduced the feast of her Nativity in his diocese. He died on 10 April 1029 and was buried in the monastery church of Saint Pere-de-Chartres.</span></span></span></span>
