      *Reflection: 1*



      Jesus is going to the Father and will send the Holy Spirit, the advocate, to his disciples; he is not leaving them unaided. Jesus teaches that among the three Persons in God there is not only interaction but also harmonious action. When the Holy Spirit comes, He will show the world its sin, the sin of not believing in Jesus, the sin of opposing righteousness by judging an condemning Jesus, the Holy One of God. The world stands convicted because by his Death and Resurrection Jesus has defeated Satan forever. The Holy Spirit disturbs and challenges our conscience if we sin, making us aware of the danger of sin. He convinces us that to be made righteous by God we must believe in Jesus, the One He has sent, that in him alone is salvation. He convinces us about the judgement to come, that God is going to judge everyone according to justice. </span></span></span></span>

      *Reflection: 2*



      In today’s Gospel, Jesus tries to console his sad, disheartened disciples at the Last Supper, for they are at a loss, hearing the news of their master’s imminent departure. So, he assures them that they will not be left alone. He will send the Holy Spirit upon them as a friend, guide, consoler, and teacher. Then Jesus explains the three different roles of the Holy Spirit in their lives.



      First, He will convince the world about the seriousness of sin. Thus, the Holy Spirit will lead us to repent of our sins and seek forgiveness from Jesus. The Divine Advocate will demonstrate that not believing in Jesus is the real sin. It is the Holy Spirit Who would prick the hearts of the Jews on the day of Pentecost, convicting them of their sin of crucifying their Messiah. In the same way, He convicts us of wrongdoing and convinces us of God's truth.



      Second, He will accuse them of unrighteousness because He will show that Jesus was the Just One who was never guilty of sin (cf. John 8:46; Hebrews 4:15) and therefore is in glory beside His Father. It also means that the Spirit convinces us of the righteousness of Christ, which means that that Jesus was right in his teachings and promises, as proved by God His Father Who granted him Resurrection and Ascension into Heaven. Although Jesus was condemned to death, it was actually Satan, the ruler of this world, who was condemned through Jesus’ death. (Navarre Bible Commentary).



      Third, the Holy Spirit gives us the inner and unshakable conviction that we shall all stand before the judgment seat of God. When we heed God’s judgments, we find true peace, joy, and reconciliation with God.



      Life message: We need to allow the Holy Spirit to do what He wishes in and through our lives so that He may release us from the grip of sin and set us ablaze with the fire of God’s love.

      The Gospel passage focuses on three aspects of the workings of the Holy Spirit. First, the Holy Spirit is in the business of pricking our consciences whenever we sin, and he encourages us to repent. Our repentance is responding to the merciful promptings of the Holy Spirit. Second, the Holy Spirit convinces us that to be made righteous by God the Father, we have to believe in Jesus, whose precious blood cleanses us of our sins. Thirdly, the Holy Spirit, in exposing to us what sin is and by convincing us about righteousness, makes clear to us that there will be judgment. A life attuned to the Spirit would be conscious of the dangers of sin, of the salvific acts that won for us, and the certainty of judgment.

    body: |
      A Roman by birth, Augustine and 40 other Benedictine monks were deputed by Gregory the Great in 596 to evangelize England. Disturbed by rumours of the brutality of the locals, the band attempted to return mid-way. However, admonished by the Pope, himself a Benedictine, they persisted. Landing in the spring of 597, with interpreters from France, they were received hospitably by King Ethelbert of Kent and invited to live at Canterbury. Entering in solemn procession, bearing an image of Our Lord and a Cross, and chanting an ancient litany, they immediately established their Benedictine routine together with the preaching mission. The King, who had married the Frankish Princess Bertha, a Catholic, was deeply impressed by Augustine’s spiritual message and personal sanctity, and asked for baptism at Pentecost. More than 10,000 of his subjects were baptized the day following Christmas.



      The following autumn, Augustine was consecrated bishop by St Virgilius, Archbishop of Arles, and was made Archbishop of Canterbury with jurisdiction over all the southern British dioceses which were being established. He failed, however, in enlisting the support of the Celtic Bishops of Wales in the evangelization of the Anglo-Saxon conquerors and getting them to conform to Roman practice in the matter of the Easter date.



      About seven years after his arrival in England, St Augustine passed to his reward in 605, leaving behind a rich legacy of no less than 12 new dioceses.
