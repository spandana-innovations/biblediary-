      Reflection: 1



      Here we see a clear instance of prejudice against Jesus. The people of his home town thought that they knew his parents and the family to which he belonged. They pretended to know all about him, and were not open to appreciate his extraordinary wisdom, knowledge and power to work miracles, and so took offence at him. Their minds were closed to the great things they had heard from him and seen him doing. This is what happens to us when we close our minds to the truth. But the people of other towns accepted Jesus’ teaching and honoured him. Are we ready to recognize the real worth and dignity of people we know and who live among us?



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2Lack of faith prevents us from seeing miracles1) Who is this? People around you always seek to categorize you; it’s like you’re already labeled from the beginning. The same thing happened to Jesus, they labeled him. It seems that where you come from, where you live, and your social status matters a lot… even more than whether or not you live in the Truth. This leads us to become a sick society, in which a rich person’s lie is accepted over the truth of a homeless person. Don’t give up, live and announce the truth. I understand you can get discouraged when you feel mistreated and see unfair situations around you… but – how much you have in your wallet or where you come from should not condition the mission you have been given by Jesus. You have come into this world for that purpose. Let me tell you a secret: people will always have an opinion about you, or something to say… therefore continue working for Christ. Don’t let their comments affect you. Keep on sowing and we will enjoy the harvest together.



      2) Stumbling block. If you live in the truth, you will obviously be a stumbling block for many. If you outshine others at what you do, many will seek to set you aside. Unfortunately, there is envy in our society and also within the church, like in any institution. But never stop looking at Jesus Christ the crucified, because that’s where your hope rests. Look to Jesus and the promise that you’re going to be resurrected. Fight for your life and what you are; the key is your conviction. Are you convinced about what you believe in? Are you sure of the reason why you are working for Christ? If so, just continue to work.



      3) Contempt. On many occasions you will not receive a hug or encouragement from the people around you, but remember that the same thing happened to Jesus. When everything goes well you will be applauded by many people who are close to you, they will even cheer you on! But, when things start to go wrong, you’re in a tough situation, or you ‘re out of money, you might even be blocked from their WhatsApp. Remember, our life implies walking forward constantly and struggling on because Heaven is our goal!



      (*Fr. Luis A. Zazano* - digitalmissioners.com)

    title: Saint of the Day
    saintName: "Saint Alphonsus De Liguori Bishop Confessor, Doctor of the Church (1696 - 1787)"
    ref: "Saint Alphonsus De Liguori Bishop Confessor, Doctor of the Church (1696 - 1787)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      As a boy, Alphonsus, the eldest of a family of seven children, annually made a retreat with his father. He obtained a doctorate in law at 16 but gave up his highly successful practice at 27, seeking God’s will in prayer and works of charity. Ordained priest in 1726, he founded the Redemptorists [Congregation of the Most Holy Redeemer] in 1732 and travelled about Naples (Italy), gaining numerous conversions through his “Mission” apostolate, emphasizing that “the most perfect charity consists in being zealous about our neighbour’s spiritual good.”



      After 1752 he devoted himself so zealously and effectively to writing as to become known as the “Prince of Moral Theologians”, his remarkable devotion to Our Lady and the Blessed Sacrament spurring him to compose such significant dogmatic and ascetical works as *The Glories of Mary*, *The Way of Salvation* and *The True Spouse of Christ* to name but a few. Alphonsus particularly favoured short, affective petitions and acts of love, emphasizing that “he who prays is saved; he who does not pray is damned.”



      Weakened by severe austerities Alphonsus fell victim to a severe attack of gout and rheumatic fever which left him partially paralysed to the day of his death. It was only at 79, however, did the Pope agree to relieve him of his episcopal duties.



      The first and only professional “Moral Theologian” to have been canonized [1839], Saint Alphonsus, the patron of Confessors and of Moral theologians, was declared Doctor of the Church in 1871.
