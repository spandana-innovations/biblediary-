      <span class="subhead">The context:</span> The *Magnificat* and Hannah’s song (1400 B.C.) are properly mentioned together, because the former is literarily and thematically dependent on the latter. Mary as a young Jewish girl knew Hannah’s song as it was sung on every Jewish New Year Day in the Temple and the synagogues. Both Hannah and Mary are mothers rejoicing at the birth of an unexpected child. Hannah praises God that He has seen fit to end the curse of her barrenness, while Mary glorifies the Lord because He has chosen her to bear the promised Messiah. Each knew, to her sorrow, that she would have to give up her son one day. Just as Hannah dedicated her child Samuel to the Lord, so Mary offered her son Jesus for our salvation. On hearing Elizabeth’s greetings, Mary sang, praising and thanking God for the great things He had done for her. He had filled her with graces, overshadowed her with His Holy Spirit and made her the mother of His Son Jesus. Mary praised God also for the mercy He had worked by humbling the proud, by ousting the mighty from their thrones, and by exalting the lowly and filling the hungry with good things, a social, political, and economic revolution.



      <span class="subhead">Life messages:</span> 1) We need to sing songs of praise and gratitude to God as Mary did because of the great gift of life God has given us through our parents and for the gift of early training we have received from them in a Christian home.2) Let us also glorify God every day through our works of charity for the gift of our particular vocation in life, and for the opportunities God gives us every day for doing good to others.

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      Once again, Mary stands at the centre of today’s Gospel reading. The Magnificat intoned by Mary is a hymn of praise in which she narrates God’s great and generous deeds and His boundless mercy. This hymn has become the Church’s evening prayer. According to this hymn, the little ones will be big, the poor will be rich, and the outcasts will become residents of God’s house. On the other side, the proud-hearted, the powerful, and the rich are brought down (cf. Jas 1:9-10). This new view of things and the new values contained in this hymn have entered this world through Jesus Christ. Let us learn to look at God and reality from this changed perspective: God is with the marginalised. Where do we stand? Do we know ourselves as people who are dependent on God’s mercy and kindness, or do we know ourselves as self-sufficient people who need God only for cosmetic purposes?

    ref: "Saint Frances Xavier Cabrini - Virgin (1850 - 1917)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Born on 15 July 1850, Frances was the thirteenth child of Augustine and Stella Cabrini, well-to-do Italian peasants. Graduating brilliantly at 18, she devoted herself to nursing the sick poor in her home town. At 24 she was asked by the Vicar of Codogno, Don Antonio Serrati to take charge of a private orphanage named House of Providence, the institute from which emerged her own new congregation, the Missionary Sisters of the Sacred Heart, at the promptings of her Bishop, Domenico Gelmini. As a result of her organizing and administrative skills, her tact and her ability to read character, her congregation grew prodigiously.



      Mother Cabrini was 39 when she set her sights on the Chinese missions, but, in obedience to Pope Leo XIII, proceeded to the USA where the huge number of poor Italian immigrants, largely illiterate and exploited outright by unscrupulous agents, some even losing their faith due to Protestant influences, became her special concern. As she went about opening orphanages, oratories and day schools in the face of every kind of difficulty, Cabrini, a woman of fine sensibilities, true humility and great holiness, taught her Sisters that the welfare of the poor did not depend upon them, but rather on God working through them - “Pray! Pray always, and ask without ceasing for the spirit of prayer:…..Have faith and you will behold miracles!” Repeatedly, she miraculously multiplied dwindling food supplies or cash reserves.



      Mother Cabrini died suddenly on 22 December 1917, aged just 67. She was canonized by Pope Pius XII on 7 July 1946, the first American to be so honoured by the Church.
