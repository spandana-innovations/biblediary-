  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Luke 21: 20-28."
    audio: null
    body: |
      Using biblical and apocalyptic images Jesus foretells the brutal attack of the Roman army on the city of Jerusalem forty years later, killing most of its residents who resisted, and demolishing the Temple. Jesus combines this prophecy of the destruction of Jerusalem with events preceding the end of the world because most of the Jews believed that if the Temple were destroyed their world would end. In his prophecy Jesus identifies the faithlessness of the chosen people and their moral corruption as the main causes the destruction is being permitted by God. That is why Jesus calls these days as *the “time of punishment”* and “days of retribution.” Jewish prophets Isaiah (63:4), Jeremiah (5:29), Hosiah (9:7) and Daniel (9:27) had already given God’s prophetic warnings about the future destruction of Jerusalem and its residents. The Temple was desecrated by the Greek Antiochus IV Epiphanes from 167 to 165 BC. The “horrible abomination” perhaps refers to an inscription placed on the portal of the temple dedicating it to the Roman god Olympian Zeus. Jesus warns that these desecrations will be repeated by the Romans. Many will be murdered, and other, healthy residents will be led away into captivity to Rome and other pagan territories. The holy city itself, its Temple in ruins, will be trampled on by the Gentiles. Then Jesus speaks of various cataclysmic and apocalyptic signs to signal the end of the world using the Hebrew Biblical images. They conclude with Daniel’s vision of the *“Son of Man”* riding on a cloud coming with great power and glory. But Jesus gives assurance to his loyal followers that it is a time for them to *“stand up straight and raise your heads, for your redeeming is near at hand”.*



      <span class="subhead">Life messages</span>: 1) Sufferings and tribulations are part and parcel of Christian life. They should help us reflect on the end of our lives and the final end of our world, and so urge us to live by the vision and values of the Gospel, sharing *agape* love with others by rendering them humble, loving, sacrificial service.

      Today’s Gospel encourages us: “When these things begin to take place, look up and raise your heads, because your redemption is drawing near.” Many events unfold around us, often interpreted differently by various voices. As believers, how do we view them? Some arise from human anger or folly, while others reflect nature’s response to our indifference. These are reminders to turn to God in prayer and take meaningful actions to address what we can. By doing so, we prepare for the coming of the Son of Man. The first reading assures us that God’s judgement is true and just – bringing both reward and punishment. Are we ready to face this just judge? Let us start now, conforming our lives to His will and using today’s challenges as opportunities to grow in faith and love.

    ref: "Blessed James Alberione, Priest, Founder (1884-1971)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      Having finished high school at sixteen, frail James, fourth son of a devout peasant family, joined the seminary of Alba. On the midnight of 31 December 1900, the night that divided the two centuries, as he prayed, long and fervently, before the Blessed Sacrament in the Cathedral of Alba, a special light came from the monstrance and “he felt deeply obliged to prepare himself to do something for the Lord and for the people of the new century.” That “something” translated itself into the apostolate of the “good press” as he termed it, at a time when the Church looked upon the media hostilely in matters associated with the Gospel.



      Ordained priest in 1907, the Lord willed and guided James to use the media of social communications to spread the Gospel in the spirit of Paul the Apostle. In 1914, he laid the foundation of the *Society of St Paul* by opening a press training school in Alba. In 1915, he founded the *Daughters of St Paul* with the same mission. A man of vision, he started the Union of Lay Pauline Co-operators in 1917, giving lay involvement in the Pauline apostolate of the media due importance. Reading the signs of the times, he went on to found seven other Institutes, the ten collectively making up what is called the Pauline Family, the ideal of personal holiness and the mission of giving to the world ‘Jesus Master, the Way, the Truth and the Life’ being its source of unity and animation.



      He was beatified in 2003.
