    ref: "Luke 24:35–48"

    body: |
      Today’s Gospel is the special message of Jesus to discouraged Christians. Are you fearful, doubtful, and depressed? Do you wonder what your daily devotion and faithfulness is worth? Do you keep asking yourself why the good *guys*finish last? Then what you should do today and many days is, sneak a look at the back of the book.</span></span></span></span>



      What Jesus means is, He took on all your problems and more: the problems of the rest of His people, too. He took on all the burdens of our sins, brought them up to the cross with Him, shed all His blood and died to free us of these burdens.</span></span></span></span>



      Here Jesus stands, gloriously risen, shocking even His closest friends and disciples, so that they think He must be a ghost. But Jesus dispels that timid notion. “Why do such ideas cross your mind?” He asks. “Touch me, and see that a ghost does not have flesh and bones as I do.” Then He let them feel the glorified wounds in His hands and his feet.</span></span></span></span>



      When they still seemed unable to believe this great news, He sat down and ate lunch with them.</span></span></span></span>



      In His very death, Jesus had conquered. He defeated sin, self, and Satan, and so He rose victorious. Now He says to you and me, “All right, I have conquered the world. Now it’s your turn!”</span></span></span></span>



      (Bernard C. Mischke PSC & Fritz Mishcke OSC, *Pray Today’s Gospel,* ST PAULS, Mumbai, India)</span></span></span></span>

      In today’s Gospel we meet the risen Jesus appearing to his disciples and saying to them, ‘Peace to you!’ They have not yet come to full belief that Jesus has been raised from the dead. So Jesus demonstrates to them that he is not a spirit by eating a piece of broiled fish. He gives them the conviction that all that has happened to him is in accordance with what is written about him in the Scriptures. He is preparing them to be witnesses as is evident from Peter bearing witness in today’s First Reading. Do I have a strong faith in the risen Jesus whereby I am ready to be his witness like Peter and others, even to the extent of giving up my life?</span></span></span></span>

      Saint Isidore</span></span> - *Bishop Confessor, Doctor of the Church (c 560-636)*</span></span></span></span>



      Hailing from a noble Roman family in Cartagena, Spain, Saint Isidore was the younger brother of three other saints, viz., Leander, Archbishop of Seville, Fulgentius, Bishop of Eeija, and Florentina, the nun who is said to have governed 1,000 religious in 40 convents. </span></span></span></span>



      In 599 Isidore succeeded his own brother at Seville. He so governed the great See for 37 years as to strengthen </span></span>Church discipline and encourage monasticism and, in time, even converting to the Faith from Arianism the Visigoths, ruling Spain for almost 200 years.</span></span></span></span>



      Among his synods, the fourth national council at Toledo in 633 was particularly important in that it obliged each Bishop to establish a seminary in his respective diocese and to encourage the study of Greek, Hebrew, law and medicine. An amazingly learned man, considered the last of the ancient Christian philosophers and great Latin Fathers, Isidore compiled a Spanish missal and breviary and so is credited with being the author of the beautiful Mozarabic Liturgy. </span></span></span></span>



      This “School-master of the Middle Ages” who had mastered every branch of science, the best known among his versatile writings being his *Etymologiae*, a systematic anthology of knowledge which became the most popular text book for instruction in theology, asceticism, history, geography, astronomy, grammar, biography etc in the Middle Ages, died in 636, was canonized in 1598 and declared Doctor of the Church in 1722.</span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Reflection:</span></span></span> *“The suffering of adversity does not degrade you but exalts you. Human tribulation teaches you; it does not destroy you. The more we are afflicted in this world, the greater is our assurance for the next. The more we sorrow in the present, the greater will be our joy in the future”*(Saint Isidore of Seville).</span></span></span></span>
