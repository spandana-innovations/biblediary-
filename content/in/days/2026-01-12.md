    body: |
      Reflection: 1



      Jesus called his first disciples, Simon and his brother Andrew, and James and John his brother. All the four were fishermen and were at work. As soon as Jesus called them, they left their work and belongings, giving up their accustomed life and followed him. Discipleship under Jesus demands detachment from familiar work, family relations and possessions. We are called to sacrifice what is dear to us and follow Jesus. The four fishermen whom Jesus called took their new vocation in all seriousness. As disciples of Jesus, we are also called to change our lives, make the sacrifices he demands and follow him faithfully. Without denying ourselves and taking up our crosses, we cannot follow him. </span></span></span></span>



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      Today’s Gospel describes the beginning of Jesus’ preaching and healing ministry and the call of the apostles who were to continue that ministry. Jesus started public ministry immediately after John the Baptist was arrested. Following John’s pattern, Jesus, too, invited the listeners to repent as a preparation for believing in the Gospel, or the Good News, of the Kingdom of God. *Repentance* means an about-face turn to God resulting in a change of mind, heart, behaviour, and life. It also means sorrow for having refused God’s love and a resolution to make amends. *Believing* in the Gospel demands from the hearers a resolution to take Jesus’ words seriously, to translate them into action, and to put trust in Jesus’ authority. Jesus preached the*Gospel,*or Good News, that God is a loving, forgiving, caring, and merciful Father Who wants to liberate us and save us from our sins through His Son, Jesus. According to Mark, Jesus selected four fishermen, Andrew and his brother Simon (later named Peter by Jesus), with James and his brother John, right from their fishing boats. Jesus wanted these ordinary, hard-working people as assistants in ministry because they would be very responsive instruments in the hands of God.</span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life messages</span>: 1) In order to be effective instruments in the hands of God and to continue Jesus’ preaching, healing and saving ministry, we, too, need to repent of our sins on a daily basis and to renew our lives by cooperating with God’s grace, relying on the power of God. </span></span></span>

      The Gospel reading describes the beginning of the public ministry of Jesus. He announces the nearness of the kingdom of God and calls upon people to repent and believe. In order to carry out the kingdom mission and collaborate with Him, He then calls the first disciples – Peter and Andrew, James and John – to follow Him. Discipleship is a radical call to follow Jesus for the sake of the kingdom of God. Following Him entails leaving behind one’s past life and work, detachment from family and wealth, and embracing a simple lifestyle. As we listen to the invitation of Jesus at the beginning of a new week, may we also respond generously and wholeheartedly to His call like the four disciples, so as to collaborate with the work of God. </span></span>

    saintName: "Saint Benedict Biscop - Abbot Confessor (c. 628-690)"
    ref: "Saint Benedict Biscop - Abbot Confessor (c. 628-690)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      An Anglo-Saxon nobleman and a native of Northumbria (England), Benedict, also called Benet Biscop or Biscop Baducing, spent his youth in the service of King Osway. In 653, on his return from a pilgrimage to Rome with Saint Wilfrid, he introduced the Roman rite substituting the Celtic rites observed by the Irish missionaries in Northern England. During a second journey to Rome, twelve years later, Benedict Biscop himself became a Benedictine monk and spent three years at Lerins (South France), until he and Saint Adrian were appointed by the Pope to conduct the new Archbishop of Canterbury, Saint Theodore of Tarsus, to his See.</span></span></span></span>



      Benedict was, soon after, made Abbot of Saint Peter’s monastery in Canterbury. In 675 he founded the great monastic establishment at Wearmouth on land given by the King of Northumbria, and seven years later at Saint Paul’s at Jarrow. He is known to have brought back a large collection of books and art treasures from Rome for these monasteries besides persuading the head cantor of Saint Peter’s, Rome to come to Britain and teach his monks the Roman chant. </span></span></span></span>



      The first English Prelate to build a church of stone, hiring French masons, he also introduced into Britain the art of making window glass. After three years of suffering in bed, Benedict died at Wearmouth Abbey on 12 January 690. However, 200 years on, his monasteries continued to flourish as centres of religion and culture.</span></span></span></span>
