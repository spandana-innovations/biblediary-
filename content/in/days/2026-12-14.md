  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "Mathew 21: 23-27"
    audio: null
    body: |
      After casting out the animal-merchants and money-changers from the Temple immediately after the Palm Sunday procession, Jesus started teaching in the Temple courts. Hence, the chief priests and elders of the people approached Jesus, questioning his authority to enter the city in a triumphal procession, allowing the children to acclaim him, curing the sick, casting out merchants and moneychangers, and teaching in the Temple area. It was a trap. If Jesus claimed Divine authority, as the Messiah, they would bring a charge of blasphemy. Jesus could not claim only human authority without denying His Very Being — both Son of God and Son of Man. But even if He could, His arrest as a mad zealot would give scandal, another sin, and would damage the simple Faith of the people in the Temple and what it stood for, destroying Jesus’ whole Messianic Mission. So Jesus refused to answer, unless they would first answer His challenging counter-question about John the Baptist and his message – was this from God or man? Was this Divine or human? If they answered Divine, the questioners would be asked to explain why they had not accepted John’s message and his witness-bearing that Jesus was the Messiah. If they answered human, they would have to face the anger of the crowd who had accepted John as a prophet. Hence, they kept silent, opting for a shameful self-humiliation.</span></span></span></span>



      <span class="subhead">Life message:</span></span></span> 1) In religious matters we should not ask whether our stand is safe, politically correct, or useful. Instead, believing and knowing that what God reveals and Jesus teaches is Truth, we need both to live out and to stand for this Truth drawing on the courage of our Christian convictions even if it our support should cost us our earthly life. </span></span></span></span>

    title: Reflection
    ref: null

    body: |
      Through their question about the source of Jesus’ authority with which He drove out the business people from the Temple premises, the chief priests and elders are actually accusing Jesus of having no authority at all because He was not part of any authoritative structure, whether political or religious. The counter-question of Jesus concerning the baptism of John the Baptist is not meant just to trap them, but it actually contains the answer to the question raised by them. Jesus was declared to be the Son of God when He received His baptism from John the Baptist. Thus, Jesus’ authority came from the heavenly Father at His baptism. It is said about Saint John of the Cross, the great Carmelite saint whose memory we celebrate today, that he found power and strength in the powerlessness and the weakness of the cross. Where do we draw the strength for our life from? From the Father? From the Cross of Christ?</span></span></span>

    audio: null
    body: |
      John was born premature in 1542. Given the kind of upbringing fate provided, he obtained his education at a paupers’ school. After that he worked for seven years in a hospital, simultaneously frequenting a Jesuit school at Medina. At 21 he became a Carmelite and strictly followed the ancient unmitigated rule of the Order in all its austerity and was ordained a priest four years later.</span></span></span>



      At the persuasion of Saint Teresa of Avila he did for the Carmelite Order of men what she was doing for the nuns. Thus the first reformed house for Discalced Friars was established at Duruelo on 28 November 1568, with the first three friars took their vows in “surpassing poverty, austerity and fervour”. Other foundations followed in rapid succession, and for five years John of the Cross, as he now called himself, was confessor to Saint Teresa and her nuns at Avila. The reform caused much internal strife and passionate disagreements which led to his imprisonment for nine months and mistreated and slandered until his miraculous escape. But it was precisely during his incarceration that he had his greatest spiritual illuminations.</span></span></span>



      John died on 14 December 1591 and his incorrupt body was transferred to Segovia in 1592. Beatified in 1675, canonized in 1726 John of the Cross is known as the supreme “Mystic of the mystics”, the Church’s Doctor (since 1926) of mystical theology for his masterly exposition of Catholic mysticism in his “Ascent of Mount Carmel”, “The Dark Night of the Soul”, “The Spiritual Canticle”, and “The Living Flame of Love”. </span></span></span>
