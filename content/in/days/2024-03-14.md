  - key: homily
    title: Homily Tip
    ref: "John 5:31–47"
    audio: null
    body: |
      In today’s Gospel passage, Jesus defends His Messianic claims. The Jews demanded proofs for Jesus’ Messianic claims, quoting Dt 19:15 which requires two or three witnesses to substantiate a person’s claims. Here, Jesus presents four witnesses who approved His Messianic and Divine claims*:1)*John the Baptist, 2) his own miracles, 3) his Heavenly Father, and 4) the Hebrew Scriptures (our Old Testament).1) John the Baptist, whom many Jews considered a prophet, bore witness to Jesus as the “Lamb of God” and the Holy One whose paths he had come to prepare. 2) The miracles Jesus worked could only have been done by the power of God his Father. 3) God the Father attested to the Divinity of Jesus at Jesus’ Baptism (cfr. Jn 1:31-34); at the Transfiguration (cfr. Mt 17:1-8), and later, in the presence of the whole crowd (cfr. Jn 12:28-30). 4) The Hebrew Scriptures, especially, the Law and the Prophets, [Toa & Nebim] bear testimony to Jesus’ Divine and Messianic claims. It was the Spirit of God Who prompted the prophets of the Old Testament to record their Messianic prophecies. Then Jesus identifies four obstacles which prevented the Jews from recognizing him as the Messiah and Son of God: 1) their lack of love of God, 2) their striving after human glory, 3) their prejudiced interpretation of Sacred texts, and 4) their lack of Faith in Moses and the prophets.</span></span></span>






      <span class="subhead">*Life message:*</span>When we have doubts about Faith and the Church’s teachings we need to: </span></span></span>



      1) Read the Bible with trusting Faith; </span></span></span>



      2) Pray for an increase of Faith; </span></span></span>



      3) Learn the teachings of the Church, starting with the *Catechism of the Catholic Church* and the official documents of the Church; </span></span></span>



      4) Accept the mysteries of our Faith, relying on the Divine Authority and veracity of Jesus; and </span></span></span>



      5) Examine how strong our own Christian testimony is. Does our life reflect the light of Christ so much that it brings light to the darkness in others? </span></span></span>

  - key: reflection
    title: Reflection
    ref: null
    audio: null
    body: |
      The Scriptures lead us to God. They make God’s will known to us and provide us with possibilities for an appropriate response. The prophets of old had been preparing the people for the arrival of the Messiah. And yet they refused to recognize him when at last he did come. Today’s Gospel reveals how shut people can be to God’s saving action. Jesus appeals to the Jewish system of justice which accepts the testimony of two witnesses. Jesus, in fact, lists four witnesses – John the Baptist, the Scriptures, God Himself and God’s own works – to testify that God had sent him. The knowledge that the Jews had of the Scriptures simply failed to enable them to accept Jesus. How much of our knowledge of the Bible brings us close to Jesus today?</span></span></span></span>

      St Matilda, *Queen (895 - 968)*</span></span></span></span>



      Daughter of Count Dietrich of Westphalia and Reinhild of Denmark, St Matilda was brought up in the Benedictine convent at Herford (central Germany), where her grandmother was Abbess. Here she became known for her rare talents and virtues. At 14 she married Henry the Fowler of Saxony who later was chosen to succeed Conrad as King of Germany. As Queen she proved herself a model of piety and prudence, and was ever intent on succouring the oppressed and the poor. </span></span></span></span>



      Left a widow in 936, Matilda redoubled her prayers, mortifications and almsgiving, and founded numerous hospitals, churches and Benedictine abbeys. But this liberality so exasperated two of her sons, Otto and Henry, that they banished her from court and deprived her of all her property until misfortune overtook them in turn and they became reconciled with her and begged for forgiveness.</span></span></span></span>



      Of her children, the eldest son became Otto the Great, Emperor of Germany; another son was St Bruno, Archbishop of Cologne; a daughter Gerberga married Louis IV of France, and Hedwig became the mother of Hugh Capet, the founder of the Capetian dynasty.</span></span></span></span>



      She died on 14 March 968 at the monastery of Quedlinburg and was buried there with Henry.</span></span></span></span>
