      Reflection: 1



      To encourage the disciples ahead of the troubles they will have to face, Jesus tells them that he has overcome the world. The time is at hand when they will be scattered and they will leave him alone. But Jesus knows for sure that he is not alone; the Father is with him. Therefore, Jesus is in perfect peace, and is committed to the mission the Father has given him for the salvation of the world. He shares with his disciples his peace and his mission. Whatever be the sufferings and persecutions they have to face, they are never left alone, and therefore they can take heart.



      *Fr (Dr) George Kaitholil, SSP*Reflection: 2



      In this Last Supper discourse, Jesus explains his Divine origin and his relationship to God his Father in clear terms without using any metaphors. The apostles acknowledge that they understand the Divinity of Jesus. But Jesus prophesies that they will soon desert him and seek their own safety, while he will be arrested, brought to trial and crucified. Our Faith is tested every day when we live in a world filled with agnostics, atheists, and pleasure-seekers who see true believers as superstitious people and hate them. That is why Jesus gave his apostles and all his future disciples the assurance of the anointing of the Holy Spirit Who would strengthen them and guide them.



      The Second Vatican Council teaches in connection with this passage: “The Lord Jesus who said *`Be of good cheer, I have overcome the world’* (John 16:33), did not by these words promise complete victory to his Church in this world. This sacred Council rejoices that the earth which has been sown with the seed of the Gospel is now bringing forth fruit in many places under the guidance of the Spirit of the Lord, who is filling the world” (*Presbyterorum Ordinis*, 22).



      <span class="subhead">Life messages: </span>



      1) Our Faith is firmly rooted in the Divinity of Jesus demonstrated by his Messianic miracles, most of which were foretold by the prophets. The Resurrection of Jesus is the Miracle of miracles proving Jesus’ Divinity beyond doubt.



      2) We need to get our daily infusion of spiritual strength by recognizing the presence of God – the Father, the Son, Jesus, and the Holy Spirit — living within us wherever we are, and by communicating with our indwelling God in prayer.

      Jesus speaks of a time when he would be left alone, being abandoned by his own closest disciples whom he had chosen, trained and befriended for three years. Yet Jesus is not overcome by fear and anxiety nor does he backtrack from fulfilling his mission. He has just one goal in life: to carry out the will of his Father for the salvation of the world, as he is convinced of the Father’s filial love for him. He is sure of this as he says, “Yet I am not alone, for the Father is with me.” Jesus’ conviction of the Father’s loving presence and his total dedication to his mission are inspiration and motivation for us to be courageous in the midst of misunderstanding, loneliness, persecution and rejection. His words, “Take heart, I have overcome the world,” should ring in our ears and motivate us to be his courageous disciples in today’s world of darkness and chaos.

    saintName: "Sts Marcellinus and Peter (Martyrs) (A.D. - 304)"
    ref: "Sts Marcellinus and Peter (Martyrs) (A.D. - 304)"
    audio: null
    body: |
      St Marcellinus was a Priest during the reign of Diocletian in Rome and St Peter an exorcist, i.e., someone authorized by the Church to deal with cases of demonic possession. They were said to have been condemned and cast into prison for their faith. In prison the two strengthened the faith of those of their fellow-prisoners who were Christian, and succeeded in making new converts, too, among whom were the jailer Arthemius, his wife and their daughter.



      When they were taken out for execution, Marcellinus and Peter were privately brought to a wood called the Silva Niagra and there beheaded. Their persecutors had already dug the graves before the execution so that the place of their burial would not be known to the other Christians. However, the secret was divulged by the executioner himself who later became a Christian. Two devout women, Lucilla and Firmina, rescued their relics and interred them honourably in the catacomb of Tiburtius on the Via Lavicana. Emperor Constantine had a basilica built over the crypt in which they were buried. He later buried his own mother, St Helena, there, too.



      Pope Damasus I composed a poetic epitaph for Sts Marcellinus and Peter that was apparently founded on the report of their executioner. They are commemorated in the Canon of the Mass (Eucharistic Prayer I).
